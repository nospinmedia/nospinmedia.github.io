Computer Castle BBS Memorial

Newton, New Hampshire · 1987–1997

Preserving the history of one of New Hampshire's largest Bulletin Board Systems.

Welcome back. Whether you called Computer Castle once or a thousand times, we hope this site brings back a few memories. If you have photographs, screenshots, documentation, or stories from the Castle, we'd love to help preserve them for future generations.

Welcome

The Board That Was a Second Home

Long before broadband, before social media, before the internet most people know today, Computer Castle BBS was where a community of callers across southern New Hampshire and Massachusetts logged in — one modem screech at a time. From 1987 to 1997 it grew into the largest BBS in New Hampshire, with roughly a thousand active users dialing in for message boards, live chat rooms, and door games like Legend of the Red Dragon and Trade Wars 2002. This site exists so none of that is forgotten.

History

The Board That Was

  • 1987 — The Castle opens. Computer Castle BBS goes online in Newton, New Hampshire, run by SysOp John R. McNally (FidoNet 1:324/127).
  • Growth Over the following decade it becomes the largest BBS in New Hampshire — 19 direct dial-in lines, 15 forwarding numbers reaching into three states, 3 Telnet nodes, and a Planet Connect satellite FidoNet feed, alongside internet email and Usenet access.
  • Peak activity Around 1,000 active users called in for thousands of message and file areas, multi-room live chat ("Rapping"), and dozens of door games — Legend of the Red Dragon, Trade Wars 2002, Land of Devastation, Yankee Trader, and Global Wars, plus the "Medieval Games" lineup (Excalibur, Kannons & Katapults, The Pit, Assassin, Iron Ox, Mines of Gorr, Axrud) — with a multiplayer DOOM-over-modem door once advertised as "Coming Soon."
  • Community Over the years the system was kept running by SysOp John R. McNally and Co-SysOps including Deb McNally, Scott Currier, Benjamin Scott, Jim Wilcox, Beth Hamel, Rob Miller, Bruce Morse, and Eric Winch, with monthly in-person user meetings bringing the online community together face to face.
  • July 6, 1997 — The Castle closes. After more than ten years of nearly continuous operation, Computer Castle BBS shut down for good when NYNEX raised phone rates beyond what the system could sustain.
"We are deeply sorry to have to say that, after more then ten years of nearly continuous operation, Computer Castle BBS is going to close down. NYNEX has raised our phone rates to the point where we can no longer afford our phone lines... We would like to thank all of you who have continued to call and support us with donations of all kinds. Without you, we could never have come as far as we have." — The System Operators of Computer Castle BBS, closing announcement, July 1997

Preservation

Why This Site Exists

"I just wanted to say 'Thanks' one last time, to everyone who's helped make this possible. To all of our users, past and present, who called and made this BBS something worth calling... Thanks, everyone. Clear skies!" — Benjamin Scott, Co-SysOp, 1997

Computer Castle BBS closed in 1997, but the community it built never really logged off. This site is an independent historical preservation project dedicated to documenting Computer Castle's history — for the people who lived it, and for everyone who never got to see a real BBS logon screen. We're actively gathering photographs, stories, documentation, and any surviving artifacts from the Castle's ten years online, and this page will keep growing as more of that history surfaces.

Looking Ahead

A Future Restoration Project

Beyond preserving Computer Castle's history, we're also exploring what it would take to bring the experience itself back to life — a modern, browser-based recreation of the BBS, built to feel as close as possible to the original: the same menus, the same live chat, the same door games people actually remember. Nothing is running yet, and this is very much an early-stage exploration, but this page will be updated as that project takes shape.
