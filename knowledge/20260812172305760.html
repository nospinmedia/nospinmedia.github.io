What Is a Presidential Executive Order?

August 17, 202611 min read

What Is a Presidential Executive Order?

A presidential executive order is a written directive issued by the president of the United States to manage the operations of the federal government or exercise authority given to the president by the Constitution or federal law.

Executive orders can have significant legal and policy effects, but they do not give a president unlimited power. A valid executive order must be based on authority the president already possesses under the Constitution or authority Congress has granted through legislation.

An executive order cannot override the Constitution or lawfully disregard an act of Congress simply because the president disagrees with it.

Where Does the Power to Issue Executive Orders Come From?

The Constitution does not specifically mention executive orders or establish a general executive-order power.

Instead, their authority comes from the powers assigned to the president by Article II of the Constitution and from powers that Congress delegates to the president through federal statutes.

Article II vests the executive power in the president and requires the president to take care that federal laws are faithfully executed. Congress also frequently passes laws giving the executive branch discretion over how particular programs or policies are administered.

Executive orders are one method presidents use to exercise those constitutional and statutory powers.

Is an Executive Order a Law?

An executive order is not legislation passed by Congress.

However, a valid executive order issued under constitutional or statutory authority can have the force and effect of law.

That distinction is important. Congress creates federal statutes through the legislative process. A president cannot use an executive order to give themselves powers that neither the Constitution nor federal law provides.

The legal effect of an executive order therefore depends on the authority supporting it and what the order actually requires.

What Can an Executive Order Do?

Executive orders are frequently used to direct federal departments, agencies, and officials in carrying out their responsibilities.

Depending on the president's underlying authority, an executive order may:

  • Establish policies for executive branch agencies.
  • Direct how federal officials implement existing laws.
  • Organize or reorganize certain executive branch functions within legal limits.
  • Set priorities for federal agencies.
  • Establish procedures for federal employees or government operations.
  • Exercise authority that Congress has specifically delegated to the president.
  • Exercise certain powers that the Constitution assigns directly to the president.

Some executive orders primarily affect the internal operation of government. Others can have significant effects on individuals, businesses, federal contractors, or broader national policy when the president has legal authority to take the underlying action.

Can a President Make a New Law by Executive Order?

Not in the same way Congress can.

The Constitution gives legislative power to Congress. The president cannot bypass Congress and create a federal statute merely by calling a directive an executive order.

An executive order instead must rely on existing presidential authority.

Sometimes Congress passes a law that gives the president substantial discretion over how that law will operate. An executive order may then direct agencies on how to exercise that delegated authority.

This can make an executive order look similar to legislation in its practical effect, but the source of the president's authority remains important.

Can an Executive Order Override a Federal Law?

No.

A president cannot lawfully override a federal statute when Congress has constitutional authority over the subject and the president lacks independent constitutional authority to disregard that law.

When an executive order conflicts with an act of Congress, courts may be asked to determine whether the president had authority to act.

One of the most important cases involving this question is Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer, decided by the Supreme Court in 1952.

During the Korean War, President Harry Truman issued an executive order directing the federal government to seize and operate most of the nation's steel mills to prevent a strike from disrupting steel production.

The Supreme Court ruled that the president lacked authority to seize the mills. Congress had not authorized the seizure, and the president's constitutional powers did not independently provide the necessary authority.

What Is the Youngstown Framework?

Justice Robert Jackson wrote an influential concurring opinion in the Youngstown case describing three general situations involving presidential power. The Supreme Court has subsequently used this framework when evaluating presidential authority.

Presidential authority is generally:

  1. Strongest when the president acts with authorization from Congress. The president can rely on both executive authority and power Congress has lawfully delegated.
  2. More uncertain when Congress has neither authorized nor prohibited the action. The president must rely more heavily on independent constitutional authority, and the legal analysis depends on the circumstances.
  3. At its weakest when the president acts contrary to the expressed or implied will of Congress. In that situation, the president generally must rely on constitutional powers that belong to the executive branch independently of Congress.

This framework helps courts analyze disputes over whether presidential action exceeds executive authority.

Can an Executive Order Override the Constitution?

No.

The Constitution is the supreme law of the United States. An executive order that violates the Constitution can be challenged in federal court when the legal requirements for bringing a case are satisfied.

For example, challenges may argue that an executive order violates constitutional rights or attempts to exercise power assigned to another branch of government.

Calling an action an executive order does not shield it from constitutional limits.

Can Courts Block an Executive Order?

Yes, when a proper legal case is brought and a court determines that the challenged action is unlawful.

Courts may examine whether the president had constitutional or statutory authority for an order, whether the order exceeds authority granted by Congress, or whether its implementation violates the Constitution or federal law.

A court may prevent an unlawful executive action from being enforced when the legal requirements for judicial relief are met.

Courts do not automatically review executive orders when they are issued. A legal dispute generally must be brought by a party with the right to challenge the action.

Can Congress Stop an Executive Order?

Sometimes, but Congress cannot simply erase every executive order with a resolution expressing disagreement.

If an executive order relies on authority Congress previously granted through legislation, Congress may be able to change or repeal that underlying authority by passing another law. Congress can also sometimes restrict funding used to implement an executive policy.

Legislation must still go through the normal constitutional process. The president may veto legislation aimed at overturning an executive policy, and Congress would then need the required supermajorities to override the veto.

Congress generally cannot use ordinary legislation to eliminate powers that the Constitution gives exclusively to the president.

Congress can also codify an executive policy into federal law. Doing so can make the policy more durable because a future president cannot simply repeal a federal statute through another executive order.

Can the Next President Cancel an Executive Order?

Generally, yes.

A sitting president can usually revoke, modify, or replace executive orders issued by previous presidents, particularly when those orders concern executive branch policies within presidential control.

This is one reason executive policies can change rapidly when a new administration takes office.

A president may revoke a predecessor's order by issuing another executive order or presidential directive.

However, changing an executive order does not necessarily undo every action that occurred while the earlier order was in effect. In addition, if Congress has placed the policy into federal law, a new president cannot repeal that statute merely by issuing another order.

Do Executive Orders Automatically Expire When a President Leaves Office?

No.

An executive order does not automatically disappear at the end of a president's term.

It can remain in effect across multiple administrations until it is revoked, amended, superseded, invalidated through the courts, displaced by legislation where Congress has authority to do so, or terminated according to provisions contained in the order or applicable law.

Some executive orders have remained in effect for many years while others are quickly reversed by later presidents.

Who Has to Follow an Executive Order?

Many executive orders are directed primarily at federal departments, agencies, officials, or employees because the president heads the executive branch.

The effects can extend beyond federal employees when an order lawfully exercises authority affecting areas such as federal contracting, immigration, sanctions, government benefits, federal lands, or regulatory programs.

Whether an executive order creates obligations affecting people outside the federal government depends on the legal authority behind the order and how federal agencies implement it.

Is an Executive Order the Same as a Federal Regulation?

No.

An executive order is issued by the president. A federal regulation is generally issued by a federal agency under authority provided by law.

Many agency regulations are governed by the Administrative Procedure Act and other laws that may require procedures such as publishing a proposed rule and allowing public comment before a final rule takes effect.

An executive order can direct an agency to pursue a policy, but the agency may still have to follow legally required rulemaking procedures when implementing that directive.

The president cannot necessarily use an executive order to bypass procedures that federal law requires an agency to follow.

What Is the Difference Between an Executive Order, Memorandum, and Proclamation?

Presidents issue several types of written directives.

An executive order is a formally numbered presidential directive commonly used to direct executive branch operations or exercise presidential authority.

A presidential memorandum is another type of presidential directive. Memoranda can have significant legal effects when supported by valid presidential authority, even though they are not called executive orders.

A presidential proclamation is often used for ceremonial declarations or to announce presidential actions directed more broadly outside the executive branch. Some proclamations have substantial legal effects when issued under constitutional or statutory authority.

The title of a presidential document does not by itself determine how much legal authority it has. The underlying source of presidential power and the substance of the directive are more important.

Are Executive Orders Published?

Executive orders are generally published in the Federal Register, the federal government's official publication for presidential documents, agency rules, proposed regulations, and related materials.

Executive orders are also numbered, making it possible to identify and track individual orders across administrations.

Publication allows Congress, courts, federal agencies, researchers, journalists, and the public to examine the actual text rather than relying only on descriptions of what an order supposedly does.

Common Misunderstandings

An Executive Order Lets the President Make Any Law Without Congress

No.

An executive order must be supported by constitutional or statutory authority. It cannot create unlimited presidential power.

An Executive Order Automatically Overrides Congress

No.

A president cannot lawfully use an executive order to override a federal statute simply because the president disagrees with it.

Executive Orders Are Automatically Unconstitutional

No.

Presidents from throughout U.S. history have used executive directives. Their legality depends on the authority supporting the particular action and whether it complies with the Constitution and federal law.

Executive Orders Automatically End When a President Leaves Office

No.

An executive order can continue into later administrations unless it is revoked, modified, superseded, invalidated, or otherwise terminated.

The Supreme Court Must Approve an Executive Order

No.

Executive orders do not require advance approval from the Supreme Court. Courts become involved when a proper legal challenge is brought before them.

Congress Can Cancel Any Executive Order With a Simple Vote

Not necessarily.

Congress can legislate in areas within its constitutional authority and can alter authority it previously delegated to the president, but legislation must pass through the normal constitutional process. Congress also cannot simply eliminate powers assigned exclusively to the president by the Constitution.

Why Executive Orders Matter

Executive orders allow presidents to act relatively quickly within areas where they already possess constitutional or statutory authority. That makes them an important tool for managing the federal government and changing executive branch policy.

Their speed also explains why executive orders often become politically controversial. A president may use existing authority to make significant policy changes without waiting for new legislation from Congress.

But speed does not eliminate legal limits. Congress controls important legislative and spending powers, courts can resolve legal challenges, federal agencies may have to follow statutory procedures when implementing presidential directives, and later presidents can often reverse executive policies.

Understanding an executive order therefore requires looking beyond the announcement itself and asking a more important question: What constitutional or statutory authority allows the president to take this action?

Conclusion

A presidential executive order is a written directive through which the president exercises authority over the executive branch or uses powers granted by the Constitution or federal law.

A valid executive order can have the force and effect of law, but it is not legislation and does not give the president unlimited authority. Executive orders cannot override the Constitution, and presidential action must remain within the powers available to the executive branch.

Congress may alter policies based on authority it has delegated, courts may review challenged executive actions, and future presidents can generally modify or revoke earlier executive orders. Those checks help define both the power and the limits of executive orders in the American system of government.
