What Is the United Nations?

July 7, 20265 min read

What Is the United Nations?

The United Nations (UN) is an international organization created to promote peace, security, cooperation, and friendly relations among countries. Founded in 1945 after the end of World War II, the United Nations provides a forum where governments can work together on global issues such as conflict, human rights, humanitarian aid, climate change, public health, and economic development.

Today, nearly every internationally recognized country is a member of the United Nations, making it one of the world's largest international organizations. Although the UN cannot govern individual countries, it serves as a place where nations negotiate agreements, coordinate international responses to crises, and address challenges that affect multiple countries.

Why Was the United Nations Created?

The United Nations was established in the aftermath of World War II, a conflict that caused unprecedented destruction and loss of life.

World leaders hoped that creating an international organization dedicated to diplomacy and cooperation would help prevent another global war.

The United Nations Charter was signed on June 26, 1945, in San Francisco, California, and the organization officially began operations on October 24, 1945. That date is now observed each year as United Nations Day.

The UN replaced the earlier League of Nations, which had been created after World War I but was ultimately unable to prevent World War II.

What Does the United Nations Do?

The United Nations has a broad range of responsibilities.

Its work includes:

  • Promoting international peace and security.
  • Supporting diplomatic negotiations between countries.
  • Coordinating humanitarian assistance during disasters and conflicts.
  • Protecting and promoting human rights.
  • Supporting sustainable economic and social development.
  • Addressing global health challenges.
  • Helping countries respond to climate and environmental issues.
  • Assisting with international law and treaty development.

Many of these activities are carried out through specialized agencies and programs that operate under the broader UN system.

Who Belongs to the United Nations?

As of 2026, the United Nations has 193 member states.

Membership is open to sovereign countries that agree to accept the obligations outlined in the UN Charter and are approved through the organization's membership process.

Each member nation has one vote in the United Nations General Assembly, regardless of its population or size.

How Is the United Nations Organized?

The United Nations consists of several principal bodies, each with different responsibilities.

General Assembly

The General Assembly includes all 193 member states.

It serves as the UN's main deliberative body, where countries debate international issues and vote on resolutions. Most General Assembly resolutions are not legally binding, but they often reflect the views of the international community.

Security Council

The Security Council is responsible for maintaining international peace and security.

It has 15 members:

  • 5 permanent members: China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom, and the United States.
  • 10 non-permanent members, elected by the General Assembly for two-year terms.

The five permanent members each possess veto power, allowing any one of them to block many substantive Security Council resolutions.

Secretariat

The Secretariat carries out the day-to-day work of the United Nations.

It is led by the Secretary-General, who serves as the organization's chief administrative officer and often acts as an international diplomat during global crises.

International Court of Justice

Located in The Hague, Netherlands, the International Court of Justice (ICJ) settles legal disputes between countries and issues advisory opinions on questions of international law.

Economic and Social Council

The Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) coordinates many of the UN's economic, social, and development programs and works with numerous specialized agencies and non-governmental organizations.

What Are UN Agencies?

Many well-known international organizations operate as part of the broader United Nations system.

Examples include:

  • World Health Organization (WHO)
  • United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF)
  • United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR)
  • United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO)
  • Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO)
  • World Food Programme (WFP)

Each agency has its own mission while working within the broader framework of international cooperation.

How Are UN Decisions Made?

Different UN bodies follow different voting procedures.

In the General Assembly, most decisions are made through majority or two-thirds votes, depending on the issue.

In the Security Council, substantive resolutions generally require at least nine affirmative votes and no veto from any of the five permanent members.

Because of the veto system, proposed Security Council actions can fail even if most member countries support them.

Why Is the United Nations Sometimes Controversial?

The United Nations frequently addresses complex international issues involving war, human rights, sanctions, peacekeeping, and humanitarian assistance.

Supporters argue that the organization provides an essential forum for diplomacy, conflict resolution, humanitarian coordination, and international cooperation.

Critics have raised concerns about bureaucracy, funding, the effectiveness of some peacekeeping missions, unequal influence among member states, and the Security Council's veto system, which can prevent action even when a majority supports a proposal.

These debates have led to ongoing discussions about possible reforms to the United Nations.

Common Misunderstandings

One common misconception is that the United Nations governs the world. It does not. The UN is an organization made up of independent sovereign nations that voluntarily cooperate on international issues.

Another misconception is that all UN resolutions are legally binding. While some Security Council resolutions may be binding under international law, most General Assembly resolutions express the collective views of member states without creating binding legal obligations.

It is also sometimes assumed that every UN agency performs the same role. In reality, the United Nations consists of numerous specialized agencies and programs, each with distinct responsibilities.

Conclusion

The United Nations is an international organization founded in 1945 to promote peace, security, cooperation, and development among nations. Through its principal bodies and specialized agencies, it addresses a wide range of global challenges, from humanitarian crises and public health to international law and conflict resolution.

Although member countries do not always agree on how the organization should operate, the United Nations remains the world's primary forum for international diplomacy and continues to play a central role in addressing issues that affect people around the globe.
