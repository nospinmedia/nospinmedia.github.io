How Does the U.S. Visa System Work?

July 26, 20264 min read

How Does the U.S. Visa System Work?

A U.S. visa is an official authorization that allows a foreign national to travel to a U.S. port of entry and request permission to enter the country for a specific purpose, such as tourism, study, work, or immigration.

Having a visa does not guarantee entry into the United States. A U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) officer makes the final decision on whether a traveler may enter the country.

Who Issues U.S. Visas?

Most U.S. visas are issued by the U.S. Department of State through American embassies and consulates around the world.

Applicants typically complete an application, pay required fees, provide supporting documents, and may be required to attend an interview before a visa is approved or denied.

What Is the Difference Between Immigrant and Nonimmigrant Visas?

The United States issues two broad categories of visas.

Nonimmigrant visas are for people visiting temporarily, including tourists, students, business travelers, temporary workers, exchange visitors, and others.

Immigrant visas are for individuals who intend to live permanently in the United States and eventually become lawful permanent residents, commonly known as green card holders.

What Are Some Common Nonimmigrant Visas?

Some of the most common temporary visa categories include:

  • B-1: Business visitors.
  • B-2: Tourists and certain medical visitors.
  • F-1: Academic students.
  • J-1: Exchange visitors.
  • M-1: Vocational students.
  • H-1B: Specialty occupation workers.
  • H-2A: Temporary agricultural workers.
  • H-2B: Temporary non-agricultural workers.
  • L-1: Intracompany transfers.
  • O-1: Individuals with extraordinary ability or achievement.
  • K-1: Fiancé(e)s of U.S. citizens.

Each visa category has its own eligibility requirements and permitted activities.

What Are Immigrant Visas?

Immigrant visas generally fall into several categories, including:

  • Family-sponsored immigration.
  • Employment-based immigration.
  • Diversity Visa Lottery recipients.
  • Certain humanitarian programs authorized by law.

People who receive immigrant visas may become lawful permanent residents after entering the United States.

How Long Can Someone Stay?

The length of time someone may remain in the United States depends on the visa category and the conditions of admission.

The visa itself allows a person to seek entry, while the period of authorized stay is generally determined by CBP when the traveler is admitted. Some visa categories allow extensions or changes of status if legal requirements are met.

Can a Visa Be Denied or Revoked?

Yes.

A visa application may be denied if an applicant does not meet legal requirements or fails to provide sufficient documentation.

An issued visa may also be revoked under certain circumstances, including security concerns, fraud, changes in eligibility, or violations of U.S. immigration laws.

What Is the Difference Between a Visa and a Green Card?

A visa generally allows someone to travel to the United States and request admission for a temporary or permanent purpose.

A green card, officially known as a Permanent Resident Card, allows a person to live and work permanently in the United States as a lawful permanent resident.

The two are related but are not the same.

What Is the Visa Waiver Program?

Citizens of certain countries may travel to the United States for tourism or business without obtaining a visa through the Visa Waiver Program (VWP) if they meet program requirements.

Most travelers using the Visa Waiver Program must receive approval through the Electronic System for Travel Authorization (ESTA) before boarding a flight or ship to the United States.

Why Is the U.S. Visa System Frequently in the News?

The visa system is commonly mentioned in news involving:

  • Student visas.
  • Work visas.
  • Travel restrictions.
  • Immigration policy changes.
  • Visa processing delays.
  • International students.
  • Temporary worker programs.
  • National security measures.
  • Border enforcement.

Changes to visa rules can affect travelers, employers, universities, businesses, and families around the world.

Common Misunderstandings

One common misconception is that a visa guarantees entry into the United States. In reality, admission is determined by a CBP officer at the port of entry.

Another misunderstanding is that all visas allow someone to work. Many visa categories prohibit employment or allow work only under specific conditions.

Finally, a visa is not the same as U.S. citizenship or permanent residency. Many visas are temporary and expire after a specified period or when their conditions are no longer met.

Conclusion

The U.S. visa system allows foreign nationals to travel to the United States for specific purposes, including tourism, study, work, business, and permanent immigration. Different visa categories have different requirements and limitations, and receiving a visa does not guarantee entry. Understanding how the visa system works helps explain many of the immigration, education, employment, and travel stories that appear in the news.
