What Is an ICE Detainer (Immigration Hold)?

July 27, 20265 min read

What Is an ICE Detainer (Immigration Hold)?

An ICE detainer, sometimes called an immigration hold, is a written request issued by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), commonly using Form I-247A (Immigration Detainer – Notice of Action), asking a federal, state, tribal, or local law enforcement agency to notify ICE before releasing a person from custody. In many cases, the detainer also asks the agency to temporarily continue holding the individual for a limited period after they would otherwise be released so ICE can assume custody.

An ICE detainer is an administrative immigration request. It is not the same as a criminal arrest warrant or a search warrant signed by a judge.

Why Does ICE Issue Detainers?

ICE uses detainers to help identify and take custody of certain non-citizens who may be subject to immigration enforcement after they have been arrested or detained by another law enforcement agency.

A detainer may be issued when ICE believes an individual could be removable under federal immigration law or when ICE needs additional time to assume custody after the person's local or state case has concluded.

Issuing a detainer does not determine whether someone will ultimately be removed from the United States. Immigration status and removability are separate legal questions that may be decided through the immigration process.

How Does an ICE Detainer Work?

Although every case is different, the process generally follows these steps:

  1. A person is arrested or detained by a state or local law enforcement agency.
  2. ICE becomes aware that the individual is in custody.
  3. ICE issues an immigration detainer.
  4. The agency decides whether to honor the detainer, subject to applicable federal, state, and local laws and its own policies.
  5. If the detainer is honored, ICE may assume custody after the person's local custody would otherwise end.

Not every detainer results in ICE taking custody, and not every jurisdiction responds to detainers in the same way.

Who Receives ICE Detainers?

ICE commonly sends detainers to:

  • County jails.
  • City police departments.
  • Sheriff's offices.
  • State correctional facilities.
  • Other law enforcement agencies that have custody of an individual.

The detainer is directed to the agency currently holding the person, not to the individual.

Are Local Police Required to Honor an ICE Detainer?

Not always.

An ICE detainer is generally considered a request rather than a mandatory order. Whether a law enforcement agency honors a detainer depends on federal law, state law, local policies, and applicable court decisions.

Some jurisdictions routinely cooperate with ICE detainers. Others limit cooperation or honor detainers only under specific circumstances established by law or local policy.

As a result, procedures can vary significantly from one jurisdiction to another.

Is an ICE Detainer the Same as a Warrant?

No.

This is one of the most common misunderstandings.

An ICE detainer is an administrative immigration request. It is not the same as:

  • A judicial search warrant.
  • A judicial arrest warrant.
  • A criminal arrest warrant issued by a court.

ICE may also issue administrative immigration warrants in certain situations, but those are separate documents with different legal purposes. Administrative warrants are generally not signed by a judge and are distinct from criminal warrants issued by a court.

How Long Can Someone Be Held?

When a law enforcement agency honors an ICE detainer, ICE generally requests that the individual be held for up to 48 hours, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and federal holidays, after the person would otherwise be released from custody.

The purpose of this additional time is to allow ICE an opportunity to assume custody if appropriate under federal immigration law.

Whether an agency honors the request, and how detention is handled, depends on the applicable laws, court decisions, and local policies.

What Have Courts Said About ICE Detainers?

Federal courts have considered numerous legal questions involving ICE detainers, including the authority to continue holding individuals after they would otherwise be released.

Court decisions have helped shape how detainers are used, and many states and local governments have adopted policies based on those rulings. As a result, the treatment of ICE detainers is not identical across the United States.

Because court decisions and state laws continue to evolve, agencies may have different procedures depending on where they operate.

Does an ICE Detainer Mean Someone Will Be Deported?

No.

An ICE detainer does not determine whether someone will be removed from the United States.

Removal generally requires a separate immigration process. Depending on the circumstances, an individual may have the opportunity to appear before an immigration judge, seek relief available under immigration law, or otherwise challenge removal.

Common Misunderstandings

An ICE Detainer Is a Search Warrant

No.

A detainer is an administrative immigration request. It is different from a judicial search warrant signed by a judge.

An ICE Detainer Is a Criminal Arrest Warrant

No.

A detainer is separate from criminal warrants and does not itself establish criminal guilt.

Every Police Department Must Honor an ICE Detainer

No.

Whether a detainer is honored depends on the laws, court decisions, and policies that apply to the agency involved.

Receiving an ICE Detainer Means Someone Will Be Deported

No.

A detainer is only one step in the immigration enforcement process. Removal depends on the individual's circumstances and the applicable immigration laws.

Why This Matters

ICE detainers are frequently mentioned in news reports involving immigration enforcement, local law enforcement cooperation, and so-called sanctuary policies. Understanding what a detainer is—and what it is not—helps explain why different jurisdictions may respond differently to the same type of ICE request.

Recognizing the difference between an immigration detainer, an administrative immigration warrant, and a judicial warrant also helps readers better understand how immigration enforcement operates within the broader legal system.

Conclusion

An ICE detainer, or immigration hold, is an administrative request asking another law enforcement agency to notify ICE before releasing a person from custody and, in some circumstances, to temporarily continue holding that individual so ICE can assume custody. It is not a judicial warrant, does not itself determine whether someone will be deported, and may be handled differently depending on federal law, state law, local policies, and court decisions.
