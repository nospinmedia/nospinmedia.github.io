What Is UNRWA?

July 4, 20264 min read

What Is UNRWA?

UNRWA stands for the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East. It is a specialized agency of the United Nations that provides education, healthcare, humanitarian assistance, and social services to registered Palestinian refugees in parts of the Middle East.

UNRWA is often mentioned in news reports about the Gaza Strip, the West Bank, and regional conflicts, yet many people are unfamiliar with its purpose or how it differs from other United Nations organizations. Understanding what UNRWA does and why it exists helps provide important context for international events.

Why Was UNRWA Created?

UNRWA was established by the United Nations General Assembly in 1949, following the 1948 Arab-Israeli War.

The conflict displaced hundreds of thousands of Palestinians, creating a large refugee population across the region. The agency began operations in 1950 with the goal of providing temporary humanitarian assistance while political leaders sought a long-term solution to the refugee situation.

Although it was originally intended as a temporary organization, the absence of a permanent political resolution has resulted in UNRWA continuing its work for decades.

Where Does UNRWA Operate?

UNRWA provides services in five designated areas of operation:

  • Gaza Strip
  • West Bank, including East Jerusalem
  • Jordan
  • Lebanon
  • Syria

The agency does not operate worldwide. Its mission is limited to registered Palestinian refugees within these areas.

What Does UNRWA Do?

UNRWA provides a wide range of civilian services.

Its primary responsibilities include:

  • Operating schools for children.
  • Providing primary healthcare through clinics.
  • Delivering food assistance during emergencies.
  • Offering social services for vulnerable families.
  • Supporting infrastructure and public services within refugee camps.
  • Coordinating humanitarian relief during armed conflicts and other emergencies.

In many locations where it operates, UNRWA serves as one of the largest providers of education and basic healthcare.

Who Funds UNRWA?

Unlike many government agencies, UNRWA is not funded through mandatory United Nations membership dues.

Instead, it relies primarily on voluntary contributions from governments and other donors.

Historically, major contributors have included countries such as the United States, Germany, Japan, Canada, members of the European Union, and several other nations. Funding levels vary from year to year, and some countries have temporarily suspended or restored contributions in response to political or security developments.

Because the agency depends heavily on voluntary donations, its budget can fluctuate significantly.

Who Does UNRWA Serve?

UNRWA serves people who meet the agency's definition of a registered Palestinian refugee.

According to UNRWA, this generally includes individuals whose normal place of residence was in Mandatory Palestine before the 1948 Arab-Israeli War, who lost both their homes and means of livelihood because of the conflict, and many of their eligible descendants.

The agency maintains a registry of those eligible to receive its services.

How Is UNRWA Different From UNHCR?

One of the most common points of confusion is the difference between UNRWA and UNHCR.

UNHCR, the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees, is the UN agency responsible for assisting most refugee populations around the world.

UNRWA, by contrast, has a much narrower mission. It serves registered Palestinian refugees within its five designated areas of operation, while UNHCR serves refugees from nearly every other region of the world.

The two organizations have different histories, legal mandates, and operational responsibilities.

Why Is UNRWA Controversial?

UNRWA has been the subject of debate for many years.

Supporters argue that the agency provides essential education, healthcare, and humanitarian assistance to millions of people who would otherwise have limited access to basic services.

Critics have raised concerns about the agency's refugee registration system, its long-term role in the region, financial oversight, and allegations involving some employees. At various times, allegations against individual staff members have prompted investigations by the United Nations and reviews of funding by several donor governments.

Governments and international organizations have responded differently to these issues, and discussions about UNRWA's future remain part of broader debates surrounding the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

Common Misunderstandings

Several misconceptions frequently appear in discussions about UNRWA.

One is that UNRWA represents the entire United Nations. In reality, it is one specialized UN agency with a specific humanitarian mission.

Another misconception is that UNRWA governs the Gaza Strip or the West Bank. It does not. Its role is to provide humanitarian and public services, not to govern territory.

It is also sometimes assumed that UNRWA and UNHCR perform the same function. While both assist refugees, they operate under different mandates and serve different populations.

Conclusion

UNRWA is a specialized United Nations agency established in 1949 to provide humanitarian assistance and public services to registered Palestinian refugees in the Middle East. Its work includes education, healthcare, emergency relief, and social services across five areas of operation.

Although its humanitarian mission has continued for more than seven decades, UNRWA remains at the center of ongoing discussions about regional conflict, refugee policy, international aid, and the future of humanitarian assistance in the Middle East.
