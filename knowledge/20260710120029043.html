What Is the European Union?

July 10, 20266 min read

What Is the European Union?

The European Union (EU) is a political and economic partnership made up of 27 European countries that cooperate on a wide range of issues, including trade, travel, environmental policy, consumer protection, agriculture, and foreign affairs.

Although its member countries remain independent nations with their own governments, the European Union allows them to work together in areas where shared rules and coordinated policies provide mutual benefits. As of 2026, the European Union remains one of the world's largest economies and one of the world's most influential political and economic organizations, with 27 member states and 21 countries using the euro as their official currency.

Why Was the European Union Created?

The origins of the European Union can be traced to the years following World War II.

After two devastating world wars, European leaders sought ways to strengthen economic cooperation and reduce the likelihood of future conflicts.

The first major step came in 1951 with the creation of the European Coal and Steel Community, which encouraged countries to jointly manage industries that had been essential to wartime production.

In 1957, six countries signed the Treaty of Rome, creating the European Economic Community (EEC). Over time, additional treaties expanded cooperation, and in 1993, the Treaty on European Union, commonly known as the Maastricht Treaty, officially established the European Union.

Who Belongs to the European Union?

As of 2026, the European Union has 27 member countries.

Each member state remains an independent sovereign nation while agreeing to follow shared rules in areas covered by EU law.

Some well-known member countries include:

  • France
  • Germany
  • Italy
  • Spain
  • Poland
  • Sweden
  • Ireland
  • Greece
  • Netherlands

Other European countries, including Norway, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom, are not members of the European Union.

The United Kingdom left the EU on January 31, 2020, following a process commonly known as Brexit.

What Does the European Union Do?

The European Union develops common policies and laws in areas where member countries have agreed to cooperate.

Its responsibilities include:

  • Promoting free trade among member countries.
  • Establishing common product and consumer safety standards.
  • Supporting environmental and climate policies.
  • Coordinating agricultural programs.
  • Negotiating trade agreements with other countries.
  • Protecting competition within the single market.
  • Supporting research, education, and regional development.

Many policy areas, such as education, healthcare, and national defense, remain primarily the responsibility of individual member countries.

What Is the EU Single Market?

One of the European Union's most important achievements is the Single Market.

The Single Market allows the free movement of:

  • Goods.
  • Services.
  • People.
  • Capital.

This means businesses can often sell products across EU member states without facing customs duties or many of the regulatory barriers that normally exist between countries.

Citizens of EU member states also enjoy broad rights to live, work, study, and travel throughout much of the European Union, subject to applicable laws.

What Is the Euro?

The euro (€) is the common currency used by many—but not all—European Union member countries.

Countries that use the euro make up the eurozone.

As of 2026, the euro is the official currency of 21 of the European Union's 27 member states. Bulgaria adopted the euro on January 1, 2026, becoming the newest member of the eurozone.

Several EU countries continue to use their own national currencies. Poland, Czechia, Hungary, Romania, and Sweden have not yet adopted the euro, while Denmark has a permanent opt-out that allows it to retain the Danish krone.

As a result, membership in the European Union and membership in the eurozone are not the same thing.

How Is the European Union Governed?

The European Union has several major institutions that share responsibility for making decisions.

European Council

The European Council brings together the heads of state or government of each member country. It sets the EU's overall political direction and priorities.

European Commission

The European Commission proposes new legislation, oversees implementation of EU law, and manages many of the Union's day-to-day responsibilities.

European Parliament

Members of the European Parliament are directly elected by citizens of EU member countries. The Parliament helps debate, amend, and approve legislation.

Council of the European Union

Often called the Council of Ministers, this institution represents the governments of member states and works with the European Parliament to adopt laws and coordinate policies.

Court of Justice of the European Union

The Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) interprets EU law and helps ensure it is applied consistently across member states.

Why Is the European Union Important?

The European Union is one of the world's largest trading blocs and has significant influence over global economic and regulatory policies.

Its decisions can affect:

  • International trade.
  • Product safety standards.
  • Digital privacy regulations.
  • Environmental policy.
  • Competition law.
  • Consumer protections.

Because of the size of its economy, many companies around the world adapt their products and business practices to comply with EU regulations.

Why Is the European Union Sometimes Controversial?

Supporters argue that the European Union promotes peace, economic cooperation, free trade, and easier travel among member countries.

Critics have expressed concerns about the balance between national sovereignty and EU decision-making, the complexity of its institutions, immigration policy, economic regulations, and how authority is divided between the Union and individual member states.

These debates continue to shape discussions about the future of European integration.

Common Misunderstandings

One common misconception is that the European Union is a single country. It is not. It is a partnership of independent sovereign nations.

Another misconception is that every European country belongs to the EU. Several European countries are not members.

It is also sometimes assumed that every EU country uses the euro. In reality, several member states continue to use their own national currencies.

Finally, the European Union should not be confused with the Council of Europe, a separate international organization focused primarily on human rights and democracy.

Conclusion

The European Union is a partnership of 27 independent European countries that cooperate on economic, political, and regulatory issues. Through its shared institutions and the Single Market, the EU has become one of the world's most influential international organizations.

As of 2026, 21 member countries use the euro as their common currency, while several others continue to use national currencies under existing treaty arrangements. Although member countries retain their sovereignty, they also agree to work together in many areas to promote trade, stability, and cooperation. Understanding how the European Union operates helps explain many of the political, economic, and diplomatic developments that shape Europe and the wider world.
