What Is CISA?

August 4, 20264 min read

What Is CISA?

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) is a federal agency within the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS). Its mission is to help protect the nation's critical infrastructure from cyber threats, physical security risks, and other hazards.

CISA works with federal agencies, state and local governments, tribal and territorial governments, private companies, and international partners to improve cybersecurity and strengthen the resilience of essential services.

Although CISA plays a major role in election security and cybersecurity, it does not investigate crimes, conduct military operations, or operate intelligence programs.

When Was CISA Created?

CISA was established in 2018 when Congress passed the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency Act, which reorganized and expanded the former National Protection and Programs Directorate within the Department of Homeland Security.

The agency was created to provide a centralized federal organization focused on protecting critical infrastructure from evolving cyber and physical threats.

What Does CISA Do?

CISA's responsibilities include:

  • Helping organizations defend against cyberattacks.
  • Monitoring and sharing information about cybersecurity threats.
  • Coordinating responses to major cyber incidents.
  • Protecting critical infrastructure.
  • Supporting election security.
  • Providing cybersecurity guidance and best practices.
  • Assessing risks to essential systems and services.

Rather than regulating most private organizations, CISA primarily serves as a resource that offers technical assistance, threat information, and voluntary security guidance.

What Is Critical Infrastructure?

Critical infrastructure refers to systems and facilities that are considered essential to the nation's security, economy, public health, and daily life.

Examples include:

  • Electric power grids.
  • Water systems.
  • Communications networks.
  • Hospitals.
  • Transportation systems.
  • Financial services.
  • Emergency services.
  • Election infrastructure.

Disruptions affecting these sectors can have widespread impacts on communities and the economy.

What Role Does CISA Play in Election Security?

CISA works with state and local election officials to help protect election systems from cyber threats and other security risks.

Its election-related activities may include:

  • Sharing cyber threat intelligence.
  • Conducting voluntary security assessments.
  • Providing cybersecurity training.
  • Offering incident response assistance.
  • Helping identify and reduce vulnerabilities.

CISA does not run elections, count votes, certify election results, or determine election winners. Those responsibilities belong primarily to state and local election officials.

Does CISA Respond to Cyberattacks?

Yes.

When major cyber incidents affect government agencies or critical infrastructure, CISA often coordinates the federal response alongside other agencies.

Depending on the situation, it may:

  • Provide technical assistance.
  • Share threat information.
  • Coordinate with affected organizations.
  • Help identify vulnerabilities.
  • Recommend defensive measures.

Criminal investigations, however, are typically handled by agencies such as the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), while intelligence collection and military cyber operations are performed by other organizations.

Why Is CISA Frequently in the News?

CISA is regularly mentioned in news reports involving:

  • Election security.
  • Foreign cyber threats.
  • Ransomware attacks.
  • Data breaches.
  • Critical infrastructure protection.
  • Software vulnerabilities.
  • Federal cybersecurity guidance.

Because cyber incidents can affect governments, businesses, hospitals, utilities, and election systems, CISA frequently provides public alerts and recommendations during significant cybersecurity events.

Who Leads CISA?

Under federal law, CISA is led by a Director, who is nominated by the President and confirmed by the U.S. Senate.

The Director oversees the agency's cybersecurity, infrastructure security, emergency communications, and stakeholder engagement programs.

Current Leadership Status

As of 2026, CISA is operating under an Acting Director rather than a Senate-confirmed Director. Nick Andersen assumed the role of Acting Director in February 2026 following a leadership transition within the Department of Homeland Security.

The agency continues carrying out its day-to-day responsibilities, including cybersecurity operations, critical infrastructure protection, election security support, threat monitoring, and incident response. However, some long-term strategic initiatives and policy decisions may ultimately be guided by a future Senate-confirmed Director once one is nominated and confirmed.

Common Misunderstandings

One common misconception is that CISA runs U.S. elections. In reality, it provides security assistance and guidance but does not administer elections or certify results.

Another misunderstanding is that CISA investigates cybercrime. While it helps organizations respond to cyber incidents, criminal investigations are generally conducted by law enforcement agencies such as the FBI.

Finally, CISA does not regulate most private businesses. Instead, it works cooperatively with public and private organizations by sharing information, offering technical expertise, and promoting voluntary cybersecurity best practices.

Conclusion

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency is the federal government's lead civilian agency for protecting critical infrastructure and improving cybersecurity. Through partnerships with government agencies, private organizations, and election officials, CISA helps strengthen the security and resilience of essential systems that Americans rely on every day. Although it plays a major role in election security and cyber defense, it does not administer elections, investigate most cybercrimes, or conduct military operations.
