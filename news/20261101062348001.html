320,000 join third weekend of protests across France

More than 560 arrested after largely peaceful marches give way to clashes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 1, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

An estimated 320,000 people joined demonstrations across France on October 31, marking a third consecutive weekend of protests over living costs, education and public services. The largely peaceful marches were followed by scattered clashes and fires in several cities, with authorities reporting more than 560 arrests nationwide by Saturday night.

About 70,000 police officers and gendarmes were deployed across the country after Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez warned of a very high risk of disorder, particularly after dark on Halloween. In Paris, authorities rerouted the main demonstration away from the city center to protect stores, government buildings and tourist areas.

The French government estimated 70,000 protesters in Paris and 320,000 nationwide. That was down from government estimates of 410,000 on October 24 and 550,000 on October 17. Organizers have reported higher turnout figures during the protest movement.

The demonstrations began with students raising concerns about deteriorating school buildings, teacher shortages and university admissions, but have broadened to include complaints about the cost of living, housing, inequality and public services. High school students, labor unions, climate activists and Yellow Vest protesters were among those participating Saturday, and some demonstrators wore Halloween costumes.

Most authorized marches proceeded peacefully, but tensions increased as demonstrations ended in Paris, Marseille, Lyon and other cities. Police used tear gas and other crowd-control measures as small groups threw projectiles and set fires. Authorities said 565 people had been arrested nationwide, while several police officers were reported injured.

Youth-led demonstrations were also held elsewhere in Europe, including Germany and Portugal, reflecting similar concerns about education, housing and living costs, although the demands and circumstances varied by country.
