Ben Shelton reaches first Grand Slam final

American can end 23-year U.S. men's major title drought

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 13, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

Ben Shelton is one victory away from ending a 23-year drought for American men's tennis after rallying past fellow American Frances Tiafoe to reach his first Grand Slam singles final. The 23-year-old, seeded No. 8, defeated the No. 11-seeded Tiafoe 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5 Friday night at Arthur Ashe Stadium and will face top-seeded Alexander Zverev for the U.S. Open championship Sunday.

Shelton recovered after dropping the opening set and increasingly relied on his powerful serve, finishing with 20 aces, his most in a match at this year's tournament. A pivotal third set ended with Shelton breaking Tiafoe after a roughly 15-minute game featuring four set points. He then broke again late in the fourth set before serving out the match. The victory came two days after Shelton eliminated defending champion Carlos Alcaraz in a five-set quarterfinal that ended at 3:33 a.m., the latest finish in U.S. Open history.

The semifinal carried historical significance beyond Shelton's first major final. He became the first Black American man to reach a U.S. Open singles final since Arthur Ashe in 1972 and the first Black American man to reach any Grand Slam singles final since MaliVai Washington at Wimbledon in 1996.

Shelton now has an opportunity to become the first American man to win a Grand Slam singles championship since Andy Roddick won the 2003 U.S. Open. American men have reached major finals since then, including Taylor Fritz at the 2024 U.S. Open, but none has ended the title drought.

Standing in Shelton's way is Zverev, who defeated Karen Khachanov 6-3, 7-6 (7), 7-6 (6) in the other semifinal. Zverev has won all five previous matches against Shelton, although Sunday's championship will be their first meeting at a Grand Slam tournament.
