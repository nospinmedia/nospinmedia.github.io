Taiwan Throttles Mobile Internet During War Games

First such drill tests communications during attack or disaster

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 10, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Taiwan deliberately slowed mobile internet service across a large part of the island's central region on August 10, 2026, marking the first time it has used internet throttling during its annual Han Kuang military exercises. The drill, centered on Taichung, was designed to test how civilians and authorities would function if communications infrastructure were disrupted during a Chinese attack or a major natural disaster.

During the roughly 30-minute exercise, only basic mobile phone services remained available. ATMs, traffic signals, landline phones, fixed internet connections, and emergency services continued operating. Air raid sirens sounded in Taichung beginning at 2:30 p.m. local time, while the government sent alerts in Chinese and English telling mobile users that the exercise was simulating attacks on communications infrastructure. Authorities had announced the restrictions in advance so residents could prepare alternative ways to communicate.

The communications drill is part of Taiwan's 10-day Han Kuang exercises, which run from August 5 through August 14 and simulate responses to a potential Chinese military attack under more realistic battlefield conditions. A second mobile internet throttling exercise is scheduled for August 13 in northern Taiwan, including Taipei. Officials have said the tests are intended to encourage residents to consider backup options such as landlines, fixed internet, or prearranged family meeting locations if mobile networks become unreliable during an emergency.

Taiwan's military also conducted live-fire exercises on the strategically important Penghu Islands on Monday. Troops used M60A3 tanks, artillery, and anti-aircraft guns while practicing a layered defense against a simulated rapid coastal assault. Reservists from Penghu participated as Taiwan works to integrate reserve forces more closely into wartime planning. China claims Taiwan as its territory and has not ruled out using force to take control of the island, while Taiwan's government rejects Beijing's sovereignty claim.
