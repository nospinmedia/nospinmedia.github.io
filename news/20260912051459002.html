Houthis seize strategic island in Bab el-Mandeb Strait

Capture extends rebel reach into key global shipping chokepoint

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi forces captured Mayun Island, also known as Perim, on September 11, extending their rapid advance to the center of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, one of the world's most important shipping chokepoints. The small volcanic island divides the narrow waterway into two shipping lanes and sits between Yemen and the Horn of Africa at the southern entrance to the Red Sea. The capture was confirmed by officials from both Yemen's internationally recognized government and the Houthis.

The takeover followed the Houthis' capture of the Red Sea port of Mokha a day earlier as government-aligned forces retreated along Yemen's western coast. The advances represent the Houthis' largest territorial gains in years and place their forces in a stronger position near shipping routes connecting the Indian Ocean, Red Sea and Suez Canal. Saudi Arabia responded by striking the airport at Mokha after the Houthis seized the city, according to Houthi-run media.

The Bab el-Mandeb has become increasingly important because shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has also been disrupted by the wider regional conflict. Houthi attacks on vessels in the Red Sea have already caused many shipping companies to reroute around Africa, with overall traffic through the strait remaining about 60% below levels seen before attacks began in late 2023. The Houthis have said ships outside their embargo on Saudi-linked traffic can continue using the waterway, but the latest territorial gains increase uncertainty for carriers operating nearby.

The disruption has also complicated Saudi oil exports. Riyadh had increased use of its cross-country pipeline to move crude to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, bypassing Hormuz, before Houthi threats pushed more Saudi tankers toward longer routes through the Suez Canal and around Africa. Saudi Red Sea oil loadings fell from 3.8 million barrels per day to 2.2 million barrels per day in August, according to the International Energy Agency.
