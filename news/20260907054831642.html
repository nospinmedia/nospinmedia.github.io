Protesters block migrant coaches in Portsmouth

Police disperse crowd after overnight confrontation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 7, 2026 at 6:58 PM ET

British government officials condemned what they called "thuggish and intimidating behaviour" after hundreds of protesters gathered in Portsmouth and attempted to prevent coaches carrying newly arrived asylum seekers from leaving the area. The confrontation continued into the early hours of September 7, when police used dispersal powers to clear roads near Eastney Beach after demonstrators blocked vehicles transporting the arrivals to a processing center.

The protest began after a small boat carrying about 140 people crossed the English Channel and was brought ashore in Portsmouth on September 6, an unusual destination for a large Channel arrival. Many protesters wore black clothing and face coverings, and some blocked roads while calling for the coaches to be stopped. Police officers with riot shields were deployed as tensions increased. Authorities eventually dispersed the gathering at around 3 a.m., allowing the coaches to leave.

The demonstration was promoted by Daniel Thomas, also known online as Danny Tommo, whom British reporting identifies as a far-right activist associated with Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, better known as Tommy Robinson. Thomas livestreamed from Portsmouth and encouraged supporters to travel to the area. The unrest followed a similar anti-migrant demonstration in Dover during the weekend, reflecting growing efforts by activist networks to rapidly mobilize protesters around small-boat arrivals.

The British government defended the right to peaceful protest while condemning intimidation and disorder. Ministers said demonstrations cannot be allowed to interfere with authorities carrying out lawful operations or threaten people being transported from arrival sites. The Home Office says small-boat crossings have fallen by more than 40% this year, although officials say trafficking networks have been adapting routes and methods used to move people across the Channel.
