Russian strikes leave more than 500,000 Ukrainians without power

Zelenskyy says partners provided no advance warning of attack

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 20, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Russian ballistic missiles and jet-powered drones struck Ukraine's energy infrastructure, leaving more than 500,000 people without power as President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Kyiv again received no advance warning from partner countries that previously shared intelligence about incoming attacks.

Russia fired 16 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and the surrounding region in several waves and launched more than 200 drones across Ukraine. A 21-year-old woman was killed in Kyiv, according to city officials. More than 10 energy facilities were damaged, adding pressure to Ukraine's power system as colder weather approaches.

Zelenskyy said Ukraine had now gone without the advance warnings on multiple recent occasions. Such intelligence can give Ukrainian authorities additional time to activate air-raid warnings and prepare defenses against ballistic missiles, which travel at high speeds and can leave little time for civilians to seek shelter.

The dispute comes amid broader strains between Ukraine and the United States. Zelenskyy has said Ukraine is facing restrictions on intelligence sharing and limits involving military satellite services, while weapons deliveries have also been delayed. Ukrainian adviser Serhii Beskrestnov told the BBC that reduced access to intelligence has left insufficient time to warn civilians of some incoming missile attacks and said Ukrainians are dying as a result.

President Trump, meanwhile, has intensified his criticism of Zelenskyy and said Monday that Ukraine might need someone else to negotiate an end to the war. Zelenskyy has said decisions involving delayed weapons, intelligence restrictions and recent U.S. actions toward Russia are difficult to understand between allies.

The latest attack follows months of Russian strikes against Ukraine's energy system and Ukrainian long-range attacks against Russian oil infrastructure, leaving energy infrastructure increasingly central to the war.
