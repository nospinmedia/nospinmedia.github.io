UN court finds Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya

Historic ruling orders compensation and accountability for atrocities

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 20, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, finding that the country's security forces acted with the intent to destroy a substantial part of the Muslim minority during military operations in 2017. The judgment marks the first time the United Nations' highest court has held a state legally responsible for committing genocide.

The core genocide finding passed by a 9-to-6 vote. The court found that Myanmar's security forces killed thousands of Rohingya, committed widespread rape and other sexual violence, attacked civilians attempting to flee and destroyed hundreds of villages in northern Rakhine State. More than 700,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh during the 2017 crackdown.

The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention. Judges ordered Myanmar to provide restitution and compensation to victims, with those measures approved 10-to-5, and required the government to submit a compliance report within two years detailing restitution measures and compensation terms.

The court separately ruled 11-to-4 that Myanmar violated its obligation to preserve evidence, citing the destruction of remnants of at least five former Rohingya villages in May 2020 and construction of a government base on a former village site in 2021. By a 13-to-2 vote, judges also ordered Myanmar to investigate and prosecute those responsible.

International responses highlighted differing approaches to the judgment. The U.S. State Department said the Trump administration was examining the ruling while stressing the need to avoid politicization of the court. The European Union praised the critical role of the ICJ. An umbrella organization representing Myanmar opposition groups welcomed the verdict, saying the struggle for justice would continue and calling on the international community to ensure the judgment is implemented.

U.K. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband announced that Britain was expelling Myanmar's defense attaché and called for an urgent United Nations Security Council meeting. U.N. Secretary-General António Guterres welcomed the ruling as a reaffirmation of international efforts against impunity.

Myanmar has denied committing genocide. The ICJ's judgment is final and cannot be appealed, although the court has limited mechanisms for directly enforcing its decisions.
