Trump asked Xi if China wanted to buy U.S. weapons

State Department says federal law prohibits such arms sales

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 28, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

President Trump asked Chinese President Xi Jinping whether China would be interested in buying American weapons during Xi's recent visit to Washington, according to U.S. Ambassador to China David Perdue. The unusual exchange came as the two leaders discussed U.S. weapons sales to Taiwan, one of the most sensitive issues in relations between Washington and Beijing.

Perdue disclosed the conversation during an appearance on Fox News Sunday on September 27. He said Trump had explained to Xi that the United States sells weapons to countries around the world and then asked whether China would like to buy some. Perdue did not say how Xi responded, what weapons were discussed or whether Trump's question was intended as a serious proposal.

The administration subsequently made clear that no weapons sale to Beijing is planned. The State Department said U.S. law prohibits arms sales to China and that there is "no offer or plan to sell arms to China." The White House separately told The New York Times that the United States had no plans to sell weapons to Beijing.

The conversation occurred against the backdrop of a pending weapons package for Taiwan worth about $14 billion. Trump has not yet approved that package, which has been delayed for months. His administration previously authorized a separate record $11 billion package for Taiwan in December 2025. Beijing strongly opposes American weapons sales to the self-governing island, which China claims as its territory, while U.S. policy under the Taiwan Relations Act calls for providing Taiwan with defensive military equipment.

Xi again raised Taiwan during his Washington visit and urged Trump to oppose Taiwanese independence. Perdue said separately that there had been no change in the administration's Taiwan policy, including its approach to weapons sales.
