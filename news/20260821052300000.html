Panama Canal to Reduce Daily Ship Crossings

El Niño Drought Forces Transit Caps Beginning in September

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 21, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The Panama Canal Authority (ACP) announced it will begin restricting vessel traffic in September 2026 as worsening drought conditions linked to a developing 2026–2027 El Niño threaten water supplies needed to operate the canal's lock system. Daily transits will be reduced to 34 vessels beginning September 4 before falling to 32 vessels on September 15, reversing the authority's earlier expectation that traffic restrictions would not be necessary this year.

According to the ACP, rainfall across the canal's watershed between May and August was 34% below the historical average, while freshwater inflows into the reservoir system fell 44% below normal seasonal levels. The authority said the Neopanamax locks will be limited to nine daily transit slots, while the older Panamax locks will initially remain at 25 slots before dropping to 23 on September 15. Canal Administrator Ricaurte Vásquez said lessons learned during previous droughts in 2023 and 2024 helped officials prepare earlier for the expected water shortages.

The Panama Canal remains one of the world's most important maritime trade corridors, carrying roughly 5% of global seaborne commerce and about 40% of U.S. container traffic. Lower water levels have already forced the authority to tighten vessel draft limits, requiring some ships to reduce cargo loads before transiting. Shipping companies have also faced sharply higher costs as competition for available transit reservations intensified during recent months.

The new restrictions add another challenge for global supply chains already coping with geopolitical disruptions affecting other major shipping routes. While canal officials emphasized that the reductions are intended to preserve water resources and maintain safe operations, fewer daily crossings could increase transit delays and transportation costs if dry conditions persist through the projected El Niño period. The ACP said it will continue monitoring rainfall and reservoir levels and adjust operating restrictions as conditions evolve.
