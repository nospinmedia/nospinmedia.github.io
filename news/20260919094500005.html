Judge restores $7 billion Solar for All program

Ruling says EPA lacked authority to terminate existing grants

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 19, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A federal judge has ruled that the Environmental Protection Agency unlawfully terminated the $7 billion Solar for All program, restoring grants intended to help low-income households and disadvantaged communities gain access to residential and community solar energy. U.S. District Judge Mary McElroy in Rhode Island ruled on September 18 that the EPA lacked statutory authority to cancel grants that had already been awarded.

The program was created under the 2022 Inflation Reduction Act as part of the $27 billion Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund. The EPA awarded Solar for All grants in 2024 to 60 recipients, including states, tribal governments and nonprofit organizations. The program was designed to expand solar access for more than 900,000 households while lowering electricity costs in communities where residents often face barriers to installing or financing solar systems.

The EPA terminated the program in August 2025 after Congress repealed the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund and rescinded unobligated funding. EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin argued that the agency no longer had the legal authority or administrative funding needed to continue overseeing the grants. Labor unions and nonprofit organizations challenged the termination, arguing that money already obligated to recipients remained available and that Congress had not authorized the EPA to cancel those existing awards.

McElroy agreed with the challengers and vacated the EPA's termination. The judge pointed to more than $3 billion Congress left available for administrative expenses associated with previously awarded grants as evidence that lawmakers expected existing grants to continue. The EPA said it was reviewing the ruling and considering its options, meaning an appeal remains possible.

The decision does not create a new solar program or authorize additional grants beyond those already awarded. Instead, it revives the existing Solar for All awards that the EPA attempted to terminate.
