Oman postpones talks over Strait of Hormuz

Gulf concerns stall plan for regional shipping routes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 14, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Oman postponed a regional meeting scheduled for September 14, 2026, where Iran planned to present Gulf countries with an agreement negotiated with Oman over shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Omani Foreign Minister Sayyid Badr Albusaidi announced the delay late Sunday, saying it was made "in the interests of consensus" as governments in the region raised concerns about the proposed arrangement.

The meeting in Salalah was expected to bring together Iran, Iraq and Gulf Arab states to discuss the future management of shipping through the strategically important waterway. Iran and Oman have spent months negotiating an arrangement governing maritime traffic after shipping through Hormuz was severely disrupted during the regional conflict. Iraq had confirmed that it would participate, while Bahrain said it would not attend. Saudi Arabia also raised concerns about the proposed Iran-Oman arrangement and sought changes before the regional meeting proceeded.

Iranian officials said the postponement came at the request of several regional countries and was a joint decision by Tehran and Muscat. The proposed arrangement would establish new routes for commercial vessels through Hormuz but would not immediately reopen the strait to normal traffic. Iran has maintained that a broader reopening remains dependent on the United States meeting conditions set by Tehran.

The postponement came as pressure on global energy supplies intensified elsewhere in the region. Oil prices climbed above $108 a barrel after drone strikes forced Saudi Arabia to shut its East-West pipeline, a major alternative route that allows Saudi crude to bypass Hormuz and reach the Red Sea. The combination of the pipeline shutdown and stalled diplomatic efforts over Hormuz has increased uncertainty about how quickly normal oil shipments can resume through two of the region's most important export routes.
