$20 million taxpayer-funded Trump ad campaign draws scrutiny

“God Made Trump” among at least 13 government-funded spots

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 3, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The Trump administration is facing scrutiny over a taxpayer-funded advertising campaign with approximately $20 million allocated to promote President Trump and administration policies. The campaign includes at least 13 advertisements, including a three-minute video titled “God Made Trump.”

CNN obtained 12 video clips stored in a shared Dropbox folder belonging to LMD, the advertising agency handling the government's media campaign. The collection included “God Made Trump,” and the advertisements end with a disclosure stating “Paid for by the U.S. Government.” The video itself predates the current government advertising effort and had previously been publicly promoted by Trump.

The advertisements range from approximately 15 seconds to three minutes and include material highlighting U.S. military operations in Iran and Venezuela, Trump's UFC 250 event at the White House and Trump's life and political career. CNN reported that the campaign is being funded through U.S. Customs and Border Protection, citing sources familiar with the spending.

The administration has defended the advertisements as legitimate government communications. President Trump told ABC News that the ads were being run “for the country.” The spending has nevertheless drawn criticism over whether taxpayer money should finance advertisements that prominently praise and promote a sitting president.

The controversy has also raised questions about federal restrictions on using appropriated money for publicity or propaganda. Those restrictions do not automatically make the advertisements illegal, and criticism from lawmakers and watchdog groups is not itself proof that federal law was violated. No court ruling cited in the reporting reviewed by No Spin Media has found the campaign unlawful.

The central dispute is therefore not whether the advertisements exist or are government-funded, but whether this type of promotional government messaging is an appropriate use of taxpayer money.
