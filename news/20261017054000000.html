Trump Waives New Russia Sanctions Before They Take Effect

President Cites U.S. National Interests in Pausing Measures Passed by Congress

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 17, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

President Trump has waived several new sanctions and tariffs targeting Russia just before they were scheduled to take effect, using authority Congress included in the recently enacted Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026.

Trump issued the waiver on October 16, citing the national interests of the United States. The affected measures were scheduled to begin October 18 and were designed to increase economic pressure on Russia and countries that continue purchasing large amounts of Russian energy.

The law, which Trump signed in September after it passed Congress, includes sanctions targeting parts of Russia's financial and energy sectors as well as measures aimed at its so-called shadow fleet of tankers. It also provides for steep tariffs on goods from countries that continue buying Russian oil, natural gas and other petroleum products.

The legislation gives the president authority to waive certain provisions when he determines doing so serves U.S. national interests. Trump used that authority before the new penalties became active rather than allowing them to take effect first.

The decision could affect major purchasers of Russian energy, including India and China, which faced potential economic consequences under the legislation. The administration retains authority to allow the measures to take effect later if circumstances change.

The move comes amid broader disagreements over U.S. policy toward Russia and the war in Ukraine. Some lawmakers from both parties have pushed for greater economic pressure on Moscow, while the administration has also cited concerns about energy supplies and prices when making recent decisions involving Russian petroleum products.

The waiver does not eliminate the underlying law. Instead, it pauses application of several of its sanctions and tariffs under the presidential waiver provisions approved by Congress.
