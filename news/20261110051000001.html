France blames Iran for cyberattack on 700 grocery stores

Hackers remotely heated refrigerators, spoiling food and triggering fires

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 10, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

French authorities have formally accused a cyber unit affiliated with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps of carrying out an unusual attack that remotely manipulated refrigeration systems at about 700 grocery stores across France, spoiling food and triggering roughly 20 fires.

The attacks occurred on October 11 and affected major supermarket chains and other retailers. Hackers gained remote access to internet-connected refrigeration controllers, initiated abnormal defrost cycles and disabled temperature warnings, according to the Paris prosecutor's office. During the attack, temperatures in some refrigeration equipment reportedly climbed above 50°C, or 122°F.

French prosecutors said investigators found technical indicators they believe connect the operation to Shahid Kaveh, described as an offensive cyber unit affiliated with the IRGC's Electronic and Cyber Center. Investigators cited characteristics including the use of the Persian language and internet addresses traced to devices in Iran.

Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot called the attack unprecedented in its scale and aggression and said France would pursue European sanctions against those responsible.

The attribution comes after similar refrigeration disruptions were reported in Belgium, Luxembourg and Sweden. French authorities have not said that all of those incidents were carried out by the same group.

A separate attack also targeted refrigeration systems at French blood-storage facilities in early November. Monitoring systems detected the temperature changes before blood products or samples were damaged. Authorities have not publicly established that the blood-bank incident was connected to the Iranian operation.

The threat extends beyond France. U.S. authorities recently warned organizations operating industrial refrigeration and cold-chain equipment that attackers are exploiting internet-accessible and unpatched controllers. The FBI recommended removing refrigeration controllers from the public internet and placing access behind firewalls. Separate suspicious refrigeration failures at six U.S. military bases in August also remain under investigation, with no public attribution to Iran.
