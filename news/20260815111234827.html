American Missionary Freed After 9 Months In Niger Captivity

Kevin Rideout is in U.S. care as release details remain unclear

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 2:54 PM ET

American missionary Kevin Rideout has been released after more than nine months in captivity in Niger and is now in the care of U.S. officials, according to SIM International, the evangelical mission organization he worked with. SIM announced the release on August 14, 2026, saying Rideout was in good health and would soon be reunited with his wife, children, and extended family. President Trump also confirmed that Rideout had returned to U.S. custody.

Rideout was kidnapped outside his home in Niamey, Niger, on October 21, 2025. A security official said at the time that three armed men seized him and likely drove him out of the capital. No organization publicly claimed responsibility for the kidnapping. The U.S. Embassy in Niamey subsequently warned Americans that they faced a heightened risk of kidnapping throughout Niger, including inside the capital.

The circumstances surrounding Rideout's release have not been fully established publicly. Reuters reported that forces aligned with eastern Libyan commander Khalifa Haftar played a role in securing his freedom after months of efforts to locate him in the wider Sahel region. Sources offered differing accounts of how he was freed, including mediation, a possible ransom payment, and a claim that Libyan forces conducted an operation in Mali. Reuters said it could not independently confirm all of those details, while U.S. officials have not publicly disclosed whether money, negotiations, or military action were involved.

Niger has faced persistent violence from armed groups linked to al-Qaida and the Islamic State, while its military government has reduced security cooperation with the United States and other Western countries since taking power in 2023. Rideout's release ends a months-long ordeal for his family, but authorities have provided few details about who held him or how his freedom was secured.
