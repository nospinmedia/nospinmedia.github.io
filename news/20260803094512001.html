Myanmar Releases New Images of Aung San Suu Kyi After Red Cross Visit

First official photos since April as ICRC confirms private meeting

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Myanmar’s military-backed authorities released new photographs of Aung San Suu Kyi on August 3, 2026, following a rare visit by the International Committee of the Red Cross. The images show the 81-year-old Nobel laureate meeting privately in the capital, Naypyidaw, with ICRC resident representative Arnaud de Baecque. The ICRC said a delegate met her under its standard procedures for people deprived of liberty and spoke with her in private, but did not disclose what was discussed.

In the released photos, Aung San Suu Kyi appears standing unaided in traditional dress and shaking hands with de Baecque inside a wood‑paneled room. Additional images show her smiling beside a pink cake inscribed “Happy Birthday Aunty Suu,” referencing her June 19, 2026 birthday. These are her first officially released images since April 2026, when authorities publicized a photo amid announcements that she had been moved from prison to house arrest as part of a broader amnesty. While she appears relaxed, independent observers caution that her health cannot be reliably assessed from state-issued imagery alone.

Her younger son, Kim Aris, welcomed the ICRC contact as “the first positive development concerning her in more than five years,” while urging ongoing humanitarian access and a path to direct family communication. Rights groups say the photographs provide long-sought proof of life after years with no verified contact, but emphasize that verifiable, regular access by neutral monitors is essential to assess her condition and treatment.

Aung San Suu Kyi has been detained since February 1, 2021, when the military ousted her elected government. After a series of closed-door trials widely condemned by international observers, her sentences were pared back through limited pardons, leaving a reported total of 27-year terms. The images arrive months after junta chief Min Aung Hlaing was sworn in as president in April 2026 following heavily criticized elections, and amid renewed regional pressure from ASEAN members to allow credible engagement with all parties to Myanmar’s conflict.
