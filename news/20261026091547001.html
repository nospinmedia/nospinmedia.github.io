Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey send senior officials to Iran for security talks

First joint meeting comes as Houthi attacks raise pressure on Saudi Arabia

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 26, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey are meeting Iranian leaders in Tehran on October 26 for regional security talks as fighting involving Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis places increasing pressure on Saudi Arabia.

The delegations include Saudi Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Pakistan's army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin. Iran's delegation is being led by Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the country's Supreme National Security Council.

The meeting marks the first high-level contact between Iran and all three members of the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement since Houthi missile and drone attacks against Saudi Arabia intensified. Saudi officials said the talks are part of efforts to reduce tensions, promote diplomatic solutions and support mediation by Qatar and Pakistan.

The three countries signed the defence agreement in August 2026. Under the pact, an armed attack against one member is considered an attack against all. Ministers from the three countries held an emergency meeting in Riyadh earlier this month and agreed to provide for rapid deployment of military forces and capabilities to Saudi Arabia. Pakistan approved the agreement last week, while Turkey's parliament is still considering ratification.

The Tehran talks come amid continuing fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis. Saudi Arabia said it intercepted another wave of missiles and drones on Sunday. On Monday, the Houthis said they had attacked Saudi oil pumping stations and King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh. Saudi authorities had not confirmed those specific Houthi claims, although the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a missile headed toward Riyadh.

Regional security, developments in Palestine and Lebanon, Israeli policies and the consequences of U.S. and Israeli military action against Iran are among the issues expected to be discussed.
