Sudan mine collapse kills at least 82

Dozens remain missing as rescuers lack heavy equipment

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 17, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

At least 82 people have been killed after an informal gold mine collapsed near Nuhud in Sudan's West Kordofan province, with dozens more still unaccounted for as rescuers struggle to reach people trapped beneath the rubble. The disaster occurred at the al-Zara gold mine, in territory controlled by the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) amid Sudan's continuing civil war.

According to local officials and activists, the collapse began Sunday in one shaft and spread through interconnected shafts. By Wednesday afternoon, rescuers had recovered 82 bodies. Suleiman Abu Hmeida, a member of the grassroots Emergency Response Rooms network, said dozens of people remained unaccounted for. Earlier casualty counts varied as recovery operations continued.

The rescue effort has been severely limited by a lack of machinery. A local official said only one privately owned forklift was available. Survivor Khairal-Sayed Gabara said rescuers lacked equipment capable of removing dirt and rocks at depths greater than 30 meters, or about 100 feet. The Sudan Doctors Network said the informal mine employed unregulated workers and lacked basic safety and protective measures.

The al-Zara site is one of thousands of small-scale and informal gold mines operating across Sudan. Deadly collapses have occurred previously, including accidents that killed 14 miners in 2023 and 38 people in 2021. Gold has also become deeply intertwined with Sudan's war: experts commissioned by the United Nations have reported that large quantities of gold are smuggled from areas controlled by the RSF to help finance the paramilitary force.

Sudan's war between the RSF and the country's military began in April 2023, devastating communities and disrupting government oversight across large parts of the country. The mine disaster adds another humanitarian crisis as rescuers continue searching for those still missing.
