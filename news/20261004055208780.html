U.S. Marine arrested in Okinawa robbery-murder case

Japan lodges strong protest as Marine denies allegations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 4, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Japanese police have arrested 20-year-old U.S. Marine Lance Cpl. Devin Jacob Ballard on suspicion of robbing and killing Anna Yagi, 39, at a hotel in Naha, Okinawa. Ballard, who is stationed at Marine Corps Air Station Futenma, has denied the allegations, according to police.

Okinawa Prefectural Police allege that Yagi was killed at a hotel in Naha's Wakasa district early Saturday, October 3, and that her wallet and backpack were taken. Police said Ballard and Yagi entered the hotel together at about 2:10 a.m. Hotel staff later discovered Yagi's body after the pair failed to check out. Authorities have not publicly disclosed how she died, and the investigation into the circumstances surrounding her death remains underway.

The arrest quickly escalated into a diplomatic issue between two close allies. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said Japan had lodged a strong protest with the United States and was seeking maximum U.S. cooperation with the investigation, tighter discipline among American military personnel and measures intended to prevent similar incidents. U.S. Ambassador George Glass said the United States was cooperating fully with Japanese authorities.

The III Marine Expeditionary Force also confirmed that a Marine assigned to the command was being investigated over allegations of serious criminal conduct and offered condolences to the victim's family and friends. Ballard remains in Japanese custody while authorities investigate the case.

The arrest carries particular significance in Okinawa, which hosts a large concentration of U.S. military personnel in Japan. Crimes involving American service members have repeatedly fueled local opposition to the U.S. military presence, including mass protests following the 1995 rape of a 12-year-old Okinawan girl by three U.S. servicemen.
