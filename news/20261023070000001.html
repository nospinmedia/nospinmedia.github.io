Mississippi releases video of police shooting that killed 1-year-old Kohen Wiley

Family disputes state's finding that officer's use of force was justified

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 23, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Mississippi authorities have released body-camera footage showing the police shooting that killed 1-year-old Kohen Wiley in a Walmart parking lot in Senatobia, providing the clearest public view yet of the June encounter days after the state declined to charge the officer who fired the shots.

The shooting occurred on June 14, 2026, after police responded to an alleged shoplifting incident involving two women. Video shows officers running after the women as they entered a car with Kohen. The vehicle backed out of a parking space, struck another vehicle and then moved forward as officers surrounded it and repeatedly ordered the driver to stop.

The Mississippi Bureau of Investigation has said the vehicle drove toward officers and nearly struck one. Newly released footage shows an officer in front of the vehicle as it begins moving forward. Sgt. Hunter Foster fired into the vehicle as it moved past officers. Kohen, who was on his mother's lap in the front passenger seat, was struck and killed. The driver was also wounded.

On October 19, the office of Mississippi Attorney General Lynn Fitch announced that it had determined the officer's use of force was justified and would not bring criminal charges. The state said Foster fired in self-defense as the vehicle approached him.

Kohen's family and their attorneys dispute that conclusion. Civil rights attorney Ben Crump argues that the video shows Foster was alongside the vehicle when the fatal shot was fired and was no longer facing an imminent threat. The family is asking the U.S. Department of Justice to open a federal investigation. Neither of the two women in the vehicle has been charged with shoplifting.

The footage does not eliminate disagreement over the encounter. Several law-enforcement and use-of-force experts who reviewed video for Mississippi Today criticized officers for positioning themselves in front of the moving vehicle, while other experts said the circumstances could support Foster's belief that he faced an immediate threat.

The state's decision means Foster will not face criminal prosecution from Mississippi authorities, but the family's request for federal review remains unresolved.
