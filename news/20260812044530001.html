U.S. Credit Card Debt Climbs to $1.26 Trillion

NY Fed says balances rose while serious delinquencies remain elevated

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Americans' outstanding credit card debt climbed to $1.26 trillion during the second quarter of 2026, rising by $21 billion from the previous quarter, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of New York's latest Household Debt and Credit Report. The increase leaves credit card balances just below the record level reached in late 2025, reflecting continued reliance on revolving credit even as overall household debt edged slightly lower.

The report found total U.S. household debt slipped 0.1% to $18.8 trillion, largely because mortgage balances declined to $13.1 trillion following temporary reporting changes in mortgage servicing data. At the same time, balances continued to rise in several consumer lending categories, including auto loans, home equity lines of credit, and credit cards, indicating many households are continuing to borrow despite elevated interest rates.

Federal Reserve researchers said measures of new credit card and auto loan delinquencies have remained elevated but generally stable since 2024. The report also noted that headline delinquency figures appear higher because charged-off debts are remaining on credit reports longer than in previous years, making historical comparisons more difficult. Overall, 4.7% of outstanding household debt was in some stage of delinquency during the second quarter, while researchers said consumer balance sheets overall continue to show resilience despite ongoing financial pressures.

The quarterly report is closely watched because it provides one of the nation's most comprehensive snapshots of household borrowing and repayment trends. Rising credit card balances can signal stronger consumer spending, but they may also indicate that more households are relying on credit to manage everyday expenses while borrowing costs remain high.
