Tennessee inmate awake and speaking after failed execution

Christa Pike remains hospitalized after surviving two lethal doses

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 7, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Christa Pike, the Tennessee death row inmate who survived a failed lethal injection, is now conscious and speaking but remains hospitalized and continues to receive critical medical care, according to her attorneys. Her lawyers said her prognosis remains unclear and they expect a long recovery after the September 30 execution attempt at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville.

Pike, 50, was given two doses of the execution drug pentobarbital, but the execution was stopped after officials determined she had not lost consciousness and still had a heartbeat. Her attorneys allege the execution team had difficulty establishing and maintaining intravenous lines and that the drug entered tissue outside her veins, causing severe injuries to her arms. Pike was subsequently intubated and placed on a ventilator while doctors worked to clear the drug from her system.

Her survival is particularly unusual because lethal injection drugs were actually administered. Other U.S. prisoners have survived execution attempts after officials were unable to establish intravenous access, but Pike is believed to be the first person in the modern U.S. death penalty era known to have survived after execution drugs entered the body.

The failed execution has already produced major consequences in Tennessee. Gov. Bill Lee canceled the state's remaining execution scheduled for 2026 and ordered a comprehensive third-party review of what happened. Tennessee Department of Correction Commissioner Frank Strada also announced that he would resign. Strada has said he believes the department followed its established execution protocol, while Pike's attorneys contend that serious problems with the procedure had been foreseeable.

Pike was sentenced to death for the 1995 torture and murder of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer, a fellow student at a Job Corps program in Knoxville. Pike was 18 when the crime occurred and is the only woman on Tennessee's death row. Her attorneys are seeking to have her death sentence commuted and have also asked a court to preserve evidence from the failed execution.
