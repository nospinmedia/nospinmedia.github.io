Paramount Seeks $1.9B Bond Over Warner Deal Delay

Studio says court stay risks massive ticking fees and financing costs

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 9:10 PM ET

Paramount Skydance asked a federal judge to require 12 state attorneys general and the Writers Guild of America to post a $1.9 billion bond to cover potential losses caused by a court-ordered pause of its proposed acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. The request, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, argues that delaying the roughly $110 billion merger could expose the company to mounting contractual costs while litigation continues.

The company points to a contractual “ticking fee” that begins after September 30, 2026: $0.25 per share per quarter, which Paramount estimates at roughly $650 million per quarter, or about $7 million per day, owed to Warner Bros. Discovery stockholders until closing. The pause stems from July litigation led by California Attorney General Rob Bonta and joined by 11 other states under the Clayton Antitrust Act; the Writers Guild of America filed a separate complaint the next day. U.S. District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín issued the initial restraining order in July 2026 and later scheduled a consolidated 12-day trial beginning March 2, 2027. When granting emergency relief, the court waived the usual plaintiff security requirement, citing the public interest.

If the bond is imposed, the coalition of states and the WGA would have to secure funds reflecting the delay costs Paramount says the stay creates. If the request is denied, the stay remains in place while the case proceeds toward trial. Separately, the merger agreement includes a potential $7 billion breakup payment if the transaction ultimately collapses, adding to the financial stakes.

Federal antitrust officials cleared the merger in June 2026, but the state coalition argues the combination would reduce competition in areas including wide-release theatrical distribution and basic-cable channel licensing. Paramount has agreed not to close the transaction until after a final court ruling or mid-2027, whichever comes first, but maintains that prolonged delays significantly increase costs and execution risk.
