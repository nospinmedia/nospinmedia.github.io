Judge Approves $7.25 Billion Roundup Cancer Settlement

Payouts Could Reach $165,000 As Bayer Resolves Nationwide Claims

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 21, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A Missouri judge has granted final approval to a $7.25 billion settlement involving claims that Roundup weedkiller causes cancer, establishing a nationwide system to compensate hundreds of thousands of current and future claimants.

St. Louis Circuit Judge Timothy Boyer approved the settlement on Friday, November 20, calling the individual payouts fair and adequate. Bayer CEO Bill Anderson described the decision as a "decisive milestone" in the company's effort to resolve years of litigation over Roundup. Objectors to the settlement can still appeal.

Individual awards are expected to range from approximately $10,000 to $165,000, depending on factors including whether exposure occurred through residential or occupational use, the claimant's age at diagnosis and the type and severity of non-Hodgkin lymphoma. The agreement covers qualifying people exposed to Roundup before February 17, 2026.

Bayer will fund the settlement over 17 to 21 years. The agreement also establishes a separate $1 billion security fund intended to protect against funding shortfalls and provides more than $400 million for special circumstances. Roughly 200 lawsuits pending in federal multidistrict litigation are not included in the settlement.

The legal landscape changed significantly in June 2026, when the U.S. Supreme Court ruled 7-2 that federal pesticide law preempts certain state failure-to-warn claims because the EPA does not require a cancer warning for glyphosate. That decision weakened one avenue plaintiffs had used to pursue Roundup cases and contributed to some attorneys supporting the settlement rather than risking no recovery through continued litigation.

Bayer, which acquired Roundup maker Monsanto in 2018, maintains that glyphosate-based Roundup is safe when used as directed. The International Agency for Research on Cancer has classified glyphosate as probably carcinogenic to humans, while the EPA has concluded it is not likely to pose a cancer risk when used according to label directions.
