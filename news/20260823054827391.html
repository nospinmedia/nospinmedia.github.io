Near-Total Lunar Eclipse to Darken Moon This Week

96% of Lunar Surface Will Enter Earth's Shadow

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 23, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Skywatchers across much of the Americas, along with parts of Europe and Africa, will have an opportunity to watch an unusually deep partial lunar eclipse overnight on August 27-28. During the peak of the event, about 96.2% of the Moon's visible surface will pass into Earth's dark central shadow, known as the umbra, giving the Moon a deep reddish-orange appearance that will closely resemble a total lunar eclipse. According to astronomers, the eclipse will reach its greatest phase at approximately 12:13 a.m. EDT on August 28.

Unlike a solar eclipse, a lunar eclipse is safe to observe with the naked eye and requires no special protective equipment. The entire partial eclipse will be visible across much of the eastern and central United States, while observers farther west will see the Moon rise after the eclipse has already begun. Binoculars or small telescopes can enhance views of the advancing shadow and subtle color changes across the lunar surface, although they are not required to enjoy the event.

Lunar eclipses occur when the Sun, Earth, and Moon align so that Earth's shadow falls across the Moon during a full moon. Because sunlight passing through Earth's atmosphere is filtered and bent toward the lunar surface, the portion of the Moon inside the umbra often takes on shades of red or copper instead of disappearing completely. The deeper the Moon passes into the umbra, the more dramatic that color change typically becomes.

Astronomers say this will be one of the deepest partial lunar eclipses visible from the Western Hemisphere in the coming years and an excellent opportunity for both casual observers and photographers. Weather conditions will ultimately determine viewing quality, but anyone with a clear view of the Moon while it is above the horizon should be able to witness the event without specialized equipment.
