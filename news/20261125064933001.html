Family demands bodycam footage after Georgia officer kills 16-year-old

State investigators and teen's family give conflicting accounts of shooting

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 25, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The family of 16-year-old Tyrie Howse, who was fatally shot by a Georgia police officer earlier this month, is demanding the release of body-camera footage as relatives and attorneys dispute the state's preliminary account of what happened.

Howse's mother, Taneshia Puckett, appeared with civil rights attorneys Ben Crump, Harry Daniels and Gerald Griggs at an Atlanta news conference on Tuesday, November 24, asking authorities to publicly release the footage or at least allow the family to see it. The Georgia NAACP has also requested a meeting with investigators and called for an independent review.

Howse was killed on November 3 in the parking lot of a Krispy Kreme in Stockbridge, Georgia. According to the Georgia Bureau of Investigation, a Henry County police officer approached Howse and a 17-year-old after the older teenager handed Howse a handgun. The GBI says Howse ran from the officer and did not comply with commands to drop the weapon. The officer then shot him. Investigators said a handgun was recovered at the scene.

The family and its attorneys dispute that account. They cite witnesses who say Howse was unarmed and running away when he was shot. Attorneys said he was struck in the back of the head and right upper arm. Those claims have not yet been independently established, and the body-camera footage that could clarify important portions of the encounter has not been publicly released.

Puckett said her son, a Woodland High School honor roll junior, had gone to the area to retrieve a cellphone he left at his new job at a nearby Wingstop and was waiting for her to pick him up.

The officer involved has not been publicly identified and remains on administrative leave while the GBI conducts an independent investigation. The Henry County District Attorney's Office said it has not yet received the completed investigative file or body-camera footage and will review the case after the GBI finishes its work.

Howse's funeral is scheduled for Saturday, November 28, in Jonesboro, with civil rights leader Bishop William J. Barber II scheduled to deliver the eulogy.
