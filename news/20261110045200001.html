Trump says further Iran strikes are 'highly unlikely'

Previous pauses during the conflict were followed by renewed attacks

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 10, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

President Trump says additional U.S. attacks on Iran are now "highly unlikely," describing Iran as militarily "effectively over" after meeting with Qatar's prime minister, even as the United States maintains its naval blockade and military forces in the region.

Trump said Washington would maintain what he called the current "status quo" rather than launch another major round of strikes. His comments mark a shift from recent discussions of possible additional attacks and come as Qatar and other countries continue efforts to mediate between Washington and Tehran.

The statement does not amount to a ceasefire or formal end to the conflict. The U.S. naval blockade remains in place, and American forces remain positioned in the region. Trump has also said the military remains prepared to act again if circumstances change.

Previous announcements of pauses or postponed attacks during the conflict have later been followed by renewed U.S. military action. On April 7, Trump announced a two-week suspension of bombing to allow diplomacy to proceed and later extended the pause; U.S. strikes resumed on May 8. On May 18, Trump postponed another planned attack while negotiations continued; strikes resumed on May 27. On August 1, Trump again canceled planned strikes while citing diplomatic progress, but military operations subsequently continued.

Those earlier reversals do not establish that the current de-escalation will also end with renewed attacks, but they provide context for Trump's latest statement and distinguish it from a negotiated ceasefire or peace agreement.

Iran, meanwhile, has publicly rejected Washington's description of diplomatic progress. Iranian security official Mohsen Rezaei accused the United States of spreading "fake news" about negotiations and called on Washington to accept Iran's conditions, which include ending the blockade. Indirect diplomatic contacts involving regional mediators continue despite the competing public statements.
