Young Voters Shift Away From Trump and Republicans Ahead of Midterms

Polling Shows 2024 GOP Gains Eroding as Economy and Deportations Drive Concerns

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 17, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Young voters who moved toward President Trump and Republicans during the 2024 election are shifting back toward Democrats ahead of the November 3 midterm elections, according to recent national polling and interviews with voters.

Trump made substantial gains with younger Americans in 2024, particularly young men. An analysis of New York Times/Siena polling found 55% of young men supported Trump in 2024. Democrats now hold a narrow 1-point advantage among young men, while about 10% remain undecided. The shift is larger among young women. About 31% of young women backed Trump in 2024, while current Republican support among the group has fallen to 13%.

Interviews with young people who voted for Trump in 2024 point to several reasons for their changing views. Some cited the administration's deportation policies, while others pointed to the economy, rising costs, foreign policy, the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files and concerns about the administration's treatment of women.

Separate national polling supports the broader movement. Pew Research Center found registered voters under 30 favor Democratic congressional candidates by a substantial margin. Other recent surveys have placed Trump's approval among younger voters well below his overall support in the 2024 election.

The economy remains especially important. Young adults face high housing costs, elevated borrowing rates and higher prices for everyday expenses. Recent polling also shows significant concern among younger voters about U.S. foreign policy and immigration enforcement.

The shift does not necessarily translate directly into Democratic votes. Younger Americans historically vote at lower rates than older voters, and polling shows many remain undecided or could choose not to participate.

The movement is significant because Republicans' improved performance with young voters was one of the notable changes in the 2024 presidential election. Whether those gains persist is now being tested in the first national election of Trump's second term.
