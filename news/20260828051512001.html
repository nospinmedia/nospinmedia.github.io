Federal judge blocks Pentagon blacklist of Anthropic

Court says AI firm's designation violated constitutional protections

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 28, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

A federal judge permanently blocked the Department of Defense from enforcing its blacklist of Anthropic, ruling that the Trump administration violated the company's constitutional rights by designating the artificial intelligence developer as a national security supply chain risk. In a 59-page opinion, U.S. District Judge Rita F. Lin concluded that the government lacked a sufficient factual basis for the designation and unlawfully retaliated against the company for exercising protected speech under the First Amendment, while also violating procedural due process protections under the Fifth Amendment.

The dispute began after Anthropic and its chief executive, Dario Amodei, declined government requests to remove company restrictions preventing its Claude AI models from being used for fully autonomous lethal weapons or large-scale domestic surveillance. Following those disagreements, the Pentagon designated Anthropic as a supply chain risk and directed federal agencies and many government contractors to discontinue business with the company. Anthropic challenged the action in federal court, arguing the designation was politically motivated and unsupported by evidence.

Judge Lin ruled that the government failed to provide an adequate legal or factual justification for treating Anthropic as a supply chain threat and found the designation was arbitrary and unconstitutional. The permanent injunction immediately prevents federal agencies from enforcing the blacklist, restoring Anthropic's eligibility to compete for federal contracts while the case proceeds through any appeal. The Department of Justice is expected to challenge the ruling before the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The decision is expected to have broad implications for how the federal government uses national security authorities when dealing with domestic technology companies. Legal experts say the ruling reinforces that executive agencies retain broad contracting authority but must still comply with constitutional protections and established administrative procedures when restricting private companies from competing for government business.
