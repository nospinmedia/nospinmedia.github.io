U.S. and China open talks before Trump-Xi summit

AI safety, tariffs and critical minerals move to center stage

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 21, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

Senior officials from the United States and China opened high-level talks in New York City ahead of this week's meeting between President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping, with negotiations focused on trade, artificial intelligence and critical minerals.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng participated in the discussions. Bessent proposed creating a notification mechanism through which the two countries could alert each other about serious artificial-intelligence incidents with potential national-security consequences. The discussions included AI safety, possible weaponization of the technology and risks involving cyberattacks.

Trade remains another major issue. U.S. officials said the countries are working to establish a Board of Trade that would turn previous discussions into negotiations over specific products. Potential tariff reductions could involve nonstrategic goods including consumer products, agricultural goods, energy and medical devices. Disputes over Chinese supplies of rare-earth magnets and other critical minerals remain unresolved, as do earlier Chinese commitments involving purchases of U.S. agricultural products and Boeing aircraft.

The talks come before Xi's scheduled September 23-25 state visit to the United States. China's Foreign Ministry confirmed Sunday that Xi will make the three-day trip at Trump's invitation. The two leaders are expected to discuss the broader U.S.-China relationship as well as trade, technology and international security issues.

China enters the summit with exports remaining strong despite U.S. tariffs and other trade restrictions. Its annual trade surplus is on track to exceed $1 trillion for a second consecutive year, giving the economic relationship additional weight as negotiators consider whether portions of the existing trade truce can be extended or expanded.
