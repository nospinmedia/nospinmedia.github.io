Appeals court keeps federal voter-citizenship database blocked

DHS cannot use expanded SAVE system for voter checks before midterms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

A federal appeals court has rejected the Trump administration's attempt to resume using an expanded federal immigration database to check citizenship information on state voter rolls ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. In a 2-1 decision issued late September 4, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit declined to pause a lower-court order blocking the Department of Homeland Security from using its modified Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements, or SAVE, system for the checks.

The dispute involves changes that allowed SAVE, historically used to verify immigration status for government benefits, to search records using Social Security numbers, access Social Security Administration information and process large batches of names. The administration of President Trump has argued that the expanded system gives states another tool for identifying people who may not be eligible to vote because they are not U.S. citizens.

Chief Judge Sri Srinivasan and Judge Robert Wilkins concluded that the government had not justified lifting the injunction while the case proceeds. The judges pointed to the lower court's finding that the expanded system's disclosure of Social Security Administration information violates federal privacy protections under the Social Security Act. They also cited concerns that inaccurate citizenship information could force eligible voters to prove their citizenship to remain registered or, in some cases, result in registrations being canceled.

Judge Gregory Katsas dissented from the decision. The ruling does not permanently resolve the legality of the expanded SAVE system; it leaves the lower court's injunction in place while litigation continues.

The case is part of broader litigation over federal efforts to use citizenship information in election administration. A separate federal court in Florida previously ordered DHS to restore SAVE access to four Republican-led states, creating competing court orders as the issue moved through the federal judiciary.
