Judge orders Fulton County to turn over FBI-seized 2020 election records

County immediately appeals order requiring unredacted copies for Georgia election board

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 25, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

A Georgia judge has ordered Fulton County to provide the Georgia State Election Board with unredacted digital copies of records from the 2020 election that were seized by the FBI earlier this year, prompting an immediate appeal and emergency request to block the transfer.

Fulton County Superior Court Judge Kelly Lee Ellerbe issued the order Tuesday, November 24, requiring county officials to provide the records by 5 p.m. Wednesday, November 25. The material is contained on flash drives holding digital copies made by federal authorities after the FBI seized more than 600 boxes of ballots and other election records from a Fulton County election facility in January.

The ruling enforces an administrative subpoena approved 3-1 by the State Election Board in August as part of its continuing examination of complaints concerning Fulton County's administration of the 2020 election. The board must pay the county a $500 production fee for the records.

Ellerbe also imposed a protective order governing the material. The unredacted records cannot be publicly released while the investigations continue, and access is restricted to State Election Board members, staff and approved outside experts.

Fulton County attorneys immediately appealed and requested an emergency stay, arguing that providing unredacted election materials could violate protections for ballot secrecy and voter privacy under Georgia law. The appeal could determine whether the records are actually transferred by Wednesday's deadline.

The state proceeding is separate from the federal investigation that led to the FBI seizure. Federal authorities have continued examining the Fulton County records and have interviewed former county elections director Richard Barron, but no criminal charges have been filed in connection with the investigation.

The dispute returns attention to an election that has undergone extensive review. Three separate vote counts confirmed Joe Biden's 2020 victory in Georgia, including the original count, a statewide hand audit and a subsequent recount.

The latest ruling does not overturn those results or establish that election fraud occurred. Instead, it determines whether the State Election Board can obtain copies of records already collected by federal investigators as it conducts its own review.
