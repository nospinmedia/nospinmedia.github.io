Typhoon Dolphin Forces 1,300+ Flight Cancellations

Shanghai Braces For Landfall Late Sunday

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 9, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Typhoon Dolphin prompted Shanghai to cancel more than 1,300 flights on Sunday, August 9, 2026, as officials prepared for the storm’s expected landfall along China’s east coast later that day. Forecasters reported maximum sustained winds of about 162 km/h (101 mph) Sunday morning and warned of heavy rain, flooding, and dangerous landslide conditions across Zhejiang and northern Fujian provinces.

The Shanghai Airport Group said Pudong International Airport and Hongqiao International Airport each canceled about 60% of scheduled flights for Sunday. Authorities in Zhejiang’s Taizhou evacuated roughly 390,000 residents on Saturday, while Shanghai relocated more than 30,000 people from areas considered vulnerable to flooding. The storm’s outer rain bands also reached northern Taiwan, prompting some Taiwanese airlines to cancel Sunday flights to mainland China. Forecasters continued to project landfall in Zhejiang or northern Fujian late Sunday, with rainfall totals of 200–400 millimeters expected over several days.

Officials across coastal communities suspended ferries, cruises, and other water-based activities in parts of Zhejiang while emergency crews positioned equipment for potential flooding, storm surge, and landslides. Travelers were urged to check directly with airlines as cancellations, delays, and rebookings continued throughout the day at Pudong and Hongqiao airports. Emergency management officials warned that saturated ground and strong winds could quickly trigger flash floods, fallen trees, and localized power outages in coastal and mountainous areas.

The large-scale preparations followed several days of warnings as Typhoon Dolphin crossed Okinawa Prefecture before turning toward China’s east coast. Chinese forecasters maintained expectations for a late-Sunday or early-Monday landfall between parts of Zhejiang and northern Fujian, emphasizing that prolonged heavy rainfall, storm surge, and landslides remain the storm’s greatest threats. Flight disruptions and maritime restrictions were expected to continue into Monday as authorities inspect transportation infrastructure and assess storm damage.
