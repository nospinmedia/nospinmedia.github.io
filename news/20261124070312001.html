Pennsylvania reports 9th measles death as outbreak reaches 1,638 cases

U.S. cases climb past 5,800 after nation loses elimination status

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 24, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Pennsylvania has reported its ninth measles-associated death of 2026, as the state's outbreak reaches 1,638 confirmed cases across 46 counties and the United States confronts its largest measles resurgence in decades.

The Pennsylvania Department of Health announced Monday that the latest death involved an unvaccinated resident of Centre County who tested positive for measles. The state has recorded 330 hospitalizations, and the 2026 outbreak is Pennsylvania's largest since the early 1990s.

Pennsylvania classifies a death as measles-associated when it occurs within 30 days of symptom onset in someone with clinical evidence of measles and a positive laboratory test, provided the death was not caused by an unrelated event. That designation does not necessarily mean measles was determined to be the sole cause of death.

The state's nine measles-associated deaths have occurred in Lancaster County (3), Jefferson County (2), Centre County (1), Clinton County (1), Mifflin County (1) and Montour County (1).

Vaccination status has been a major factor in the outbreak. State health data show the overwhelming majority of cases occurred among people who were unvaccinated or undervaccinated, with only five confirmed cases involving vaccinated people.

Nationally, the United States had recorded 5,838 confirmed measles cases across 47 states through November 19, including 426 new cases in one week. That is roughly 2.6 times the 2,289 cases recorded during all of 2025, which itself was the country's highest annual total in decades.

The resurgence also cost the United States a public-health milestone it had maintained since 2000. On November 17, the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) formally withdrew the country's measles elimination status after an independent commission determined that endemic transmission had been reestablished following more than 12 months of continuous spread. Bolivia and Mexico also lost elimination status.

Measles is highly contagious but largely preventable through vaccination. Two doses of the MMR vaccine are about 97% effective at preventing the disease. Health authorities continue urging people to verify their vaccination status, particularly in communities experiencing active transmission.

Officials have also recommended an early MMR dose for some infants 6 to 11 months old facing increased exposure during outbreaks; that dose does not replace the routinely recommended childhood doses given later.
