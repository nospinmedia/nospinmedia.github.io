Cornell President Admits He Failed to Follow Up on Sexual Assault Report

Faculty Weighs No-Confidence Vote as University Faces Questions Over 2024 Allegations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 6:54 PM ET

Cornell University President Michael Kotlikoff has acknowledged that he knew a student reported being sexually assaulted at a fraternity house in 2024 but failed to follow up with her, intensifying criticism of the university's handling of the case.

Kotlikoff also acknowledged that he had not read the police report and did not know the student had withdrawn from Cornell until she filed a lawsuit. His admissions come as faculty members consider a vote of no confidence in his leadership.

The controversy involves a former student identified in court documents as Jane Doe, who alleges that seven members of Cornell's Chi Phi fraternity sexually assaulted her during an October 2024 gathering. She reported the alleged assault to university police the following month.

The seven men have denied wrongdoing, and none has been criminally charged. The Tompkins County district attorney initially declined prosecution, citing insufficient evidence.

Cornell conducted its own investigation and imposed disciplinary penalties. Two students were expelled, two were suspended, and another former student was barred from campus. Other penalties included no-contact orders and written reflection assignments.

Those outcomes have fueled questions about whether the university adequately protected the student and responded appropriately to the seriousness of her allegations.

Kotlikoff acknowledged widespread anger and a loss of trust within the university community. He defended aspects of Cornell's response while conceding that university leadership should have been better informed about the student's circumstances, including her departure from the school.

The case has also prompted renewed scrutiny of Title IX, the federal law governing how educational institutions respond to sex discrimination and sexual misconduct. University disciplinary proceedings operate separately from criminal investigations and apply different standards of evidence.

New York Governor Kathy Hochul has appointed Attorney General Letitia James as special prosecutor to examine the allegations after the local investigation drew criticism.

Cornell has announced a suspension of fraternity parties for the remainder of the academic year. The university is also pursuing changes to sexual assault investigations and student support procedures.

Separately, Cornell's Board of Trustees has retained Sally Yates, a former U.S. deputy attorney general, to lead an independent review of how the university prevents and responds to sexual misconduct.

The Faculty Senate is expected to consider its no-confidence resolution later this month. Such a vote would express faculty opposition to Kotlikoff's leadership but would not automatically remove him from office.
