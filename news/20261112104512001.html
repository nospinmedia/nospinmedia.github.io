Hoax bomb threats hit thousands of schools across Europe

About 6,300 Italian schools targeted as similar emails reach UK and Ireland

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 12, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Thousands of schools across Italy, Scotland, Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland received bomb threats by email Wednesday, triggering widespread evacuations in Italy while police elsewhere assessed the messages as hoaxes and advised schools to remain open.

About 6,300 schools in Italy received the threats, according to Italian authorities. Hundreds were evacuated in cities including Rome, Milan, Naples, Palermo and Bologna, forcing parents to collect children while police and bomb-disposal teams checked buildings. No explosives or suspicious devices had been reported.

The disruption extended across the British Isles. More than 300 schools in Northern Ireland and about 470 in Scotland received threatening messages. Schools in the Republic of Ireland were also targeted. Police in all three areas assessed the messages as non-credible, and authorities generally advised schools that evacuation was unnecessary based solely on the emails.

The message sent to Italian schools also threatened Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni. She said authorities had found no evidence of a "concrete danger" and described the campaign as intended to generate alarm and concern. Meloni convened intelligence and cybersecurity officials to review the incidents and possible connections to other recent threats.

Italian schools reopened normally Thursday, November 12, after authorities determined there was no concrete danger from the mass threats. Meloni also invited opposition party leaders to a security briefing following the incidents.

The Italian email was signed using the name "Mikhail Andropov," according to copies reviewed by Reuters and other news organizations. The same purported sender had previously claimed responsibility for bomb threats against newsrooms in Ireland and Bulgaria. CBS News reported that the same name was also used in threats involving European commercial flights earlier in November.

The school alerts follow a broader series of disruptions across Europe. More than 20 commercial flights received fake bomb threats less than a week earlier, while cyberattacks have recently affected other European infrastructure. Some European officials have raised concerns that such incidents could form part of wider hybrid disruption campaigns.

However, authorities have not attributed Wednesday's school threats to Russia, Iran or any other government, and Italian Defense Minister Guido Crosetto cautioned that such threats can also come from individuals seeking attention rather than foreign actors.
