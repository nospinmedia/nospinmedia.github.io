Trump Announces Major Oil, Gas and Mineral Exploration Plans for Greenland

Arctic Resource Push Raises Questions About Local Authority and Environmental Protections

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

President Donald Trump announced on Wednesday, October 14, 2026, that extensive oil, natural gas and mineral exploration is preparing to begin in Greenland, highlighting the Arctic territory's potential energy resources and strategic importance to U.S. national security.

In a statement posted on Truth Social, Trump described the planned exploration as "massive" and claimed that Greenland could contain some of the world's largest deposits of easily accessible oil. However, the president did not identify participating companies, specific drilling locations, investment amounts or a timetable for beginning operations.

The announcement follows a September 22 agreement between the United States, Denmark and Greenland that expanded American military access to the island while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenland's right to self-determination.

Under that agreement, the United States can expand its existing Pituffik Space Base and establish additional military facilities at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig, subject to the agreement's provisions. The arrangement also includes cooperation involving minerals and natural resources, although it does not establish a detailed commercial drilling program.

Trump emphasized Greenland's geographic position between North America and Europe, arguing that the island provides valuable early-warning capabilities against potential missile and drone attacks. The administration has connected its Arctic security priorities with broader interest in the territory's natural resources.

Despite Trump's announcement, Greenland maintains authority over its mineral and energy resources. The autonomous government manages exploration and extraction permits, meaning American companies cannot begin drilling simply because Washington supports the activity.

Greenland also ended new oil and gas exploration licensing in 2021, citing environmental concerns and the economic risks associated with petroleum development. Existing exploration rights may be subject to different conditions, but any proposed operations must comply with applicable Greenlandic laws and permitting requirements.

The island contains deposits of minerals important to advanced manufacturing, renewable energy technologies and defense equipment. However, its remote geography, limited infrastructure, harsh climate and high operating costs have historically made large-scale extraction difficult.

Industry experts have also cautioned that the existence of potentially valuable geological resources does not establish that commercially recoverable deposits are available. Exploration would be needed to determine their size, accessibility and economic viability.

The announcement raises questions about how Greenland's government will balance potential investment and employment opportunities against environmental protection, local decision-making and the long-term effects of industrial development in the Arctic.

For now, Trump's statement signals an intention to expand exploration, not confirmation that drilling has begun or that Greenland has approved new projects.
