Spokane Old Trails Fire: 37-Year-Old Arrested on Suspicion of First-Degree Arson

$1M bond; 700+ structures lost and 67,000 evacuated

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Authorities in Washington state arrested Aaron F. Farinacci, 37, on Monday, August 3, 2026, on suspicion of first-degree arson in connection with the Old Trails Fire, the largest of three wildfires burning around Spokane. The Spokane County Sheriff’s Office said Farinacci was booked and is being held on $1 million bond pending an initial court appearance on Tuesday, August 4, 2026.

Sheriff John Nowels said a citizen reported a man acting nervously near grass at the ignition area on Saturday, August 1, 2026. Deputies initially questioned and released Farinacci, but major-crimes detectives later reviewed body-camera footage and reports and matched the witness location to the fire’s origin. When detained, Farinacci possessed waterproof matches and a butane lighter, according to the sheriff. Prosecutor Preston McCollam said Farinacci’s first appearance is scheduled for Tuesday. Officials also noted he has a prior felony manslaughter conviction in Arizona. Investigators have not identified a cause for the region’s two other fires, and those probes remain active.

The Spokane-area fires have destroyed at least 700 buildings and forced roughly 67,000 people to evacuate, according to officials. Together, the three blazes had burned about 12.5 square miles (32 square kilometers) by Monday and remained uncontained. The Old Trails Fire jumped the Spokane River, accelerating damage across neighborhoods. No deaths or major injuries had been reported as of Monday, though authorities continued welfare checks and missing-person reconciliations. Governor Bob Ferguson called the destruction "likely the worst natural disaster in Spokane history," as damage assessments continued and residents began returning to assess losses where conditions allowed.

Fire officials warned that hot, dry weather is expected to return midweek, even as more than 1,000 firefighters surge into the area. Authorities urged residents to avoid flying drones over burn zones, which can ground aerial firefighting, and to follow evolving evacuation guidance while investigators finalize cause determinations for the remaining fires.
