U.S. jury convicts Huawei on 10 of 12 federal counts

Chinese tech giant acquitted of racketeering conspiracy

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 25, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A federal jury in Brooklyn, New York, convicted Chinese telecommunications giant Huawei Technologies on 10 of 12 federal criminal counts, including conspiracy to steal trade secrets and defrauding banks, while acquitting the company of racketeering conspiracy.

The verdict followed a 12-week trial before U.S. District Judge Ann M. Donnelly involving parent company Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. and three subsidiaries: Huawei Device Co. Ltd., Futurewei Technologies Inc., and Huawei Device USA Inc. Jurors convicted one or more of the companies of offenses including conspiracy to steal trade secrets, wire and bank fraud, conspiracy to defraud the United States, violations of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, and money laundering conspiracy. Huawei was acquitted of racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice.

Prosecutors presented evidence that Huawei and its subsidiaries obtained technology and confidential information from American companies. The cases included T-Mobile's Tappy testing robot, source code used on Cisco routers, proprietary equipment belonging to Fujitsu, hardware from CNEX, and antenna technology from Quintel. In the T-Mobile case, prosecutors said a Huawei employee removed part of the testing robot from a controlled laboratory after employees had already sent photographs and technical information about it to China.

The case also centered on Huawei's business in Iran. Prosecutors said Huawei concealed its relationship with Skycom, misrepresented its Iranian operations to banks and the U.S. government, and used those representations to maintain access to billions of dollars in credit, loans and U.S.-dollar clearing services.

Huawei has disputed the government's broader characterization of its conduct. Defense attorney Brian Heberlig said the company plans to appeal and pointed to the jury's rejection of the racketeering charge. China's Foreign Ministry responded Wednesday by saying Beijing opposes what it describes as U.S. efforts to suppress Chinese companies.

Huawei and its subsidiaries face potential fines, forfeiture and restitution. Sentencing is scheduled for September 14, 2027.
