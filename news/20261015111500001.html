Netflix to Cut About 1,000 Jobs in Biggest Layoff Since 2022

Streaming Giant Trims Roughly 6% of Workforce as Revenue Growth Slows

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 2:52 PM ET

Netflix is cutting approximately 1,000 jobs, or about 6% of its workforce, in the streaming company's largest round of layoffs since 2022 as management looks to reduce costs while revenue growth slows.

The company notified employees of the reductions on October 14, according to reporting from Reuters and other outlets. Netflix had approximately 16,000 employees worldwide at the end of 2025, putting the scale of the announced cuts at roughly one out of every 16 positions.

The layoffs come during a period of major change for the streaming business. Netflix remains one of the world's largest subscription entertainment services, but the company is operating in an increasingly mature market where maintaining the rapid growth rates of earlier years has become more difficult.

Netflix has expanded beyond its traditional subscription business in recent years, introducing a lower-priced advertising-supported tier and investing in live programming, games and other forms of entertainment. Those initiatives are intended to create additional sources of revenue as competition for viewers remains intense.

The latest reductions are notable because Netflix had largely avoided the large-scale workforce cuts seen at several other technology and media companies in recent years.

Netflix previously eliminated approximately 450 positions in 2022, when subscriber losses forced the company to reconsider spending and its growth strategy. The newly announced reduction is substantially larger.

The company has continued to generate billions of dollars in revenue and remains profitable, meaning the layoffs do not necessarily indicate an immediate financial crisis. Instead, they come as Netflix focuses more heavily on operating efficiency and maintaining profitability as its business grows larger.

The cuts also illustrate the continuing employment uncertainty across the broader entertainment and technology industries. Media companies have spent years restructuring operations as consumers move away from traditional television while streaming services face pressure to demonstrate sustainable profits rather than pursuing subscriber growth at almost any cost.

For the approximately 1,000 Netflix employees affected, however, the restructuring has an immediate personal impact regardless of the company's broader financial position.

Investors will now be watching whether the workforce reduction is an isolated restructuring or signals a broader shift in Netflix's spending plans as it enters its next phase of growth.
