Who controls Ukraine's battlefield internet: Musk or the U.S. government?

Starlink restored after restrictions disrupted military operations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 25, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

Ukraine says normal operating speeds have been restored to Starlink and Starshield after more than a week of restrictions disrupted battlefield communications and drone operations — but a dispute over who imposed those restrictions has left a larger question unresolved: Who ultimately controls a communications system being used by a sovereign country's military?

Ukraine's Defense Ministry said connections were again stable on equipment moving faster than 400 kilometers per hour, returning performance to the level its military needs. Ukrainian officials said the restrictions had limited connections to roughly 160 kph or less, interfering with efforts to intercept Russian attack drones, disrupting frontline communications and forcing some operations to be canceled.

The distinction between Starlink and Starshield is important. Starlink is SpaceX's commercial satellite internet system, while Starshield is its military-grade service. Ukraine uses both, and the U.S. government has contracted with SpaceX to provide military satellite capabilities to Ukraine.

Elon Musk said Saturday that Ukraine's military had violated Starlink's terms of service by using the commercial network for offensive drone strikes and said a loophole allowing that use had been closed. Ukrainian officials have said their military cannot use regular Starlink for medium- or long-range attack-drone missions.

Who ordered the broader restrictions affecting Ukraine's military remains disputed. Reuters has reported that an order to throttle Starshield came from inside the White House. CBS News, citing people familiar with the matter, reported that a decision by Musk led to restrictions after he held discussions with administration officials. The Trump administration and Pentagon have denied ordering deliberate restrictions on Ukraine's battlefield communications, while Musk has previously said the U.S. government controls Starshield's military use.

The disagreement highlights Ukraine's dependence on satellite infrastructure owned by a private American company but also purchased and used through U.S. government defense arrangements. With service restored, the immediate military problem may be resolved, but questions about who has the authority to restrict it remain unanswered.
