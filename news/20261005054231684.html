Trump taps intelligence chief to lead new AI task force

Jay Clayton will head federal 'Super Intelligence Force'

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 5, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

President Trump has tapped Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to lead a new federal artificial intelligence task force as the administration weighs how to keep the United States competitive in rapidly advancing AI while addressing growing concerns about the technology's risks.

Trump announced the Super Intelligence Force on October 4, using his preferred term for artificial intelligence. The group will coordinate federal engagement with consumers, public-interest groups, religious organizations, critical-infrastructure providers and AI companies. It will report directly to Trump and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles.

Clayton already oversees the nation's 18-agency intelligence community as director of national intelligence, putting a national-security official at the center of the administration's AI strategy. During his Senate confirmation hearing, Clayton described AI as both an opportunity and a threat and said the government needed to understand both sides of the rapidly developing technology. He previously served as chairman of the Securities and Exchange Commission during Trump's first term and later as U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York.

Other members of the new task force include Federal Trade Commission Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon technology chief Emil Michael and Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor.

The announcement follows a White House meeting with leading AI executives last week. Trump said participating companies signed a voluntary accord establishing internal and external monitoring intended to identify safety problems while allowing AI development to continue. The administration has generally favored voluntary safeguards and a lighter regulatory approach as it competes with China for technological leadership.

The appointment gives Clayton responsibility for AI policy while he remains the country's top intelligence official, further connecting the development of advanced AI with U.S. economic and national-security strategy.
