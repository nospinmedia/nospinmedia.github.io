U.S. Measles Cases Climb to 4,410 as Outbreaks Continue

Most Patients Are Unvaccinated or Have Unknown Vaccination Status

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 17, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The number of confirmed measles cases in the United States has climbed to 4,410, according to the latest federal data, as outbreaks continue in several parts of the country.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reported the updated total on October 16, with hundreds of additional infections recorded during the latest reporting period. The growing national count makes 2026 another significant year for a disease that was declared eliminated from continuous domestic transmission in the United States in 2000.

Federal data show the overwhelming majority of people infected this year were either unvaccinated or had an unknown vaccination status. Measles is highly contagious and can cause serious complications, particularly among young children and people with weakened immune systems. Hospitalizations have also been reported as outbreaks have expanded.

Pennsylvania has become one of the country's largest outbreak areas, reporting more than 1,200 cases. Federal disease investigators have been deployed to assist state and local health authorities with tracking infections and containment efforts. Pennsylvania has also expanded vaccination efforts as officials work to slow transmission.

The latest national figures represent a broader development than the Pennsylvania outbreak previously covered by No Spin Media. Cases are being tracked across multiple states and outbreaks, showing that transmission is not limited to a single region.

Health officials continue to recommend the MMR vaccine, which protects against measles, mumps and rubella. The CDC says two doses are about 97% effective at preventing measles, while one dose is about 93% effective.

Measles spreads through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes, and the virus can remain infectious in an enclosed space after that person has left.
