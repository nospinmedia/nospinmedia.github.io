Judge Says USPS Violated Court Order on Mail-Voting Rule

Regulation remains blocked despite final publication

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

A federal judge ruled that the Trump administration and the U.S. Postal Service (USPS) violated a nationwide court injunction by completing a regulation that would overhaul mail-in voting procedures ahead of the November 3, 2026 midterm elections. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani of the District of Massachusetts found that federal officials failed to comply with her earlier order prohibiting completion of the rulemaking process, although she declined to impose additional contempt sanctions.

Judge Talwani's ruling leaves in place a nationwide injunction preventing the regulation from taking effect while litigation continues. The disputed rule would establish new barcode and envelope formatting requirements for mailed ballots and require election officials to perform additional citizenship verification checks before processing ballots. The judge rejected the administration's argument that publishing the rule without enforcing it complied with the injunction, writing that the government's interpretation conflicted with the court's clear directive.

The decision followed a separate 6-3 U.S. Supreme Court ruling on August 24 that lifted an injunction in a different lawsuit brought by 23 states led by California. After that decision, the Department of Justice asked Judge Talwani to dissolve her separate injunction, arguing that publication of the regulation should be permitted because implementation would remain suspended until all legal challenges were resolved. Talwani denied that request, concluding that completing the rule itself violated the court's prior order.

The ruling preserves existing nationwide mail-voting procedures as election officials prepare to distribute overseas and military ballots in early September. Civil rights organizations, including the ACLU, welcomed the decision, while the administration is expected to continue defending the regulation as litigation moves forward. The underlying legal dispute over federal authority to regulate election procedures remains unresolved and could ultimately return to appellate courts before the midterm elections.
