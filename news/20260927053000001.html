Supreme Court blocks Missouri's GOP-backed congressional map

State will use 2022 districts for November midterms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 27, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court has blocked Missouri from using a Republican-backed congressional map enacted in 2025, clearing the way for the state to conduct its November 2026 congressional elections using district lines adopted in 2022. The unsigned ruling issued September 25 is the latest turn in a months-long fight over which congressional boundaries Missouri voters will use in the midterms.

The dispute centers on a 2025 redistricting plan approved by Missouri lawmakers and signed by Gov. Mike Kehoe. The new map significantly changed the Kansas City-area 5th Congressional District, represented by Democrat Emanuel Cleaver, and could have increased Republicans' advantage in Missouri's eight-member U.S. House delegation. Opponents gathered enough signatures to force a statewide referendum on the new map.

On September 3, the Missouri Supreme Court ruled that the referendum petition was valid and that the 2025 map never took effect under state law. The state court said the 2022 congressional districts remain in force unless Missouri voters approve the new map in the November election. The situation was complicated because Missouri had already conducted its August congressional primaries using the disputed 2025 districts, with more than 1.2 million voters participating.

Federal courts subsequently concluded that switching maps between the primary and general elections created constitutional problems and ordered Missouri to use the 2025 boundaries. The U.S. Supreme Court has now stayed those federal court orders. Its ruling says lower federal courts may not prohibit Missouri from using the 2022 map or require the state to use the 2025 map in November.

The decision means Missouri's November congressional election will proceed under the 2022 district boundaries, while voters will separately decide whether to approve the 2025 redistricting plan through the statewide referendum.
