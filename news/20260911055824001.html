Denver and civil rights groups sue to block armed ICE agents at polls

Lawsuit invokes Civil War-era law ahead of November midterms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 11, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The city of Denver and three national civil rights organizations have filed a federal lawsuit seeking to prevent armed U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers and other federal agents from conducting enforcement operations at polling places during the November 2026 midterm elections.

The lawsuit was filed Thursday in U.S. District Court in Washington, D.C. by Democracy Forward on behalf of Denver, the League of United Latin American Citizens, Common Cause and UnidosUS. It names the Department of Homeland Security, ICE, DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin and Acting ICE Director David Venturella as defendants.

The challenge relies in part on 18 U.S.C. § 592, a federal prohibition with roots in the Civil War era that bars federal officials from ordering or keeping "troops or armed men" at places where elections are held, except when necessary to repel armed enemies of the United States. Violations can carry up to five years in prison.

The lawsuit follows comments Mullin made on September 1 about the circumstances under which immigration officers could be present at voting locations. Mullin said ICE is not there to patrol polling places, but added that agents could enter one if responding to a threat or serving a warrant on someone they had been pursuing.

Trump administration officials have denied having concrete plans to station ICE agents at polling locations. The lawsuit is therefore preemptive, asking the court to declare such armed deployments unlawful and prohibit federal officers from carrying weapons at polling places and other locations where ballots are collected.

The plaintiffs argue that the possibility of armed immigration enforcement at voting sites could intimidate eligible voters, particularly Latino and naturalized citizens. That is an allegation in the lawsuit, not a finding by the court or an established intent of the administration.

Separately, Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine has said the military has no plans to deploy troops or National Guard members to polling places.
