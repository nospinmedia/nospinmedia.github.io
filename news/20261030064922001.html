Netanyahu's bloc loses majority as Israel faces deadlock

Likud alleges election was 'stolen' by Arab-led parties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 30, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's governing bloc has lost its parliamentary majority in Israel's election, leaving no major bloc with enough seats to form a government as Netanyahu's Likud party makes unsupported claims that Arab-led parties stole the election.

With nearly all ballots counted, Netanyahu's right-wing and religious bloc was projected to win 53 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, eight short of the 61 seats required for a majority. A rival bloc led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot was projected to receive 48 seats, while Israel's Arab-led parties were projected to win a record 19 seats.

The results leave Arab lawmakers potentially holding a decisive role in determining whether either side can assemble a governing coalition. Netanyahu remains prime minister while negotiations proceed, and the final composition of Israel's next government could take weeks to determine.

As the votes were counted, Likud accused Arab-led parties of widespread election misconduct and said the election had been 'stolen.' Netanyahu and his party alleged illegal funding, voter intimidation, ballot manipulation and other irregularities and called for investigations by Israeli police and the Shin Bet domestic security agency.

Election officials said they had not found evidence supporting claims of widespread fraud. The Central Elections Committee said voting proceeded with relatively few irregularities, while police said they had received no information indicating systematic election violations. The allegations have not been shown to have affected the election results.

Arab political leaders rejected Likud's accusations and said the claims were an attempt to delegitimize Arab voters after unusually strong turnout. Netanyahu and Likud maintain that their allegations deserve investigation and say they have submitted evidence to authorities.

The deadlock means neither Netanyahu nor Eisenkot currently has a clear path to the 61-seat majority needed to govern, making coalition negotiations — including possible agreements involving Arab parties — the next major stage of the election.
