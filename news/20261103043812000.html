High fuel costs follow voters into Election Day

Gas, diesel and heating oil remain well above last year's prices

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 3, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Americans are heading into Election Day with fuel costs substantially higher than a year ago, adding gasoline, diesel and heating expenses to broader concerns about affordability and the cost of living.

The latest U.S. Energy Information Administration data put regular gasoline at $4.27 per gallon nationally for the week ending October 26, about $1.23 higher than a year earlier. Diesel averaged $6.35 per gallon, up about $2.63 from last year. Retail gasoline prices have declined from their September average of $4.35, but remain elevated compared with recent years.

Heating costs are also being affected. Residential heating oil averaged $6.23 per gallon nationally during the same week. The EIA expects households primarily heated with oil, concentrated heavily in the Northeast, to face the largest increase in heating-fuel expenses this winter. Its October forecast estimated heating-oil prices would be more than 30% higher than last winter, although a forecast for milder Northeast weather could partially offset household spending increases.

Energy markets remain unusually volatile because of the conflict involving Iran and disruptions affecting Middle Eastern oil shipments. Crude prices fell more than 7% Monday as diplomatic activity raised hopes that supply conditions could improve. Those expectations weakened Tuesday after U.S. forces struck targets on Iran's Qeshm Island, sending crude prices higher again.

Fuel prices have become part of the closing economic argument in the 2026 midterm elections. President Trump has pointed to recent declines in oil prices as evidence that costs could ease, while Democrats have emphasized the higher prices consumers are currently paying. Changes in crude prices generally take time to work through refining, distribution and ultimately retail fuel prices.
