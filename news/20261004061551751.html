Trump posts Sen. Tom Cotton's number in daylight saving fight

President urges public pressure as GOP senator resists permanent change

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 4, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

President Donald Trump posted what he said was the personal cellphone number of Republican Sen. Tom Cotton of Arkansas and encouraged people to call him as part of Trump's push to make daylight saving time permanent across the United States.

Trump posted the number Saturday on Truth Social, telling supporters that Cotton was standing in the way of legislation that would end the twice-yearly changing of clocks. Reporters who called the number reached a voicemail identifying Cotton before the mailbox became full. Cotton responded publicly to Trump but did not directly address the release of his number.

The dispute centers on the Sunshine Protection Act, which would establish permanent daylight saving time rather than returning to standard time each fall. Trump argues that keeping later evening daylight throughout the year would benefit recreation, businesses and public safety. Cotton opposes the proposal because it would mean significantly darker winter mornings in many parts of the country, including children traveling to school before sunrise.

The United States has tried permanent daylight saving time before. Congress enacted it during the 1970s energy crisis, but widespread complaints about dark winter mornings led lawmakers to reverse course. The current proposal has similarly exposed disagreement over whether Americans would prefer more daylight in the morning or evening if the clock changes were eliminated.

Trump's decision to publicly share Cotton's number adds a personal pressure campaign to that policy dispute. Cotton is a fellow Republican and generally a Trump ally, but he has remained opposed to permanent daylight saving time despite the president's lobbying. Other senators have also raised concerns, leaving the legislation's path uncertain.

Most Americans appear to agree on one thing: they dislike changing the clocks. The harder question is which time should become permanent.
