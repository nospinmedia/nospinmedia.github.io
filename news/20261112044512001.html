Scott Colom wins historic Mississippi Senate race

Democrat takes seat after margin grows from 43 votes to thousands

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 12, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Democrat Scott Colom has defeated Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi's closely contested U.S. Senate race, ending more than a week of uncertainty and becoming the state's first Black U.S. senator since Reconstruction.

The race was extraordinarily close on election night. After the final update early the next morning, Colom led Hyde-Smith by just 43 votes. Election officials then spent days reviewing absentee and provisional ballots, known in Mississippi as affidavit ballots. As counties completed those reviews, Colom repeatedly gained ground. The Associated Press called the race at 2:58 p.m. Wednesday, November 11, with 99% of the vote counted across Mississippi's 82 counties and too few ballots remaining to change the outcome.

At the time of the AP race call, Colom had received 473,336 votes, or 49.2%, compared with 468,667 votes, or 48.7%, for Hyde-Smith. That put Colom ahead by 4,669 votes. Independent candidate Ty Pinkins had received 20,234 votes, or 2.1%. Pinkins' total was more than four times the margin separating the two leading candidates, providing additional context for how close the three-candidate contest was without establishing how those voters would have voted in a two-candidate race.

Colom's 49.2% plurality is sufficient to win the seat outright. Mississippi does not require a runoff in a general election for federal office when no candidate receives more than 50% of the vote.

Hyde-Smith conceded Wednesday in a video message, saying, "While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters." She congratulated Colom and wished him well as he prepares to represent the state.

Colom, the 43-year-old district attorney for Mississippi's 16th Judicial District, becomes the first Democrat elected to a U.S. Senate seat from Mississippi since John C. Stennis won reelection in 1982. Hyde-Smith had served in the Senate since 2018, initially through an appointment before winning elections that year and again in 2020.

Colom's victory also changes the national Senate picture. It represents Democrats' eighth Republican Senate-seat pickup of the 2026 midterms and brings the incoming Democratic majority to 55 seats, with Alaska's Senate contest still unresolved when the Mississippi race was called.

The result is particularly notable given Mississippi's recent voting history. President Trump carried the state by nearly 23 percentage points in 2024. Colom's campaign emphasized economic concerns, including prices, fuel costs and the effects of tariffs on Mississippi farmers. Results showed particularly strong turnout in the Mississippi Delta and majority-Black counties, while voting shifted toward Democrats across much of the state compared with 2024.

The contest also carried an unusual personal history. In 2022, President Joe Biden nominated Colom to a federal judgeship in northern Mississippi. Republican Sen. Roger Wicker and former Republican Gov. Haley Barbour supported the nomination, but Hyde-Smith blocked Colom's confirmation. Colom said during the Senate campaign that his candidacy was not about retaliation.

The results remain unofficial while certification continues. Mississippi's 82 counties have until Friday, November 13, to certify their county results. The Mississippi Secretary of State's office is scheduled to officially declare the statewide general election results on December 3, 2026. Mississippi does not have an automatic statutory recount procedure for a U.S. Senate race; a candidate seeking a review would have to pursue the matter through the courts.
