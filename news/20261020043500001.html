Kenyan cult leader convicted of murdering 190 children

More than 400 bodies have been recovered from Shakahola Forest

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 20, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Paul Mackenzie, the leader of Kenya's Good News International Church, and 28 others have been convicted of murder in the deaths of 190 children and one adult in one of the world's deadliest cult-related cases.

A Kenyan court found that Mackenzie and his associates operated a religious sect that compelled followers to fast until death, teaching that they and their children would go to heaven before the world ended. The victims covered by the murder case were found after authorities began uncovering shallow graves in Shakahola Forest near Kenya's Indian Ocean coast in 2023.

Justice Diana Kavedza Mochache rejected the defense argument that the children had voluntarily fasted. Evidence presented in the case described an organized system in which children were isolated and denied food, while church leaders and enforcers maintained control over followers. Prosecutors said starvation caused many of the deaths, while examinations of other victims found evidence of suffocation, strangulation and traumatic injuries.

Authorities have recovered more than 400 bodies from the Shakahola area during the wider investigation. Many were badly decomposed, complicating efforts to identify victims and determine how each person died. The murder convictions announced Monday cover 191 victims, while Mackenzie faces separate proceedings involving other deaths and charges including terrorism and child torture.

Mackenzie was arrested in April 2023, although Kenyan authorities had investigated him years earlier. His church was shut down in 2019, after which he moved his operation to the Shakahola Forest. The scale of the deaths has fueled criticism in Kenya over why authorities failed to stop the group sooner and prompted calls for stronger oversight of religious organizations.

Mackenzie and the others convicted are scheduled to be sentenced on November 6. His attorney said the convictions will be appealed.
