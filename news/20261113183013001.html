U.S. returns more than $12 billion in resource proceeds to Venezuela

New report details how revenues have been distributed since Maduro's ouster

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 13, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The United States has disbursed more than $12 billion in proceeds from Venezuelan oil and other natural resources back to Venezuela since Nicolás Maduro was ousted in January, according to a newly released State Department accounting report.

The 53-page report, prepared by accounting firm KPMG, covers transactions from January 3 through September 30. It shows $12.26 billion was deposited into a U.S.-managed escrow account from sales of Venezuelan resources. By the end of September, $12.11 billion — nearly 99% of the collected money — had been disbursed, leaving approximately $168.6 million.

More than $12.1 billion of the revenue came from crude oil, petroleum products and natural gas, with smaller amounts generated from gold, fertilizer and other minerals. The funds are subject to U.S. sanctions and anti-money-laundering reviews before being released.

Most of the money was distributed inside Venezuela through foreign currency auctions conducted by commercial banks. The report lists approximately $9.4 billion going to legal entities and $2.9 billion to individuals through those transactions.

U.S. authorities also directly paid about $133 million to vendors for goods and services supporting Venezuela. Those expenditures included nearly $24 million for dialysis and coal-mining equipment, $8.7 million to settle vaccine procurement debts with the Pan American Health Organization and $5 million to Miami-based law firm Greenberg Traurig.

The public report does not identify the 20 companies that purchased oil and gold or the final recipients of the currency auctions, leaving portions of the system undisclosed despite the administration's stated goal of providing greater transparency over Venezuela's resource revenues.
