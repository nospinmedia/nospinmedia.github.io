Supreme Court Leaves Maryland Gun Restrictions In Place

Justice Thomas signals narrower challenge could return

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 17, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court declined on Monday, November 16, to hear Novotny v. Moore, No. 25-1324, a Second Amendment challenge to Maryland restrictions on carrying firearms in designated "sensitive places." The decision leaves a lower-court ruling largely upholding the restrictions in place but does not establish a new nationwide Supreme Court precedent on where states may prohibit firearms.

Maryland enacted Senate Bill 1 in 2023 after the Supreme Court's New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen decision changed the legal framework courts use to evaluate gun restrictions. The Maryland law expanded locations where licensed gun owners may not carry firearms, including schools, healthcare facilities, parks, mass transit, establishments serving alcohol and areas associated with public demonstrations.

In January 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 4th Circuit, ruling in the consolidated appeal Kipke v. Moore, upheld most of Maryland's location-based restrictions. Courts did not uphold every provision, including a default rule broadly prohibiting firearms on private property open to the public unless the owner expressly permitted them.

Justice Clarence Thomas wrote separately regarding the denial of review. He said the challenge, brought by lead petitioner Katherine Novotny against Maryland Governor Wes Moore, bundled multiple restrictions involving different locations and historical questions, making it unsuitable for resolving the broader constitutional issue. Thomas nevertheless indicated the court should eventually address the boundaries of what governments may designate as a "sensitive place."

The Supreme Court separately struck down a similar Hawaii default private-property restriction in June 2026. The justices are also scheduled to hear arguments on December 2 in a major Second Amendment challenge involving state restrictions on assault-style semiautomatic rifles.
