Carney Sets Conditions for Restarting U.S. Trade Talks

Canada Urges More Constructive Negotiations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 2, 2026 at 12:53 PM ET

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada remains willing to resume trade negotiations with the United States, but only if discussions move forward in a more constructive manner. Speaking in Ottawa on Sept. 1, Carney criticized recent public comments from senior members of the Trump administration, saying inflammatory rhetoric had made productive negotiations more difficult and urging both governments to focus on substantive policy issues rather than public exchanges.

Trade talks between the two countries stalled in August after negotiators failed to reach agreement on several key issues, including tariffs, automotive manufacturing and protections affecting sectors covered by the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement (CUSMA). Following the collapse of negotiations, the United States imposed new tariffs on selected Canadian imports, while Canada announced plans to respond with retaliatory tariffs on approximately $20 billion worth of U.S. goods beginning Sept. 8 unless progress is made.

Carney said Canada remains committed to reaching a mutually beneficial agreement that preserves the country's ability to make independent economic decisions while maintaining the highly integrated North American supply chain. He argued that prolonged trade tensions would increase costs for businesses and consumers on both sides of the border, particularly in manufacturing industries that depend on cross-border production and transportation.

The comments come as officials in both countries continue to signal that negotiations could resume, although no new meeting has been announced. Economists have warned that an extended tariff dispute between the United States and Canada could disrupt regional supply chains and increase costs across multiple industries. For now, both governments appear to be maintaining their current positions while leaving open the possibility of renewed negotiations in the coming weeks.
