Canada extends federal gas tax relief into 2027

Fuel tax holiday to phase out next spring

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 2:53 PM ET

The Canadian government announced it will extend its temporary suspension of federal fuel excise taxes through January 31, 2027, delaying a planned expiration that would have taken effect after the Labour Day weekend. Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne said the extension is intended to help households and businesses manage elevated fuel costs as global energy markets remain under pressure.

The tax holiday, first introduced in April 2026 by the government of Prime Minister Mark Carney, removes 10 Canadian cents per litre from federal gasoline taxes and 4 cents per litre from diesel and aviation fuel. Under the revised plan, the tax suspension will remain fully in effect through the end of January before being restored gradually. Beginning February 1, 2027, half of the federal excise tax will return, with the full rate scheduled to be reinstated on April 1, 2027.

According to Finance Canada, the original program was expected to reduce federal revenue by about C$2.4 billion, while the extension is projected to add another C$2.9 billion, bringing the total cost of the relief program to approximately C$5.3 billion. Government officials said the measure is designed to provide temporary support while fuel prices remain elevated due to ongoing uncertainty in global energy markets, including disruptions affecting international oil supplies.

The extension has received support from groups that argue lower fuel taxes provide immediate relief for consumers and businesses facing higher transportation costs. Others have questioned the fiscal impact, noting that the reduced tax revenue will increase federal borrowing unless offset by spending reductions or higher revenue elsewhere. The announcement comes as fuel prices remain a significant cost-of-living concern across Canada, particularly for commuters, commercial transportation, and agricultural producers.
