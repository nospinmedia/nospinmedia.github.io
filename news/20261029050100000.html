Qatar presents Iran with new draft aimed at ending war

Tehran calls the ideas unacceptable but keeps diplomatic channel open

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Qatari mediators have presented Iran with a new draft framework aimed at ending the eight-month war involving Iran, the United States and Israel, but Iranian officials are disputing whether what was delivered constitutes an acceptable peace offer.

An official briefed on the negotiations told Reuters that Qatar presented the draft in Tehran on Sunday, with Iran expected to respond by the end of the week. The framework sets out a seven-day timeline of daily confidence-building measures intended to address concerns held by both Tehran and Washington. The official said the process could begin early next week and several countries could serve as guarantors.

A senior Iranian official involved in the negotiations offered a sharply different description, saying Qatar had presented only “unacceptable ideas and not an offer.” The official said Iran would not show flexibility over its nuclear position. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, however, said Tehran continues to give diplomacy a chance and remains in close contact with intermediaries.

The latest Qatari effort follows an earlier seven-day proposal from Iran. Under that plan, Tehran offered a path toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz if Washington met conditions that included ending the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports and lifting sanctions on Iranian oil exports. President Trump rejected that proposal, leading to further mediation. Washington has sought to include Iran's nuclear program in negotiations from the outset, while Tehran says its conditions must be addressed first.

Qatar and Pakistan have been leading mediation efforts as the conflict continues to disrupt global shipping and energy supplies and fuels concerns about further regional escalation. The latest negotiations therefore represent an active diplomatic channel, but significant disagreements remain over both the substance and sequencing of any agreement.
