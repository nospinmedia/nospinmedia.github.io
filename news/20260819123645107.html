NASA Images Reveal 60-Foot Crater From SpaceX Moon Impact

Orbiter captures first detailed views of August lunar collision

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 21, 2026 at 12:17 PM ET

NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) has released high-resolution before-and-after images showing the 60-foot (18-meter) crater created when a spent SpaceX Falcon 9 upper stage struck the Moon on August 5, 2026. The newly released images provide the clearest view yet of the impact site, revealing a distinctive ejecta pattern and confirming how the collision altered the lunar surface.

The 8,800-pound rocket stage struck the Moon at roughly 5,400 mph after spending more than a year in deep space following a January 2025 mission carrying commercial lunar landers. During imaging passes on August 11 and 12, LRO flew about 60 miles above the surface and used its high-resolution camera to document the fresh crater, which NASA measured at approximately 18 meters wide and less than 10 feet deep. The impact exposed fresh subsurface material, allowing scientists to compare newly excavated terrain with the surrounding weathered lunar surface.

South Korea's Danuri lunar orbiter helped identify the impact location before NASA captured the detailed follow-up imagery. Because researchers know the rocket's mass, trajectory and impact speed, the event provides an unusually well-documented opportunity to refine models of crater formation, ejecta behavior and lunar surface evolution. Scientists say such calibrated data improves understanding of both artificial impacts and naturally occurring meteoroid strikes.

The findings come as government agencies and commercial companies prepare for increased lunar exploration under programs such as NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) initiative. Researchers say observations from this impact will help improve future mission planning, hazard assessments and scientific models as traffic to the Moon continues to grow.
