UEFA Nations Vote To Authorize World Cup Boycott If FIFA Pursues Private Equity Sale

Dispute Over Commercial Control Opens Major Rift In Global Soccer

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 31, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The 55 member associations of UEFA voted unanimously to authorize a potential boycott of future FIFA competitions, including the FIFA World Cup, if FIFA moves forward with plans to sell a minority ownership stake in a new commercial entity managing its premier tournaments. The vote represents one of the most significant governance disputes in modern international soccer and places renewed pressure on FIFA President Gianni Infantino's proposal to attract outside investment.

According to Reuters, FIFA has explored creating a commercial subsidiary that would manage the business operations of major competitions while selling a minority interest to private investors. UEFA officials argue that introducing private equity into the commercial structure of the World Cup could reduce the influence of national football associations and place greater emphasis on financial returns rather than the long-term interests of the sport.

UEFA President Aleksander Čeferin said football's leading international competitions should remain under the stewardship of the sport's governing institutions rather than outside investors. The unanimous vote does not immediately remove European teams from FIFA events but authorizes a boycott should FIFA formally proceed with the proposed transaction without addressing UEFA's concerns.

Reuters has also reported that the proposed commercial venture is expected to seek outside investment led by Thrive Eternal, an investment vehicle founded by Joshua Kushner, the brother of President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner. FIFA has emphasized that any outside investment would involve only a minority stake in the commercial entity and would not transfer control over the governance or rules of international football.

European nations account for many of the world's highest-ranked men's and women's national teams and generate a substantial share of the World Cup's global television audience, sponsorship revenue and commercial value. Analysts say a prolonged dispute between FIFA and UEFA could have significant financial and organizational consequences for international football if negotiations fail to produce a compromise.

FIFA has not finalized any investment agreement and discussions surrounding the proposed commercial structure remain ongoing. Officials from both organizations are expected to continue negotiations in the coming weeks.
