Senior U.S. Army soldier arrested in Britain over suspected Russia-linked espionage

NATO-assigned officer held under U.K. national security law

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 11, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A 41-year-old senior U.S. Army soldier assigned to a NATO command in Britain has been arrested on suspicion of espionage-related offenses connected to Russia, according to British and U.S. authorities.

The soldier was arrested November 10 near Gloucester, England, under Britain's National Security Act 2023. Counter Terrorism Policing South East said he is suspected of obtaining or disclosing protected information and engaging in conduct intended to assist a foreign intelligence service. Authorities have not publicly identified the soldier, and the allegations have not resulted in a conviction.

The U.S. Army said the soldier is assigned to NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, whose headquarters is at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire. The multinational headquarters is designed to deploy rapidly and command NATO forces during military operations and crises.

British counterterrorism officers carried out the arrest with assistance from the FBI, U.S. Army Counterintelligence Command and other U.S. military authorities. Police also searched two locations in Gloucestershire as part of the investigation. The soldier was being held at a London police station while the investigation continued.

FBI Director Kash Patel described the allegations as being of the "utmost concern" to the United States and its allies. U.S. Army counterintelligence officials said the case highlights the continuing threat posed by insiders with access to sensitive information.

British police emphasized that the investigation is separate from other recent national-security cases involving U.S. military facilities in the United Kingdom. Those include investigations into suspected Iran-linked threats at British air bases used by American forces.

Authorities have disclosed few details about what information the soldier is suspected of obtaining or providing, whether any material reached Russian intelligence, or how long the alleged activity may have occurred. The investigation remains active.
