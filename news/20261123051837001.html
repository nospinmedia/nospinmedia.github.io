Intelligence chief defends Trump calling Putin a 'friend'

Clayton says the label does not apply to Russia's war in Ukraine

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 23, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton defended President Trump's description of Russian President Vladimir Putin as a "friend," while drawing a distinction between Trump's personal relationship with Putin and Russia's actions in Ukraine.

Appearing on ABC's This Week with Martha Raddatz on November 22, Clayton defended Trump's language during the November 19 meeting with Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China. "In that circle of that conversation, using the term 'friend' is entirely appropriate," Clayton said.

But when Raddatz asked whether Putin was a friend of the United States, Clayton pointed directly to Russia's war against Ukraine. "He is not America's friend in Ukraine. Not at all. Not at all," he said. Clayton also appeared on CNN's State of the Union with Dana Bash, where he said he trusted Trump's judgment about the language he uses in diplomatic settings.

President Trump posted a photograph of himself shaking hands with Putin on Truth Social with the caption "My Friend." When reporters separately asked whether Putin was a friend to the United States, Trump said, "I can't speak to America," while adding that he has "fantastic relationships" with Putin and considers that beneficial.

The remarks drew criticism in the United States and abroad. Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer criticized Trump's approach to the Shenzhen meeting, while Sen. Jack Reed, the ranking Democrat on the Senate Armed Services Committee, also objected to the characterization. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Putin "is not our friend and will never be," and Reform UK deputy leader Richard Tice called describing Putin as a friend "a grave mistake."

Russia continues its war against Ukraine as U.S.-backed diplomatic efforts seek an agreement to end the conflict.
