Russian military helicopter crosses into Polish airspace

Poland scrambles fighters after 42-second NATO border violation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 24, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

A Russian Mi-8 military helicopter briefly entered Polish airspace from Russia's Kaliningrad region, prompting Poland to scramble fighter aircraft and place ground-based air defenses on standby in another incident involving Russian aircraft crossing into NATO territory.

Poland's Operational Command of the Armed Forces said the helicopter entered Polish territory north of Braniewo at 11:08 a.m. on September 23. The aircraft traveled approximately 300 meters inside Polish airspace and remained there for about 42 seconds before returning to Russian territory. Polish radar systems tracked the helicopter throughout the incident.

Polish military authorities said the violation appeared to be another attempt by Russia to test the readiness and response times of NATO air defenses. The incident follows previous Russian drone incursions into Polish and other NATO countries' airspace, which have increased concerns along the alliance's eastern flank as Russia's war against Ukraine continues.

The helicopter intrusion occurred as Polish President Karol Nawrocki renewed his push for a permanent U.S. military installation in Poland. Nawrocki said he expects Warsaw and Washington to reach an agreement on a base that has previously been proposed under the name Fort Trump. Polish officials have said such an installation could host between 3,000 and 5,000 U.S. troops.

Poland already hosts roughly 10,000 American troops, many serving on a rotational basis, and Polish officials have said they ultimately want the U.S. presence to reach about 15,000 troops. Nawrocki said infrastructure planning for a permanent installation is progressing, although no construction deadline or final location has been announced.

The latest airspace violation did not result in an attack or reported injuries, but it adds to concerns about repeated encounters between Russian military aircraft and NATO defenses along the alliance's eastern border.
