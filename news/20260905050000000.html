Russian drone strikes Ukraine's SBU headquarters

Zelenskyy orders a retaliatory response

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

A Russian jet-powered drone struck the headquarters of Ukraine's Security Service of Ukraine (SBU) in central Kyiv on September 4, injuring at least 12 people and prompting President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to order preparations for a retaliatory response. Ukrainian officials said the drone was aimed directly at the office of SBU chief Oleksandr Poklad, who was not reported injured in the attack.

The strike hit the SBU's central building on Volodymyrska Street, across from St. Sophia Cathedral, a UNESCO World Heritage site. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said nine people were hospitalized, including two in serious condition. Photographs from the scene showed emergency crews responding to fires and extensive damage at the security-service complex. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha described the attack as a major escalation and said the nearby cathedral had also been placed at risk.

Zelenskyy said he instructed Poklad to prepare an "appropriate, tangible response" that, where possible, would mirror the Russian attack, with Ukraine's military supporting the operation. In a later update, Zelenskyy said the geographic target of Ukraine's response had already been determined and that officials would choose the timing to maximize its effectiveness. The Ukrainian government had not publicly identified the intended Russian target.

The strike came amid intensified Russian drone and missile attacks on Kyiv and shortly before anticipated diplomatic activity involving U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. Ukraine has also been contending with shortages of Patriot air-defense interceptors as Russia increasingly uses fast jet-powered drones in attacks on the capital. The SBU is a central part of Ukraine's domestic security and intelligence apparatus and has also conducted operations against Russian military targets during the war.
