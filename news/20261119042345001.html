Israeli tanks cross Gaza ceasefire boundary

First major ground operation since 2025 truce forces families to flee

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 19, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Israeli forces launched a ground operation in central Gaza on Wednesday, November 18, sending tanks west across the ceasefire's "Yellow Line" and prompting Palestinians to flee areas near the Nuseirat and Bureij refugee camps. It is the first major ground operation publicly announced by the Israeli military beyond that boundary since the ceasefire took effect on October 10, 2025.

The Israeli military said troops and intelligence personnel were conducting a targeted operation to dismantle a tunnel that posed a threat to its forces. Tanks moved along Salah al-Din Road, Gaza's main north-south route, while residents reported gunfire and tank shelling. COGAT, the Israeli defense ministry body responsible for civilian affairs in the Palestinian territories, warned international aid organizations against movement in the area and closed a section of Salah al-Din Road near the Al-Bureij and Nuseirat camps. Families carrying belongings fled nearby tent encampments and residential areas.

The Yellow Line was established under the ceasefire as the boundary to which Israeli forces withdrew during the agreement's first phase. The arrangement left the Israeli military controlling roughly 60% of the Gaza Strip. Hamas called the new advance and resulting displacement a violation of the ceasefire and urged mediators to intervene. Israel maintains that it retains the ability to operate when it identifies an immediate threat.

Medics reported one Palestinian killed in an airstrike near the ground operation. Two other Palestinians were reported killed by Israeli fire in separate incidents elsewhere in Gaza that day. Those two deaths were not reported as part of the central Gaza ground operation itself.

The incursion comes as the U.S.-backed Board of Peace warns that the ceasefire remains at risk because major provisions have not been implemented. In a report submitted to the UN Security Council, the board warned of an "imminent collapse" of the ceasefire and said Israel had not yet permitted the interim Palestinian technocratic committee, the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza, to enter the Strip and begin administering Gaza. The committee is led by Ali Shaath and is intended to help establish conditions for an international stabilization force.
