Hurricane Isaias strengthens as Gulf Coast braces for landfall

Life-threatening storm surge threatens Florida, Alabama and Mississippi

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 8, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Hurricane Isaias strengthened early Thursday, October 8, becoming the first hurricane of the 2026 Atlantic season as it moved toward the northern Gulf Coast. The National Hurricane Center reported maximum sustained winds of 75 mph at 1 a.m. CDT, with the storm centered about 445 miles south-southwest of the mouth of the Mississippi River and moving east-northeast at 9 mph. Additional intensification is expected before landfall.

Isaias is forecast to reach the northern Gulf Coast late Friday or early Saturday, threatening the Florida Panhandle, southern Alabama and southern Mississippi with hurricane-force winds, flooding and life-threatening storm surge. A hurricane warning extends from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Bay/Gulf County line in Florida, while a storm surge warning covers a broader stretch from the mouth of the Mississippi River to Steinhatchee, Florida. The storm is expected to produce 3 to 7 inches of rain, with isolated totals up to 10 inches, across portions of the central Gulf Coast.

Preparations were already disrupting communities and businesses Thursday. Florida and Alabama declared states of emergency for dozens of counties, some Florida coastal areas ordered mandatory evacuations, and several school districts canceled classes. The National Weather Service warned communities within the hurricane warning area to prepare for potentially long-duration power outages. Offshore oil and gas operators had also temporarily halted about 25% of Gulf oil production, removing roughly 511,000 barrels per day from production as of Wednesday.

Isaias formed extraordinarily late in the Atlantic season. It became the season's first hurricane just one day short of tying a 121-year-old record from 1905 for the latest first Atlantic hurricane. A strong El Niño has helped suppress Atlantic hurricane activity this year, but conditions in the Gulf have become favorable for rapid strengthening.
