Russia and Ukraine return to U.S.-mediated peace talks after nine months

Second day begins as negotiators discuss major prisoner exchange

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 11, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Russian and Ukrainian officials have resumed direct U.S.-mediated peace talks for the first time in nine months, opening a new diplomatic effort in Abu Dhabi aimed at finding a path toward ending the war. The first session lasted nearly four hours, and a second day of trilateral negotiations began November 11.

President Trump's envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner represented the United States. Ukraine's delegation was led by Rustem Umerov, head of the country's Foreign Intelligence Service, while Russian presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky headed Moscow's team. U.S. officials are also pressing to bring European representatives more directly into future rounds of the main negotiations.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the first day's discussions as constructive and substantive. Humanitarian issues are among the immediate areas under discussion, including the return of civilians and children and a potentially large prisoner exchange. Zelenskyy said negotiators are considering exchanges involving either 1,000 prisoners from each side or as many as 1,500 from each side.

Major differences over a broader peace agreement remain. Russian President Vladimir Putin has demanded that Ukraine withdraw from four regions Moscow claims to have annexed, abandon its effort to join NATO and accept restrictions on its military. Russia does not fully control all four claimed regions. Kyiv rejects surrendering territory or accepting limits on its military and Western security relationships.

Ukraine continues to argue that an immediate ceasefire should precede negotiations on a broader settlement and that territorial questions ultimately require direct talks between Zelenskyy and Putin. Moscow has resisted those proposals while seeking an agreement addressing its wider security demands.

The Abu Dhabi meetings follow previous rounds of negotiations that failed to produce a comprehensive settlement. Fighting continues despite the renewed diplomatic effort, leaving substantial differences to resolve even as negotiators pursue narrower agreements such as prisoner exchanges.
