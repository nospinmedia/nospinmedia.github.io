Nauru Officially Renames Itself the Republic of Naoero

UN listing updated; code changes to NRO and citizens become dei-Naoero

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 7:21 AM ET

The Pacific island nation long known internationally as Nauru has officially adopted the indigenous name Republic of Naoero, a move its government says restores the country’s own identity in global use. President David Adeang announced that the state’s international code will change from NRU to NRO and that citizens will be referred to as dei-Naoero, aligning external usage with the national language.

- New official name: Republic of Naoero - International country code: NRO (replacing NRU) - Demonym: dei-Naoero - UN update: The United Nations says it was notified on June 26, 2026, and its member listing now reflects Naoero - Legislative path: Parliament endorsed the change in May 2026 under the Constitution (Amendment) (No. 2) Act 2026, following a bill introduced on January 29, 2026; plans for a referendum were later dropped after legal review

Officials said government registries, documents, and state assets are being updated to the new name, with steps underway to ensure recognition across bilateral and international forums. The government indicated national aircraft and ships will be retitled, and it is asking foreign ministries and international organizations to adopt the new designation in their systems. Because many global databases and maps synchronize on different schedules, practical changes may roll out over time even as official recognition proceeds.

With roughly 12,000 residents, the Republic of Naoero is among the world’s smallest countries by population. The renaming follows a pattern seen elsewhere as states retire exonyms or colonial-era names in favor of indigenous forms; recent examples include Türkiye and Eswatini. Officials emphasized that the shift is about accuracy and continuity rather than a break with constitutional tradition, noting that “Naoero” already featured in national symbols and was permitted under the existing constitutional framework.
