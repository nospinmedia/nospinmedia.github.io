Salvadoran detainee dies after medical emergency at Newark ICE jail

Second death at Delaney Hall in 8 months intensifies closure push

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Edwin Lopez-Cornejo, 41, a Salvadoran national held by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) at the Delaney Hall Detention Facility in Newark, New Jersey, died on Saturday, August 1, 2026, after a reported “medical emergency.” ICE announced the death on Monday, saying he was transported to a hospital and pronounced dead; the official cause is pending. It is the second detainee death linked to Delaney Hall in eight months, fueling urgent calls to shutter the site.

ICE said Lopez-Cornejo was arrested in Plainfield, New Jersey, on June 18, 2026, and was held pending removal under a reinstated 2006 deportation order. The Newark facility, operated by GEO Group, has a capacity of about 1,000 detainees and reopened in 2025 amid expanded federal immigration enforcement. ICE stated that Lopez-Cornejo “received proper medical care” while detained; advocates and relatives have questioned the adequacy of care, and the cause of death remains undetermined.

Elected officials escalated oversight demands. Sen. Cory Booker said Delaney Hall “must be closed immediately and permanently.” Governor Mikie Sherrill said her administration is working with the congressional delegation and continues to face obstruction to a full state health inspection. Newark Mayor Ras Baraka called the death “a heartbreaking reminder” of alleged deficiencies at the privately run facility. Rep. Rob Menendez said Lopez-Cornejo reported difficulties before collapsing; CPR was performed prior to transport. Lopez-Cornejo’s mother said in a video circulated by advocates that he had longstanding health conditions and reported numbness on the right side of his face and hand the day before his death.

Context: In December 2025, another detainee, Jean Wilson Brutus, 41, died after a sudden collapse at Delaney Hall; officials said that case was ruled a natural death. Since the start of President Trump’s second term, at least 56 people have died in ICE custody, according to a national newswire’s tally, underscoring heightened scrutiny of detention conditions. Protests, alleged hunger strikes, and legal efforts to secure unfettered state health inspections at Delaney Hall have intensified in recent months.
