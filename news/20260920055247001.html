AI giants sued over alleged agreement to slow development

ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Gemini subscribers claim antitrust violation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 20, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Four paying AI customers have filed a federal antitrust lawsuit accusing Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google of illegally agreeing to coordinate a slowdown in artificial intelligence development. The case, Buist v. Anthropic PBC, was filed September 18 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California under Section 1 of the Sherman Act. The allegations have not been proven in court.

The plaintiffs — Charles Buist, Cheyenne Hunt, Christine Bullock and Nick Spetsas — subscribe to ChatGPT, Claude, Grok or Gemini and are seeking to represent a nationwide class of paying customers. They argue that subscribers would receive less value if competing AI companies collectively slowed improvements while continuing to charge for their services. The lawsuit seeks class-action status, an injunction against the companies and a declaration that the alleged agreement violates federal antitrust law.

The complaint centers on events beginning September 12, when Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay calling for industrywide coordination to slow frontier AI development while stronger safety measures are established. The lawsuit points to subsequent supportive public responses from OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, SpaceXAI leader Elon Musk and Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis as evidence of an agreement among competitors. The plaintiffs characterize that alleged coordination as a restriction on how quickly competing products improve.

Amodei has acknowledged that coordination among AI companies could raise antitrust concerns and has suggested government involvement or a narrow legal exemption could allow certain safety discussions. The plaintiffs argue that Congress has provided no exemption covering the conduct alleged in their complaint.

The lawsuit arrives amid an industrywide debate over whether rapidly advancing AI systems should be slowed for safety or continue developing at the current pace. None of the four defendant companies had responded to requests for comment in the reporting reviewed for this story.
