Microsoft outage disrupts services worldwide

Businesses, governments and airlines report problems as engineers work on fixes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 13, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A widespread Microsoft outage disrupted cloud and productivity services around the world, affecting businesses, government agencies, schools and other organizations that rely on the company's systems.

The problems began Wednesday with reports of users unable to access Microsoft 365 and Outlook, including login, email and synchronization failures. What initially appeared as thousands of user reports in the United States developed into a broader global disruption as organizations across multiple countries reported problems accessing Microsoft services.

The outage also reached the transportation sector. Some airlines reported communications disruptions connected to the Microsoft problems, adding operational complications for an industry that depends heavily on cloud-based communications and information systems. Other businesses and public agencies reported difficulties accessing tools used for routine operations and communication.

Microsoft acknowledged the service problems and said its engineering teams were working urgently to identify the underlying issue and restore service. Several attempted fixes did not immediately return all affected services to normal, extending the disruption as engineers continued recovery efforts. Reports of problems persisted into Thursday as Microsoft worked to stabilize its systems.

The outage highlighted how extensively businesses and public institutions depend on a relatively small number of cloud-service providers. A failure involving widely used services such as Microsoft 365 can quickly affect organizations far beyond the technology company itself, particularly when email, communications and other business systems depend on the same infrastructure.

The full cause of the outage and the total number of affected users had not yet been established as restoration efforts continued. Microsoft said engineers remained focused on returning services to normal operation.
