State Department Makes Visa Bond Program Permanent

Selected Travelers May Be Required To Post Bonds Of Up To $20,000

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 1, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The U.S. Department of State has finalized a rule making its Visa Bond Program a permanent part of the nonimmigrant visa process for certain B-1 business and B-2 tourism applicants from designated countries with high visa overstay rates or other immigration risk factors. The rule takes effect August 3 and allows consular officers to require refundable visa bonds of $10,000, $15,000, or $20,000 before issuing a visa, replacing the temporary pilot program that had lower bond amounts.

State Department officials said the program is intended to encourage compliance with U.S. immigration laws by providing a financial incentive for visitors to depart before their authorized stay expires. Travelers who comply with the terms of their admission receive their bond back, while those who overstay or otherwise violate the conditions of their visa may forfeit the money. The department said the program primarily applies to applicants from approximately 50 countries, many located in Africa, though additional countries may be added or removed based on future immigration data and national security considerations.

According to the State Department, the pilot program contributed to a reduction in visa overstays among covered applicants. Officials said the permanent rule removes the previous $5,000 bond option, raising the maximum possible bond from $15,000 under the pilot program to $20,000. The agency argues the change strengthens immigration enforcement while preserving a legal pathway for temporary visitors who comply with U.S. entry requirements.

Immigration advocates and travel industry groups criticized the policy, arguing that the higher financial requirements could discourage legitimate tourism, business travel and family visits, particularly for applicants from lower-income countries. Supporters counter that refundable bonds are a targeted enforcement tool designed to reduce visa overstays without broadly restricting lawful travel.
