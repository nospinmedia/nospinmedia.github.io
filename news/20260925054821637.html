Diplomats stage mass walkout as Netanyahu addresses UN

Israeli leader calls departing delegates 'moral cowards'

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 25, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Dozens of diplomats walked out of the United Nations General Assembly as Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu approached the podium Thursday, staging a highly visible protest as he defended Israel's military actions and rejected mounting international criticism.

The walkout came after the Palestinian Mission to the United Nations called on diplomatic missions to leave the chamber during Netanyahu's address in protest of Israel's actions in Gaza and the West Bank. Large sections of the hall emptied, while other delegates remained and some supporters applauded. Netanyahu opened by telling those leaving that if any other "moral cowards" remained, they should leave as well.

Netanyahu used much of the speech to defend Israel's military campaigns across the Middle East. He praised cooperation with the United States and thanked President Trump for his leadership during joint U.S.-Israeli strikes against Iran. Netanyahu claimed some countries whose representatives walked out had privately thanked Israel for its actions against Iran, though he did not publicly identify those governments.

The Israeli leader also rejected accusations that Israel is committing genocide in Gaza, calling the allegation the "biggest lie of the century." Israel has repeatedly denied genocide allegations and says its military operations are directed at armed groups and threats to the country. Netanyahu also criticized governments including Britain and France, which have moved to recognize a Palestinian state, and attacked New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, a prominent critic of the Israeli government.

The diplomatic protest inside the UN was accompanied by demonstrations elsewhere in New York City. Hundreds of protesters gathered as Netanyahu spoke, and demonstrators blocked traffic near Union Square. Police arrested several people during the protests.

The walkout underscored the widening diplomatic divisions surrounding Israel's military operations and its relationship with governments that have increasingly criticized its conduct in Gaza and the West Bank.
