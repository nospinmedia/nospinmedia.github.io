Google Maps Adopts 'Lake America' for U.S. Users

Canada Keeps 'Lake Ontario' as Naming Dispute Grows

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 31, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Google Maps has begun displaying "Lake America" for users in the United States following an update to the federal Geographic Names Information System (GNIS) ordered by President Donald Trump. Under Google's longstanding policy of reflecting official government geographic databases in each country, users in Canada continue to see "Lake Ontario," while many users in other countries see a combined label showing "Lake Ontario (Lake America)." The update mirrors Google's earlier implementation of the "Gulf of America" designation for U.S. users after a separate federal naming change.

The revised labeling has prompted criticism from Canadian officials, who continue to recognize the lake exclusively as Lake Ontario. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada will maintain its historic naming conventions, noting that the lake's name has been used for centuries and is derived from the Indigenous Wendat word Ontari'io. Ontario Premier Doug Ford also criticized the change, while provincial officials said they are reviewing government websites that embed Google Maps after some displayed the U.S. label to Canadian visitors.

The differing map labels highlight how technology companies handle official geographic names that vary by jurisdiction. Google said it follows authoritative government naming databases for users within each country while displaying multiple names internationally when governments recognize different official designations. Other mapping services have taken different approaches, with some continuing to display Lake Ontario regardless of a user's location.

The issue has become part of a broader debate over executive authority, digital mapping standards, and the influence of government naming decisions on widely used online platforms. While the federal database change affects how some U.S. agencies and companies present the lake domestically, it does not alter Canada's official geographic records or international treaties governing the shared Great Lakes.
