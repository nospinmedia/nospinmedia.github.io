FAA moves to fire 2 LaGuardia controllers who left shifts early

Discipline follows fatal March runway collision

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 28, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The Federal Aviation Administration is moving to fire two air traffic controllers who allegedly left their scheduled shifts early at New York's LaGuardia Airport on the night of a runway collision that killed two pilots. A government source familiar with the case said the FAA considers the unauthorized departures timecard fraud. The controllers left before the March 22, 2026 accident, although investigators have not established that their departures caused or contributed to the collision.

Just before midnight, Jazz Aviation Flight 646, operating as Air Canada Express Flight 8646, collided with airport rescue vehicle Rescue 35 while landing on Runway 4. The National Transportation Safety Board says the CRJ-900 was substantially damaged and both the captain and first officer were killed. Of the remaining passengers, flight attendants and firefighters involved, 39 people were transported to hospitals, including six with serious injuries.

Two controllers were working in the LaGuardia tower when the crash occurred. The two employees now facing dismissal would have provided additional staffing had they remained for their full shifts. NTSB Chair Jennifer Homendy said after the accident that having two controllers in the tower during the midnight shift is common, while investigators are separately examining staffing and longstanding concerns about controller fatigue. The NTSB's investigation into the crash remains ongoing.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said most controllers complete their assigned shifts but warned that employees who misuse taxpayer-funded time, increase workloads for colleagues or affect the airspace will be held accountable. Controllers interviewed by Reuters described unauthorized early departures, sometimes called “early shoves,” as an unofficial practice that has occurred at some facilities. The National Air Traffic Controllers Association said it is discussing the disciplinary proceedings with FAA leadership.

The FAA says it is also increasing controller hiring as the national system continues to face staffing shortages, while the LaGuardia case raises separate questions about attendance, supervision and accountability.
