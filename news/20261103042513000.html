Trump approval stands at 32% before midterms

Reuters/Ipsos poll finds cost of living dominates voter concerns

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 3, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

President Trump's job approval rating stood at 32% on the eve of the 2026 midterm elections, remaining close to the lowest level recorded by Reuters/Ipsos during his political career, according to a national poll completed Monday.

The Reuters/Ipsos survey found Trump's approval unchanged from the previous week and one percentage point above the 31% recorded in mid-October. The poll surveyed 1,765 U.S. adults online and had a margin of error of about 2 percentage points.

On the generic congressional ballot, 46% of registered voters said they would support a Democratic candidate compared with 38% who preferred a Republican, an eight-point difference. That gap had narrowed from 11 points in the previous week's Reuters/Ipsos polling. Generic-ballot surveys measure national voter preference and do not predict results in individual House or Senate races.

Trump also remained a significant factor for voters who do not identify with either major party. Among independent registered voters, 50% said their midterm vote was intended as a vote against Trump, while 13% said they were voting to support him. Another 33% said Trump was not a factor in their decision.

The cost of living remained the dominant concern in the survey, with 78% of respondents describing it as a very important or top issue in deciding their vote. Respondents gave Democrats an advantage over Republicans on handling living costs, 43% to 32%.

The poll also found an enthusiasm difference between the parties: 49% of Democratic voters described themselves as very enthusiastic about voting, compared with 33% of Republican voters.
