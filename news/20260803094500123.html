Blanche rescinds $1.8B ‘anti-weaponization’ fund

Paves way for Tuesday committee vote on AG nomination

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 3, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche issued a binding order late Sunday, August 2, 2026, formally rescinding the $1.8 billion “Anti‑Weaponization Fund” that had become the chief obstacle to his confirmation. The order also narrows the tax‑audit protections tied to President Donald Trump’s IRS settlement. With the written directive in place, a spokesperson for Sen. John Cornyn said an agreement had been reached, clearing a key hurdle before the Senate Judiciary Committee’s planned vote on Tuesday, August 4, 2026.

In the document, Blanche rescinds his original May 18, 2026 order that created the fund and states “there is no Fund,” noting that no members were appointed, no claims were processed, and no taxpayer dollars were transferred. The new order clarifies that the settlement’s audit‑immunity provision applies only retroactively to already‑open matters, not to future tax filings by Trump or his family. Sen. Thom Tillis had warned Blanche’s nomination would fail without such written constraints; Cornyn’s office now confirms those assurances are in hand ahead of the committee’s vote, which Chairman Sen. Chuck Grassley has slated for Tuesday.

What Blanche’s order does, at a glance: - Rescinds the fund established on May 18, 2026 - Confirms no claims, appointments, or transfers occurred - Limits audit immunity to past open claims, not future returns

The move eases a weeks‑long GOP impasse after Trump mused about reviving payouts via standalone legislation, fueling demands from Cornyn and Tillis for binding language. By issuing a formal directive, Blanche attempts to remove lingering ambiguity that had persisted despite prior testimony that the fund was “dead.” The committee vote will indicate whether those assurances are sufficient to advance the nomination before the August recess.

Context: Republicans on the panel previously postponed a July 30, 2026 vote as talks continued. Reporting throughout June and July documented DOJ statements that the fund would not proceed, even as courts and senators pressed for formal rescission and tighter limits on the tax‑immunity provisions.
