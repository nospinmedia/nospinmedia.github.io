Trump picks Reflecting Pool for final midterm pitch

Troubled renovation returns to spotlight before Election Day

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 2, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

President Trump will deliver his closing midterm-election pitch Monday afternoon at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool in Washington, putting a renovation project that has faced delays, rising costs and repeated repairs back into the national spotlight one day before Election Day.

The White House formally announced the speech after President Trump told reporters Sunday that appearing at the site would "show how beautiful it is." The Washington event follows a month-long campaign push in which Trump staged more than 25 rallies, largely in conservative areas. He said his Sunday evening rally in Kansas, following an appearance in Iowa, would be his final campaign stop outside Washington.

The Reflecting Pool has been one of President Trump's prominent Washington renovation projects. In April, he said contractors could repair the landmark within days for about $1.5 million and have the work finished before Independence Day. The project ultimately finished nearly three months behind schedule, and its cost grew to more than 10 times Trump's initial estimate.

President Trump declared the work complete in June, but algae later appeared and sections of the new blue liner began peeling. The pool was subsequently drained again for repairs. Trump blamed vandalism for the damage, while prosecutors later said evidence pointed instead to problems with the liner's installation.

The Justice Department charged former Olympic canoeist David Hearn with damaging the pool before prosecutors dropped the case after concluding he was not responsible. President Trump publicly criticized U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro over the decision, saying she had "choked" and "folded like an umbrella." A judge later permanently dismissed the case.

In October, the Justice Department's inspector general declined to investigate allegations of prosecutorial misconduct raised by Hearn's attorneys, determining they fell outside the office's jurisdiction and referring the matter to the department's Office of Professional Responsibility.
