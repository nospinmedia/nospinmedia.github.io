Gunmen Open Fire at Church Funeral in Jordan, Killing 7

About 40 People Wounded in Rare Mass Shooting at Greek Orthodox Church

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 24, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in southern Jordan, killing seven people and wounding about 40 others in a rare mass shooting in the kingdom.

The attack occurred Thursday at a church in the village of Hamoud, in Jordan's southern Karak governorate, as mourners gathered for a funeral. The area is roughly 110 kilometers south of the capital, Amman.

Jordanian security forces and emergency responders were dispatched to the scene following the shooting, and wounded victims were transported to hospitals for treatment. Authorities launched an investigation into the attack and worked to determine the circumstances surrounding the violence.

Officials had not publicly established a motive in the initial reporting. Authorities also had not announced that the shooting was terrorism or concluded that the church was targeted specifically because it was Christian. Those questions remained part of the investigation.

The shooting drew particular attention because large-scale gun violence is relatively uncommon in Jordan, a country that has generally remained stable despite conflicts and political tensions elsewhere in the Middle East. Christians make up a small but longstanding part of Jordan's population and include Greek Orthodox communities with deep historical roots in the country.

Initial reports differed on the number of people wounded, with some accounts citing 34 injuries before later reports put the figure at about 40. The death toll was reported as seven by Jordanian officials and multiple international news organizations.

Jordanian authorities increased security attention following the attack as investigators worked to identify those responsible and determine why the funeral was targeted.

With the motive still under investigation, the available reporting does not establish whether the attack was driven by religion, terrorism, a personal dispute or another cause.
