U.S. offers $20 million for information on Iran’s nuclear program

Reward targets procurement networks and covert facilities

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The United States is offering a reward of up to $20 million for information that could help identify or disrupt networks supporting Iran’s nuclear program, adding another tool to Washington’s pressure campaign against Tehran.

The reward was announced through the U.S. State Department’s Rewards for Justice program on Saturday, November 7. The government is seeking information about people, companies and facilities involved in acquiring materials or technology that could support Iran’s nuclear activities.

Information sought by the program includes details about front companies, procurement agents, illicit acquisition networks and covert facilities connected to Iran’s nuclear program. The reward can reach $20 million for information meeting the government’s criteria and contributing to efforts to identify or disrupt those activities.

The announcement comes as the United States continues to use diplomatic, economic and military pressure against Iran. It also follows renewed questions about the condition of Iran’s nuclear infrastructure after U.S. military operations against Iranian targets. President Trump has previously described damage from U.S. strikes in sweeping terms, while the new intelligence effort indicates that American officials continue seeking information about Iran’s remaining nuclear capabilities and procurement networks.

The reward program is separate from negotiations over Iran’s nuclear activities. Tehran recently transmitted a proposal to the United States through Qatar setting conditions for renewed negotiations, making the reward announcement a distinct development from the ongoing diplomatic effort.

Rewards for Justice is a State Department national-security program that offers payments for information involving terrorism, foreign interference and other threats to U.S. security. Offering a reward does not establish that any particular person or organization has committed a crime, and payment depends on the usefulness of information provided to the government.
