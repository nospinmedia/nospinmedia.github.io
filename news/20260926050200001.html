Hurricane Nolo threatens Hawaii with catastrophic flooding

Up to 25 inches of rain could hit parts of Big Island

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 26, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Hurricane Nolo strengthened into a Category 2 hurricane south of Hawaii on September 25, with maximum sustained winds reaching 105 mph as forecasters warned of potentially catastrophic flooding across parts of the Big Island. The storm was moving slowly north-northeast at about 5 mph, and the National Hurricane Center expected its center to remain south of the main Hawaiian Islands before turning westward.

A Hurricane Watch and Tropical Storm Warning were in effect for Hawaii County, while Maui County was under a Tropical Storm Watch. Tropical storm conditions were beginning on the Big Island Friday night, with hurricane conditions possible early Saturday, particularly around steep terrain. Hurricane-force winds extended as far as 45 miles from Nolo's center, while tropical-storm-force winds reached outward up to 140 miles.

Rain poses one of the greatest threats. Eastern and southern portions of the Big Island could receive as much as 25 inches, creating the potential for catastrophic, life-threatening flash flooding and mudslides. Western portions of the island were forecast to receive 2 to 4 inches, with isolated totals up to 8 inches. Maui could receive 4 to 6 inches, with localized totals reaching 10 inches, while other Hawaiian islands could see 1 to 3 inches and isolated flooding.

Hawaii Governor Josh Green requested a federal emergency declaration covering four counties as the state prepared for Nolo. The request was awaiting federal approval as of Friday. All state parks on the Big Island and Maui were ordered closed because of the storm. Forecasters said Nolo could strengthen further and become a major hurricane, meaning Category 3 or stronger, during the weekend even if its center remains offshore.

Nolo follows Hurricane Lowell, which brought damaging winds and heavy rain to Kauai and Niihau earlier this month, and Hurricane Lala, which caused landslides, widespread power outages and destroyed about 100 homes in August.
