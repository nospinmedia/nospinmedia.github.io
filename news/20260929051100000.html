Pennsylvania measles outbreak surpasses 900 cases

176 people hospitalized as infections spread across 39 counties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 29, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Pennsylvania's measles outbreak has grown to 903 confirmed cases, with infections now reported across 39 of the state's 67 counties, according to the Pennsylvania Department of Health. The state reported 68 new infections since September 23, making the outbreak Pennsylvania's largest measles resurgence in decades.

At least 176 people have been hospitalized, up from 164 on September 23. State officials have also recorded four measles-associated deaths. Two were unvaccinated adults, ages 18 and 40, while two were infants who were too young to receive the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine. Federal and state officials have differed over how measles-related deaths should be classified, and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has been reviewing its national classification process.

Lancaster County remains the hardest-hit area. Pennsylvania health officials say only four of the state's 903 cases — less than 1% — occurred among people considered fully vaccinated for their age. The state says two doses of the MMR vaccine provide about 97% protection against measles.

The outbreak comes during a broader national resurgence. As of September 24, the CDC had recorded 3,659 confirmed measles cases nationwide in 2026. U.S. kindergarten MMR vaccination coverage has fallen from 95.2% in the 2019-2020 school year to 92.4% in 2025-2026, leaving an estimated 280,000 kindergarteners at risk during the latest school year.

Pennsylvania has expanded vaccination efforts since its outbreak began in late April. State health officials reported nearly 5,200 MMR vaccinations at 156 pop-up clinics in affected communities and more than 7,700 doses administered by state health center staff statewide during 2026. Additional clinics are planned as officials work to contain continued community transmission.
