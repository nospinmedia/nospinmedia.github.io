DOJ dismantles Chinese hacking network targeting U.S. agencies

Court-authorized seizures disrupt global espionage infrastructure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 27, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The U.S. Department of Justice and the Federal Bureau of Investigation announced a court-authorized operation to dismantle key infrastructure used in a long-running Chinese cyber espionage campaign that targeted sensitive American government agencies, financial institutions and critical infrastructure. Federal authorities seized internet domains supporting two hacking platforms known as QScan and QTRouter, which investigators said enabled cyber operators to disguise attacks by routing them through thousands of compromised internet-connected devices spread across more than 130 countries.

According to court documents unsealed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of California, the platforms were developed and operated by a state-sponsored group known as QTFY, working through the China-based Nanjing Xinjiuwei Network Technology Company. Prosecutors allege the company provided hacking services to China's Ministry of State Security and the People's Liberation Army, allowing customers to identify vulnerable devices, compromise networks and conceal the origin of cyber intrusions. Investigators said the infrastructure had been used since at least 2018 to conduct both successful break-ins and attempted intrusions against organizations including the Department of Justice, Federal Reserve, U.S. Senate, Department of Energy, Department of Health and Human Services, National Institutes of Health and NASA.

Officials said QScan searched for vulnerable internet-connected devices that could be hijacked, while QTRouter transformed those compromised systems into a global proxy network that masked malicious traffic and made attacks appear to originate near intended targets. Attorney General Todd Blanche said disabling the platforms disrupted an important capability used to conduct cyber espionage against the United States and its allies. The FBI, NSA and U.S. Cyber Command also released technical guidance to help organizations identify and defend against similar activity.

A spokesperson for the Chinese Embassy in Washington said it was not familiar with the specific allegations, rejected claims of state-sponsored hacking and accused the United States of politicizing cybersecurity issues. U.S. officials said the domain seizures significantly disrupted the operation but cautioned that sophisticated state-backed cyber groups often rebuild infrastructure and continue espionage campaigns using new tools.
