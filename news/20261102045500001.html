Judge dismisses DOJ lawsuit over Alaska Senate race

Justice Department says it will appeal before Election Day

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 2, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

A federal judge dismissed a Justice Department lawsuit seeking to block certification of Alaska's U.S. Senate election if Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan loses, rejecting the government's request to investigate ballots cast for another candidate with a similar name. The Justice Department said shortly afterward that it would appeal.

The dispute centers on retired teacher Dan J. Sullivan, a nonpartisan candidate who appears on the ranked-choice ballot alongside Sen. Dan S. Sullivan, the Republican incumbent. Democratic former Rep. Mary Peltola is also running in the closely watched race. Peltola finished first in Alaska's open primary, followed by the senator, while Dan J. Sullivan received about 2.5% of the vote and qualified for the general election.

The Justice Department filed its lawsuit less than two weeks before the November 3 election, seeking an investigation into whether voters selecting Dan J. Sullivan actually intended to vote for the senator. President Trump and Sullivan have alleged that the second Sullivan was recruited to confuse voters and hurt the incumbent. Peltola has denied involvement in recruiting him, and Dan J. Sullivan has said he entered the race on his own.

U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason ruled that the government's requested relief was "infeasible" because early voting was already underway and ballots that had been cast could not be matched back to individual voters. She also said the government had not provided a workable way to segregate votes for Dan J. Sullivan while preserving ballot secrecy.

Gleason additionally cited states' constitutional authority to administer elections and the Supreme Court's principle cautioning federal courts against changing election rules shortly before voting concludes.

The Justice Department filed notice that it would appeal, saying voters should not be misled by a candidate it described as frivolous. A separate federal criminal investigation into whether Dan J. Sullivan was recruited as a decoy remains underway, including grand jury subpoenas and FBI contacts with some donors.
