Student protests spread across France as thousands are arrested

More than 1,200 schools affected as education grievances fuel unrest

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 3, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

A student protest movement that began over conditions in schools around Paris has spread across France, affecting more than 1,200 schools and leading to more than 5,000 arrests, according to reporting published October 3. Students have protested teacher shortages, overcrowded classrooms, deteriorating buildings and other education problems, while some demonstrations and blockades have escalated into fires, vandalism and clashes with police.

The movement began in the Paris suburbs in late September before expanding nationwide. Reuters reported that students in Seine-Saint-Denis, one of the areas at the center of the protests, described missing teachers, inadequate facilities and overcrowding as major grievances. The unrest has since reached schools and universities across France, with demonstrations also occurring in major cities and smaller communities.

Violence surrounding some protests has intensified the government's response. AP News reported more than 1,900 arrests and 305 police officers injured during an earlier stage of the unrest, while French authorities have deployed riot police and mobile gendarmes. Fires and property damage have been reported at schools, and hundreds of educational facilities have temporarily closed, shifted to remote learning or experienced disruptions.

French officials have distinguished between students raising legitimate concerns and people they say are exploiting the demonstrations to commit violence. Education Minister Édouard Geffray has accused radical-left groups of taking advantage of the movement and specifically criticized La France Insoumise, while party leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon has supported the students' grievances and criticized police tactics. Those accusations remain part of a political dispute over responsibility for the violence rather than an established explanation for the origins of the protests.

The government has also begun talks with student organizations, parents and education representatives as it attempts to address the underlying complaints while restoring order. The protests are unfolding as France approaches a presidential election campaign, increasing political attention on both education policy and the government's handling of the unrest.
