Pope Leo XIV Diagnosed With Lung Cancer After Surgery

Doctors Remove Lower Lobe of Right Lung as Pontiff Recovers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Pope Leo XIV has been diagnosed with lung adenocarcinoma, a form of lung cancer, after undergoing surgery on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, at Agostino Gemelli Hospital in Rome. Doctors removed the lower lobe of the 71-year-old pontiff's right lung after discovering a small growth during routine medical examinations. The Vatican confirmed that the operation was successful, with no complications, and that the Pope is recovering in the hospital.

The surgical team, led by Professor Stefano Margaritora, chief of thoracic surgery at Gemelli Hospital, used a video-assisted technique to remove the affected portion of the lung. The operation lasted approximately 90 minutes, although the Pope spent about three hours in the operating room. Tissue examined during surgery confirmed the diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma, which doctors had suspected following earlier examinations. According to the Vatican, Leo regained consciousness normally and returned to his hospital room after a brief observation period.

The diagnosis represents the first major publicly disclosed health challenge of Leo's papacy, which began in May 2025. Born Robert Francis Prevost in Chicago, Leo became the first American pope and leads the worldwide Catholic Church. His health is therefore a matter of international concern, particularly as the Vatican evaluates his recovery and upcoming commitments.

Doctors have not publicly established the cancer's stage or confirmed whether it has spread beyond the affected lung tissue. Additional examinations of the removed tissue, including assessments relevant to possible lymph node involvement, are expected to help determine whether further treatment will be necessary. Although surgery can be an effective treatment for localized lung cancer, the Vatican has not announced a definitive prognosis or a decision regarding chemotherapy or other therapies.

The Pope is expected to remain hospitalized for approximately one week, with public engagements canceled through October 20. His planned November 6–17 visit to Uruguay, Argentina, and Peru remains uncertain pending his recovery and further medical findings.

On Wednesday, October 14, the Vatican reported that Leo had spent a calm first night in the hospital following the operation. Further medical updates are expected as doctors assess his condition.
