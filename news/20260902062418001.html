Ohio Student's Death Spurs Scrutiny of ICE Monitoring

Family Says Loss of Status Deepened Emotional Distress

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 2, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The death of a 20-year-old Haitian college student in Springfield, Ohio, has prompted renewed scrutiny of federal immigration enforcement policies after his family said he became increasingly distressed following the loss of his Temporary Protected Status (TPS) and a requirement from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) to wear an electronic ankle monitor. Pierre Damas Bel died on Aug. 31 after stepping into traffic on Interstate 70, according to the Ohio State Highway Patrol. Authorities ruled the death a suicide.

Bel had recently graduated with honors from Springfield High School and enrolled at Wright State University, where he planned to study neuroscience. Family members said he struggled emotionally after ICE required him to wear a GPS ankle monitor while his immigration case remained pending. His father, Pierre Ronal Bel, said his son described feeling humiliated by the monitoring requirement despite having an active asylum application and an immigration court hearing scheduled for October 2026. Advocacy organizations also said Bel experienced bullying related to the ankle monitor, although those accounts have not been independently verified by authorities.

The case comes amid broader legal and political debate over the Trump administration's immigration policies affecting Haitian nationals. Following federal court actions allowing changes to TPS protections for many Haitians, ICE expanded supervision requirements for some immigrants with pending cases, including the use of electronic monitoring in certain circumstances. Civil rights organizations have criticized those policies, while federal officials have said electronic monitoring is used to help ensure compliance with immigration proceedings.

Ohio Gov. Mike DeWine called Bel's death a tragedy and said he was concerned about the circumstances surrounding the case. Immigration advocates and several members of Congress have since called for additional oversight of ICE's monitoring policies and their potential mental health impacts. Federal officials have not indicated any immediate changes to the program, and Bel's death remains the subject of public discussion over immigration enforcement and humanitarian considerations.
