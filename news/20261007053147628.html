Bulgaria ends search after drone attack sinks cargo ship

Origin of Black Sea strike remains unconfirmed as Russia is accused

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 7, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Bulgaria has ended its search for the missing crew of a commercial ship that sank after an attack involving aerial and sea drones in the Black Sea, one day after two vessels were struck about 70 nautical miles off the country's coast. Bulgarian officials said Wednesday that no signs of survivors had been found.

The Togo-flagged Alfa Watan sank after suffering severe damage in Tuesday's attack inside Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone. Vice-Admiral Rumen Nikolov of Bulgaria's Maritime Administration said search teams recovered a boat, three life rafts and pieces of equipment but found no sailors or drone debris. Officials have not disclosed how many crew members were aboard the vessel.

A second ship, the Palau-flagged Able, was also struck and caught fire. All 18 crew members were evacuated by Bulgarian rescuers, with two sailors seriously injured. The Able was carrying grain, while the cargo aboard the Alfa Watan has not been disclosed.

Who launched the drones remains disputed. Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said authorities have not determined whether they came from Russia or Ukraine. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, however, said cooperation between Ukraine's navy and Bulgarian counterparts established that Russia carried out a combined attack using sea and aerial drones. Russia has not commented on the Bulgarian incident. A maritime security source cited by Reuters said an initial assessment also suggested the attack was probably Russian.

The incident occurred in Bulgaria's economic zone but outside its 12-nautical-mile territorial waters. It followed another deadly maritime incident near Romania a day earlier that Ukraine also blamed on Russian drones. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned attacks on civilian shipping, while maritime security firms warned that drone threats appear to be expanding farther into the western Black Sea.

Following the attack, Bulgaria announced plans to acquire five additional surface-to-air missile systems as concerns grow about the security of shipping near NATO members' waters.
