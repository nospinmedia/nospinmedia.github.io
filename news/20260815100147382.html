Luigi Mangione Pleads Guilty In UnitedHealthcare CEO Killing

Defense seeks dismissal of New York murder case

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 6:49 PM ET

Luigi Mangione, 28, pleaded guilty in Manhattan federal court on August 14, 2026, to two stalking charges resulting in death and admitted that he shot and killed UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson outside a Manhattan hotel on December 4, 2024. Mangione told U.S. District Judge Margaret Garnett that he tracked Thompson as the executive traveled to his company's investor conference and acknowledged that he knew the shooting was illegal. Federal prosecutors plan to seek a life sentence, with sentencing scheduled for December 18.

The guilty plea was entered without a negotiated plea agreement. Mangione admitted that he researched Thompson's movements, posed as an investor to obtain information about the UnitedHealthcare conference and traveled to New York with a firearm he made using a 3D printer. Thompson, 50, was shot outside the New York Hilton Midtown. Mangione was arrested five days later in Altoona, Pennsylvania, after a nationwide manhunt.

The federal plea immediately changed the legal battle surrounding Mangione's separate New York prosecution. His attorneys filed a motion asking a state judge to dismiss the pending second-degree murder case under New York's double-jeopardy protections, arguing that he should not face a second prosecution for the same killing after accepting federal criminal responsibility. The Manhattan District Attorney's Office said it will oppose the motion and remains committed to pursuing the state case. Jury selection is currently scheduled to begin September 8.

The federal and state cases involve different criminal statutes, and Mangione's guilty plea does not automatically end the New York prosecution. A state judge must now determine whether New York's broader statutory protections against successive prosecutions permit the murder case to continue. That ruling could also face appeals, potentially affecting the September trial schedule. Mangione remains in federal custody while awaiting sentencing.
