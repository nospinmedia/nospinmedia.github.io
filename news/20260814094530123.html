White House Says Transshipment Costs U.S. Up To $26B A Year

Report Alleges 40+ Countries Help Evade China Tariffs

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The White House released a report alleging that Chinese exporters are routing products through third countries to disguise their origin and avoid U.S. tariffs, a practice officials describe as transshipment. White House trade adviser Peter Navarro said the networks involve more than 40 countries and are costing the U.S. Treasury between $19 billion and $26 billion annually in lost customs revenue. The report was released ahead of expected September 2026 discussions between President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping, placing trade enforcement at the forefront of the agenda.

The administration estimates that approximately $75 billion worth of goods each year are rerouted through intermediary countries, where products may undergo limited processing or be relabeled before entering the United States. Officials said U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) is expanding the use of AI-assisted screening tools to identify suspicious supply-chain patterns and strengthen country-of-origin enforcement. Importers found to have falsely declared product origins could face retroactive duties and other penalties.

Administration officials said the strategy is intended to close loopholes that allow Chinese-made products to avoid existing tariffs, while emphasizing that enforcement efforts may also involve companies and intermediaries located in other countries. Trade specialists note that increased scrutiny could lead to additional inspections and documentation requirements for importers, potentially affecting legitimate supply chains alongside those found to be violating customs rules.

While the report does not identify every country allegedly involved, it signals a broader enforcement strategy that combines advanced screening technology with tougher customs oversight. Officials said additional guidance and enforcement actions are expected as the administration continues preparations for upcoming trade discussions with China.
