Chipotle Pulls Jalapeños In Minnesota Salmonella Probe

110 Illnesses Reported As Contaminated Peppers Removed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 5, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Chipotle Mexican Grill said on August 4, 2026, it removed jalapeño peppers from select restaurants after a public-health investigation in Minnesota tied a produce lot to a salmonella cluster. The company said it proactively swapped the peppers with fresh inventory from different growers while cooperating with state and federal investigators.

The Minnesota Department of Health reported 110 confirmed salmonella cases linked to the cluster. Of 84 patients interviewed, 75 said they had eaten at a Chipotle in the two weeks before illness. Investigators said exposures occurred between June 14, 2026, and July 14, 2026, with most between June 24, 2026, and July 2, 2026. Officials emphasized that some patients reported eating at other Mexican-style quick-service restaurants, indicating the contaminated ingredient may have been served beyond a single chain.

State officials said they are continuing traceback with federal partners to determine the source and distribution footprint. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration said it began a traceback review of multiple ingredients on July 22, 2026, while acknowledging Chipotle’s parallel supply-chain checks. Senior MDH epidemiologist Carlota Medus said that, given the company’s measures, the department is not currently concerned about Chipotle’s ongoing operations but cautioned that case counts can rise as testing and interviews catch up and that it is too soon to say whether the outbreak is over.

The announcement hit investor sentiment: shares of Chipotle fell 8.8% to $34.18 in afternoon trading on August 4, 2026. The development also lands amid heightened consumer sensitivity to produce-linked illnesses following a separate nationwide cyclosporiasis outbreak this summer.

- Key facts: 110 cases; 75/84 interviewed reported eating at Chipotle; exposure window June 14–July 14 (peak June 24–July 2); FDA traceback started July 22; jalapeños removed and replaced from different growers; health officials say contaminated food was also served at other restaurants.
