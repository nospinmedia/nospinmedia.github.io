Judge blocks Trump's White House media ban

CNN, MS NOW and Politico regain access for 14 days

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 24, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A federal judge has temporarily blocked the Trump administration from barring CNN, MS NOW and Politico from the White House, ordering officials to immediately restore press credentials that were revoked after President Trump announced a ban on the three news organizations.

U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly granted the outlets a temporary restraining order early September 24, finding that they had met the legal requirements for emergency relief while their lawsuit proceeds. The order restores their White House hard passes and prevents administration officials from enforcing the ban for 14 days, unless the court orders otherwise. Trump announced the restrictions on September 18, criticizing the outlets' coverage.

Kelly concluded that the reporters' credentials had been revoked without constitutionally adequate due process. He cited previous court decisions establishing that journalists with White House hard passes are entitled to notice and an opportunity to respond before those credentials are suspended or revoked. The judge also said the government's arguments offered little support for its assertion that national security concerns justified the restrictions.

The three news organizations sued the administration after losing access, arguing that the ban violated both the First Amendment and the Fifth Amendment. Justice Department lawyers countered that access to the White House is a privilege and argued that the president has broad authority over who is admitted to the complex. Kelly's temporary order does not resolve the full lawsuit or permanently determine the administration's authority over press access.

The dispute also disrupted the White House press pool. Major television networks suspended pooled video coverage after the three organizations were excluded, leaving some presidential events without the television coverage normally shared among news organizations.

Kelly previously handled a 2018 case involving CNN correspondent Jim Acosta, ordering the White House to temporarily restore Acosta's credentials after finding that the administration had not provided adequate due process.
