NASA Veteran Mike Fincke Retires After 30 Years

Crew-11 pilot logged 549 days in space across four missions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 13, 2026 at 10:36 AM ET

Astronaut Mike Fincke is retiring from NASA after a 30-year career, the agency announced on August 12, 2026. Fincke completed four spaceflights, spent 549 days in orbit, and conducted nine spacewalks totaling 48 hours, 37 minutes. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman credited Fincke with helping advance multiple generations of U.S. human spaceflight, from the early International Space Station (ISS) assembly missions through the commercial crew era. His 549 days in space rank fourth among all NASA astronauts for cumulative time in orbit.

Selected as part of NASA's 1996 astronaut class, Fincke first flew aboard Soyuz during Expedition 9 in 2004, later commanded Expedition 18 aboard the ISS in 2008–2009, participated in the STS-134 mission aboard Space Shuttle Endeavour in 2011, and most recently served as pilot of SpaceX Crew-11, which launched in August 2025 and returned in January 2026. During that mission he served as an ISS flight engineer for Expedition 73 before becoming commander of Expedition 74.

Fincke's career reflects the evolution of NASA's human spaceflight program over three decades, spanning long-duration ISS missions, Space Shuttle operations, and commercial crew flights. During Crew-11, NASA carried out its first medical evacuation from the ISS. Fincke later identified himself as the crew member involved and said the medical issue was stabilized before the crew safely returned to Earth. NASA said the mission completed most of its planned objectives.

Fincke is among the few NASA astronauts trained and certified to fly across four human spaceflight systems: Russia's Soyuz, the Space Shuttle, SpaceX Crew Dragon, and Boeing Starliner. In addition to commanding Expedition 18 and helping prepare the ISS for six-person crews, he flew on the final mission of Space Shuttle Endeavour and later supported NASA's commercial crew program through leadership roles involving both Boeing and SpaceX. NASA said he plans to remain active in spaceflight education, mentoring, and public outreach following his retirement.
