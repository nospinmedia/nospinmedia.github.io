FBI arrests woman accused of spying for China

California realtor allegedly surveilled Taiwan president's son

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 5, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The FBI arrested a 34-year-old California real estate agent at Los Angeles International Airport on allegations that she secretly worked for the Chinese government and surveilled the U.S.-based son of Taiwan's president.

Wanying "Heather" Zhang, an Irvine realtor, was arrested Sunday before boarding a flight from LAX to Shanghai. She is charged with acting as an unregistered agent of a foreign government. The allegations are contained in a federal criminal complaint and have not been proven in court.

According to the complaint, Zhang and another person traveled from Las Vegas to the Seattle area in September 2025, rented a vehicle and went to the home of Ting-Yu Lai, the son of Taiwanese President Lai Ching-te. Investigators allege that security-camera footage showed them appearing to record Lai, his wife and their child. The FBI says Zhang also transmitted information including vehicle license-plate numbers through WeChat.

Federal investigators allege Zhang was communicating with at least two people she appeared to acknowledge were Chinese government officials. Messages obtained through a search warrant for her iCloud account allegedly show Zhang discussing the surveillance assignment and reporting back after locating the target. The complaint says she had met one of the officials during a trip to China in June 2025 after being introduced through a former classmate.

An FBI counterintelligence agent wrote that information about the location, homes and vehicles of close relatives of a senior Taiwanese official could potentially provide leverage to the People's Republic of China during a future conflict. China claims democratically governed Taiwan as its territory, while Taiwan rejects Beijing's sovereignty claims.

Zhang is presumed innocent unless proven guilty, and the investigation remains ongoing.
