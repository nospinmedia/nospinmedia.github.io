Trump Sets November Start for Overhaul of Historic D.C. Golf Course

Announcement Complicates Court Fight After More Than 400 Trees Were Removed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 19, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

President Trump says a major redevelopment of Washington's historic East Potomac Golf Links will begin November 15, announcing a concrete construction date while the federal government is already locked in a court fight over changes to the publicly owned property.

Trump said the course will close for approximately 15 months while the Interior Department and golf architect Tom Fazio transform it into what Trump described as a world-class course capable of hosting major tournaments. He said the facility will remain open to the public after the renovation.

The announcement immediately drew attention from U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes, who is overseeing a lawsuit challenging the government's plans. Government attorneys had told the court as recently as Friday that Trump's broader redevelopment remained preliminary and that no final agency action had occurred. After Trump's Sunday announcement that a plan had been developed and work would begin next month, Reyes ordered the administration to explain whether its position had changed.

The legal dispute intensified after National Park Service crews removed more than 400 trees from East Potomac. The government says that work was part of a 2019 landscape plan intended to improve safety and restore the course's historic links-style character, rather than Trump's proposed redesign. Reyes questioned that explanation and temporarily barred additional tree removal through October 26.

The judge had previously required the government to provide advance notice before undertaking substantive or irreversible work at the course. The administration has asked that Reyes be removed from the case, accusing her of bias and hostility toward the administration. Plaintiffs, meanwhile, are seeking a longer-lasting preliminary injunction.

The lawsuit was brought by the DC Preservation League and two local golfers, who argue that the federal government is moving forward without completing required environmental and historic-preservation reviews. East Potomac is a century-old public golf facility on federal parkland along the Potomac River.

The November 15 date does not itself violate the temporary order stopping tree removal through October 26, but the announced construction plan adds a new issue for the court as the broader lawsuit continues.
