Treasury proposes new tax-exemption rules for private schools

Up to 18,000 institutions could be affected

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 4, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The Treasury Department and Internal Revenue Service proposed new regulations on September 3 that could cost private schools, colleges and universities their federal tax-exempt status if they engage in racial discrimination. The proposal could affect roughly 18,000 private educational institutions that qualify for exemption under Section 501(c)(3), ranging from K-12 schools to colleges, universities, professional schools and trade schools.

The proposal would replace longstanding IRS guidance that allows certain race-conscious policies designed to promote diversity. Under the new standard, tax-exempt private schools could not discriminate based on race, color, or national or ethnic origin in admissions, scholarships, loans, academic programs, athletics, facilities or other institutional activities. The rule would not prohibit assistance based on race-neutral criteria such as financial need, geography, first-generation status or hardship.

The administration argues that federal tax benefits should not subsidize practices it considers discriminatory. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said schools cannot avoid federal nondiscrimination requirements by describing racial preferences as diversity initiatives. Losing 501(c)(3) status could have major financial consequences because an institution could become subject to federal income taxes while donations to it could lose their charitable tax deduction. Other financing arrangements tied to tax-exempt status could also be affected.

Treasury and the IRS cite several major Supreme Court decisions in support of the proposal, including Brown v. Board of Education, Bob Jones University v. United States and Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard. The 2023 Students for Fair Admissions ruling sharply restricted the consideration of race in college admissions, while the 1983 Bob Jones decision upheld the IRS's authority to deny tax-exempt status to private schools whose racially discriminatory policies violate established public policy. Higher-education groups are expected to scrutinize whether the government can extend that principle to the broader range of practices covered by the proposed regulation. If finalized, the rules would apply to taxable years beginning on or after May 31, 2027.
