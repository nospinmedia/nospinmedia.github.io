Senate Confirms Dr. Erica Schwartz to Lead CDC in 51–44 Vote

First permanent director in nearly a year as agency confronts major health challenges

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 6, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Dr. Erica Schwartz was confirmed on August 5, 2026, as director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention in a 51–44 Senate vote, restoring permanent leadership to the nation's leading public health agency after nearly a year without a Senate-confirmed director. President Trump's nominee becomes the CDC's 22nd director following months of leadership turnover and workforce reductions.

The vote was largely along party lines, with Sen. Tim Kaine of Virginia the only Democrat voting in favor of confirmation. Schwartz, 54, is a physician, attorney, retired U.S. Public Health Service rear admiral, former deputy surgeon general, and former chief medical officer for the U.S. Coast Guard. During her July 15 confirmation hearing, she pledged to uphold scientific integrity and told senators she would "never betray the science," helping secure support from key lawmakers.

Schwartz takes over an agency that has lost more than 3,000 employees during recent restructuring while responding to several significant public health issues. The CDC is monitoring a multistate Cyclospora outbreak linked to recalled iceberg lettuce, the nation's highest measles case count in decades, and continued H5N1 avian influenza surveillance in animals and exposed workers. Federal officials say those efforts will remain among the agency's immediate priorities as the new director assumes leadership.

Her confirmation follows a turbulent period for the CDC. Dr. David Weldon's nomination was withdrawn in March 2025, and Dr. Susan Monarez was removed less than a month after her confirmation in August 2025. Dr. Jay Bhattacharya, who also serves as director of the National Institutes of Health, has overseen the CDC on an acting basis since February 2026. Schwartz now becomes the agency's first permanent leader in nearly a year as it continues disease surveillance, emergency preparedness, and public health guidance nationwide.
