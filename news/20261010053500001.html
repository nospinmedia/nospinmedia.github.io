Trump Strikes Russian Diesel Deal With Putin, Eases Sanctions

Up to 4.8 Million Tons Promised as Ukraine Condemns Agreement

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 10, 2026 at 12:17 PM ET

President Trump announced an agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Friday, October 9, 2026, to release millions of tons of Russian diesel into American and global markets. The administration simultaneously eased U.S. sanctions on Russian diesel, reversing part of Washington's economic pressure on Moscow as rising fuel prices strain American consumers and businesses.

Following a telephone conversation with Putin, Trump announced that Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 metric tons of diesel, equivalent to approximately 2.25 million barrels. He outlined additional deliveries of 500,000 tons in November and 1 million tons afterward, followed by another 3 million tons depending on Russian refinery conditions. The proposed shipments total approximately 4.8 million metric tons, although the announcement does not establish that all deliveries will occur as planned.

The U.S. Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued a temporary general license allowing transactions involving Russian diesel through April 7, 2027. The authorization permits qualifying sales, deliveries, and imports, including shipments to the United States. It does not represent a complete removal of American sanctions against Russia.

The agreement comes as diesel prices have climbed sharply amid disruptions to global energy supplies, including the war involving Iran and Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries. Diesel is essential for trucking, agriculture, construction, and freight transportation, making higher prices a significant concern for businesses and consumers.

Trump predicted that additional Russian supplies would lower diesel prices. However, energy analysts questioned whether the agreement would provide substantial or lasting relief. Redirecting existing Russian exports to different buyers would not necessarily increase total global diesel availability, particularly while refinery capacity remains constrained.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy sharply criticized the agreement, arguing that easing sanctions without reciprocal Russian concessions would provide Moscow with additional revenue while its war against Ukraine continues. He described the decision as a sign of weakness and warned that increased Russian petroleum sales could help finance further military operations.

The announcement also drew criticism from American lawmakers, including some Republicans, who questioned whether easing sanctions undermines efforts to pressure Russia into ending the war.

The agreement creates a significant foreign policy dilemma for Washington: pursuing lower domestic energy prices while maintaining economic pressure on Russia. Its actual impact will depend on how much diesel Russia delivers, where those shipments are sold, and whether additional supply meaningfully reduces market prices.
