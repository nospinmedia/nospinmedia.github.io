Seven Sailors Missing After Drones Strike Tankers Near Romania

Attacks Raise New Security Concerns Along Black Sea Shipping Route

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 16, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

Seven sailors were missing after drones struck two chemical tankers near Romania's Black Sea coast on October 15, 2026, setting both vessels on fire near the mouth of the Sulina Canal, an important shipping route connecting the Black Sea with the Danube River.

The San Marino-flagged Wine Trader was about 13 nautical miles southeast of Sulina when it was struck. Five of the 12 crew members were rescued, while Romanian authorities continued searching for the remaining seven in rough seas. The tanker was empty of cargo at the time of the attack.

A second tanker, the Comoros-flagged Yara J, was struck the previous evening while anchored closer to Sulina. Its crew extinguished the resulting fire, and all aboard were reported safe. The vessel was later able to proceed toward the Romanian port of Constanța under its own power.

Romanian President Nicușor Dan said both vessels had been hit by drones but did not publicly identify who launched them. A NATO military source told Reuters the drones were of Russian origin, but Romanian authorities had not independently attributed the attacks to Russia as investigations continued.

The incidents are part of a series of recent attacks on commercial shipping in the western Black Sea near Romania and Bulgaria, both NATO members. The region is also a major route for Ukrainian exports, particularly agricultural products transported through the Danube corridor after Russia's invasion disrupted traditional Black Sea shipping routes.

Romanian and Bulgarian leaders met with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte as the alliance reviewed security in the region. NATO has been increasing surveillance and counter-drone capabilities along its eastern flank as drone activity near member states has intensified.

The attacks occurred near, and in at least one case inside, Romanian territorial waters, adding concern about the risk that the war in Ukraine could increasingly affect civilian shipping near NATO territory.
