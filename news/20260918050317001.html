Judge requires 30-day warning before Kennedy Center demolition

Order adds safeguard as building closes for repairs

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 18, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

A federal judge has ordered the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts to provide at least 30 days' notice before demolishing the building or making other major physical changes, adding another legal restriction as the Washington cultural institution closes amid structural and financial concerns.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper issued the requirement after President Trump raised the possibility that the Kennedy Center could ultimately be torn down. The center's board voted this week to temporarily close most of the complex for renovations, citing safety problems and deterioration. Recent concerns have included a partial ceiling collapse following heavy rain and problems identified during an inspection of the roof terrace. Congress has allocated $257 million for repairs to the center.

The order is part of an ongoing lawsuit brought by Rep. Joyce Beatty of Ohio, an ex officio member of the Kennedy Center board. Cooper previously ruled that the center could not add Trump's name to the building without congressional authorization, writing that Congress established the institution as a memorial to President John F. Kennedy and that changing its official designation requires congressional action. Trump's name, which had been placed on the facade, was removed in June 2026 following the court fight.

The latest ruling does not prohibit renovations or permanently bar demolition. Instead, it requires advance notice before significant physical changes, giving the court and parties in the case time to respond if demolition or another major alteration is proposed. The Kennedy Center remains temporarily closed while officials assess repairs and the future of the complex.
