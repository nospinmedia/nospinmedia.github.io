Trump casts Florida midterm ballot early after years of attacking mail voting

President now urges Republicans to vote early, absentee, by mail or in person

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 25, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

President Donald Trump cast his midterm ballot early and in person Saturday in West Palm Beach, Florida, as he increasingly encourages Republicans to use early voting methods he has criticized in the past.

Trump and first lady Melania Trump voted at a Palm Beach County polling location before the president traveled to Georgia for a campaign rally. Wearing an “I Voted” sticker afterward, Trump was asked whether he had voted along the Republican Party line and replied, “Very much so.”

The fact that Trump voted early in person does not itself contradict his criticism of voting by mail. Trump has frequently distinguished between casting a ballot at a polling place and sending one through the mail. However, he has also personally voted by mail in previous elections despite years of attacking the practice and making unsupported claims that widespread mail voting leads to widespread fraud.

His message to Republican voters has also changed significantly. On Saturday, Trump urged Floridians to participate immediately and explicitly included mail voting among the options Republicans should use. “Whether you VOTE early, absentee, by mail, or in-person, Republicans must SWAMP” Democrats with turnout, Trump wrote in a social media post.

That represents a practical shift from Trump's earlier campaigns, when his repeated attacks on mail and early voting sometimes conflicted with Republican efforts to persuade supporters to cast ballots before Election Day. Trump and other Republican leaders now openly encourage supporters to use the voting methods legally available in their states.

Trump's Saturday ballot included several closely watched Florida races. He has endorsed Republican Byron Donalds for governor and Republican Sen. Ashley Moody, who is seeking the remaining two years of the Senate term previously held by Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

The president's decision to vote early came as both parties intensified their turnout operations ahead of the November 3 midterm elections.
