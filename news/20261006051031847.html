New York declares disaster emergency as measles spreads

108 cases reported as state expands vaccine access

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 6, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul has declared a statewide disaster emergency over a growing measles outbreak, giving health officials expanded authority to vaccinate and test residents as cases rise in under-immunized communities. New York reported 108 confirmed measles cases in 2026 through October 3, including 92 cases since July 15 across 18 counties.

Of the state's 108 cases, seven have been reported in New York City and 101 elsewhere in the state. The recent surge has been concentrated largely in rural communities with lower immunization rates. Local health departments have administered more than 1,000 doses of the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine in rural areas since July 15 as officials work to contain transmission.

The emergency order broadens who can administer MMR vaccines, adding paramedics, advanced emergency medical personnel, pharmacists and midwives to the eligible pool. Registered nurses are also temporarily authorized to order testing for suspected measles cases, while pharmacists can vaccinate children as young as 2 years old. The order requires MMR vaccinations to be reported to state or New York City immunization registries within 72 hours. Patient or guardian consent is still required for vaccination.

The declaration took effect October 5 and is scheduled to remain in force through November 4. New York's outbreak comes amid a much larger national resurgence. Nearly 3,900 measles cases have been reported across the United States this year, the country's highest annual total since 1991. Neighboring Pennsylvania has surpassed 1,000 cases in what health officials describe as the nation's largest state outbreak in more than three decades.

Measles was declared eliminated from continuous domestic transmission in the United States in 2000, but declining vaccination coverage and a series of outbreaks have put that status under renewed scrutiny.
