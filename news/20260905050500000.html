U.S. employers add 162,000 jobs in August

Hiring rebounds sharply as unemployment holds at 4.1%

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

U.S. employers added 162,000 jobs in August, far exceeding economists' expectations and marking a sharp rebound after months of weak employment growth. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday that the unemployment rate remained unchanged at 4.1%, while the number of people participating in the labor force increased substantially during the month.

The August gain was more than five times the average monthly increase of 31,000 jobs recorded over the previous 12 months. Food services and drinking places led the increase with 59,000 jobs, while local government education added 42,000. The information sector moved in the opposite direction, losing 23,000 jobs. Average hourly earnings for private-sector workers rose 10 cents, or 0.3%, to $37.75, leaving wages 3.1% higher than a year earlier.

Previous estimates were also revised significantly higher. June payroll growth was increased by 11,000, from 20,000 to 31,000, while July was revised upward by 44,000, changing an initially reported loss of 23,000 jobs into a gain of 21,000. Together, the revisions added 55,000 jobs to the government's previous estimates for those two months.

The separate household survey showed the civilian labor force expanding by 683,000 people in August. Employment measured by that survey increased by 569,000, while the labor-force participation rate rose from 61.4% to 61.6%. About 7 million people remained unemployed. The stronger-than-expected report could influence the Federal Reserve's assessment of the economy as policymakers weigh employment conditions alongside inflation in determining future interest-rate policy.
