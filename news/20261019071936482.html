Texas Senate Race Is Statistically Tied as Early Voting Begins

CBS Poll Shows Talarico at 51% and Paxton at 49% Among Likely Voters

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 19, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

A new CBS News/YouGov poll shows an unusually close U.S. Senate race in Texas, with Democratic state Rep. James Talarico receiving 51% support and Republican Attorney General Ken Paxton at 49% among likely voters. The two-point difference is within the poll's margin of error, making the survey a statistical tie as early voting begins.

The poll surveyed 1,492 registered Texas voters from October 8 through October 16. The margin of error for likely voters is approximately 3.5 percentage points, meaning the results should not be interpreted as showing either candidate ahead.

The competitiveness is notable in a state where Republicans have dominated statewide elections for decades. Texas has not elected a Democrat to statewide office since 1994, although several recent statewide contests have been closer than the state's historical Republican advantage.

CBS found that the candidates are relying on different coalitions. Paxton draws overwhelming support from Republican voters and benefits from Republicans continuing to outnumber Democrats in the state. Talarico receives strong Democratic support while performing particularly well among independent and younger voters. The survey also found Democrats currently expressing greater certainty than Republicans that they will vote, making turnout an important variable in the final weeks.

The race has attracted extraordinary national attention and spending. More than $500 million has been spent or reserved across the Texas Senate contest as both parties compete for a seat that could affect control of the Senate. Paxton has also received substantial outside support, including from a Republican super PAC that recently received $158 million from Elon Musk.

Other recent Texas polling has also shown a competitive race rather than a clear advantage for either candidate. A University of Texas/Texas Politics Project survey released earlier this month measured Talarico at 50% and Paxton at 47%, another result within that poll's margin of error.

With early voting beginning October 19, the remaining campaign will increasingly depend on which side converts voter preference into actual turnout.
