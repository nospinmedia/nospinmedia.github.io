Pakistan mosque attack kills at least 21

More than 100 injured as police compound is stormed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 19, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

At least 21 people were killed and more than 100 others were injured in a coordinated attack on a mosque and police compound in Kohat, Pakistan, during Friday prayers on September 18. Updated casualty figures vary: Pakistani health officials reported 21 deaths, while a later hospital update reported by Anadolu Agency put the toll at 24, including 18 police officers and six civilians.

The attack began when an explosives-laden vehicle was driven into the Old Police Lines compound in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, near Pakistan's border with Afghanistan. Gunmen then entered the compound, triggering a prolonged confrontation with police and security forces. The complex houses police and counterterrorism units, and many officers and worshippers were inside when the assault began.

Pakistani security forces continued clearing the compound into Saturday. Police said eight attackers were ultimately killed during the operation, which lasted roughly 20 hours. The assault caused extensive damage around the mosque and police facilities as emergency crews transported casualties to area hospitals.

The militant alliance Ittehad-ul-Mujahideen Pakistan, which includes militants associated with commander Hafiz Gul Bahadur, claimed responsibility for the attack, according to Pakistani reporting. The assault comes amid increased militant violence in northwestern Pakistan, where police and security forces have repeatedly been targeted.

Authorities continued assessing casualties and damage on Saturday, meaning the death toll could change as hospitals and security officials reconcile figures from the attack and subsequent fighting.
