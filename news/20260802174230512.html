Capital One Says AML Review Drove Closure of 300+ Trump Accounts

Bank moves to toss Florida ‘debanking’ suit after new filing

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Capital One Financial told a federal court that it ended its banking relationship with the Trump Organization after an internal anti-money-laundering review—not for political reasons—seeking dismissal of the family company’s ‘debanking’ lawsuit. In a filing made public this weekend, the bank said it issued closure notices in March 2021 following “months of analysis” by its AML team, marking the first time a U.S. bank has formally tied money-laundering compliance concerns to President Trump’s family business.

The case is pending in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida. The bank said it is not accusing the company of committing money laundering, but that transaction patterns triggered standard compliance protocols and that federal confidentiality rules limit what it can disclose. The filing also says the company’s allegations of political bias are “misguided” and rely on “cherry‑picked” excerpts from documents already before the court.

- More than 300 Trump‑affiliated accounts received closure notices in March 2021 - The Trump Organization and Eric Trump sued in March 2025, alleging unlawful political discrimination - The court has dismissed two earlier complaints; an amended complaint was filed in July 2026

The dispute could shape how banks defend account terminations amid rising claims of ideological “debanking.” Capital One’s filing argues the decisions followed federal AML requirements and internal risk standards that banks face penalties for ignoring. The lawsuit lands in a tense policy climate: since returning to the White House, President Trump signed an August 2025 executive order directing agencies to curb discriminatory debanking, and in January 2026 he brought a separate debanking suit against JPMorgan Chase.

While reiterating that it made no allegation of criminal conduct, Capital One said the activity it identified is among the types flagged in federal banking guidance and that disclosure of additional details could violate confidentiality obligations. The plaintiffs maintain their accounts were closed for political reasons after the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot; the judge will now decide whether the latest complaint survives or the case is dismissed.
