Audi announces $4.2 billion Georgia factory creating 4,500 jobs

First U.S. Audi plant expected to produce 200,000 vehicles a year

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 25, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Audi plans to invest $4.2 billion in a new automobile factory in central Georgia, creating approximately 4,500 jobs and giving the German luxury automaker its first U.S. assembly plant.

President Trump announced the project Saturday during a campaign rally in Perry, Georgia, joined by Audi CEO Gernot Döllner. Audi, which is owned by Volkswagen, subsequently confirmed the investment and said construction is scheduled to begin in 2027, with vehicle production expected to start in the early 2030s.

The plant near Macon is expected to have capacity to produce approximately 200,000 vehicles per year. Döllner said Audi plans to manufacture some of its largest and most expensive SUVs at the facility. Reuters reported that the expected models include the Q7, Q8 and Q9.

Audi also plans additional U.S. production in South Carolina, where approximately 25,000 vehicles a year are expected to be assembled at Volkswagen's Scout Motors facility, according to Reuters.

Trump credited his administration's tariff policies with encouraging Audi to move production to the United States. Audi did not publicly attribute the decision solely to tariffs. Döllner said the project is a long-term investment reflecting the company's confidence in the American market. Bloomberg reported that Volkswagen and the administration have also discussed possible tariff relief connected to expanded U.S. manufacturing.

The timing gives the announcement a political dimension. Trump unveiled the project at a rally supporting Republican candidates just days before the November 3 midterm elections. But regardless of the campaign setting, Audi's announced investment, employment target and production plans represent a concrete expansion of automobile manufacturing in the United States.
