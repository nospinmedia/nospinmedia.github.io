Twin Wildfires Kill 2 On Greek Island Near Athens

More than 570 people evacuated by sea as crews battle fast-moving flames

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Two fast-moving wildfires on the Greek island of Salamis, near Athens, killed two people and forced the emergency evacuation of more than 570 residents and visitors by sea after flames rapidly spread into coastal communities. The fires broke out about 20 minutes apart on August 16, overwhelming emergency responders as strong winds pushed the blazes toward homes and beaches.

Greek authorities said the victims, identified by local officials as an elderly couple, were found in the Peristeria area after becoming trapped by the advancing fire. The Hellenic Coast Guard, firefighters, and private vessels helped evacuate hundreds of people from beaches and waterfront communities while medical teams treated several others for burns and smoke inhalation. Emergency officials deployed hundreds of firefighters, aircraft, and helicopters to prevent the two fire fronts from merging.

By August 17, officials said the major fire fronts had been brought under control, although crews remained on the island to extinguish hotspots and protect damaged communities. Authorities also reported responding to 36 separate wildfires across Greece within the previous 24 hours as extreme summer conditions continued to elevate wildfire risks throughout southern Europe.

The Salamis fires are the latest in a series of destructive wildfires affecting Mediterranean countries during another intense fire season. Officials warned that climate conditions and dry vegetation continue to increase the likelihood of rapidly spreading fires, even as weather forecasts suggest some improvement in wind conditions.
