Thieves steal Renoir paintings from French Riviera museum

Two works remain missing after $10 million heist

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 9, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

French police are searching for two thieves who broke into the Renoir Museum in Cagnes-sur-Mer and targeted four paintings by impressionist master Pierre-Auguste Renoir, escaping with two while dropping the other two in the museum grounds. The four targeted works were valued at roughly €9 million, or about $10.5 million, combined.

The break-in occurred shortly before 6 a.m. on September 8 at Renoir's former home on the French Riviera. Investigators said the thieves cut through the property's perimeter fence using a powered cutting tool and entered through a window. The break-in occurred around a security guard shift change, with the alarm sounding at approximately 5:48 a.m. Police arrived within minutes, forcing the suspects to flee before they could get away with all four paintings.

The two recovered works were Portrait of Madame Stephen Pichon (1895) and Coco Reading (1905), which were found abandoned in the museum garden. Still missing are Portrait of Madame Colonna Romano (1910), valued at more than $2.3 million, and Young Woman at the Well (1886), valued at approximately $230,000. The missing works were on loan from the Musée d'Orsay in Paris.

Authorities have opened an investigation into organized theft and criminal conspiracy. Surveillance cameras captured two suspects, but they remained at large as investigators examined how the thieves planned the early-morning raid and whether they deliberately exploited the guard change.

The museum occupies Renoir's final residence, where the artist lived from 1907 until his death in 1919. Its collection includes paintings, sculptures, photographs, furniture and other objects associated with his life and work. The robbery comes amid heightened scrutiny of security at French museums following other high-profile thefts from cultural institutions.
