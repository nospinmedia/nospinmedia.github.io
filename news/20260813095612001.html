Dallas judge orders lifesaving care as surrogate gives birth

Interstate fight over parental rights and treatment moves to Texas courts

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 13, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

An Alaskan surrogate, McKenna West, delivered a baby boy in Dallas on August 12, 2026, a day after a Dallas County judge ordered that the newborn receive lifesaving medical care and barred removal from Texas. The pregnancy had been been complicated by hypoplastic left heart syndrome (HLHS), identified around the 20-week scan, and the case has become a high-profile test of parental authority and medical decision-making when surrogacy and fetal anomalies collide across state lines.

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton intervened ahead of the birth and said he asked UT Southwestern Medical Center and Children's Medical Center of Dallas to provide immediate care. The court’s emergency orders required medically necessary treatment upon delivery, directed providers to notify the court of key developments, and appointed a guardian ad litem. A related restraining order placed medical decision-making with the intended parents, Nausheen Gilkar and Omar Ahmed, within the limits of the court’s lifesaving-care mandate, and West’s attorney said she was not permitted contact with the child after delivery. It was not immediately clear what specific care the infant received in the hours after birth, or where he is now being treated.

According to Paxton’s office, physicians determined the baby was a candidate for surgery, and the infant is receiving stabilizing care while preparing for treatment. Gilkar and Ahmed have denied claims that they sought to withhold care, and their attorney said they consulted pediatric heart specialists in Dallas and Los Angeles before the birth to arrange lifesaving treatment. A California trial court had previously recognized Gilkar and Ahmed as the child's legal parents, and a California appellate court declined to stay that ruling while the interstate dispute continues. Court filings also reflect a disagreement over the child's name, with the intended parents referring to him as Rumi, while Paxton and West have referred to him as Gabriel. The surrogacy match was arranged through Worldwide Surrogacy Specialists, which urged mediation and a child-focused resolution.

Parallel legal proceedings in Texas and California are ongoing over custody and authority for medical decisions. A court appearance had been set for August 25, 2026, though the timeline could change following the birth and emergency orders.
