Trump Administration Sues California Over 20,000 Mail Ballots

DOJ Seeks to Block Votes From People It Says Are Likely Noncitizens

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 16, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The Trump administration sued California election officials on October 15, 2026, asking a federal judge to prevent the state from counting mail ballots from more than 20,000 registered voters whom the federal government says are likely noncitizens until their citizenship status can be verified.

The lawsuit, filed by the U.S. Justice Department, follows a demand that state and Los Angeles County election officials review a password-protected thumb drive containing the names of voters flagged by federal agencies. Officials were given 48 hours to verify their citizenship or agree to set aside their ballots. California officials declined, questioning the reliability of the federal government's information and the legality of taking such action shortly before the November 3 midterm elections.

According to the federal complaint, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services identified more than 20,000 registered voters it believed were likely noncitizens. Homeland Security Investigations then conducted additional analysis and reached a similar conclusion. Because California automatically mails ballots to registered voters, the Justice Department alleges those individuals have received or will receive ballots. The government's identification of the voters as likely noncitizens remains an allegation and has not been established by a court.

The Justice Department is asking the court to require California to set aside ballots associated with the flagged voters until their citizenship can be independently verified. Federal law prohibits noncitizens from voting in federal elections.

California officials dispute the government's claims. California Secretary of State Shirley Weber has said the federal government has not provided sufficient evidence showing that the people on its list are actually ineligible to vote. State officials have also raised concerns about making changes affecting voter eligibility so close to an election and about potentially preventing eligible U.S. citizens from having their ballots counted.

The lawsuit is part of increased federal scrutiny of state voter rolls by President Trump's administration ahead of the midterm elections. The court has not yet determined whether the approximately 20,000 people identified by federal authorities are noncitizens or whether their ballots can legally be withheld from the count.
