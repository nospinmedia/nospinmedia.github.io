Louisiana Girl Dies After Rare Brain-Eating Amoeba Infection

State reports first Naegleria fowleri case since 2013

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 12:17 PM ET

An 8-year-old Louisiana girl, Lillian Smart of Ruston, has died after contracting Naegleria fowleri, the rare organism commonly known as the "brain-eating amoeba." The Louisiana Department of Health (LDH) confirmed the infection, saying health investigators believe she was exposed while swimming in Lake Claiborne in Claiborne Parish. Smart died on August 22, one day after celebrating her eighth birthday, following rapid brain swelling caused by the infection known as primary amebic meningoencephalitis (PAM).

Naegleria fowleri is a naturally occurring microscopic amoeba found in warm freshwater lakes, ponds and rivers. Infection occurs when contaminated water is forced up the nose, allowing the organism to travel to the brain through the olfactory nerve. Health officials emphasized that people cannot become infected by drinking contaminated water and that the disease does not spread from person to person. Although the amoeba is common in warm freshwater environments, human infections remain extremely rare and are almost always fatal.

State health officials said this is Louisiana's first confirmed Naegleria fowleri infection since 2013. Because the organism naturally exists in many freshwater bodies during hot weather, officials said environmental testing is generally not useful for determining whether a lake is safe, as amoeba levels can change quickly with weather and water conditions. Instead, the department recommends reducing the risk of exposure by using nose clips or holding the nose shut when swimming, avoiding jumping or diving into warm freshwater and limiting activities that disturb sediment near the bottom.

According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, only about 180 confirmed infections were reported in the United States between 1937 and 2025, but nearly all were fatal. Health experts encourage anyone who develops severe headache, fever, vomiting or a stiff neck after recent exposure to warm freshwater to seek immediate medical attention because early treatment offers the best chance of survival.
