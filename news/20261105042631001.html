Hong Kong activist Joshua Wong sentenced to 10 years

New national security term keeps prominent democracy figure imprisoned

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 5, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Hong Kong pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong, 30, was sentenced Thursday to 10 years in prison after pleading guilty to conspiring to collude with foreign entities under the city's national security law imposed by Beijing in 2020.

High Court Judge William Tam said the punishment reflected the gravity of the offense. Prosecutors accused Wong of conspiring with self-exiled activist Nathan Law and others in 2020 to seek sanctions, blockades or other actions by foreign governments, organizations and individuals against the governments of Hong Kong and China. Wong pleaded guilty in September 2026. The court used 13 years as a starting point before reducing the sentence because of his guilty plea and other mitigating factors.

Wong was already serving a separate four-year, eight-month sentence connected to an unofficial 2020 pro-democracy primary. He had been expected to be released in January 2027. The remaining two months of that sentence will run concurrently with the new term, meaning the latest conviction substantially extends his imprisonment.

Wong rose to prominence as a teenage activist and became one of the best-known figures in Hong Kong's democracy movement. He played a prominent role in the 2014 Umbrella Movement and later co-founded the political party Demosisto, which disbanded when the national security law took effect.

Wong has said he never advocated Hong Kong independence or regime change and believed at the time that his activities were lawful. Supporters and rights advocates have criticized his prosecution as part of a broader restriction of political dissent. Hong Kong and Chinese authorities maintain that national security prosecutions are based on evidence and that the law was necessary to restore stability following the 2019 protests.
