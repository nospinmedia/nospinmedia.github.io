Supreme Court rejects bid to restore plea deal for alleged 9/11 mastermind

Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and two co-defendants again face possible death sentences

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 20, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court has rejected an effort by Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, the alleged mastermind of the September 11, 2001 attacks, to restore plea agreements that would have allowed him and two co-defendants to avoid the death penalty.

The justices declined on October 19 to hear Mohammed's appeal, leaving intact a lower-court ruling that allowed the Pentagon to withdraw the agreements. The Supreme Court did not provide an explanation for turning away the case, as is customary when it declines an appeal.

The agreements involved Mohammed and co-defendants Walid bin Attash and Mustafa al-Hawsawi, who have been held at the U.S. military prison at Guantánamo Bay, Cuba. Under the deals negotiated with military prosecutors, the defendants would have pleaded guilty to charges connected to the 9/11 attacks in exchange for life sentences rather than facing capital trials.

The agreements were announced in 2024, but then-Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin moved to revoke them shortly afterward. That decision triggered a prolonged legal dispute over whether Austin had the authority to cancel agreements that had already been approved through the military commission system.

An appeals court ultimately sided with the Pentagon, concluding that the defense secretary had authority over the plea agreements. Mohammed then asked the Supreme Court to intervene, arguing that the agreements should be enforced.

The Supreme Court's decision does not determine Mohammed's guilt or impose a sentence. Instead, it leaves the military commission cases against the three men on a path where prosecutors may continue seeking the death penalty.

The prosecutions have remained unresolved for more than two decades, complicated in part by legal disputes over evidence, the defendants' treatment in CIA custody and the rules governing the military commission system.
