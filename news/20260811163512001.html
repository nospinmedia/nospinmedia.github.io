FDA Proposes Mandatory Notices For New Food Ingredients

Rule would end voluntary GRAS notifications; 120-day comment period begins

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 11, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on August 10, 2026 proposed requiring food manufacturers to notify the agency before marketing new ingredients through the Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) pathway. The proposal would replace the current voluntary notification system with mandatory reporting, addressing a long-criticized process that has allowed companies to determine an ingredient is safe without informing the FDA.

Under the proposed rule, manufacturers would be required to submit information describing a new ingredient, its intended use, and the scientific basis supporting its GRAS determination. Acting FDA Commissioner Kyle Diamantas said the proposal would close a decades-old information gap by giving the agency greater visibility into ingredients entering the food supply while remaining within existing legal authority. The FDA said it expects to review submitted notices within 180 days. A 120-day public comment period will precede any final rule.

The proposal would not require FDA approval before companies market new GRAS ingredients. Manufacturers would continue to be responsible for establishing that an ingredient is safe, while the FDA would maintain a public inventory of submitted notices to improve transparency. Health officials also said work continues on developing the federal government's first definition of ultra-processed foods, although no draft definition has yet been released.

Consumer advocates welcomed the proposal as a step toward greater transparency but said broader reforms would still require congressional action if the FDA were to gain authority to require premarket approval of all new GRAS ingredients. Food manufacturers may also face additional documentation and planning requirements if the rule is finalized.
