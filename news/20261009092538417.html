Judge weighs Trump's White House ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico

Three news outlets seek continued access as court ruling approaches

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 9, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Three major news organizations are asking a federal judge to prevent President Trump's administration from barring their journalists from the White House, arguing that the restrictions violate constitutional protections for freedom of the press. During a hearing in Washington, D.C., on October 8, 2026, attorneys representing CNN, MS NOW and Politico urged U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly to extend an existing court order restoring their access while their lawsuit proceeds.

The dispute began in September 2026, when President Trump announced that the three organizations would lose access to White House grounds because of reporting he characterized as false and misleading. The administration has also accused the outlets of publishing classified information and violating journalistic standards. The news organizations dispute those allegations and maintain that the restrictions amount to government retaliation against unfavorable coverage.

Judge Kelly temporarily blocked the ban on September 24, finding that the administration's actions likely violated the organizations' Fifth Amendment right to due process. The temporary restraining order restored their access while the court considered whether longer-lasting protections were warranted.

At Thursday's hearing, attorney Theodore J. Boutrous, representing the news organizations, argued that the administration was engaging in unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination by penalizing journalists for reporting the president dislikes. He said the restrictions could discourage news organizations from publishing critical coverage, raising concerns under the First Amendment.

Justice Department attorney Michael Velchik defended the administration's position, arguing that the organizations had engaged in questionable reporting practices, including inaccurate quotations, misleading interview editing and reliance on anonymous sources. He maintained that false reporting could affect government policy and national security.

Judge Kelly questioned the government's justification, describing some of its legal arguments as an uphill challenge. He also pressed federal attorneys to identify the specific national security concerns supporting the restrictions.

The news organizations additionally alleged that the administration continued limiting access despite the temporary court order. Their attorneys cited instances in which CNN was selectively excluded from White House broadcast pool assignments. CNN confirmed that it had subsequently been permitted to resume some pool duties.

The judge extended the existing temporary restrictions against the administration's ban through Tuesday, October 13, and said he intends to decide by then whether to issue a preliminary injunction preserving the journalists' access while the broader lawsuit continues.

The case raises a constitutional question about the government's authority to control access to official facilities. The administration argues that White House access is a privilege subject to presidential discretion, while the news organizations maintain that the government cannot selectively exclude journalists because of their reporting or viewpoints.
