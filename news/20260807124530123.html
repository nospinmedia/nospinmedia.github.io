Michigan Lifts Lettuce Warning As Cyclospora Cases Slow

State says normal salad purchases can resume as outbreak declines

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 7, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Michigan health officials lifted the state's lettuce advisory on August 6, 2026, saying the Cyclospora outbreak has declined enough that residents can resume buying bagged lettuce and pre-packaged salad mixes. The Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS) said illness reports and diarrhea-related emergency department visits continue to fall, indicating the outbreak has moved beyond its peak.

State data show 12,485 reported cases since June 22, 2026, including 279 hospitalizations and 2 deaths. MDHHS said most exposures occurred from late June through early July and that the contaminated produce is likely no longer in the retail food supply. Chief Medical Executive Dr. Natasha Bagdasarian said the updated guidance reflects improving outbreak trends while encouraging consumers to continue following normal food-safety practices.

The change comes as the FDA and CDC continue investigating a related multistate Cyclospora outbreak spanning 15 states. Federal investigators linked part of that outbreak to shredded iceberg lettuce supplied by Taylor Farms de Mexico, prompting a voluntary recall in July. Health officials continue to emphasize that not every Cyclospora illness reported nationwide is connected to the recalled lettuce and that multiple investigations remain active.

Although the statewide advisory has been lifted, Michigan health officials encourage consumers to continue washing fresh produce, separating raw foods from ready-to-eat items, and seeking medical care if prolonged symptoms develop after eating fresh fruits or vegetables. Officials say current evidence indicates the risk from routine retail lettuce purchases is now low.
