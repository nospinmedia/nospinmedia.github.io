Attorney General Blanche Declines To Promise DOJ Independence

Says President Trump's views will be considered in some cases

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche said August 16 that he would not pledge to keep the Department of Justice (DOJ) fully independent from the White House, marking a significant departure from expectations that have guided the department since the Watergate era. Speaking on NBC's Meet the Press, Blanche said "no attorney general should ever pledge" complete independence and acknowledged that President Trump should have a role in shaping certain prosecutorial decisions.

Blanche said the president, as the nation's chief executive, is entitled to establish law enforcement priorities and that his views would be considered where appropriate. At the same time, he insisted he would refuse any request he believed was unlawful or unethical and said the Justice Department would continue to pursue cases with integrity and without political favoritism. His remarks came less than a week after his 50-49 Senate confirmation and swearing-in as attorney general.

The comments immediately renewed debate over the long-standing norms governing contacts between the White House and federal prosecutors. The DOJ Justice Manual limits communications about pending criminal and civil matters in an effort to protect prosecutorial decisions from political influence. Supporters argue presidential oversight of executive agencies is consistent with the Constitution, while critics contend that allowing White House views to influence individual cases risks weakening safeguards established after President Richard Nixon's resignation in 1974.

Blanche, who previously served as one of President Trump's personal defense attorneys before joining the administration, is expected to face continued congressional scrutiny over how the department manages communications with the White House. His remarks are likely to remain a focal point as lawmakers examine the balance between presidential authority and prosecutorial independence during upcoming oversight hearings.
