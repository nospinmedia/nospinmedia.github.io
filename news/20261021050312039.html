Nigeria confirms first Ebola case of latest outbreak

44 contacts monitored after infection detected in Abuja

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 21, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Nigeria has confirmed its first Ebola case of the current African outbreak after a woman in Abuja tested positive for the virus, prompting health authorities to isolate the patient and monitor 44 identified contacts.

The woman began experiencing symptoms on October 7 and sought treatment at a private hospital in Abuja on October 11. Laboratory testing confirmed infection with Bundibugyo ebolavirus on October 20. Nigeria's Federal Ministry of Health and Social Welfare said she remains in isolation and is receiving medical care.

The patient's route before arriving in Nigeria is still being investigated. She had traveled to eastern Democratic Republic of Congo for business and then passed through Uganda in early October. Uganda's Health Ministry said she showed no symptoms while transiting the country and emphasized that her passage through Uganda does not establish where she became infected. Uganda had previously reported Ebola cases but was declared free of the virus in August.

Nigeria's health minister, Muhammad Ali Pate, said contact tracing is underway, with 44 people already identified for monitoring. The response includes the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention, the Federal Capital Territory health authorities, the World Health Organization and other regional health partners.

The WHO said Nigeria is the fifth country to detect the Bundibugyo virus since the current outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of Congo on May 15. Kenya has also detected infections during the expanding outbreak. Nigeria previously contained Ebola in 2014, when the country recorded 20 cases before transmission was stopped within several months.

Health officials say investigations are continuing to determine the woman's likely source of infection and identify anyone else who may have been exposed.
