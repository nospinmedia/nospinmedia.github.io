Newsom moves to commute all 548 California death sentences

382 cases still require state Supreme Court approval

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 6, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

California Gov. Gavin Newsom announced plans Thursday to commute the death sentences of all 548 people on the state's death row to life in prison without the possibility of parole, a move described by the Death Penalty Information Center as the largest mass clemency action in U.S. history.

The action does not immediately finalize every commutation. Under the California Constitution, the state Supreme Court must approve clemency for people convicted of more than one felony. That requirement applies to 382 of the 548 prisoners, meaning those commutations will take effect only if a majority of the court approves them. Newsom's decision is final for the remaining prisoners who do not require court review.

Newsom said state-sanctioned killing is incompatible with California's values and argued that the death penalty undermines the legal system. The Democratic governor has opposed capital punishment throughout his tenure and imposed a moratorium on executions in 2019, shortly after taking office. California has not carried out an execution since 2006, although capital punishment remains legal in the state and voters upheld it in statewide ballot measures in 2012 and 2016.

The decision drew sharply different reactions. Death penalty opponents and civil rights organizations praised the action, pointing to concerns about wrongful convictions, racial disparities and the reliability of capital punishment. Some victims' relatives and prosecutors criticized the blanket approach, arguing that the cases involved individual jury verdicts and decades of legal proceedings.

If all 548 commutations take effect, California's action would reduce the total U.S. death-row population by nearly 30%. The announcement also came as Florida, Alabama and Indiana carried out executions Thursday, underscoring major differences among states over the continued use of capital punishment.
