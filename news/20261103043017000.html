Learjet may have broken apart before Florida crash

20-year-old co-pilot identified as investigators focus on weather

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 3, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

Federal investigators say a Learjet 60 that crashed in Palm Beach County, Florida, killing all nine people aboard, may have begun breaking apart before hitting the ground as investigators examine icing, turbulence and severe weather encountered during the flight.

The private jet departed Austin, Texas, on Sunday and was headed to North Eleuthera in the Bahamas. The National Transportation Safety Board said the crew reported icing and moderate turbulence while flying at about 35,000 feet. After encountering precipitation, the pilots requested permission to climb to 37,000 feet. Less than 30 seconds later, the aircraft began a rapid descent and the crew transmitted a Mayday call.

The jet's descent rate increased dramatically before it crashed near a roadway and residential area, leaving a large crater. NTSB member Tom Chapman said investigators have evidence suggesting some parts of the aircraft may have separated before impact, although officials have not determined what caused the crash. Weather conditions are a major focus of the investigation. At least one person on the ground suffered a leg injury.

Family members identified the co-pilot as 20-year-old Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras, a Florida-born pilot whose family said she began flying as a teenager. Authorities had not formally identified all nine victims as of the latest reporting, although relatives have publicly identified several passengers and crew members.

Investigators are examining wreckage, flight information, maintenance records and air traffic control communications. The NTSB cautioned that its findings remain preliminary and that the investigation into the cause is ongoing.
