Father of USS Lincoln sailor detained by ICE

Immigration case unfolds as carrier returns from record deployment

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 24, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The father of an active-duty U.S. Navy sailor assigned to the USS Abraham Lincoln has been placed in federal immigration custody after being stopped by U.S. Border Patrol in Key West, Florida, while his son remained deployed aboard the aircraft carrier. Luis Manuel Aviles Roa, 48, a Nicaraguan national who has lived in the United States for about 19 years, was taken into custody on August 22 and transferred to Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) pending removal proceedings.

His son, Joshua Aviles, said he learned of the arrest while serving aboard the Lincoln after more than nine months at sea during the carrier's Middle East deployment. Joshua said his father has a driver's license, Social Security card, and a valid work permit, and that the family has been waiting for a decision on a pending green card application. In a statement, the Department of Homeland Security said Luis Manuel Aviles Roa entered the country illegally and remains in ICE custody. The agency added that having a family member serving in the military does not exempt someone from federal immigration enforcement.

The case has drawn attention because the USS Abraham Lincoln recently completed a deployment that exceeded 200 consecutive days without a port call, setting a modern U.S. Navy endurance record and prompting concerns from some lawmakers and families about crew welfare. Joshua Aviles wrote that he has continued working 12-hour days while worrying about his father's detention, saying the uncertainty has made it difficult to focus on his duties.

Immigration advocates note that some immediate family members of active-duty military personnel may qualify for discretionary relief programs such as Military Parole in Place, but eligibility depends on individual circumstances and the program does not automatically prevent detention or removal proceedings. DHS has not indicated whether that relief applies in this case, and immigration proceedings against Luis Manuel Aviles Roa remain ongoing.
