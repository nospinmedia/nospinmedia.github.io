Jury Finds Live Nation Monopoly

Ticketmaster verdict shakes music industry

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished April 15, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

A federal jury in New York ruled on April 15, 2026 that Live Nation Entertainment and its ticketing subsidiary Ticketmaster maintained and abused an illegal monopoly in the live entertainment industry.

The verdict followed a five-week trial involving the U.S. Department of Justice and a coalition of 34 state attorneys general, including New York Attorney General Letitia James. Prosecutors argued that Live Nation used its dominant position in concert promotion, venue ownership, and ticket sales to limit competition and increase costs for fans.

Jurors concluded that the company’s business practices allowed it to control access to major concert venues and leverage that influence over artists and promoters. Evidence presented during the trial focused on how promotion deals and venue relationships could discourage competitors from entering the market.

Live Nation has repeatedly denied the allegations and maintained that ticket pricing and service fees are largely determined by venues and event organizers. Company representatives argued during the trial that the entertainment industry remains competitive and that its scale helps deliver large tours and events more efficiently.

The jury’s decision now moves the case into a remedies phase, where the court will determine what corrective measures may be required. Potential outcomes could include financial penalties or structural changes to the company’s business operations.

Legal analysts say the ruling could become one of the most significant antitrust decisions involving the live entertainment industry in decades, particularly because Ticketmaster controls ticketing systems used by many large concert venues across the United States.

The court has ordered both sides to submit proposals outlining possible remedies before the next phase of litigation begins later in 2026.
