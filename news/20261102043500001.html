All 9 killed after Learjet crashes in Florida

Pilots reported icing and turbulence before steep dive

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 2, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

All nine people aboard a private Learjet 60 were killed Sunday when the aircraft plunged into a neighborhood in Palm Beach County, Florida, erupting into a fireball and leaving a crater near a busy roadway. U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy confirmed that everyone aboard the aircraft was killed.

The 22-year-old Learjet 60, tail number N722BD, was owned by Delaware-based LR60271 LLC. It departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was headed to North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas when it crashed near Greenacres, Florida, at about 12:50 p.m. The aircraft was flying east at roughly 35,000 feet when its pilots reported turbulence, icing and precipitation and requested permission to climb another 2,000 feet.

The situation deteriorated rapidly. Air traffic control recordings and flight-tracking data showed the aircraft entering a steep descent, initially falling about 10,000 feet per minute before the rate increased to 20,000 feet per minute and eventually as much as 30,000 feet per minute. The pilots declared an emergency as the jet accelerated beyond its design limits. Authorities have not determined what caused the crash, and the reported weather conditions have not been established as the cause.

The aircraft crashed near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North, close to Palm Beach County / President Donald J. Trump International Airport. The impact damaged power lines and water mains, while about 600 residents of a nearby mobile-home community were evacuated. John I. Leonard Community High School was closed Monday as authorities continued responding to the crash and its effects on the surrounding area.

The National Transportation Safety Board is leading the investigation, with the Federal Aviation Administration providing support. Investigators are expected to examine the wreckage, flight information, weather conditions and the crew's actions before determining a probable cause.
