AfD seeks path to power after historic German state win

Party falls short of majority as rivals reject cooperation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 8, 2026 at 2:54 PM ET

Germany's far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) is trying to build support to govern the eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt after winning nearly 44% of the vote in Sunday's election but falling short of an outright majority. The result gave the AfD 39 of 83 seats, leaving it dependent on support from other lawmakers if it is to form what would be Germany's first far-right state government since World War II.

The AfD more than doubled its vote share from the 2021 election, when it received about 21%. Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democratic Union (CDU) finished a distant second with roughly 17%, a sharp decline in a state the party had governed since 2002. AfD state leader Ulrich Siegmund, 35, called the victory a mandate for political change, while national co-leader Alice Weidel argued that the result requires other parties to reconsider their refusal to cooperate with the AfD.

Merz rejected that possibility Monday, saying the CDU would maintain its longstanding political 'firewall' against cooperation with the AfD. He acknowledged that the election had shaken his party 'to its very foundations.' The AfD is classified as a right-wing extremist organization by Saxony-Anhalt's domestic intelligence office, a designation the party rejects.

One possible route to power involves the smaller Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance (BSW), which cleared the 5% threshold for representation. BSW's lead candidate Thomas Schulze said his party is willing to talk with all parties, including the AfD. The two parties differ significantly but share opposition to current German support for Ukraine and favor restoring closer relations with Russia.

The election has implications beyond Saxony-Anhalt. The AfD is currently leading national opinion polling and says the state victory is a step toward competing for national power in Germany's 2029 federal election. For now, however, the immediate question is whether any party or individual lawmakers will provide the support the AfD needs to govern the state.
