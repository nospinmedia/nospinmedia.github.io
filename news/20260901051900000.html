Congo Ebola outbreak passes 6,000 confirmed cases

Largest outbreak in country's history leaves 2,911 dead

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 1, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

The Democratic Republic of Congo is confronting the largest Ebola outbreak in its history, with government figures showing 6,041 confirmed infections and 2,911 deaths as of August 31, 2026. The outbreak has now surpassed the country's previous worst Ebola epidemic, which lasted from 2018 to 2020, marking a major escalation in a public-health emergency that has spread across multiple provinces.

The outbreak is being driven by the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, a comparatively rare form of the virus for which there are currently no approved vaccines or treatments specifically available. Congolese authorities said response teams are operating across six affected provinces, with particular attention focused on Ituri, North Kivu, and Haut-Uélé, where the burden of infection has been especially serious.

The scale of the outbreak presents an unusual challenge because many of the medical tools developed during earlier Ebola emergencies were designed primarily for the Zaire ebolavirus species. International health organizations and medical groups have been working alongside Congolese authorities as the number of infections continues to rise, while experimental approaches targeting the Bundibugyo strain remain under development.

The current case and death totals were released by the Democratic Republic of Congo's communications ministry and independently reported by multiple international health and news organizations. The milestone of more than 6,000 confirmed cases establishes this outbreak as larger than any previous Ebola epidemic recorded within the country, although the 2014-2016 West Africa Ebola epidemic remains the deadliest Ebola outbreak globally.
