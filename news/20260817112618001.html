Xi Marks Jiang Zemin Centenary With Call For 'Fighting Spirit'

Chinese leader links Party unity to stability amid global tensions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 4:17 PM ET

Chinese President Xi Jinping used a ceremony marking the 100th anniversary of former leader Jiang Zemin's birth to urge Communist Party officials to maintain what he called an "indomitable fighting spirit" as China faces mounting economic challenges and geopolitical pressure. Speaking at Beijing's Great Hall of the People on August 17, Xi said strong Party leadership remains essential to China's long-term stability and development.

During the address, Xi praised Jiang's leadership following the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests, describing the government's response as necessary to preserve national stability. The remarks represented a rare public reaffirmation of the Chinese Communist Party's longstanding position on one of the country's most politically sensitive events. Xi also called on officials to prepare for what he described as future "storms" by strengthening technological self-reliance, Party discipline, and national resilience.

The speech came days after the death of former Premier Zhu Rongji, who served alongside Jiang during a period of major economic reforms that included China's entry into the World Trade Organization. While acknowledging that era, Xi emphasized a governance model centered on stronger state leadership, political unity, and strategic independence in response to current domestic and international challenges.

Analysts view the address as an indication that Beijing intends to continue prioritizing political stability and centralized Party control while navigating slower economic growth and heightened competition with Western nations. The remarks also reinforce the leadership's effort to frame historical events as support for its current policy direction.
