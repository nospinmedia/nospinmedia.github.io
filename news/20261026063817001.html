María Corina Machado returns to Venezuela after 10 months abroad

Opposition leader reaches Caracas as supporters call for new elections

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 26, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Venezuelan opposition leader and Nobel Peace Prize laureate María Corina Machado returned to Venezuela on October 25 after about 10 months abroad, traveling across the country to Caracas as thousands of supporters gathered along her route.

Machado first appeared publicly at a church in western Lara state, where people sang Venezuela's national anthem and shouted calls for freedom. She then traveled by road toward Caracas, a journey Reuters reported took about eight hours because of crowds. Thousands joined or waited for her caravan as it approached the capital Sunday night.

Machado had spent more than a year largely in hiding following Venezuela's disputed 2024 presidential election. Authorities barred her from running despite her victory in the opposition primary, and she instead backed former diplomat Edmundo González. Voting records collected by the opposition and reviewed by independent observers indicated González defeated then-President Nicolás Maduro, although Venezuela's electoral authorities declared Maduro the winner.

Machado secretly left Venezuela in December 2025 to travel to Norway, where she received the Nobel Peace Prize. She remained abroad as political conditions inside Venezuela changed dramatically. U.S. forces captured Maduro in January 2026, and acting President Delcy Rodríguez, a longtime Maduro ally, has governed since then while working with the Trump administration.

Machado and her allies are now pressing for a new presidential election. No election date has been established. Her advisers say she plans to meet families of political prisoners, victims of the deadly earthquakes that struck Venezuela in June, international representatives and leaders of the country's democratic movement.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said U.S.-supported reconciliation talks helped create conditions for Machado's safe return. President Trump, however, said Sunday that he did not know about her return and continued to speak favorably about Rodríguez's management of Venezuela.
