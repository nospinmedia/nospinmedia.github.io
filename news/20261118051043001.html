Ukraine collapses major span of Russia's Crimean Bridge

Strike comes days before Putin meets Trump and Xi

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 18, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Ukraine's attack on the Crimean Bridge collapsed a major section of the crossing into the Kerch Strait, with imagery indicating that a roughly 227-meter (745-foot) road arch span fell into the water after a multi-stage operation that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said took weeks to prepare.

The strike came shortly before Russian President Vladimir Putin is scheduled to meet President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China. The three leaders are scheduled to meet Thursday, giving the destruction of one of Russia's most politically symbolic infrastructure projects added significance immediately before Putin's high-profile appearance.

Zelenskyy said the final attack was the seventh stage of an operation in which Ukrainian forces first targeted Russian air defenses, radar and electronic-warfare systems protecting the area. He said Ukraine then used domestically produced weapons including Palianytsia drones against the road section and Neptune missiles, Ruta weapons and FP-5 Flamingo cruise missiles against the railway section.

Geolocated imagery showed the collapsed road section and damage to the parallel railway portion. Russian authorities suspended road and rail traffic following the attack before later restoring limited rail service on one track. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said the bridge would be rebuilt "even better than before."

The approximately 19-kilometer (12-mile) bridge is a major transportation link between Russia and occupied Crimea and has supported Russian military logistics. Damage to it increases Russia's dependence on overland supply routes through occupied southern Ukraine.

That alternative network was also hit. Russian regional officials reported a Ukrainian strike on a section of the Rostov-Crimea highway near the Mius River on the same night, further pressuring transportation routes serving occupied Crimea.

The long-term military effect remains uncertain, but the collapse represents substantially greater damage than the initial strikes NSM reported earlier this week.
