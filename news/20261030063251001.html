Alaska governor raises possibility of halting certification

State investigation examines same-name Senate candidate controversy

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 30, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Alaska Gov. Mike Dunleavy says an investigation into allegations surrounding the state's U.S. Senate race could potentially affect certification of the election, but stopped short of saying the state would refuse to certify a victory by Democratic candidate Mary Peltola.

Dunleavy, a Republican, ordered the Alaska Department of Public Safety to investigate whether state laws were violated in efforts to recruit Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a retired teacher who shares a name with Republican incumbent Sen. Dan Sullivan. The investigation followed reporting that people associated with Peltola's campaign and other Democratic operatives sought candidates who could potentially draw votes from Sullivan.

Asked Thursday whether Alaska would certify the election if Peltola wins while the investigation remains underway, Dunleavy said officials would follow the law and examine the findings. He said that if investigators uncover illegal conduct, it could affect whether the results are certified or whether someone is ultimately seated in the Senate.

Peltola has denied knowing about efforts to recruit Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. and has characterized the controversy as a political distraction. Her campaign has denied involvement with either Sullivan campaign. Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. has also denied running at Democrats' direction.

The controversy does not mean Sullivan Jr.'s candidacy is illegal. An Alaska elections official initially sought to keep him off the ballot, concluding his candidacy was intended to confuse voters, but the Alaska Supreme Court ruled that he qualified to run. On the ballot, incumbent Dan S. Sullivan is identified as a Republican and incumbent, while Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. appears without a party affiliation.

The state investigation is separate from an expanding Justice Department investigation and other inquiries surrounding the race. No criminal charges have been announced, and it remains unclear whether investigators will determine that any election laws were violated.
