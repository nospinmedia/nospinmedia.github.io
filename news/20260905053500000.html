AfD leads ahead of Saxony-Anhalt state election

Germany's political firewall faces a major test Sunday

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Voters in the eastern German state of Saxony-Anhalt head to the polls on September 6 with the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) holding a commanding lead in surveys, creating the possibility that the party could take control of a German state government for the first time. About 1.7 million eligible voters will choose a new state parliament, with the outcome also being watched as a test of Germany's longstanding refusal by other major parties to form governing coalitions with the AfD.

The AfD's lead candidate, Ulrich Siegmund, has built a substantial following through social media while campaigning heavily on immigration, public safety and dissatisfaction with Germany's political establishment. Polling ahead of the election has placed the AfD at roughly 40%, well ahead of the governing Christian Democratic Union (CDU). The CDU is led in the state election by Minister-President Sven Schulze, who has rejected cooperation with the AfD.

Winning the largest share of votes would not automatically give the AfD control of the state government. Saxony-Anhalt uses proportional representation, and the ability to elect a minister-president depends on the distribution of seats and whether a governing majority can be assembled. Germany's established parties have maintained a political "firewall," known as the Brandmauer, against entering coalitions with the AfD. That position could leave the CDU and other parties attempting to construct an alternative governing coalition even if the AfD finishes first.

The election carries additional significance because Saxony-Anhalt's domestic intelligence service has classified the state's AfD organization as a confirmed right-wing extremist organization. Siegmund has rejected that characterization. The state has about 2.1 million residents and was part of communist East Germany before reunification in 1990, a region where the AfD has developed some of its strongest electoral support.

The result will also be closely watched nationally as Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Germany's established parties assess the AfD's continuing growth in eastern Germany.
