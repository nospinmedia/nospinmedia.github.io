Congo Ebola Deaths Surpass 1,700 As Outbreak Accelerates

WHO fast-tracks Bundibugyo vaccine trials as hunger undermines response

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 5, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Ebola deaths in the Democratic Republic of the Congo climbed to 1,707 as of August 4, 2026, with 3,802 confirmed cases since the outbreak was declared on May 15, 2026. Health officials say transmission is intensifying across eastern provinces, with Ituri remaining the epicenter and infections confirmed in additional areas including Kisangani in Tshopo. Authorities and responders caution the outbreak has not yet peaked.

Dominant drivers of spread include untraced community transmission and insecurity. The Africa CDC’s Dr. Jean Kaseya said roughly 80% of new infections are arising outside established contact chains following his visit to Bunia on August 4. Response capacity has been further strained by intermittent attacks on care sites and by wage disputes among healthcare workers in hard‑hit hubs such as Mongbwalu and Bunia. The WHO’s Dr. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus arrived in Kinshasa on August 4 to help coordinate operations.

The outbreak is driven by the rare Bundibugyo species of Ebola, for which there are currently no approved vaccines or therapeutics. In parallel with field operations, the WHO has accelerated work on candidate tools: two post‑exposure prophylaxis drug trials have begun in Ituri, and vaccine candidates are moving into expedited evaluation abroad. Officials emphasize that standard measures—rapid isolation, safe burials, and rigorous infection prevention—remain essential while trials proceed.

Acute hunger is complicating containment. The World Food Programme warns that movement restrictions, market disruptions, and conflict have deepened food insecurity in outbreak zones, with Ituri among the worst affected. WFP has scaled up hot meals for patients and contacts and is seeking additional funding to sustain airlifts and logistics that keep treatment centers supplied. Earlier snapshots from international health agencies show lower case totals because they reflect earlier reporting windows; today’s figures reflect the latest national data consolidated by reporters on the ground.
