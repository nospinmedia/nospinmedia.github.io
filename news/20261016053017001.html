U.S. Producer Prices Jump Most in 4½ Years

Gasoline and Energy Costs Drive Sharp September Increase

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 16, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

U.S. producer prices rose 1.3% in September, the largest monthly increase in 4½ years, as sharply higher gasoline and other energy costs pushed up prices businesses receive for goods and services. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics said the Producer Price Index for final demand increased at its fastest monthly pace since March 2022.

The September increase followed gains of 0.5% in August and 0.2% in July. Over the 12 months through September, producer prices climbed 6.3%, showing substantially stronger wholesale inflation pressures as elevated energy costs worked through the economy.

Energy was a major driver. Prices for final-demand goods increased 2.6% during September, while final-demand services rose 0.8%. Wholesale gasoline prices surged 20.3%, while diesel fuel prices increased about 21%, according to the government's data. Higher transportation and other service costs also contributed to the overall increase.

The producer-price report arrived one day after separate government data showed consumer inflation also accelerated in September. The Consumer Price Index increased 0.6% for the month and 3.7% from a year earlier, with gasoline accounting for more than half of the monthly increase. Together, the reports show energy prices putting renewed upward pressure on both business and household costs.

Separate Commerce Department data released Thursday showed U.S. retail sales increased 0.8% in September, but much of the increase reflected consumers paying more for gasoline rather than a comparable increase in the amount of goods purchased. Economists cited by Reuters said underlying consumer spending appeared softer once the effects of higher fuel prices were removed.

The latest inflation figures add another consideration for the Federal Reserve as policymakers determine whether additional interest-rate increases are needed to contain inflation without unnecessarily weakening economic growth and employment.
