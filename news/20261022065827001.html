Trump administration asks Supreme Court to restart Big Bend border wall

Emergency appeal seeks to lift injunction blocking construction across West Texas

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 22, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The Trump administration asked the U.S. Supreme Court on October 21 to allow border wall construction to resume across the Big Bend region of West Texas, challenging a federal judge's injunction that halted the work.

The Justice Department filed an emergency application asking the justices to suspend an October 2 preliminary injunction issued by U.S. District Judge Kathleen Cardone. Her order bars the federal government from building border barriers or conducting ground-disturbing construction in the Big Bend Sector unless it complies with federal laws that the Department of Homeland Security had attempted to waive.

The dispute centers on federal authority under the Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act of 1996. DHS invoked that law to waive dozens of legal requirements, primarily environmental protections, to accelerate construction across a roughly 517-mile section of the U.S.-Mexico border that includes Big Bend National Park, Big Bend Ranch State Park and surrounding public and private land.

Cardone concluded that the challengers were likely to succeed in arguing that DHS exceeded its authority. Her ruling noted evidence that the Big Bend Sector has the lowest rate of illegal entry along the U.S.-Mexico border, despite the government's use of a statutory provision authorizing waivers for areas of high illegal entry.

The administration argues that Congress deliberately gave the DHS secretary broad authority to waive legal requirements that could delay construction of border barriers. Its Supreme Court filing says the injunction improperly overrides that authority and prevents the government from carrying out border-security projects that it considers urgently necessary.

The lawsuit was brought by the Center for Biological Diversity, Friends of the Ruidosa Church and Big Bend river guide Danny William Miller Jr. They argue the projects threaten wildlife, public lands, private interests and a historic adobe church near the border.

The Supreme Court has not yet ruled on the emergency request. Granting it would allow construction to resume while the underlying legal challenge continues; it would not resolve the ultimate legality of the administration's waiver authority.
