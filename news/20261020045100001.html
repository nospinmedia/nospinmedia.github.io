Trump points to France riots in midterm warning to Americans

President says similar unrest could happen in U.S. if Democrats win

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 20, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

President Trump is pointing to the violent unrest in France as a warning to American voters ahead of the November midterm elections, arguing that similar turmoil could reach the United States if Democrats gain power.

Speaking at a campaign rally in Kansas, Trump blamed France's problems on immigration and left-wing political movements and argued that the United States could face similar consequences. He said his administration would not allow what is happening in France to happen in the United States and urged voters to support Republican candidates in the midterms.

Trump's explanation for the French unrest is disputed. The demonstrations began with student protests over deteriorating schools and shortages of teachers before expanding amid broader concerns about the cost of living and fuel prices. French authorities have blamed much of the subsequent rioting, looting and arson on ultra-left groups and criminal elements. French officials have not attributed the unrest to immigrants.

The argument is becoming a broader Republican midterm message rather than a statement from Trump alone. House Speaker Mike Johnson has called the French riots a warning about what could happen if Democrats win congressional power, while Vice President JD Vance has also pointed to the unrest while criticizing Democratic immigration policies.

France has experienced some of its worst unrest in years, with more than 2,400 arrests following widespread violence and looting. Authorities have responded with localized curfews in Paris and several other cities. The unrest grew out of demonstrations involving hundreds of thousands of people, most of whom protested peacefully before violence erupted in several locations.

Trump's remarks show how the turmoil in France has now entered the U.S. midterm campaign, with Republicans using the events to make arguments about immigration, public safety and Democratic policies.
