FBI warns ShinyHunters after alleged leader arrested

Group claimed massive breach of FBI employee data

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 30, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The FBI has issued a public warning to members of the cybercrime group ShinyHunters after Dutch authorities arrested a 24-year-old Amsterdam man described by the bureau as one of the group's alleged leaders. FBI Cyber Division Assistant Director Brett Leatherman addressed remaining members directly on September 29, telling them that investigators are continuing to pursue the group following the arrest.

Dutch police arrested the suspect on September 15 on suspicion of involvement with ShinyHunters. A Rotterdam court has ordered him held for another 90 days while investigators examine seized data-storage devices. The suspect is separately accused of attempting to solicit two murders abroad after investigators said they discovered related information on his laptop. Police emphasized that those allegations are separate from the ShinyHunters investigation.

The arrest comes amid an investigation into a major breach of FBIJobs.gov. ShinyHunters claimed it accessed the FBI's recruitment systems through Oracle PeopleSoft and stole sensitive information about FBI personnel and job applicants. The group has said it exploited a PeopleSoft vulnerability, but the precise entry point used in the FBI intrusion has not been publicly established by the bureau. Security researchers have separately documented ShinyHunters exploiting CVE-2026-35273 against PeopleSoft systems. The FBI has confirmed unauthorized activity affecting FBIJobs.gov but has not publicly validated all of the hackers' technical claims or the full amount of data they say was taken.

ShinyHunters says the FBI attack was not motivated by a ransom demand. Instead, the group demanded that the bureau correct or remove a May 15 Public Service Announcement describing ShinyHunters as a cybercriminal group specializing in large-scale data breaches and extortion and detailing harassment tactics associated with its operations. The hackers set a September 30 deadline, although they later characterized the ultimatum as a publicity campaign and said they did not intend to release the claimed FBI data when the deadline expired.

The FBI says ShinyHunters and associated hackers have allegedly breached more than 140 organizations since last year and collected at least $70 million in extortion payments.
