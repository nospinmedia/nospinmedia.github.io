Fired Stars and Stripes leaders sue Pentagon over dismissals

Lawsuit alleges retaliation and First Amendment violations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 28, 2026 at 2:54 PM ET

Three former senior leaders of Stars and Stripes filed a federal lawsuit against the Department of Defense, alleging they were unlawfully dismissed in retaliation for defending the editorial independence of the congressionally authorized military news organization. Former publisher Max Lederer, editor-in-chief Erik Slavin, and Middle East correspondent Lara Korte filed the lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell, and senior defense aide Andrew Brey, seeking an injunction that would block their terminations while the case proceeds.

The lawsuit alleges the Pentagon violated the First Amendment and federal administrative law by retaliating against the three employees after they publicly defended the newsroom's editorial independence and following investigative reporting on supply shortages and mental health concerns aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln. According to the complaint, Pentagon officials directed Lederer to dismiss Slavin and Korte. When he refused and instead submitted retirement paperwork, the lawsuit says all three were fired within days. The Defense Department has said the dismissals were based on insubordination.

The plaintiffs argue the stated reason for their terminations was a pretext intended to punish protected speech and interfere with the newsroom's editorial independence. The complaint contends that allowing political officials to remove journalists over their reporting would undermine the publication's long-standing mission of providing independent news to U.S. service members around the world. The lawsuit seeks reinstatement, declaratory relief and an order preventing the Pentagon from enforcing the dismissals.

Stars and Stripes has operated for decades as an independent news organization serving the U.S. military while receiving congressional funding. The case is expected to become a closely watched test of the constitutional protections afforded to journalists working for federally supported news organizations. The Department of Justice is expected to defend the administration's actions as the litigation moves forward.
