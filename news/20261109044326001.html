Nvidia opens independent probe involving CEO Jensen Huang

Board examines whether foreign intelligence targeted him

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 9, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Nvidia has launched an independent investigation into whether CEO and co-founder Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation after he acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife amid reports linking the relationship to a woman suspected by U.S. authorities of working for Chinese intelligence.

The company's board said a committee of independent directors, assisted by outside legal counsel, will examine the available information. Nvidia said the board currently has no reason to believe U.S. national security information or Nvidia trade secrets were compromised. Huang supports the investigation and has committed to cooperating, while remaining CEO during the review.

The inquiry follows reporting about Alice Huang, a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed International, an Nvidia customer. U.S. authorities investigated her and concluded she was a Chinese intelligence asset, according to reporting cited by Reuters. Megaspeed has separately faced U.S. scrutiny over suspicions that it helped China obtain advanced artificial-intelligence chips restricted from export. Jensen Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife in a message to Nvidia employees and took responsibility for his conduct.

The controversy has also reached Congress. Republican leaders of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, House Intelligence Committee and House Select Committee on China previously requested that Huang provide a closed-door briefing on November 18 concerning the allegations and Nvidia's safeguards against foreign espionage. Nvidia has said executives routinely interact with customers worldwide and that Asian cloud customers receive ordinary business information rather than sensitive company or U.S. national-security information.

The investigation comes at a sensitive time for Nvidia, whose chips are central to the global artificial-intelligence industry and whose ability to sell advanced processors in China has been heavily shaped by U.S. export restrictions. China, meanwhile, rejected the espionage allegations Monday, calling them unsubstantiated and a smear campaign.
