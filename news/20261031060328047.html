Judge opens misconduct inquiry in Trump golf course case

DOJ lawyers face scrutiny over statements about D.C. renovation plans

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 31, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

A federal judge has opened a formal inquiry into whether Justice Department and other government lawyers misled the court about the Trump administration's plans to renovate East Potomac Golf Links, a historic public golf course in Washington, D.C.

U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes announced the inquiry Friday after questioning Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward Jr., the Justice Department's third-highest-ranking official, about apparent conflicts between statements made in court and the administration's actions. Reyes said the inquiry will examine whether officials made misrepresentations, withheld relevant information or otherwise violated professional ethics rules. She emphasized that opening the inquiry does not mean misconduct has been established.

The dispute centers on how far the administration's plans had progressed while government attorneys maintained that no final renovation plan existed. President Trump publicly announced plans to transform the municipal course into a championship-level facility and said it would close November 15. The government subsequently acknowledged that a design by golf architect Tom Fazio was being prepared for review by the National Capital Planning Commission. Woodward told the court Friday that work on the submission had been underway for several weeks.

That timeline drew scrutiny because government lawyers had previously told the court that the administration did not have a finalized plan. Reyes questioned Woodward for more than two hours and expressed concern that the court may have received materially misleading or incomplete information.

Reyes ordered government officials and attorneys to preserve relevant documents and communications while the inquiry proceeds. She also said a special master could assist in investigating the conduct, although the government will have an opportunity to object to the appointment.

The underlying lawsuit was filed by the D.C. Preservation League and two local golfers, who are challenging the administration's redevelopment plans. Reyes has separately barred additional tree removal at the course while that litigation continues.
