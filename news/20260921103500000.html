Italy plans school ban on burqas and niqabs

Government will also set legal cap on foreign students per class

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 21, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni says her government will move to ban burqas and niqabs in schools and establish a legal maximum for the number of foreign students allowed in each classroom, expanding Italy's policies aimed at the integration of immigrant families.

Meloni announced the planned measures on September 20 at an annual gathering of the youth wing of her Brothers of Italy party. She said a proposal would soon be presented to the Cabinet that would establish the classroom limit, require parents of foreign students experiencing serious integration difficulties to learn Italian, and prohibit burqas and niqabs in schools. Meloni did not specify what the new maximum number of foreign students would be.

Students who are not Italian citizens currently account for about 11.6% of enrollment in Italian schools, or nearly 12 out of every 100 students. Meloni argued that students who do not speak Italian can integrate more easily when surrounded by classmates and teachers who speak the language, but said integration becomes more difficult when large numbers of students in the same classroom cannot speak Italian.

Italy already restricts face coverings under a 1975 law that prohibits clothing or other items that prevent identification without a justified reason. A 2008 ruling clarified that full-face coverings may be worn for religious or cultural reasons provided the wearer reveals their face when identification is required. Italy also already has a 30% limit on foreign students per class and school, although Meloni did not explain how the newly proposed legal cap would differ from that existing policy.

The measures have not yet become law. The government must first approve the changes, after which parliamentary approval would be required. The proposals arrive as immigration and integration remain major political issues in Italy ahead of the country's 2027 general election.
