President Trump Invokes Executive Privilege in Kennedy Center Lawsuit

Claim Seeks to Shield White House Communications About Center's Management and Closure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 16, 2026 at 6:52 PM ET

President Trump has formally invoked executive privilege to withhold certain records and communications sought by Democratic Rep. Joyce Beatty of Ohio in her federal lawsuit challenging decisions involving the Kennedy Center, including its planned two-year closure for renovations.

White House Deputy Counsel Joshua Hoyt informed a federal judge that Trump was asserting the presidential communications privilege, a form of executive privilege, over information involving the president, his advisers and Kennedy Center employees. The administration argues the communications concern Trump's official presidential responsibilities and the management and operation of the performing arts institution.

Trump is also chairman of the Kennedy Center's Board of Trustees, making the dispute unusual. The administration argues that the president has statutory responsibilities involving the center, including appointing members of its board and its Advisory Committee on the Arts, and therefore needs confidential advice about its management, finances, fundraising and activities.

Beatty, an ex officio member of the board, argues that the privilege should not prevent her from obtaining information about decisions Trump made as the center's chairman. Her attorneys have asked U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper to compel production of the disputed material. The judge has not yet ruled on whether Trump's privilege claim applies to the requested communications.

The dispute intensified during a deposition of Kennedy Center Executive Director and Chief Operating Officer Matthew Floca, when a Justice Department attorney repeatedly instructed him not to answer questions about communications involving Trump or the White House. Beatty is seeking information about the center's proposed closure, possible demolition and other management decisions.

The lawsuit also challenges changes made to the Kennedy Center after Trump became chairman. In May 2026, Cooper blocked the planned closure and ordered Trump's name removed from the building's facade. The administration is continuing to defend its management decisions in court.

Executive privilege is not absolute, and courts can require disclosure when the need for information outweighs the government's confidentiality interests. The unresolved question is whether that protection extends to the communications at issue in Trump's dual roles as president and Kennedy Center chairman.
