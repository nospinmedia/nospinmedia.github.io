Russia Strikes Ukrainian Power Plants After Trump Announces Ceasefire

Zelenskyy Reports New Attacks as Moscow Has Yet to Confirm Energy Truce

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 12, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Russian forces struck Ukrainian power infrastructure on October 11, 2026, hours after President Trump announced that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to immediately stop attacking each other's energy facilities. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed an attack on a power plant in Kharkiv and reported additional strikes on energy infrastructure, raising serious questions about whether the announced ceasefire had actually taken effect.

Trump announced the agreement Sunday morning, declaring that both countries had accepted an immediate halt to attacks on energy infrastructure. However, the announcement surprised Ukrainian officials, who said they had not received details of a finalized agreement. Russia had not publicly confirmed the arrangement when the attacks were reported.

Later Sunday, Zelenskyy reported that Russian forces had attacked Kharkiv's CHP-5 combined heat and power plant, a facility that produces electricity and supplies heat. Ukrainian officials also reported a drone attack on a power station in Kyiv, causing electricity outages in several districts. A local official told ABC News that the Kharkiv facility had been struck twice and was burning.

Zelenskyy said Russian drones remained active over Ukraine throughout the day, alongside glide bomb attacks and additional strikes targeting energy infrastructure. He emphasized that Ukraine supported an agreement to stop attacks on energy facilities but expected Russia to demonstrate the same commitment.

The dispute comes amid growing tensions between Washington and Kyiv over Ukrainian strikes against Russian oil refineries. Trump has criticized those operations, arguing that they contribute to rising diesel prices. His administration recently announced an agreement allowing Russian diesel supplies into international markets, drawing objections from Ukrainian officials and criticism from some American lawmakers.

Ukraine has maintained that attacks on Russian energy facilities are intended to weaken Moscow's ability to finance and sustain its military operations. Zelenskyy has offered to suspend those strikes if Russia reciprocates by ending attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure.

The latest attacks underscore the difference between announcing a ceasefire and establishing one that both governments acknowledge and enforce. Without confirmed terms, implementation procedures, and compliance from both sides, the status of Trump's proposed agreement remains uncertain.
