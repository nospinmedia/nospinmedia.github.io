Meta agrees to up to $18B settlement over teen social media claims

Instagram and Facebook will face mandatory youth safety limits

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 27, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Meta Platforms has agreed to a proposed settlement worth up to $18 billion and sweeping new child-safety restrictions across Instagram and Facebook, resolving claims brought by 48 states, the District of Columbia and U.S. territories. Announced on August 26, the agreement ended a federal trial in Oakland, California, where states alleged Meta designed platform features that encouraged excessive use by young people and improperly collected personal data from children under 13. The settlement still requires final court approval.

The agreement introduces mandatory protections for users under 18, including a default two-hour daily limit across Instagram and Facebook that can only be disabled with parental permission. Teen accounts will also be blocked from using the apps between midnight and 6 a.m., receive no push notifications during weekday school hours, and encounter mandatory 15-minute, 60-minute and 90-minute Productive Pause reminders before reaching the daily limit. Meta must also provide non-personalized feed options within four months, implement broader platform safeguards within six months, and deploy expanded age-assurance technology within one year. Additional measures include stronger age verification, reduced visibility of like counts, restrictions on certain appearance-altering filters and expanded controls over algorithmic recommendations.

The financial package consists of a guaranteed $12.7 billion paid over 10 years, with an additional $5.3 billion contingent on competing platforms, including TikTok and YouTube, adopting comparable youth-safety safeguards and contributing matching payments. If those conditions are met, the total settlement value could reach up to $18 billion. California is expected to receive the largest share, approximately $2.2 billion over the payment period. U.S. District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers welcomed the agreement during court proceedings and indicated she expects to approve the proposed consent decree after completing her review. An independent compliance monitor will oversee Meta's implementation of the required safeguards for up to a decade.

Florida declined to join the settlement after Attorney General James Uthmeier argued the penalties were insufficient, while New Mexico was excluded after securing a separate $942 million judgment against Meta earlier in 2026. Meta denied wrongdoing and said the agreement builds on safety measures already introduced for teen users while resolving years of multistate litigation.
