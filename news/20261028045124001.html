Rare Ukraine-Russia truce holds around starving occupied town

Aid and evacuations planned for civilians trapped in Oleshky

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 28, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A rare localized ceasefire between Ukraine and Russia appeared to be holding around the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky as preparations began to deliver humanitarian aid and evacuate civilians facing severe shortages of food, water and medicine.

The truce took effect at 3 a.m. Ukrainian time on October 27 and is scheduled to run through November 1. Both sides reported no violations during its first day. The agreement was brokered through U.S. First Lady Melania Trump, with involvement from Pope Leo XIV, and is intended to provide safe passage for humanitarian convoys and residents who want to leave.

About 1,700 to 2,000 people are believed to remain in Oleshky, compared with a prewar population of roughly 24,000. Many of those still there are elderly or ill. Residents have endured shortages of electricity, gas, heating, clean water and medicine, while some have survived on small amounts of rice or buckwheat supplemented with vegetables or wild plants.

Oleshky, across the Dnipro River from the city of Kherson, has been under Russian occupation since 2022. The town was also devastated by flooding following the 2023 destruction of the Kakhovka Dam. Mines and continued fighting have made routes into and out of the area dangerous, and officials said demining could take several days before humanitarian operations are fully underway.

The ceasefire does not represent a broader halt to the war. Fighting and strikes continued elsewhere in Ukraine while the Oleshky agreement was in effect. Ukrainian negotiator David Arakhamia described the arrangement as the biggest de-escalation since 2022 and said a successful operation could help build confidence for additional agreements.
