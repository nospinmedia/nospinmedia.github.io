Venezuela's interim leader promises elections but sets no date

Delcy Rodríguez tells UN country is moving toward 'full democracy'

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 25, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Venezuela's interim President Delcy Rodríguez told the United Nations General Assembly that the country will hold elections as part of a transition toward what she called "full democracy," but she did not announce when Venezuelans will vote.

"Elections will be held in Venezuela," Rodríguez told the General Assembly on September 23. She said her government has opened a political dialogue with opposition groups aimed at establishing equal conditions and said Venezuelans would determine the appropriate time for an election. Rodríguez also said the country's institutions would be prepared to provide an inclusive and transparent process when voting takes place.

Rodríguez became interim president after U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro in January 2026 and brought him to the United States to face federal criminal charges. Her appearance in New York was the first U.S. trip by a Venezuelan head of state since 2018 and came as Washington and Caracas rapidly rebuild relations after years of hostility.

The election pledge followed Rodríguez's first face-to-face meeting with President Trump on the sidelines of the UN gathering. Trump told Rodríguez that Venezuela ultimately needs to hold elections but did not demand a specific date, according to U.S. officials familiar with their roughly 15-minute meeting. The two governments have also been discussing Venezuela's massive sovereign debt along with cooperation involving energy, mining and security.

The timing of an election remains a central unresolved issue. U.S. officials have described plans that include changes to Venezuela's Supreme Court and electoral authorities before setting a voting date. Critics of the developing U.S.-Venezuela relationship have argued that Washington is placing too much emphasis on oil and investment agreements while allowing elections to be delayed.

Rodríguez said rebuilding Venezuela would not be easy but portrayed the country's renewed international engagement as the beginning of a new period following years of political and economic turmoil.
