Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns after 33 years

Colette Kress takes over as interim CEO as Congress seeks answers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 13, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Nvidia co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12 after 33 years leading the chipmaker, ending the tenure of one of the most influential executives in the global artificial intelligence industry. Nvidia appointed longtime Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO and named Harvey C. Jones as the company's first chair of the board.

Huang's departure follows scrutiny over reported personal relationships, including an alleged affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed who has been suspected by U.S. intelligence of working for Beijing. Attorney General Todd Blanche said November 12 that the Justice Department has not reached a conclusion on whether Alice Huang is a Chinese spy, while Beijing has denied the allegation. Separate reporting has alleged Huang also had relationships with two Nvidia employees.

The resignation initially sent Nvidia shares down more than 13%, temporarily wiping roughly $500 billion to $600 billion from the company's market value. Shares later recovered much of that decline, closing 4.3% lower at $166.19, representing a closing market-value loss of roughly $182 billion.

Congressional scrutiny is continuing. Senate Banking Committee Chair Tim Scott requested Huang's testimony for December 3 and said he would consider a subpoena if Huang does not appear voluntarily. House committee chairs Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford requested a closed-door transcribed interview with Huang for December 1. They also requested an interview with Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter for December 2 and testimony from Under Secretary of Commerce Jeffrey Kessler on December 3. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has agreed to testify voluntarily before the Senate Banking Committee.

President Trump said he spoke with Huang on Wednesday and indicated his assessment of the departure would depend on whether the espionage allegations are substantiated. Nvidia now faces its first major leadership transition since Huang co-founded the company in 1993 while federal and congressional scrutiny continues.
