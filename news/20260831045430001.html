U.S. Strikes Iranian Launchers on Larak Island

Iran Fires Missiles at U.S. Bases in Jordan

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 31, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

U.S. forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on August 30, marking the first confirmed American military action against Iran in about a month. U.S. Central Command spokesperson Tim Hawkins said personnel from Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps had been observed preparing rockets carrying sea mines for launch into the strait. CENTCOM described the operation as a limited action against forces it said posed an imminent threat to civilian mariners and commercial shipping.

Iran reported deaths and injuries from the strike and retaliated early August 31 by firing ballistic missiles toward U.S. military sites in Jordan. Jordan's armed forces said air defenses intercepted eight missiles that entered Jordanian airspace. Iranian media said the attacks targeted bases used to support the U.S. operation against Larak Island. Iran also claimed it attacked Al Minhad Air Base in the United Arab Emirates, but the UAE Defense Ministry denied that the base had been struck. The UAE separately confirmed that its air force intercepted an Iranian-origin drone over its territorial waters.

The renewed fighting ended a period of relative military calm that followed the last confirmed U.S. strikes inside Iran on July 29. On August 1, President Trump said additional strikes would be paused after appeals from Gulf allies. The latest confrontation comes only days after the U.S. military said it had finished clearing sea mines from international shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz.

The waterway remains one of the world's most important energy corridors, normally carrying about 20% of global oil. Commercial traffic is still far below prewar levels, and oil prices rose after the renewed U.S.-Iran confrontation. Iranian state television also reported that a supertanker caught fire after striking two naval mines in the southern strait, but provided no identifying information about the vessel or crew. Separately, maritime authorities reported another tanker had been struck by an unidentified projectile over the weekend without casualties.
