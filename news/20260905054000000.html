UN backs maps that show continents at their true relative sizes

164–1 vote promotes Equal Earth over Mercator projection

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 2:50 PM ET

The United Nations General Assembly voted 164–1 on September 4 for a nonbinding resolution encouraging wider use of world maps that accurately represent the relative sizes of continents, particularly Africa. The United States cast the only opposing vote and six countries abstained from the African-led measure, which promotes equal-area projections such as the Equal Earth design as alternatives to the centuries-old Mercator projection.

The resolution, titled Correct the Map: Rebalancing Global Cartographic Representation and Promoting Equitable Representation of the World's Regions, Particularly Africa, was championed by Togo on behalf of African countries and supported by the African Union. It encourages governments, international institutions, educational systems, publishers and digital platforms to progressively use cartographic representations that more accurately reflect actual land areas. Because the resolution is nonbinding, countries and organizations are not prohibited from continuing to use Mercator maps.

The Mercator projection, developed by Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator in 1569, was designed primarily for navigation. Its geometry preserves directions but increasingly enlarges land areas toward the poles, substantially distorting their relative sizes. One frequently cited example is Greenland, which can appear comparable in size to Africa on a Mercator map even though Africa is approximately 14 times larger.

The campaign promotes the Equal Earth projection, introduced in 2018, which preserves the relative area of countries and continents while presenting the world in a familiar-looking format. Supporters argue that more proportional maps would improve geographic understanding and counter longstanding misconceptions about Africa's physical scale. The resolution does not challenge continued use of Mercator maps for maritime and aerial navigation, where their properties remain useful.

The African Union Commission welcomed the vote and said it would work with Togo and other partners to encourage implementation. Togo is also planning discussions with international organizations and technology companies about expanding use of proportional world maps.
