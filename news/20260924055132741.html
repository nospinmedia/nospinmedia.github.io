U.S. and China extend trade truce through January

Agreement announced as Xi arrives for White House summit

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 24, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

The United States and China have agreed to extend their trade truce by two months, pushing a looming November 10 deadline to January 10, 2027, as Chinese President Xi Jinping arrived in Washington for a state visit and summit with President Trump.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced the extension after meeting Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng in Washington on September 23. Bessent said the additional two months would give the countries more time to pursue a broader economic agreement rather than continuing with a series of smaller deals. He said it remains uncertain whether such a larger agreement can be completed by January.

The existing truce grew out of an agreement reached by Trump and Xi in Busan, South Korea, in October 2025. It halted further escalation in the trade dispute and included reductions in some U.S. tariffs on Chinese imports, Chinese commitments involving purchases of American agricultural products and measures affecting exports of rare earths and other critical minerals. Those materials remain an important issue because they are widely used in electronics, vehicles and defense technology.

Xi and his wife, Peng Liyuan, arrived at Joint Base Andrews in Maryland on Wednesday evening, where Trump and first lady Melania Trump personally greeted them. Xi's visit is expected to include discussions on trade, technology, artificial intelligence, Taiwan and China's relationship with Iran.

The extension removes an immediate deadline but does not resolve the larger economic disputes between the two countries. U.S. officials have continued to raise concerns about China's trade practices and supplies of critical minerals, while Beijing has objected to American tariffs and technology restrictions. The two governments are also discussing cooperation on AI safety and communication procedures for serious AI-related incidents.

The trade announcement represents a significant new development from earlier talks that had left the future of the truce unresolved, with negotiations now continuing under the new January deadline.
