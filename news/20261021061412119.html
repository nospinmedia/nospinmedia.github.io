U.S. pending home sales fall to record low

Contracts plunge 7.3% as rising mortgage rates squeeze buyers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 21, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Contracts to buy previously owned U.S. homes fell sharply in September, sending pending home sales to their lowest level since the National Association of Realtors began tracking the measure in 2001.

The Pending Home Sales Index dropped 7.3% from August to 66.0, substantially worse than the 0.5% decline economists surveyed by Reuters had expected. Contract activity was also 11.3% lower than a year earlier, and pending sales declined in all four major U.S. regions.

The West recorded the largest monthly decline at 9.2%, followed by the South. The Midwest and Northeast also posted decreases. Pending sales are based on signed contracts for existing homes and typically provide an indication of completed sales one or two months later.

The decline coincided with another increase in borrowing costs. The average rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage reached 7.40% in mid-October, according to Freddie Mac, the highest level in nearly three years. Higher mortgage rates increase monthly payments and reduce the amount prospective buyers can afford to borrow.

NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun attributed September's weakness primarily to rapidly rising mortgage rates. He said employment and income growth can support housing demand, but changes in mortgage rates have a larger effect on affordability. Yun also noted that relatively few distressed properties have helped keep home prices steady even as buyers and sellers hold back.

September's decline followed a 0.3% increase in August and marked the third monthly decrease in four months. The latest figures show that higher borrowing costs continue to limit activity even as more homes become available in parts of the country.
