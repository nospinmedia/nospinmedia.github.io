20.8 million Medicare enrollees to receive $90 rebate

Most payments expected around October 8

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 4, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The federal government will send a one-time $90 Medicare premium rebate to about 20.8 million Americans enrolled in Original Medicare Part B, with most eligible beneficiaries expected to receive the money by direct deposit on or around October 8. President Trump announced the payments, which will come from the Medicare Improvement Fund and are intended to offset part of beneficiaries' Part B premium costs.

Eligibility is narrower than the overall Medicare population. According to the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, recipients must be enrolled in Original Medicare Part B, live in the United States, not receive Medicaid assistance paying their Medicare premium and not pay the higher Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount, or IRMAA. People enrolled in Medicare Advantage are not eligible for the rebate.

Most eligible beneficiaries will receive the $90 automatically through direct deposit from the Social Security Administration. Those without direct deposit will instead receive a Treasury Department check later in October. CMS says beneficiaries do not need to apply. People who want to verify eligibility can contact 1-800-MEDICARE, while payment-status questions can be directed to the Social Security Administration beginning October 15.

The money comes from roughly $2 billion Congress allocated to the Medicare Improvement Fund, which was created in 2008 to finance improvements to the original fee-for-service Medicare program. The Trump administration says this is the first time the fund has been used to make direct payments intended to reduce beneficiaries' Medicare costs.

The payments arrive roughly a month before the November midterm elections, when control of Congress is at stake. The administration has announced other direct-payment initiatives this election season, while presenting the Medicare rebate as part of its broader effort to reduce healthcare costs.
