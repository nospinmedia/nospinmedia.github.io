China commits to at least 20 million metric tons of U.S. coal purchases

White House says shipments will span 2027 and 2028

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 27, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

China has committed to importing at least 10 million metric tons of U.S. coal in 2027 and another 10 million metric tons in 2028, according to the White House, creating a two-year purchase commitment of at least 20 million metric tons. The coal agreement was one of several economic measures announced following talks between President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping during Xi's visit to Washington.

The commitment was announced as the two countries moved to expand economic cooperation through the newly operationalized U.S.-China Board of Trade. The United States and China also reached consensus on recommendations for more favorable tariff treatment covering about $30 billion in non-sensitive goods from each country. U.S. products identified for potential preferential treatment include agricultural goods, seafood, timber, cosmetics and medical devices, while Chinese products include small appliances, toys, holiday decorations and children's car seats.

The coal purchase is one of the summit's more concrete commodity commitments. Bloomberg reported that China is expected to import at least 20 million metric tons of American coal across the two years if the commitment is carried out as described by the White House. The two governments also launched an agricultural market-access working group and established a Board of Investment to discuss investment opportunities and barriers facing companies in both countries.

The announcement does come with an important qualification: reporting on the summit indicates that China's own official account did not separately detail the coal commitment, while the White House fact sheet explicitly included the purchase amounts. The countries also continue to negotiate over rare earths and other critical minerals, areas where significant trade disputes remain unresolved.

The coal agreement follows other summit developments already announced, including the extension of the U.S.-China trade truce and plans for continued talks on advanced technology and artificial intelligence. Unlike those broader diplomatic initiatives, the coal provision establishes a specific quantity and timetable for future U.S. energy exports.
