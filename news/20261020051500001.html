DOJ charges 28 green card holders with election crimes in California

Federal prosecutors say most illegally cast ballots in U.S. elections

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 20, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

The Justice Department has charged 28 lawful permanent residents in California with federal election-related crimes, alleging they illegally registered to vote and, in most cases, cast ballots despite not being U.S. citizens.

Federal authorities arrested 11 of the defendants on October 19 as part of Operation Protect the Vote, an effort involving all four U.S. Attorney's Offices in California. The remaining defendants were also charged but were not among those arrested during Monday's operation.

All 28 defendants are lawful permanent residents, commonly known as green card holders. They are legally permitted to live and work in the United States but cannot vote in federal elections because they are not U.S. citizens.

The allegations vary by defendant. Prosecutors say some falsely identified themselves as U.S. citizens while registering to vote and later cast ballots. Others are accused of making false statements about their registration or voting histories while applying for U.S. citizenship. Charges include fraudulent voter registration, unlawful voting by a noncitizen and false statements in naturalization proceedings. Some are felony charges carrying maximum sentences of five years, while unlawful voting by a noncitizen can be charged as a misdemeanor carrying up to one year.

The charges also intersect with a broader dispute between the Trump administration and California over the state's voter rolls. The DOJ says each of the 28 defendants appeared on a list of more than 20,000 people federal officials identified as likely noncitizen registrants. The department previously asked California to set aside mail ballots associated with those individuals while their citizenship status was checked and sued after the state declined.

California officials dispute the administration's broader claims about the state's voter rolls and say there is no evidence of widespread noncitizen voting. The 28 criminal cases are accusations, and the defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty.
