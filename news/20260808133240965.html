Senate Passes Lindsey Graham Sanctions Bill 86–11

President Trump given up to 100% tariff power on top buyers of Russian energy

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 8, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

The Senate voted 86–11 on August 7, 2026, to pass the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, a bipartisan measure intended to increase economic pressure on Vladimir Putin, Russia's war effort, and Iran. The bill now moves to the House of Representatives. Under the legislation, President Trump would receive explicit authority to impose tariffs of up to 100% on imports from countries that are among the five largest purchasers of Russian oil or natural gas.

The measure also expands sanctions targeting Putin, senior Russian political and military officials, Russian financial institutions, energy projects, and the so-called "shadow fleet" of aging tankers used to evade existing restrictions. The tariff authority includes exemptions for countries importing less than 15% of their natural gas from Russia while taking verifiable steps to reduce those purchases. Nations most likely to be affected include China and India, two of the largest buyers of Russian crude. A national-interest waiver would allow the White House to suspend certain sanctions or tariffs after notifying Congress.

Debate focused largely on the bill's expanded tariff authority. An amendment by Sen. Rand Paul and Sen. Ron Wyden to remove that provision failed 32–64. Sen. Raphael Warnock lifted his hold on the legislation after receiving a written commitment from U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer outlining how the tariff authority would be applied and when it could be lifted. Supporters argue the measure strengthens pressure on Russia, while critics warn broad tariff powers could increase costs for American consumers.

The legislation caps a yearlong effort that began under the late Sen. Lindsey Graham. His sister, Sen. Darline Graham of South Carolina, who was appointed after his July death, voted in favor of the bill. The House is expected to consider the legislation later this month, where some lawmakers, including Rep. Gregory Meeks, have raised concerns about the breadth of the proposed tariff authority.
