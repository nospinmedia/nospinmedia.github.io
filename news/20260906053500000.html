Kennedy Center ceiling collapses after heavy rain

Incident renews push for two-year renovation shutdown

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 4:18 PM ET

A section of the ceiling in the Grand Foyer of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts collapsed Friday evening after heavy rain swept through Washington, D.C., prompting officials to temporarily close the building. Debris fell into the foyer connecting the center's major performance spaces, but no injuries were reported. Kennedy Center officials said the incident demonstrated the urgency of addressing maintenance problems at the 55-year-old performing arts complex.

The Kennedy Center attributed the collapse to longstanding deferred maintenance. Roma Daravi, the center's vice president for public relations, said the incident was another example of why the building needs extensive renovation. The center's board has already approved President Trump's plan to close the facility for approximately two years while major construction work is performed.

The proposed renovation has become part of a broader legal and political dispute over the federally established memorial to President John F. Kennedy. Congress has approved $257 million for work at the center, while the Trump administration is seeking additional private donations. Administration attorneys have argued in court that extensive investment is necessary to prevent further deterioration of the building. Opponents of the planned closure agree that repairs are needed but dispute whether the entire center must remain closed for two years.

The renovation dispute also involves Trump's name on the building. A federal district court previously ruled that the Kennedy Center board exceeded its authority when it added Trump's name and ordered it removed. The board subsequently voted to place an inscription on the facade reading that the center was restored and renovated by President Donald J. Trump, with installation planned by September 8. A second inscription would recognize the Trump Kennedy Center Fund if the renovation fund reaches $100 million.

The ceiling failure now adds a physical infrastructure problem to a dispute that had already involved the scope of renovations, funding, the duration of the proposed closure and changes to the national memorial's exterior.
