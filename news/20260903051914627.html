U.S. diesel prices climb to four-year high

Higher fuel costs pressure freight and farming

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The average U.S. retail price for diesel climbed to $5.69 per gallon on September 2, its highest level in about four years and within roughly 13 cents of the all-time national record set in June 2022, according to AAA. Diesel powers much of the nation's commercial trucking, rail, construction, agricultural equipment, and other heavy industries, making sustained price increases a concern for businesses that depend on transportation and freight movement.

Energy analysts attribute the increase to several overlapping supply pressures. Ongoing military tensions affecting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz have raised concerns about global oil flows, while damage to Russian refining capacity and export restrictions have reduced the availability of diesel and other refined products on world markets. At the same time, U.S. distillate inventories remain near their lowest seasonal levels in decades, leaving little cushion if additional supply disruptions occur.

The tight market has also driven wholesale diesel prices and refining margins higher. Analysts say refiners continue to face strong international demand for diesel exports, particularly from regions experiencing supply shortages, contributing to elevated prices in the United States despite stable domestic refining operations. Higher diesel prices typically affect transportation costs before filtering into the prices consumers pay for groceries, manufactured goods, agricultural products, and other items moved through national supply chains.

The price surge comes as many farmers enter the fall harvest season and freight companies prepare for increased shipping volumes ahead of the holiday retail period. Industry experts say diesel markets often remain volatile when inventories are limited because even modest supply disruptions can trigger significant price swings. Whether prices ease in the coming weeks will likely depend on global refining output, geopolitical developments affecting energy shipments, and changes in U.S. distillate inventories.
