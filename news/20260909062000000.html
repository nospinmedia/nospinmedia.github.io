Hurricane Lowell leaves widespread damage across western Hawaiʻi

Nearly all Kauaʻi utility customers lost power

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 9, 2026 at 2:49 PM ET

Hurricane Lowell battered western Hawaiʻi with damaging winds, torrential rain and powerful surf, leaving widespread destruction across Kauaʻi as crews began clearing fallen trees, debris and damaged roads. Nearly all of the approximately 35,000 customers served by the Kauaʻi Island Utility Cooperative lost electricity during the storm, making the outages one of the most significant new developments since Lowell first began threatening the islands.

Although Lowell's center remained offshore as it passed west of the Hawaiian Islands, its broad wind field brought tropical-storm and hurricane conditions to parts of the state. Gusts on Kauaʻi reached approximately 80 mph, while sustained winds at Lihue Airport exceeded 50 mph. Around 300 people sought refuge in shelters on Kauaʻi as officials urged residents to conserve water and avoid unnecessary travel while damage assessments continued.

The storm caused extensive damage to roads and property. Along Kauaʻi's southern coast, large waves tore apart sections of a coastal road and deposited sand and lava rocks into a beachfront park. Fallen trees and boulders blocked other roads, leaving some neighborhoods temporarily isolated. CBS News documented a waterfront home that had been lifted from its foundation and pushed across a street.

Heavy rainfall compounded the damage. Hanalei recorded 17.76 inches of rain, while some locations on Kauaʻi faced storm totals potentially reaching 24 inches. On Oʻahu, crews cleared sand and rocks from a major coastal highway after floodwaters crossed the roadway. Flash flooding and mudslides remained concerns even as Lowell weakened and moved away from the islands.

Gov. Josh Green had ordered schools and government offices closed on Kauaʻi, Niʻihau and Oʻahu ahead of the storm. Hurricane and tropical storm warnings were later discontinued as Lowell weakened, shifting the focus toward restoring electricity, clearing roads and assessing the full extent of the damage.
