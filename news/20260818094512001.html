Kindergarten Vaccine Exemptions Hit Record 4.2%

MMR coverage slips to 92.4% as 41 states see rises

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Routine childhood vaccine exemptions among incoming U.S. kindergartners hit a record 4.2% for the 2025–2026 school year, according to new federal surveillance data released by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention on August 17, 2026. Exemptions increased in 41 states and Washington, D.C., signaling widening pockets of susceptibility to measles and other preventable diseases as the school year begins.

Key national coverage also slipped below herd-immunity targets. The share of kindergartners documented as receiving the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine fell to 92.4%, and DTaP coverage to 92.0%, both beneath the commonly cited 95% community-protection threshold. CDC estimates indicate roughly 155,000 kindergartners held at least one exemption in 2025–2026, while officials said about 280,000 attended school without documentation of completing the two‑dose MMR series. In 24 states, overall exemption rates exceeded 5%.

Public health officials warn these gaps heighten the risk of school‑based clusters and broader community outbreaks. This year’s U.S. measles tally has already surpassed last year’s total of 2,289 cases, making 2026 the worst year since 1991; concentrations of unvaccinated students mean a single case can more readily ignite prolonged transmission. Medical groups emphasize that small national declines can mask much larger local deficits where exemptions are clustered.

Policy developments form the backdrop. On August 10, 2026, President Trump signed an executive order urging a slimmer childhood vaccine schedule and calling to split combination shots like MMR into separate injections over multiple visits. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has pressed broader changes to immunization guidance. Experts note that school‑entry requirements are set by states and that proposed federal changes face legal, logistical, and supply hurdles; they also caution that policy turbulence may further erode confidence just as schools work to lift coverage. The CDC, in a brief statement accompanying the new data, said vaccination coverage among entering kindergartners decreased slightly and encouraged parents to discuss options with their clinicians.
