Eight Killed in Alaska Charter Plane Crash Near Military Radar Site

NTSB Investigates Failed Landing Attempt in Heavy Fog

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 22, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

All eight people aboard a chartered Cessna 441 Conquest II were killed after the aircraft crashed while attempting to land at the remote Cape Newenham Long Range Radar Site Airport in western Alaska. The plane, operated by Security Aviation under contract to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, was carrying two pilots, two Corps employees, and four private contractors when it went down on August 20 near the Bering Sea coast, about 450 miles west of Anchorage.

According to the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB), the aircraft encountered deteriorating weather as it approached the isolated gravel runway. Clint Johnson, the agency's Alaska regional chief, said preliminary information indicates the crew abandoned its first landing attempt after failing to establish adequate visual contact in heavy fog. During a second instrument approach, the aircraft crashed roughly 500 meters (0.3 miles) short of the runway. Search teams reached the site and confirmed there were no survivors.

The Cape Newenham Long Range Radar Site is operated by the Pacific Air Forces Regional Support Center and forms part of the military's northern radar network used to monitor airspace approaching Alaska. The airport is surrounded by rugged terrain, and changing weather frequently complicates flight operations. Lt. Gen. Robert Davis, commander of Alaskan Command and the 11th Air Force, confirmed the fatalities. Calista Brice, an Alaska infrastructure company, said two of its employees were among the contractors killed in the crash.

The NTSB has dispatched a team of investigators to document the wreckage, recover avionics equipment, and review flight data, weather information, maintenance records, and pilot communications. Investigators emphasized that it is too early to determine the cause of the crash, and a preliminary report is expected in the coming weeks while the full investigation continues.
