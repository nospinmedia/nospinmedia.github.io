Stellantis Recalls 955,000 Vehicles Over Backup Camera Failure

OTA fix rolling out; nearly 850,000 in U.S.

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Automaker Stellantis said on August 17, 2026 it is recalling nearly 955,000 vehicles worldwide to fix a software defect that can prevent the rearview camera image from appearing when the vehicle is shifted into reverse. The company said it is not aware of any crashes or injuries linked to the issue and will deploy an over-the-air (OTA) radio software update as the remedy.

In the United States, the action covers nearly 850,000 vehicles across the 2026–2027 model years for multiple Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep, and Ram models. The recall includes select versions of the Pacifica, Voyager, Charger, Cherokee, Compass, Gladiator, Grand Cherokee, Grand Wagoneer, Wrangler, Ram 1500, Ram 2500, ProMaster, and other affected vehicles. Owners can confirm whether their specific vehicle is included by checking their VIN through Stellantis or the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) recall database.

Stellantis said the glitch stems from internal radio software that disrupts communication with the central screen, producing a blank or failed image while reversing. Owners will receive an on-screen prompt to download and install the OTA update once it is available for their vehicle. Until the update is applied, drivers should use mirrors and take extra care when backing up, the company advised.

Rearview cameras have been required on all new U.S. light vehicles since May 2018 under federal rear-visibility rules, reflecting regulators’ focus on reducing back-over crashes. The Stellantis campaign underscores how modern vehicles’ safety-critical functions increasingly depend on infotainment and connectivity software—and how OTA updates are now a common recall remedy.
