Judge extends block on new USPS mail-ballot rules

States keep existing procedures as midterms begin

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A federal judge in Boston issued a preliminary injunction Friday preventing the U.S. Postal Service from enforcing new mail-ballot requirements stemming from an executive order signed by President Trump in March 2026. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani replaced an earlier temporary restraining order with the longer-lasting injunction as states begin sending absentee ballots for the November 3, 2026 midterm elections.

The Postal Service's final rule would require states to meet new ballot-envelope requirements, including individualized barcodes, and submit names and addresses of mail voters through an online federal portal. Talwani concluded that states challenging the rule were likely to succeed in showing it was unconstitutional and said election officials could not realistically overhaul their systems this late in the election cycle. She wrote that states are required by their own laws to mail millions of ballots on established schedules and that forcing a late change would create a substantial risk of eligible voters being disenfranchised.

The injunction allows states to use the new Postal Service system voluntarily, but no state had announced plans to do so as of Friday. North Carolina began sending its first absentee ballots Friday, while additional states are expected to begin mailing ballots in the coming weeks. Nearly one-third of U.S. voters cast ballots by mail, according to reporting cited by the Associated Press.

The litigation follows multiple challenges to Trump's executive order and the Postal Service regulations implementing it. A coalition of 24 states and the District of Columbia, along with voting-rights groups, challenged the latest rule, arguing that the Postal Service lacks authority to establish election procedures normally controlled by states and Congress. The Trump administration had already asked the U.S. Supreme Court to lift Talwani's temporary order and told the district court Friday that it plans to appeal the preliminary injunction to the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals.
