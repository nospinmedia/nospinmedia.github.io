Texas carries out first U.S. execution since Tennessee failure

Jamaal Howard put to death as lawyers raised concerns over lethal injection drug

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 8, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Texas executed Jamaal Howard, 46, Wednesday night, making him the first person put to death in the United States since Christa Pike survived Tennessee's failed lethal injection attempt one week earlier. Howard was pronounced dead at 8:03 p.m. at the state prison in Huntsville after receiving a lethal dose of pentobarbital, the same drug used during Pike's unsuccessful execution.

Howard was convicted of killing Vickie Swartout, 42, during a 2000 robbery at a convenience store in Silsbee, Texas, north of Beaumont. Surveillance video captured the robbery, during which Howard shot Swartout. He was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death. Before his execution Wednesday, Howard apologized to members of Swartout's family and asked for their forgiveness.

Howard's attorneys unsuccessfully sought to delay the execution after Pike survived two doses of pentobarbital during Tennessee's September 30 attempt to execute her. His lawyers asked Texas Gov. Greg Abbott for a 30-day delay while they sought information about the safety and history of Texas' supply of pentobarbital. They also pursued court challenges arguing Howard was intellectually disabled and faced potential risks from the execution drug. State and federal courts declined to stop the execution.

Texas uses pentobarbital compounded by pharmacies because pharmaceutical manufacturers generally refuse to provide their drugs for executions. The state tests its existing supply and can extend expiration dates when testing determines the drug remains potent. That practice has previously faced legal challenges over whether aging compounded drugs could cause pain during executions.

Howard became the 30th person executed in the United States in 2026. Texas has carried out six executions this year and has additional executions scheduled, while the failed Tennessee procedure has renewed scrutiny of lethal injection practices nationally. Pike survived after receiving pentobarbital twice and later regained consciousness.
