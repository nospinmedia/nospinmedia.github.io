U.S. inflation rises less than expected in August

Fed rate-hike expectations ease as consumer spending surges

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 1, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

A key measure of U.S. inflation rose less than economists expected in August, potentially giving the Federal Reserve more time before deciding whether to raise interest rates again. The Personal Consumption Expenditures price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, increased 0.3% from July and 3.4% from a year earlier, according to the Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis.

Economists surveyed by Reuters had expected the monthly PCE index to rise 0.4%. July's monthly increase was revised down from 0.2% to 0.1%, while its annual increase was revised from 3.7% to 3.4% following methodology changes. Excluding food and energy, core PCE prices increased 0.2% in August and 3.0% from a year earlier. Both headline and core inflation remain above the Fed's 2% target.

The report does not mean inflation is falling outright. August's monthly increase accelerated from July, with gasoline prices rebounding 4.4%. But the lower-than-expected readings reduced immediate pressure on the Fed, which raised its benchmark interest rate earlier in September to a range of 3.75% to 4.00%, its first increase in three years. After the inflation report, financial markets reduced their expectations for another rate increase at the Fed's October 27-28 meeting.

At the same time, Americans continued spending heavily despite higher prices and borrowing costs. Consumer spending jumped 0.9% in August after a revised 0.1% increase in July. Adjusted for inflation, spending increased 0.6%. However, inflation-adjusted disposable income was flat and the personal saving rate declined from 4.6% to 4.1%, its lowest level since November 2022.

The combination leaves the Fed balancing inflation that remains above target against an economy and consumer sector that have continued to show resilience. Another rate increase later this year remains possible even if policymakers decide to wait in October.
