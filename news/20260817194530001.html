Trump Threatens Strikes on Oman Over Hormuz Talks

Warning follows Iran–Oman transit plan as 60-day window closes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

President Trump warned on August 17, 2026 that the United States could bomb Oman if the Gulf nation interferes with U.S. efforts involving the Strait of Hormuz, an unusually direct military threat against a longtime U.S. security partner and regional mediator. The remark came during a phone interview with Fox News chief foreign correspondent Trey Yingst as negotiations involving Iran, Oman and access to the strait remained unresolved.

“If Oman gets in the way, we’ll bomb the s--- out of them,” Trump told Yingst. Trump later said from the Oval Office that Oman had not been behaving well but that the United States could deal with the situation. The comments came as Iran said it was close to finalizing a joint statement with Oman on a plan governing commercial transit through the Strait of Hormuz.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei said an “understanding” had been reached with Oman regarding the map of the transit route through the strait and that the two governments were working on remaining details. The Strait of Hormuz runs between Iran and Oman and is one of the world’s most important energy corridors, carrying roughly one-fifth of globally traded oil and natural gas under normal conditions. Commercial traffic through the waterway has been sharply disrupted during the conflict, contributing to higher shipping, insurance and energy costs.

The dispute comes as the 60-day negotiating period established under a June 17 U.S.-Iran memorandum reached its deadline on August 17. The agreement called for negotiations toward a broader settlement, including Iran’s nuclear program, after an interim halt in military operations. The ceasefire itself quickly deteriorated, with renewed U.S. and Iranian strikes in July. Trump said Monday that the United States would not extend the negotiating period and reiterated that preventing Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon remains his primary objective.

Oman has long maintained relationships with both Washington and Tehran and has repeatedly served as an intermediary during periods when direct U.S.-Iran diplomacy was limited. That role makes Trump’s threat particularly significant because Oman is simultaneously involved in discussions intended to restore more regular shipping through the strait.

The comments are also prompting action in Congress. Sen. Tim Kaine of Virginia announced a new War Powers Resolution aimed at restricting the president’s ability to initiate hostilities against Oman without congressional authorization. The move adds to an ongoing congressional debate over the scope of presidential military authority during the conflict with Iran.
