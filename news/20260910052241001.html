Tate brothers ordered to remain jailed in Miami

U.K. extradition fight involves 59 criminal charges

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 10, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A federal judge in Miami has denied Andrew Tate and Tristan Tate release from custody while they fight extradition to the United Kingdom, where the brothers face a combined 59 criminal charges, including allegations of rape and human trafficking for sexual exploitation. The brothers deny the allegations against them.

U.S. Magistrate Judge Lauren F. Louis ruled September 9 that the brothers had not demonstrated that they were neither flight risks nor dangers to the community, or that special circumstances justified their release. The Tates, who hold both U.S. and British citizenship, have been detained since their July 18 arrests in Miami following an extradition request from British authorities. Their wealth, international connections and ability to travel were among the issues raised during the detention proceedings.

The British cases involve allegations spanning 2010 through 2017. The brothers are accused of offenses including rape, assault and facilitating human trafficking for sexual exploitation. British authorities are seeking to bring them to the U.K. for prosecution, while the Tates' attorneys have argued for their release during what could be a lengthy extradition process. Their legal team has said it is considering an appeal of the detention ruling.

The extradition dispute comes as the brothers also face separate legal proceedings in Romania. On September 4, Romanian prosecutors indicted Andrew Tate on charges including trafficking minors, money laundering, sex with a minor and witness tampering. Tristan Tate was indicted for complicity. Those allegations are separate from the criminal cases underlying Britain's extradition request, and both brothers have denied wrongdoing.

The Miami ruling does not determine whether the Tates are guilty of any of the allegations or whether they ultimately will be extradited. It determines that they will remain in federal custody while the U.S. court considers Britain's request to transfer them for prosecution.
