Kushner quietly meets with Hakeem Jeffries in New York

Private talks signal planning ahead of possible House power shift

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 24, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries and Jared Kushner, President Trump's son-in-law and an outside adviser to the administration, recently held a private meeting in New York City as both parties prepare for the November 2026 midterm elections. People familiar with the discussion said the meeting covered areas of potential bipartisan cooperation, including housing, immigration, and the rising cost of living, at a time when Democrats are seeking to regain control of the House of Representatives.

The meeting, first reported by The New York Times, was arranged through a mutual acquaintance at a private residence. According to people familiar with the conversation, Kushner suggested Jeffries hold a follow-up meeting with White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. While Kushner does not hold a formal White House position, he continues to advise the administration on key policy matters. If Democrats win a House majority this fall, Jeffries would be positioned to become Speaker, making early communication between both sides politically significant.

The discussion also revived a bipartisan relationship established during negotiations over the First Step Act, the criminal justice reform law enacted during President Trump's first term. Despite sharp public disagreements between Republicans and Democrats over immigration, spending, and oversight of the administration, both sides appear to be considering how divided government could function if House control changes after the election. House Speaker Mike Johnson said he was unaware of the meeting and noted Kushner is not involved in the day-to-day operations of the administration.

Jeffries did not publicly confirm the meeting but reiterated that House Democrats oppose what he described as a "my-way-or-the-highway" governing approach while remaining committed to addressing affordability and other issues affecting Americans. The private discussions have drawn attention from both parties, with some Democrats questioning the wisdom of back-channel outreach while others argued that maintaining lines of communication could prove valuable if the balance of power shifts after November.
