Texas Appeals Court Cuts Alex Jones Sandy Hook Judgment

Punitive Damages Reduced Under State Law Cap

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 22, 2026 at 7:43 PM ET

A Texas appeals court has reduced a nearly $50 million judgment against Alex Jones to about $5.6 million, ruling that the punitive damages awarded to the parents of a child killed in the 2012 Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting exceeded limits established under Texas law. The decision leaves intact $4.1 million in compensatory damages awarded to Neil Heslin and Scarlett Lewis, whose six-year-old son Jesse Lewis was among the 20 children killed in the shooting.

The Texas Third Court of Appeals concluded that the trial court improperly allowed punitive damages to exceed the cap established by Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code Chapter 41. Under that statute, exemplary damages in the case were limited to approximately $1.5 million, replacing the more than $45 million in punitive damages awarded by the jury. The appellate panel found that the evidence presented did not support bypassing the statutory limits after liability had already been established through a default judgment.

The lawsuit stemmed from Jones' repeated false claims that the Sandy Hook massacre was a hoax, leading some of his followers to harass grieving families for years. Although the Texas ruling substantially reduces the judgment in this case, it does not affect the separate $1.4 billion judgment entered against Jones in Connecticut, where additional Sandy Hook families successfully sued him for defamation and intentional infliction of emotional distress. The U.S. Supreme Court previously declined to hear Jones' appeal in that case.

Jones and his company, Free Speech Systems, remain in Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings, where both the Texas and Connecticut judgments continue to play a significant role in determining how assets will ultimately be distributed to creditors, including the Sandy Hook families. While the Texas ruling reduces one portion of Jones' legal liability, the much larger Connecticut judgment remains the dominant financial claim in the ongoing bankruptcy case.
