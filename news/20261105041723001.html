Democrats take control of House and Senate

Affordability agenda faces test under divided government

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 5, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Democrats have won control of both the U.S. House of Representatives and the U.S. Senate in the 2026 midterm elections, ending Republican control of Congress and creating divided government for the final two years of President Trump’s term.

Democrats needed a net gain of four Senate seats after Republicans entered the election with a 53-47 majority. Victories in several Republican-held states gave Democrats enough seats to take the chamber, while the party also captured enough House districts to surpass the 218-seat threshold needed for control. The final size of both majorities remains unsettled as votes continue to be counted in some races.

Economic concerns were a major part of the campaign. Exit polling showed widespread dissatisfaction with the direction of the country and negative views of the economy, while affordability, energy prices and other household costs featured prominently in competitive races. Democratic candidates campaigned on lowering costs, and incoming House leaders have identified affordability as a central legislative priority.

Among the priorities outlined by Hakeem Jeffries are reversing enacted Medicaid cuts, restoring expired Affordable Care Act premium tax credits, rolling back tariffs and seeking an end to the war with Iran. Winning Congress gives Democrats control over which legislation advances and, in the Senate, greater power over presidential nominations.

Their ability to enact that agenda will be limited by divided government. President Trump remains in the White House and can veto legislation approved by Congress, meaning major policy changes would generally require Republican cooperation or enough congressional support to override a veto. Democrats will nevertheless gain substantial influence over federal spending, congressional investigations and the legislative agenda when the new Congress takes office.
