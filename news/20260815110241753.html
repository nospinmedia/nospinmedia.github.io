Israeli Airstrike Kills 7 In Southern Lebanon

Strike near Ansar adds pressure to fragile June ceasefire

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

An Israeli airstrike struck the outskirts of Ansar in southern Lebanon before dawn on August 15, 2026, killing seven people and wounding two others, according to Lebanon's Health Ministry. The strike destroyed a home near the Nabatieh district, while additional Israeli strikes were reported elsewhere in southern Lebanon. The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said it targeted Hezbollah infrastructure in Ansar and on the nearby Ali al-Taher ridge after what it described as hostile activity directed at Israeli troops.

The attack is one of the deadliest reported incidents since a June 20 ceasefire sharply reduced fighting between Israel and Hezbollah. Lebanese state media also reported strikes in the valley between Ansar and Zarariyeh, describing them as among the deepest attacks into southern Lebanon since the truce took effect. Residents reported powerful explosions across parts of Nabatieh Governorate, while emergency crews searched damaged buildings and transported casualties to nearby hospitals.

Although the ceasefire significantly reduced cross-border fighting, Israeli forces have continued carrying out strikes they say are aimed at preventing Hezbollah from rebuilding military positions or launching attacks. Hezbollah has rejected calls to disarm under a broader security framework announced later in June, and negotiations over implementing the agreement have remained difficult. The latest strike highlights the continuing volatility along the border despite weeks of lower overall violence.

Since fighting intensified earlier this year, thousands of people have been killed in Lebanon, according to Lebanese authorities, while Israeli officials have reported military and civilian casualties from cross-border attacks. Diplomatic efforts to preserve the ceasefire remain ongoing, but Saturday's strike underscores the risk that localized military operations could trigger a broader escalation if tensions continue to rise.
