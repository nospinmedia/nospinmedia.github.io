ICE Conducts Immigration Enforcement at Bradley International Airport

Connecticut Officials Criticize Domestic Traveler Operations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 28, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Federal immigration officers have recently conducted targeted enforcement operations at Bradley International Airport in Windsor Locks, Connecticut, prompting criticism from state officials and renewed debate over immigration enforcement inside domestic airports. The Connecticut Airport Authority (CAA) confirmed that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents carried out several operations in recent weeks inside federally controlled areas of the airport. According to the authority, the agents were dressed in plain clothes, were not readily identifiable as ICE personnel, and airport staff were not notified in advance or involved in the operations. The CAA said it does not have the authority to stop federal law enforcement activities occurring within areas under federal jurisdiction.

Governor Ned Lamont criticized the operations, saying travelers attempting to board domestic flights should not unexpectedly encounter immigration enforcement while passing through airport security. State officials also confirmed that Connecticut State Police assigned to Bradley did not participate in the operations because the state's Trust Act limits cooperation with federal civil immigration enforcement. Representative John B. Larson condemned the actions and alleged that ICE identified some travelers using pre-flight passenger information. However, federal authorities have not publicly confirmed the investigative methods used to identify individuals or commented on Larson's specific claims.

The Bradley operations come as the Trump administration continues expanding immigration enforcement nationwide. DHS has stated that ICE is increasing enforcement efforts and prioritizing individuals who are in the United States unlawfully, while critics argue that conducting arrests inside airport terminals creates uncertainty for travelers and raises concerns about transparency. The Connecticut Airport Authority emphasized that its role is to operate safe and efficient airport facilities rather than participate in immigration enforcement, and it reiterated that federal agencies retain authority within federally controlled airport areas.

The incident highlights the continuing legal and political tension between federal immigration authority and state policies that limit cooperation with civil immigration enforcement. As Connecticut officials seek additional information about those detained, the debate over where and how immigration enforcement should occur is expected to continue.
