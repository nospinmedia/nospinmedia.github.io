House rejects amendment to fix Supreme Court at nine justices

Measure falls short of required two-thirds vote

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The U.S. House of Representatives rejected a proposed constitutional amendment that would have permanently fixed the U.S. Supreme Court at nine justices, with the measure failing in a 212-206 vote on September 2. Because constitutional amendments require approval by a two-thirds majority of the House, the proposal fell well short of the votes needed to advance to the Senate.

The resolution was introduced by Rep. Andy Biggs of Arizona and supported by House Republican leadership. Republicans argued that placing the current size of the Supreme Court into the Constitution would prevent future efforts by Congress to expand the Court, often referred to as "court packing." Rep. Jim Jordan, chairman of the House Judiciary Committee, said the amendment would help preserve judicial independence by preventing changes to the Court's size for political purposes.

Nearly every House Democrat voted against the proposal, with Rep. Don Davis of North Carolina the only Democrat to support it. Democratic leaders argued that Congress has long held the constitutional authority to determine the size of the Supreme Court through legislation and said permanently fixing the number of justices would unnecessarily limit future lawmakers. Opponents also noted that the Court's size has changed several times throughout U.S. history before Congress set it at nine justices through the Judiciary Act of 1869.

Because the amendment failed in the House, it will not advance to the Senate. Even if it had cleared the House, it would have required approval by two-thirds of the Senate before being submitted to the states, where 38 states would have needed to ratify it before becoming part of the Constitution. The vote highlights continuing political disagreement over the structure of the Supreme Court and whether Congress should retain the ability to change its size in the future.
