Trump announces phased Hamas disarmament agreement

Israeli support and implementation terms remain uncertain

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 31, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

President Trump announced on July 30, 2026, that his international Board of Peace had reached an agreement for the phased disarmament of Hamas and other armed groups in Gaza. U.S. officials described the development as a major step in the administration’s 20-point Gaza peace plan, but Israel has not formally endorsed the latest terms and statements from Hamas officials indicate that important conditions remain unresolved.

Under the proposed roadmap, Hamas would surrender heavy weapons, permit the destruction of tunnels and other military infrastructure, and transfer security responsibilities to a newly trained Palestinian police force supported by an International Stabilization Force. Israeli troops would withdraw gradually as independent monitors verify compliance in individual areas. U.S. officials told AP News that full implementation could take approximately 200 to 350 days, reflecting the difficulty of locating weapons, dismantling tunnels and establishing replacement security institutions.

The plan also calls for civilian administration of Gaza by the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza, a Palestinian technocratic body intended to operate with international oversight. Egypt, Qatar and Turkey helped mediate the negotiations. Trump said the agreement would allow a new Palestinian government to serve Gaza’s population while addressing Israel’s security concerns.

Despite the announcement, substantial uncertainty remains. Hamas negotiator Ghazi Hamad disputed suggestions that the group had accepted unconditional or immediate disarmament, indicating that any weapons transfer would depend on an end to Israeli military operations and guarantees of a complete Israeli withdrawal. An Israeli official also expressed dissatisfaction with elements of the proposal, while the Israeli government had not publicly confirmed its acceptance when the agreement was announced.

Israeli strikes continued in Gaza as the diplomatic announcement unfolded, underscoring that the roadmap has not yet ended violence on the ground. The agreement therefore represents a potentially significant negotiating breakthrough rather than a completed peace settlement, with its success dependent on formal approval, detailed verification rules and compliance by both Hamas and Israel.
