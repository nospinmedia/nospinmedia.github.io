Drone strike kills at least 153 at Abyei market

Attack pushes Sudan war deeper into disputed border region

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 21, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

A drone strike on Amiet Market in the disputed Abyei region between Sudan and South Sudan killed at least 153 people, making it one of the deadliest recent attacks connected to Sudan's civil war.

Yohana Akol Ngor, Abyei's caretaker information minister, blamed Sudan's military for the October 19 attack and said the death toll had risen sharply from an earlier count of 73. The strike hit a fuel depot inside the market, triggering a major fire. The toll was expected to rise further.

A senior Sudanese military officer acknowledged that the army carried out a drone strike, saying fuel tankers arriving from South Sudan and believed to be supplying the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, or RSF, were the target. The officer said civilians were not targeted. Local and South Sudanese officials, however, reported civilian casualties, and the United Nations said women and children were among those killed. The competing accounts of who was killed have not been independently resolved.

Doctors Without Borders said more than 20 wounded people, including patients with severe burns, were treated at Ameth Bek Hospital. U.N. peacekeepers deployed medical personnel, firefighting equipment and rapid-response forces as authorities worked to verify casualties and control the fire. South Sudan also summoned Sudan's ambassador and called for disputes involving Abyei to be handled diplomatically.

Abyei's political status has remained unresolved since a 2005 peace agreement that helped end an earlier Sudanese civil war. The oil-rich territory remained disputed after South Sudan became independent in 2011. The latest attack comes as Sudan's military and the RSF continue the civil war that began in April 2023, with recent attacks killing more than 250 people.
