U.S. and Japan Confirm Rare Joint Yen-Buying Intervention

Dollar drops to mid‑156s as both sides signal readiness for more

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 3, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The United States and Japan confirmed a coordinated move to support the yen, a rare joint action that immediately weakened the dollar. President Trump and Japan’s Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said on Monday, August 3, 2026 that authorities stepped into foreign‑exchange markets to counter “excessive volatility,” a notable escalation after the yen’s slide to 40‑year lows. Early Monday trading saw the dollar around ¥156.3, after hovering above ¥163 late last week.

Katayama said the operation was carried out with the U.S. Department of the Treasury to stabilize markets, adding Tokyo “will not hesitate” to act again if needed. On Sunday, August 2, President Trump described the intervention as a “signal of friendship” and said the U.S. derived “financial benefit” from aiding a key ally. The confirmation marks the first coordinated yen‑buying effort by Washington and Tokyo since the 2011 post‑earthquake G7 action and follows a September 2025 U.S.–Japan statement allowing intervention to address disorderly moves.

Analysts said the move underscores concern that a prolonged yen sell‑off could strain financial conditions at home and abroad. The stronger yen eases import costs for Japan but can pressure equities and exporters; a softer dollar can make U.S. goods cheaper in yen terms. Officials in both countries indicated they are monitoring markets closely and remain prepared to repeat the operation if volatility returns.

Additional context from recent reporting helps quantify the scale: preliminary Bank of Japan data indicated Tokyo may have deployed as much as $58.97 billion in earlier yen purchases ahead of the confirmed joint action, highlighting how solo steps had offered only temporary relief. Japan’s top currency diplomat Atsushi Mimura has repeatedly signaled that authorities are ready to act decisively under the bilateral framework if speculative pressure rebuilds.
