Tourist plane crashes near Peru’s Nazca Lines

All 13 aboard killed as investigators examine sightseeing flight

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 2, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A sightseeing aircraft carrying European tourists crashed near the Nazca Lines archaeological site in southern Peru on August 1, 2026, killing all 13 people aboard. Peru’s Ministry of Transport and Communications said the dead included 11 foreign tourists and two Peruvian crew members traveling aboard a Cessna Caravan C-208 operated by the local aviation company Aerodiana.

The passengers included seven Italian citizens, two Germans and two Spaniards. According to the ministry’s account, the aircraft departed Pisco Airport at approximately 12:10 p.m. local time and reported an emergency near the city of Nazca shortly before 1 p.m. Radio communication was then lost. The plane crashed in the Socos area, about 6 kilometers from Nazca and approximately 19 kilometers from the Nazca Lines, before catching fire upon impact.

Emergency personnel reached the wreckage, but authorities said there were no survivors. Peru’s aviation authorities activated accident-investigation procedures, with the Commission for the Investigation of Aviation Accidents and the Directorate General of Civil Aeronautics tasked with determining what caused the aircraft to go down. Officials had not identified a mechanical failure, pilot error or weather as the cause as of the latest reporting.

Tourist flights over the Nazca Lines had faced recent disruption because of high winds and reduced visibility. Flights from the nearby María Reiche Airport were suspended the previous day after winds exceeded approximately 40 kilometers per hour, forcing some aircraft to divert. Authorities have not established whether those conditions contributed to Saturday’s crash.

The Nazca Lines are a UNESCO World Heritage Site consisting of enormous ancient geoglyphs etched into Peru’s desert. The formations are best viewed from the air and attract roughly 100,000 visitors annually, making sightseeing flights an important part of the region’s tourism industry.
