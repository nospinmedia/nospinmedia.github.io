GOP super PAC pauses new spending in North Carolina Senate race

More than $30 million already spent backing Michael Whatley

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 3, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The Senate Leadership Fund, the main super PAC aligned with Senate Republican leadership, has paused future advertising spending in North Carolina after already spending more than $30 million on the state's closely watched U.S. Senate race. The move affects Republican nominee Michael Whatley, a former Republican National Committee chairman backed by President Trump, who is running against former Democratic Gov. Roy Cooper for the seat being vacated by retiring Republican Sen. Thom Tillis.

CBS News reported, citing sources familiar with the decision, that the Republican group is redirecting millions of dollars toward other Senate contests, including Kansas, where Republican Sen. Roger Marshall is seeking reelection against Democratic pastor Adam Hamilton. As of Friday, the Senate Leadership Fund had reserved $9.8 million in advertising in Kansas, according to political advertising tracker AdImpact.

The shift comes as polling has generally shown Cooper ahead of Whatley. CBS News reported that Cooper has held an edge in polling, while The Guardian cited a recent AARP survey showing Cooper ahead by 11 percentage points. Independent election analysts have also moved the race in Cooper's direction, although North Carolina remains a state with a history of close statewide elections.

The campaigns are publicly rejecting the idea that the contest is settled. Cooper campaign manager Jeff Allen described North Carolina as a “50-50 state” and noted that a Democrat has not won a U.S. Senate or presidential contest there in nearly two decades. Whatley campaign spokesman DJ Griffin said the Republican campaign believes it has the resources needed to compete and argued that late-deciding voters could change the race.

President Trump, who campaigned for Whatley in North Carolina last month, was asked Friday whether he was worried about the Republican candidate's chances. “I am,” Trump said, before adding that he hoped Whatley would win.

The spending change comes during the final weeks before the November 3 midterm elections, as both parties direct money toward Senate races they consider competitive.
