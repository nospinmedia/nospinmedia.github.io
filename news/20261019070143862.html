Cost-of-Living Protests Spread Across Four European Countries

Separate Demonstrations Hit France, Italy, Belgium and Portugal

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 19, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Hundreds of thousands of people took to the streets across France, Italy, Belgium and Portugal over the weekend as separate protests highlighted growing frustration over living costs, housing, wages, education and public services. The demonstrations were not a single coordinated European movement, but they occurred at roughly the same time and shared several economic concerns.

France saw by far the largest demonstrations and the most serious violence. More than 550,000 people participated nationwide on Saturday, according to the French Interior Ministry. Protests that began with students demanding better school conditions broadened to include concerns about rising living costs and deteriorating public services. Rioting, looting and fires later erupted in several cities, producing thousands of arrests over the weekend.

In Italy, demonstrations against rising living costs and other issues turned violent in Milan, where masked protesters threw objects at police, vandalized shops and set fires near Piazza del Duomo. Police responded with tear gas and water cannon. Additional clashes were reported in cities including Bologna, while Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni condemned the violence but distinguished it from the right to demonstrate peacefully.

In Portugal, tens of thousands marched through Lisbon over high housing costs, low wages and broader affordability concerns. Portuguese home prices have more than doubled since 2017, while rents on new leases have risen sharply. Demonstrators demanded higher wages and pensions and action on housing costs.

Belgium also experienced unrest. Police used tear gas and water cannon during demonstrations in Brussels, where students have been protesting rising university registration fees and education policies. Demonstrations were also reported in Liège.

Although the protests have different origins and demands, their simultaneous appearance has drawn attention to economic pressures facing households across Europe. Analysts cautioned against treating them as one unified movement, while noting overlapping concerns about affordability, education and future economic prospects.
