Appeals court questions DOJ bid to segregate 20,000 California ballots

Judges press government over federal authority and reliability of voter list

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 26, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

A federal appeals court panel expressed skepticism about the Justice Department's emergency attempt to require California election officials to set aside roughly 20,000 mail ballots linked to voters the federal government suspects may not be U.S. citizens.

During expedited arguments on October 25, a three-judge panel of the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals questioned whether federal courts have jurisdiction to impose the requested restrictions and how far the federal government's authority extends over state election procedures. The dispute comes just days before the November 3 midterm elections.

The Justice Department has argued that California failed to act quickly enough after receiving federal data identifying people it considers likely noncitizens. The government wants ballots associated with those voters segregated while their eligibility is reviewed, arguing that federal and state laws prohibit noncitizens from voting.

Attorneys representing California and Los Angeles County challenged the reliability of the government's list and warned that eligible voters could have their ballots withheld. A review of 200 records from the federal list found a mixture of canceled or inactive registrations, people with no voting history and individuals who appeared to have been born in the United States. The findings add to questions raised earlier by California election officials about whether the federal data accurately identifies ineligible voters.

The appellate judges also raised constitutional questions involving the 10th Amendment and the Constitution's Elections Clause, which gives states primary responsibility for administering elections while granting Congress authority to regulate certain aspects of federal elections. One judge suggested that an injunction could help determine whether noncitizens were actually voting, while other questions focused on whether the federal government had established sufficient grounds for immediate court intervention.

The panel did not issue a ruling during the hearing but indicated that a decision would come quickly because voting and ballot processing are already underway.
