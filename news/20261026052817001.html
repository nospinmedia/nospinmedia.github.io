Lula wins historic fourth term as Brazil’s president

Flávio Bolsonaro refuses to concede and seeks to annul result

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 26, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a historic fourth term after defeating Flávio Bolsonaro in the country's presidential runoff, completing a political comeback after trailing his challenger in the first round. Brazil's electoral court declared Lula the winner with 51.8% of the vote after all ballots were counted.

Lula defeated Bolsonaro by nearly 4.4 million votes, becoming the first Brazilian presidential candidate since the end of military rule in 1985 to win after finishing second in the opening round. His fourth nonconsecutive term begins January 5, 2027. Bolsonaro, a senator and the son of former President Jair Bolsonaro, refused to concede and said he would seek to have the election annulled.

Bolsonaro's challenge centers on a decision by Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin to extend voting by two hours shortly before polls were scheduled to close. Both campaigns had requested additional voting time: Lula's campaign cited transportation problems affecting voters in São Paulo and Salvador, while Bolsonaro's campaign cited heavy rain in southern Brazil. Some polling places nevertheless closed at the original deadline. Electoral court president Kassio Nunes Marques said the extension did not affect the result.

The result represents another comeback for Lula, now 80, who previously served two presidential terms beginning in 2003 and returned to office after defeating Jair Bolsonaro in 2022. Lula had faced concerns about living costs and public security during the campaign, while Flávio Bolsonaro finished the first round more than 2.2 million votes ahead of him.

Lula will enter his next term facing a strongly conservative Congress and economic pressures that include high interest rates and public debt exceeding 82% of GDP. Bolsonaro's Liberal Party and allied parties could hold opposition majorities in both chambers, potentially limiting Lula's ability to advance his agenda.
