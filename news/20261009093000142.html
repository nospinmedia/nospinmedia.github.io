Russian attacks kill at least 35 in Kramatorsk

Passenger buses destroyed as Ukraine reports 78 deaths nationwide in two days

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 9, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Russian attacks killed at least 35 people in the eastern Ukrainian city of Kramatorsk on Thursday, October 8, 2026, including passengers caught in a devastating aerial bomb strike near two public buses. Ukrainian authorities reported another 20 people wounded in the bombing, while additional attacks across the country disrupted electricity and water supplies as winter approaches.

The initial attack occurred during the morning in Kramatorsk, a Ukrainian-controlled city in the Donetsk region near the front line. A Russian guided aerial bomb struck a street where two passenger buses were operating, leaving burned-out vehicles and debris scattered across the roadway. Authorities initially reported 30 deaths from the bombing before additional fatalities were recorded in the city following further attacks.

Donetsk Regional Governor Vadym Filashkin accused Russian forces of deliberately targeting civilians. Moscow has repeatedly denied intentionally attacking civilian populations. Ukrainian officials reported that some victims remained unidentified because of the extensive damage caused by the explosion.

Emergency responders faced additional danger as Russian drones continued operating near the attack site. Authorities suspended public transportation in Kramatorsk and neighboring Sloviansk because of the deteriorating security situation.

The Kramatorsk bombing was part of a broader wave of Russian attacks across Ukraine. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy reported that 78 people had been killed and 215 wounded nationwide over two days. Those figures included casualties from a separate missile strike on October 7 that destroyed a residential building in Pryluky, killing 22 people.

In Kyiv, Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported four deaths and 19 injuries from additional attacks. Damage to infrastructure interrupted electricity and water supplies, adding pressure to Ukraine's energy system ahead of winter.

Zelenskyy criticized the United States for pursuing economic discussions with Russia while Ukraine continued facing deadly attacks. He called for additional American assistance, including expanded access to Starlink satellite services, stronger air defenses and increased sanctions against Moscow.

Ukrainian negotiators were scheduled to travel to the United States for discussions beginning October 9, with air defense assistance among the expected subjects. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio described the war as a dangerous stalemate, while Zelenskyy disputed that characterization and argued that additional international pressure could change Russia's calculations.

The latest attacks highlight the continuing danger to civilians in Ukrainian cities, even as diplomatic efforts seek a negotiated end to the conflict.
