Wisconsin reports first measles death since 1990

Infant dies as state outbreak reaches 35-year high

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 6, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

An infant in southwestern Wisconsin has died from measles, marking the state's first death from the highly contagious disease since 1990, health officials announced Thursday. The death comes during Wisconsin's largest measles outbreak in more than three decades and amid a broader resurgence of the virus across the United States.

The Wisconsin Department of Health Services said a local coroner determined measles was the primary cause of the infant's death. Officials did not release additional identifying information about the child, citing the family's privacy.

Wisconsin has reported 431 confirmed measles cases across 13 counties this year, the state's highest total in at least 35 years. Nine people have been hospitalized. State health officials said all but three of the people infected were unvaccinated, highlighting the concentration of cases among people without protection from the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine.

The Wisconsin death was the third measles-related death reported in the United States this week and the eighth nationally in 2026. Six deaths have been reported in Pennsylvania and another involved a child in New York, according to health authorities and national reporting.

Measles spreads through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes and can remain infectious in an enclosed space after that person leaves. Complications can include pneumonia, brain swelling and, in rare cases, death. Public health officials say the MMR vaccine remains the most effective way to prevent infection and severe complications.

The United States eliminated continuous domestic measles transmission in 2000, but outbreaks can occur when the virus is brought into communities where vaccination rates are too low to prevent sustained spread. Wisconsin health officials are urging residents to verify their vaccination status as they continue investigating exposures and attempting to contain the current outbreak.
