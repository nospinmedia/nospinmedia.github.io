Pennsylvania Reports Sixth Measles-Associated Death

State Outbreak Surpasses 1,100 Cases as Health Officials Review Latest Fatality

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 13, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Pennsylvania has recorded its sixth measles-associated death of 2026 after a 39-year-old Franklin County woman died following a measles infection, adding to concerns over one of the state's largest outbreaks in decades.

The Franklin County Coroner's Office classified the death as measles-associated after the woman developed complications following infection. Reports said she was unvaccinated and suffered a probable acute cardiac event. The Pennsylvania Department of Health was reviewing the death, meaning the state's assessment had not yet been finalized when the fatality was announced.

Pennsylvania had recorded 1,136 measles cases and 219 hospitalizations as of October 9, according to state health data. The outbreak has affected communities across the state and comes during a broader national resurgence that has pushed the United States above 4,000 measles cases in 2026.

The Franklin County death follows five other measles-associated deaths reported in Pennsylvania this year. Three occurred in Lancaster County, including two infants, while deaths were also reported in Mifflin County and Jefferson County.

Measles is a highly contagious respiratory disease that can cause pneumonia, brain inflammation and other serious complications. Vaccination remains the primary method of prevention. Pennsylvania data show that fewer than 1% of confirmed cases during the outbreak have occurred among people documented as fully vaccinated.

The outbreak also has an unusual age distribution. Adults account for roughly 67% of Pennsylvania's reported cases, a larger share than seen nationally, where infections have been more concentrated among children and adolescents.

The latest death comes as state and local health departments continue vaccination efforts, contact tracing and monitoring for additional infections. Officials have urged residents who are uncertain about their vaccination status to review their records and consult health care providers about measles, mumps and rubella vaccination.
