Trump Backs 10% Cap On Credit Card Interest Rates

President Says He Will Work With Democrats As Banking Industry Pushes Back

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 22, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump said he is willing to work with Democrats to cap credit card interest rates at 10%, potentially reviving a consumer-finance proposal that previously stalled amid opposition from banks and some congressional Republicans.

During a Saturday, November 21 interview on Fox News Channel's Saturday in America, Trump was asked whether he would work with Democrats on legislation imposing the 10% ceiling. "I would do it. These credit card companies have made a lot," Trump said, adding that he had not previously realized Democrats were offering to advance the proposal.

Trump first proposed a 10% credit card interest-rate cap during his 2024 presidential campaign and renewed the idea in January 2026, calling for a temporary one-year limit. The proposal did not advance in Congress. The political landscape is now changing after the 2026 midterm elections, with Democrats set to control both the House and Senate when the new Congress begins in January 2027.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren, who is positioned to chair the Senate Banking Committee, welcomed Trump's remarks and called for Congress to move forward with a cap. There is also existing bipartisan support: Republican Sen. Josh Hawley and independent Sen. Bernie Sanders have introduced legislation establishing a 10% ceiling, while lawmakers including Democratic Rep. Maxine Waters and Republican Rep. Anna Paulina Luna have supported efforts to limit rates.

The proposal could have major consequences for consumers and lenders. U.S. credit card balances have climbed above $1.2 trillion, while research examining a 10% ceiling has estimated consumers could save roughly $100 billion annually in interest charges.

Banking groups including the Consumer Bankers Association and American Bankers Association oppose the proposal. They argue that a strict cap could cause lenders to reduce credit availability for borrowers with lower credit scores, increase fees and scale back rewards programs.

Trump's comments do not themselves change credit card rates. Any nationwide cap would require Congress to pass legislation before it could take effect.
