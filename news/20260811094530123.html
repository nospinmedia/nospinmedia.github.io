Syrian Court Sentences Bashar al-Assad to Death in Absentia

First domestic war-crimes conviction of ousted ruler

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

A Syrian criminal court sentenced Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia on August 11, 2026, convicting the ousted president of war crimes and crimes against humanity committed during the country's 14-year conflict. Presiding Judge Fakhareddine al-Aryan delivered the verdict at the Fourth Criminal Court in Damascus during proceedings broadcast live on state television, marking the first time a Syrian court has convicted the former leader.

The court also sentenced Assad's brother, Maher al-Assad, to death in absentia and imposed a death sentence on Atef Najib, Assad's cousin and former head of the Political Security Branch in Daraa, who was tried while in custody. Prosecutors linked Najib to the crackdown on anti-government demonstrations in 2011, an event widely viewed as helping ignite Syria's civil war. The conflict has since been estimated to have claimed roughly 500,000 lives.

The ruling is largely symbolic unless Syrian authorities obtain custody of the Assad brothers, who have remained in Russia since the collapse of the former Syrian government in December 2024. Under Syrian law, defendants convicted in absentia are entitled to a new trial if they are later apprehended or voluntarily return to face the charges.

The case is part of Syria's broader transitional justice effort following the change in government. Public hearings began in April 2026, examining allegations of torture, unlawful killings, and other abuses committed during Assad's rule. Legal experts say the verdict could influence ongoing investigations and prosecutions involving former Syrian officials in European courts that exercise universal jurisdiction over alleged war crimes.
