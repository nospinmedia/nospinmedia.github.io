Amy Acton wins Ohio governor's race

Democrat becomes state's first woman elected governor

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 4, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Democrat Amy Acton won Ohio's governor's race Tuesday, flipping the office from Republican control and becoming the first woman elected governor of Ohio.

Acton, a physician who served as director of the Ohio Department of Health, became widely known during the COVID-19 pandemic, when she appeared alongside then-Gov. Mike DeWine during the state's early response to the outbreak. Her public profile later helped propel her into statewide politics and ultimately the race to succeed DeWine, who was unable to seek another consecutive term.

The victory represents a significant change in a state that has moved increasingly toward Republicans in recent national elections. Republicans had controlled the Ohio governor's office since 2011, and the state supported Republican presidential candidates in 2016, 2020 and 2024. Acton's win demonstrates that Ohio voters were willing to split from that recent pattern in a statewide contest.

The campaign also drew attention over political advertising. Bloomberg reported that Acton's Republican opponent faced backlash over campaign advertising, adding another dimension to a race already being closely watched for signs of whether Democrats could regain ground in Ohio.

As governor, Acton will have substantial influence over state policy, including health care, education, economic development and the administration of Ohio's government. Her ability to enact major parts of her agenda, however, will also depend on the makeup of the Ohio General Assembly and its willingness to work with the incoming administration.

Acton's election adds Ohio to a broader pattern of Democratic gains in the 2026 midterms, though the governor's race was a separate state contest rather than part of the Democratic takeover of Congress.
