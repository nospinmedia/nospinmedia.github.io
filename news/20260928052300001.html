Collins' Kavanaugh vote returns to center of Maine Senate race

First reelection bid since Roe was overturned puts abortion back in focus

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 28, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Maine Sen. Susan Collins is facing voters for the first time since the U.S. Supreme Court overturned Roe v. Wade, with Democrats and abortion-rights groups focusing heavily on her pivotal 2018 vote to confirm Justice Brett Kavanaugh. Collins, a Republican seeking a sixth term on November 3, is running against Democratic nominee Troy Jackson, a former Maine Senate president and logger.

Collins' vote was crucial to Kavanaugh's 50-48 confirmation. Kavanaugh later joined the Supreme Court's 6-3 majority in the 2022 Dobbs decision that eliminated the federal constitutional right to abortion. Collins criticized that ruling but has continued to defend her confirmation vote, telling reporters in June, "I do not regret that vote." Collins has said Kavanaugh told her before his confirmation that he considered Roe settled law. She has also maintained her support for abortion rights and in 2022 co-sponsored legislation intended to establish federal protections in law.

The issue has become a major focus of campaign spending. The Democratic-aligned group Affordable Maine has spent nearly $10 million on advertising highlighting Collins' support for Kavanaugh and other judges appointed by President Trump, while the political action committee Win Senate has spent another $32 million focused on Collins. Planned Parenthood Votes has spent at least $500,000 on advertisements featuring a woman who says she regrets supporting Collins because of the Kavanaugh vote. Jackson has endorsements from Planned Parenthood and Reproductive Freedom for All and has organized a Women for Troy coalition covering all 16 Maine counties.

Abortion rights remain broadly supported in Maine, but polling suggests the issue may not be voters' highest priority. A September New York Times/Siena poll found almost no likely Maine voters spontaneously named abortion as their most important issue in deciding their congressional vote. A separate CNN/SSRS survey found most likely Maine voters remained unhappy with the overturning of Roe, while broader economic concerns could carry greater weight.
