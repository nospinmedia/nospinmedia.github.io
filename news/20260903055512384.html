Ed Markey wins Massachusetts Democratic Senate primary

Veteran incumbent defeats Seth Moulton decisively

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 6:46 PM ET

Sen. Ed Markey won the Massachusetts Democratic Senate primary, defeating Rep. Seth Moulton by a wide margin to secure another term as the party's nominee. With more than 99% of ballots counted, Markey received about 64.8% of the vote compared with 35.2% for Moulton, according to election results reported after polls closed. The victory sends the veteran senator to the November general election.

Moulton, a 47-year-old Marine Corps veteran representing Massachusetts' 6th Congressional District, centered his campaign on the need for generational change within Democratic leadership. Markey, who is 80, countered by emphasizing his legislative record on climate policy, technology, and consumer protections while highlighting support from progressive organizations and prominent Democratic leaders. The race became one of the highest-profile Democratic primaries of the 2026 election cycle because it pitted an established incumbent against a well-known member of the party's younger generation.

The primary also saw other longtime Massachusetts Democrats successfully defend their seats. Rep. Richard Neal won renomination in the 1st Congressional District, while Rep. Stephen Lynch prevailed in the 8th Congressional District, preserving the state's incumbent congressional delegation heading into the general election. Markey now advances to face Republican nominee John Deaton in November.

Massachusetts has consistently favored Democratic candidates in statewide federal elections, but the Senate primary drew national attention as another test of whether voters were prepared to replace experienced lawmakers with a younger generation of leaders. The outcome suggested that many Democratic primary voters remained supportive of established incumbents despite arguments that new leadership would be better positioned to address emerging issues such as artificial intelligence, economic change, and long-term policy challenges.
