Hundreds of drones target Moscow as refinery is hit

Two killed during final day of Russian voting

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 20, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Hundreds of drones targeted Moscow and the surrounding region overnight into September 20, killing at least two people and damaging the Moscow Oil Refinery as Russia entered the final day of its parliamentary elections, according to Russian authorities. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said Russian defenses had intercepted more than 1,600 drones since Saturday, including 450 headed toward the capital. Ukraine had not commented on the attacks as of Sunday morning.

Moscow regional Governor Andrei Vorobyov said a 44-year-old woman and an elderly man were killed in separate locations in the wider Moscow region. A drone crash also triggered a fire at a 21-story apartment building in the Ramenskoye district, leading authorities to evacuate about 400 people, including 70 children. Sobyanin said several drones reached the Moscow refinery and that one struck an apartment, while no casualties were reported inside Moscow itself.

The extent of damage at the refinery was not immediately clear. The facility processed about 11.6 million metric tons of crude oil in 2024, producing roughly 2.9 million tons of gasoline and 3.2 million tons of diesel, according to the latest available data cited by Reuters. Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining facilities have already contributed to reduced refining capacity, fuel shortages, higher prices and lines at filling stations in parts of Russia.

The barrage came on the final day of Russia's three-day State Duma parliamentary elections. Sobyanin alleged that the attack was intended to disrupt voting, a claim Ukraine had not publicly addressed. The strikes also followed President Trump's announcement earlier in the week that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to stop targeting each other's energy infrastructure, although attacks on energy facilities have continued.
