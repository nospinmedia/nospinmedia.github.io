Three senior Justice Department officials resign

Departures come days after midterm elections shift Congress

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 7, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Three senior Justice Department officials are leaving their positions following the 2026 midterm elections, creating vacancies across divisions responsible for civil rights, national security and federal prosecutions in South Florida.

Harmeet Dhillon, assistant attorney general for the Civil Rights Division, announced her resignation on Thursday, November 5, after approximately 18 months leading the division. Two additional departures followed: John Eisenberg, assistant attorney general for the National Security Division, and Jason Reding Quiñones, U.S. attorney for the Southern District of Florida.

Dhillon said her departure had been planned before Election Day and coordinated with White House officials, making the timing alone insufficient to establish that the midterm results caused her resignation. Her tenure included Justice Department actions involving state voter rolls, university diversity programs and investigations of antisemitism on college campuses.

Eisenberg led the division responsible for national security matters, including counterterrorism, counterintelligence and other threats involving foreign governments. Reding Quiñones headed the federal prosecutor's office covering South Florida. Their departures create additional senior vacancies as the department prepares for a changed political environment in Washington.

The resignations follow midterm elections that shifted congressional power toward Democrats, increasing the likelihood of additional hearings, document requests and other oversight of the administration and Justice Department. The department can use acting officials to temporarily fill leadership positions, while permanent replacements for positions requiring Senate confirmation would have to go through the confirmation process.

The timing has prompted attention because several departures occurred within days of the election, but the available reporting does not establish a single reason behind all three resignations. The officials held separate positions with different responsibilities, and Dhillon has specifically said her exit was arranged before voters went to the polls.
