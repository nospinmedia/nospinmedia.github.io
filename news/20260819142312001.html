Pakistan Challenges Court Order on Imran Khan Hospital Transfer

Government seeks review of ruling granting private medical care

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 19, 2026 at 11:12 AM ET

Pakistan's government announced it will file a review petition challenging a Supreme Court of Pakistan order directing that jailed former Prime Minister Imran Khan be transferred from Adiala Jail in Rawalpindi to Shifa International Hospital in Islamabad for medical treatment. Law Minister Azam Nazeer Tarar argued that prison regulations generally require inmates to receive care within correctional facilities or at government hospitals and said the court's directive should be reconsidered.

The order, issued on August 18, 2026, directed authorities to move the 73-year-old founder of the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) party to the private hospital for evaluation by a medical board that includes specialists and one of his personal physicians, Dr. Faisal Sultan. The ruling also granted Khan weekly family visits and phone contact with his sons while the medical order remains in effect. Khan has been imprisoned since August 2023 following multiple convictions that he and his supporters maintain are politically motivated.

The government's planned appeal sets up a broader legal dispute over the balance between judicial authority, detainee rights and established prison regulations. Khan's legal team has argued that specialist treatment is necessary because of ongoing concerns about his health, including reported vision problems, while government officials maintain existing prison procedures should apply equally to all inmates.

The Supreme Court will now decide whether to uphold, modify or reverse its earlier directive. The outcome could influence how Pakistan's courts handle future requests involving medical treatment, visitation rights and detention conditions for high-profile prisoners while adding another chapter to the country's long-running political and legal conflict surrounding the former prime minister.
