Iran Warns Neighbors Against Backing New U.S. Sanctions

Security Chief Threatens Retaliation Over Regional Cooperation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 23, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Iran has warned neighboring countries that they will be treated as enemies if they join a new U.S. campaign to further isolate Tehran's economy, marking another escalation in regional tensions nearly six months into the conflict. In his first major televised interview since becoming secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, Mohsen Rezaei said governments that cooperate with Washington's expanding sanctions effort risk having their economic interests targeted by Iran. He also warned that Tehran could move against alternative oil-shipping routes serving the Persian Gulf if neighboring states participate in what he described as an American-led economic war.

Rezaei delivered the remarks during an interview with state broadcaster IRIB, saying Iran was not seeking to broaden the conflict but would retaliate if surrounding countries aligned themselves with the latest U.S. pressure campaign. He also warned that American commercial interests in the region could become targets if Washington intensifies its economic measures. The comments followed President Trump's announcement of a significantly expanded sanctions effort designed to increase economic pressure on Tehran and discourage other nations from conducting business with Iran.

The warning comes as regional governments reassess trade and energy policies amid continued instability around the Strait of Hormuz, a strategic waterway that carried roughly 20% of the world's oil supply before the conflict. Rezaei said discussions with Oman over management of the strait remain ongoing and indicated Iran intends to impose transit fees while maintaining pressure over access to the waterway. The United Arab Emirates has already suspended trade and financial transactions with Iran, underscoring the growing economic divide across the Gulf.

The latest rhetoric highlights the widening shift from direct military confrontation toward economic and commercial pressure. While diplomatic efforts continue through regional intermediaries, officials have provided no indication that either Washington or Tehran is prepared to ease its position, leaving neighboring countries caught between competing security and economic interests.
