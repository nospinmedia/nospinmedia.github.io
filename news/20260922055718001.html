Moscow refinery halts all crude processing after drone attack

Damage threatens major share of capital region's fuel supply

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 22, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Production at the Moscow oil refinery has been halted after a Ukrainian drone attack damaged both of the facility's primary crude-processing units, resulting in a 100% suspension of crude processing, according to industry sources familiar with the plant's operations.

The refinery, operated by Gazprom Neft, was struck on Sunday, September 20, during a large Ukrainian drone attack targeting Moscow and surrounding areas. Fires damaged the refinery's AVT-6 primary distillation unit, which accounts for about 53% of its crude-processing capacity, and its Euro+ combined processing unit, responsible for the remaining 47%. With both units affected, the refinery's crude-processing operations have stopped completely.

The disruption could have significant consequences for fuel supplies around the Russian capital. The refinery accounts for approximately 40% of Moscow's fuel market and about 70% of gasoline and aviation fuel supplies across the capital region. Fuel produced by the plant was removed from sale on the St. Petersburg International Mercantile Exchange beginning September 21 following the shutdown.

How long the refinery remains offline is uncertain. Industry estimates cited in reporting range from several weeks to as long as six months, depending on the extent of the damage and availability of replacement equipment. A prolonged shutdown could increase pressure on Russia to redirect fuel from other refineries to maintain supplies in Moscow and surrounding areas.

Ukraine has increasingly targeted Russian refineries and other energy infrastructure with long-range drones, arguing that the facilities provide fuel and revenue supporting Russia's military operations. The Moscow refinery strike was part of the same weekend barrage NSM previously reported after hundreds of drones targeted the capital region.
