Hochul removes local DA from Cornell sexual assault investigation

Attorney General Letitia James takes over as special prosecutor

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 2, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul has removed the Tompkins County District Attorney's Office from a reopened investigation into allegations that a former Cornell University student was gang-raped at a fraternity house in 2024, appointing Attorney General Letitia James as special prosecutor.

Hochul issued an executive order Thursday giving James' office authority over the investigation. The governor said recently released information raised serious questions about both the original Cornell University Police Department investigation and District Attorney Matthew Van Houten's decision not to pursue criminal charges. Hochul said those questions had undermined her confidence and public confidence in his office's ability to handle the case. James said her office would conduct a process guided by the facts and the law. If warranted, her office can present evidence to a grand jury.

The woman, identified as Jane Doe, filed a civil lawsuit in September alleging that seven members of the Chi Phi fraternity sexually assaulted her at the fraternity house in October 2024 after she became intoxicated. The allegations have not been proven in court, and lawyers representing some of the men have denied wrongdoing. No criminal charges have been filed.

Van Houten initially declined to prosecute the case but announced this week that he was reopening the investigation after the lawsuit presented allegations and information that he said differed from what his office had previously reviewed. Cornell has also agreed to an independent review of how the university handled the woman's allegations. The university says it suspended the Chi Phi chapter and took disciplinary action against students following its internal investigation.

The case has also renewed debate over New York's sexual assault laws. Current state law distinguishes between people who become intoxicated voluntarily and those who are given intoxicants without their knowledge or consent when determining legal incapacity to consent. State lawmakers are now pushing legislation that would change how the law addresses sexual activity involving people who are too intoxicated to consent.
