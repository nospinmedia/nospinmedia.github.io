Two survivors rescued 10 days after devastating Nepal floods

Crews pull victims alive from hydropower tunnel and buried home

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 7:42 PM ET

Rescue teams in Nepal pulled two people alive from flood debris on September 5, 10 days after catastrophic flooding swept through communities and infrastructure in the Himalayan nation. One survivor, 49-year-old Chinese hydropower worker Lu Haitao, was rescued from a tunnel at the Upper Trishuli-1 Hydropower Project in Rasuwa district. Separately, rescuers found 64-year-old Chandrika Kumari Shrestha alive inside a partially buried home in neighboring Nuwakot district.

Lu was located during a search involving the Nepali Army and international rescue specialists. He had been trapped approximately 225 meters inside a hydropower tunnel and was reported dehydrated but conscious and in stable condition after his extraction. Shrestha was discovered by Nepalese security personnel in the upper portion of a house surrounded by mud and debris in Betrawati, part of Bidur Municipality-10. Both survivors were transported for medical treatment following their rescues.

The discoveries came as emergency crews continued searching areas devastated by the August 26 floods. The disaster caused extensive damage along the Trishuli River corridor, destroying homes, roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure and leaving large numbers of people missing. Search teams have focused particular attention on damaged hydroelectric facilities and tunnels where workers may have been trapped when floodwaters and debris struck.

The rescue of Lu followed the recovery of other workers from hydropower infrastructure as crews expanded searches into tunnels and other difficult-to-reach areas. International personnel and specialized equipment have joined Nepal's military, police and emergency agencies in the continuing operation. Authorities have cautioned that unstable debris, damaged infrastructure and flooded underground passages are complicating rescue efforts.

Finding survivors more than a week after the flooding has provided renewed urgency to searches of remaining structures and tunnels, although officials have emphasized that conditions remain dangerous for both trapped people and rescuers.
