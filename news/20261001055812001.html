Musk, Luckey and Gingrich tapped to study future warfare

Pentagon gives Project Meridian 120 days to map battlefield technology

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 1, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has tapped Elon Musk, Palmer Luckey and former House Speaker Newt Gingrich to co-lead a new Pentagon initiative examining how technology could reshape warfare and what weapons and capabilities the U.S. military may need in future conflicts. The effort, called Project Meridian, was announced during Hegseth's State of the Force address at Marine Corps Base Quantico in Virginia on September 30.

The project will operate under the direction of Pentagon technology chief Emil Michael and has 120 days to complete its work. Hegseth said the group will study future battlefields, identify weapons and technologies U.S. forces may need and recommend actionable steps for developing, testing and fielding those capabilities. The final findings are expected to include an unclassified public report and a classified annex.

Hegseth said rapid advances in artificial intelligence, autonomous systems, directed energy, robotics and biotechnology could substantially change warfare. He argued that forecasting those changes should include expertise from outside the Pentagon rather than relying exclusively on the military's existing institutions. Other participants from technology, academia and policy are expected to join the project.

The appointments also raise conflict-of-interest questions because companies associated with two of the project's leaders already conduct substantial business with the Pentagon. Musk leads SpaceX, while Luckey founded defense technology company Anduril Industries. Both companies hold major U.S. defense contracts. The Pentagon has not indicated that Project Meridian itself will award contracts, and Hegseth described the initiative as a study of future warfare rather than an effort to establish new military strategy or policy.

Project Meridian is part of a broader Pentagon push toward emerging military technology. Hegseth simultaneously announced plans for a new Autonomous Warfare Command, a four-star organization intended to accelerate the development and deployment of drones, robotics and other autonomous systems across the armed forces.
