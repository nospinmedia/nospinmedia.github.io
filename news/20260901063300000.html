Vance and El-Sayed trade sharp attacks in Michigan Senate race

Vice president campaigns for Mike Rogers as rhetoric intensifies

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 1, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Vice President JD Vance sharply escalated campaign rhetoric during a Michigan rally on August 31, 2026, calling Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed "very, very evil" while campaigning for Republican nominee Mike Rogers. Speaking at an event organized by MAGA Inc. in Sterling Heights, Vance accused El-Sayed of promoting division and also warned him to stop making comments about Second Lady Usha Vance, saying, "keep my wife's name the hell out of your mouth."

El-Sayed rejected the vice president's accusations, arguing that the Trump administration's policies—not his campaign—have harmed working families. He pointed to issues including health care, tariffs, and the cost of living while defending his previous criticism of Vance. The exchange marked one of the most personal confrontations of the campaign and highlighted the increasingly combative tone surrounding Michigan's closely watched Senate contest.

The race between Mike Rogers and Abdul El-Sayed is considered one of the most competitive Senate elections of the 2026 midterms, with both parties investing heavily in advertising and campaign appearances. National political figures have made repeated visits to Michigan as Republicans seek to defend and expand their Senate position while Democrats attempt to keep the open seat in their column. The campaign has focused on economic policy, immigration, foreign policy, and issues affecting key suburban and working-class voters.

Political analysts expect campaign rhetoric to remain intense as Election Day approaches, with outside organizations continuing to spend millions of dollars on advertising and voter outreach. Both campaigns have framed the election as nationally significant, arguing that control of the U.S. Senate could hinge on the outcome of Michigan's race.
