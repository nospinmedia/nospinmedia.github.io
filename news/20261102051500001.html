Russian strikes knock out power and water across parts of Ukraine

New overnight attacks kill 1 as both sides target infrastructure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 2, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Russian drone and missile attacks continued across Ukraine overnight into Monday, damaging power and water infrastructure and causing new casualties as both sides intensified long-range strikes against infrastructure away from the front lines.

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 193 strike drones during the latest overnight attack from November 1 into November 2, with Ukrainian defenses intercepting or suppressing 154. Ukrainian authorities reported one person killed in Izium and at least 17 people injured across the Kharkiv and Odesa regions. The military figures are based on Ukrainian reporting and could not be independently verified.

The new attack followed a larger Russian barrage a day earlier involving 433 drones and missiles. Ukraine said 371 drones were intercepted or suppressed during that assault. Strikes damaged energy facilities in multiple regions, including Dnipropetrovsk, where more than 100,000 customers lost electricity. Grid operator Ukrenergo imposed power restrictions while crews worked to repair damaged equipment.

Infrastructure disruptions expanded beyond electricity Monday. A Russian drone strike damaged facilities in Bila Tserkva, south of Kyiv, disrupting the city's municipal water supply and prompting emergency repair work.

Ukraine continued its own long-range attacks inside Russia. Ukrainian drones struck the industrial city of Kstovo in Russia's Nizhny Novgorod region, where Ukrainian and Russian reporting indicated damage around the Lukoil oil refinery and a nearby combined heat-and-power plant. The city is hundreds of miles from the Ukrainian border.

The escalation followed the expiration of a temporary ceasefire around Kherson. President Trump had said Russian President Vladimir Putin agreed to pause attacks there for one week following a request from Washington, though fighting elsewhere continued.

Both countries have increasingly targeted energy infrastructure as colder weather approaches.
