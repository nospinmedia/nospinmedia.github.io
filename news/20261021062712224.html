Kagan says Supreme Court rulings are worsening political polarization

Justice criticizes decisions on voting rights, campaign finance and redistricting

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 21, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Supreme Court Justice Elena Kagan criticized several decisions by the court's conservative majority, arguing that rulings involving elections and voting have contributed to political polarization and weakened the way American democracy functions.

Speaking Tuesday at Catholic University's law school in Washington, D.C., Kagan focused on Supreme Court decisions that weakened parts of the Voting Rights Act, reduced campaign finance restrictions and limited the ability of federal courts to intervene in partisan gerrymandering disputes. Kagan dissented in several of the decisions she discussed.

Kagan said the court has failed to adequately consider how its rulings affect the political system. She pointed specifically to partisan redistricting, saying both major parties have increasingly pursued maps designed to maximize their electoral advantage and that the practice has contributed to greater polarization.

The court ruled in 2019 that claims of excessive partisan gerrymandering present political questions beyond the reach of federal courts. Conservative majorities have also struck down campaign finance restrictions on free-speech grounds and, more recently, further narrowed protections under the Voting Rights Act. Supporters of those rulings have argued that the court is applying constitutional and statutory limits rather than deciding cases based on their political consequences.

Kagan also rejected the idea that the court's conservative majority automatically sides with President Trump. She cited recent cases involving tariffs, birthright citizenship and mail voting in which the court pushed back against positions taken by his administration.

Her comments come less than two weeks before the November 3 midterm elections, when control of Congress will be decided and disputes over voting rules and congressional districts remain prominent in several states.
