Chicago Bears Legend Mike Ditka Dies at 86

Hall of Fame Tight End Coached Iconic 1985 Team to Super Bowl Victory

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 10, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Mike Ditka, the Hall of Fame tight end and outspoken head coach who led the Chicago Bears to one of the most celebrated championships in NFL history, died on Friday, October 9, 2026, at age 86. His family announced his death, prompting tributes from across professional football. A cause of death was not disclosed.

Known as Iron Mike and Da Coach, Ditka became synonymous with Chicago football through a career that included championships as a player and coach. His greatest coaching achievement came during the 1985 NFL season, when the Bears finished 15-1 before defeating the New England Patriots 46-10 in Super Bowl XX on January 26, 1986.

The team featured one of the most dominant defenses in NFL history, with stars including Walter Payton, Mike Singletary, Richard Dent, and William Perry. Its success extended beyond football through the popular Super Bowl Shuffle music video, helping turn the Bears into a national cultural phenomenon.

Ditka's influence began long before his coaching career. Selected fifth overall in the 1961 NFL Draft by Chicago after playing at the University of Pittsburgh, he helped transform the tight end position from primarily a blocking role into a major receiving threat. During his rookie season, he caught 56 passes for 1,076 yards and 12 touchdowns, becoming the first NFL tight end to surpass 1,000 receiving yards in a season.

He played 12 NFL seasons with the Bears, Philadelphia Eagles, and Dallas Cowboys, finishing with 427 receptions, 5,812 receiving yards, and 43 touchdowns. He earned five Pro Bowl selections and was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1988, becoming the first tight end to receive that honor.

Ditka won the 1963 NFL championship with Chicago and Super Bowl VI as a Dallas player. He later helped coach the Cowboys to victory in Super Bowl XII before returning to Chicago as head coach in 1982.

During 11 seasons coaching the Bears, Ditka led the franchise to six division championships and earned two NFL Coach of the Year awards. He later coached the New Orleans Saints from 1997 through 1999 and became a familiar television football analyst.

Following his death, the Chicago Bears and Pro Football Hall of Fame honored his contributions. Current Bears head coach Ben Johnson described Ditka as a defining figure whose influence remains visible throughout the organization.

Beyond his championships, Ditka leaves a legacy of toughness, competitive intensity, and innovation that helped shape modern professional football.
