Toyota Recalls 655,000 Camrys Over Instrument Display Defect

Software update fixes turn-signal and safety warning failure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Toyota Motor Corporation is recalling about 655,000 2025–2026 Toyota Camry Hybrid sedans worldwide after a software defect can cause the 7-inch digital combination meter to go blank during startup. The malfunction can prevent drivers from seeing the speedometer, fuel gauge, warning lights, and turn-signal indicators while also disabling audible safety alerts such as seat belt and key reminder chimes. Regulators say the defect increases the risk of a crash because drivers and other motorists may not receive important safety information.

More than 508,000 vehicles in the United States are included in the recall. Toyota said the issue affects certain Camry Hybrid models built between December 2023 and July 2026 that are equipped with the 7-inch instrument display. Vehicles fitted with the optional 12.3-inch digital instrument cluster are not affected. The company identified the problem as a software error that can prevent the display from booting correctly when the vehicle is started.

Toyota will correct the problem by installing updated software in the combination meter free of charge at dealerships. The company expects to begin mailing owner notification letters on September 21, 2026, with notifications continuing into early October. Owners can also check whether their vehicle is included by using Toyota's recall lookup service or the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's VIN lookup tool.

The recall affects one of Toyota's best-selling passenger cars and highlights the growing role software plays in modern vehicle safety systems. While no recall can eliminate every potential defect, manufacturers are increasingly relying on software updates to address problems that once required replacement parts or mechanical repairs.
