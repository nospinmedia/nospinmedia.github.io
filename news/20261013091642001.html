NATO Commander Says Alliance Is Ready to Fight Tonight

Top General Unveils Three-Stage Defense Strategy Amid Rising Tensions With Russia

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 13, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

NATO's top military commander declared Monday, October 12, 2026, that the alliance is prepared to defend its territory immediately if attacked, while emphasizing that war is neither imminent nor inevitable. Speaking at the Warsaw Security Forum in Poland, U.S. Air Force General Alexus Grynkewich outlined a defensive strategy intended to make any military aggression against a NATO member unsuccessful.

Grynkewich, who serves as Supreme Allied Commander Europe and commander of U.S. European Command, described NATO's preparedness using the phrase 'ready to fight tonight.' His remarks came amid growing European concerns about suspected Russian sabotage, drone incursions and other hostile activities affecting alliance members.

The general presented what he called NATO's Theory of Victory, a three-stage approach summarized as Block, Blunt and Break. He said the strategy was designed to stop an invading force, prevent further advances and, if necessary, dismantle the military systems supporting an attack.

Under the first stage, Block, NATO ground forces would move to prevent an enemy from seizing allied territory, supported by air, naval, space and cyber capabilities.

The second stage, Blunt, would use coordinated military operations, advanced sensors and autonomous systems to disrupt additional attacks and prevent enemy forces from gaining momentum.

The final stage, Break, would target the attacking force's ability to continue fighting, including its logistics, communications and command structures. Grynkewich emphasized that the objective was to defeat aggression against NATO territory rather than pursue a broader war.

According to the general, the alliance's 32 member countries agreed on a classified version of the defensive concept approximately three months ago. He said NATO was working to translate that strategy into operational readiness through military exercises, improved coordination and investments in modern defense capabilities.

Although Grynkewich did not identify Russia as the intended target of his warning, his remarks followed repeated allegations by European governments that Moscow has supported sabotage, arson and other covert operations across the continent. Russia has denied involvement in various incidents attributed to its intelligence services.

The general also reaffirmed the importance of American participation in NATO and continued support for Ukraine. His message sought to reassure the alliance's approximately one billion residents while discouraging potential adversaries from testing NATO's collective defenses.

Grynkewich stressed that military preparedness serves a deterrent purpose: convincing potential attackers that aggression would fail before a conflict begins.
