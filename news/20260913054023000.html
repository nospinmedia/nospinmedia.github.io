BRICS reaches consensus on Middle East tensions

New Delhi Declaration calls for maximum restraint

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 13, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Leaders of BRICS unanimously adopted the New Delhi Declaration on September 12, overcoming divisions within the expanded group to call for “maximum restraint” amid escalating conflict in the Middle East. The declaration was approved on the first day of the 18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi, hosted by Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, and calls on parties to avoid actions that could further aggravate regional tensions.

The declaration expresses “deep concern” over the continuing escalation in the Middle East/West Asia while acknowledging the individual national positions of BRICS members. It calls for protection of civilians and civilian infrastructure, respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity, and greater diplomatic efforts toward a long-term settlement. Members also emphasized maintaining the flow of global trade, supply chains and energy in accordance with international law.

Reaching consensus was significant because BRICS includes countries with sharply different positions on the conflict, particularly Iran and the United Arab Emirates. Earlier meetings under India's 2026 BRICS presidency had failed to produce a common position on the war. At a foreign ministers' meeting in May, members acknowledged differing views after Iran sought explicit criticism of U.S. and Israeli actions while the UAE opposed language that did not address Iranian attacks. Negotiations over the leaders' declaration continued for several days before agreement was reached.

The declaration also rejected unilateral economic measures inconsistent with international law and criticized tariff and non-tariff measures that distort trade. On the Middle East, the document avoided assigning responsibility to a particular BRICS member and instead emphasized dialogue, consultation and diplomacy. The bloc also reiterated its opposition to attacks or threats against peaceful nuclear facilities under International Atomic Energy Agency safeguards.

BRICS now brings together 11 member countries and represents roughly half of the world's population, giving its ability to reach consensus on major international conflicts added diplomatic significance.
