Steve Hilton proposes dismantling California DMV as standalone agency

GOP candidate would cap annual vehicle registration at $73

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 11, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

California Republican gubernatorial candidate Steve Hilton has proposed eliminating the Department of Motor Vehicles as a stand-alone state agency and capping annual vehicle registration costs at a flat $73 per vehicle. Hilton unveiled the plan on September 8 outside a DMV office in West Hollywood as part of his broader "Califordable" campaign platform.

Under the proposal, the DMV would first undergo a three-month audit before being reorganized. A smaller state operation would remain responsible for functions including driver's licenses, fraud prevention, REAL ID compliance, records and safety oversight, while routine vehicle title, registration and renewal services would move to county offices and certified local service centers.

Hilton's campaign says no existing DMV field office would close until an equivalent or better in-person service option was available nearby. Properties no longer needed by the state could be offered to community colleges and apprenticeship programs for job-training uses or potentially sold.

The most significant financial element is Hilton's proposed $73 annual registration cap. His campaign estimates that the change would reduce state registration revenue from roughly $11 billion to $2.7 billion. Hilton says the lost revenue would be offset through a separate spending-cutting initiative he calls Operation Zero Waste, but that is a campaign projection rather than an independently verified finding that the savings would fully cover the difference.

The proposal would require legislative action, including what Hilton's campaign has described as a "No More DMV Act." ABC7 also reported that Hilton wants more licensing functions handled online and other DMV responsibilities shifted to counties and local governments.

Democratic gubernatorial candidate Xavier Becerra's campaign has criticized the proposal, arguing that outsourcing driver-safety functions could create risks. Hilton and Becerra are scheduled to debate on September 30 ahead of the November general election.
