U.S. destroys five Iranian oil tankers after missile attacks

Iran retaliates as Brent crude pushes above $100

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 9, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

The U.S. military says it destroyed five Iranian oil tankers after Iranian forces attempted ballistic-missile attacks on an American warship, escalating a conflict that is also putting renewed pressure on global energy prices. Brent crude briefly climbed above $100 a barrel Wednesday, reaching its highest level in about six weeks as traders weighed growing risks to Middle East oil supplies and shipping routes.

U.S. Central Command said forces struck the tankers Kivik, Charminar, Horizon 1 and Riesco in the Gulf of Oman and Derya near Iran's Kharg Island. CENTCOM said crews were warned to abandon the vessels before the strikes. The action followed two attempted Iranian ballistic-missile attacks on a U.S. Navy warship. The military said the ship avoided the missiles and no American personnel were injured.

Iran responded by launching missiles toward U.S. targets in Jordan. Jordan's military said its air defenses intercepted 18 ballistic missiles, while two others landed in uninhabited areas. No casualties were reported. Iran's Revolutionary Guard also warned crews aboard tankers near ports in Kuwait and Bahrain to leave their vessels, raising additional concerns about commercial shipping in the region.

The latest strikes came only days after U.S. forces disabled three other Iranian tankers following attempted attacks on American warships. The repeated exchanges are occurring around the Strait of Hormuz, a critical route for global petroleum shipments that has already faced severe disruption during the U.S.-Iran conflict.

Oil markets are reacting to the increased threat to supply. Brent reached about $100.19 a barrel early Wednesday before easing slightly, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude rose above $94. Higher crude prices can eventually translate into increased gasoline, transportation and manufacturing costs if sustained.
