Peggy Flanagan Wins Minnesota Democratic Senate Primary

Lisa Demuth defeats Trump-backed Mike Lindell in GOP governor's race

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Minnesota Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan won the Democratic primary for the U.S. Senate on August 11, 2026, defeating U.S. Rep. Angie Craig in one of the nation's most closely watched Democratic contests. Flanagan, a citizen of the White Earth Nation, advances to the November general election against Republican Michele Tafoya in the race to succeed retiring Sen. Tina Smith. If elected, Flanagan would become the first Native American woman elected to the U.S. Senate.

The primary drew national attention as a contest between the party's progressive and more moderate wings. Flanagan campaigned on expanding affordable housing, strengthening labor protections, and opposing the Trump administration's immigration policies, while Craig argued her record of winning competitive congressional races made her the strongest candidate for November. Flanagan secured support from several prominent progressive leaders during the campaign and won despite Craig's financial advantage.

Minnesota voters also settled the Republican race for governor, where House Speaker Lisa Demuth defeated Mike Lindell, the MyPillow founder who was endorsed by President Trump, along with former gubernatorial candidate Kendall Qualls. Demuth will face Democratic nominee Amy Klobuchar in November to succeed outgoing Gov. Tim Walz, who is not seeking another term.

The results finalize two of Minnesota's highest-profile statewide races ahead of the November 3, 2026 election. Both contests are expected to draw significant national attention as Democrats defend an open Senate seat while Republicans attempt to win the governor's office for the first time since 2006.
