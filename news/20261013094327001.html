Man Dies After Falling From Roller Coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia

Fatal Incident Prompts Precautionary Ride Closures Across Multiple States

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 13, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A man died after falling from a roller coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia on Monday, October 12, 2026, prompting an investigation and precautionary closures of similar rides at Six Flags parks across the country.

Police and witnesses said the man fell from the Goliath roller coaster at the amusement park in Mableton, Georgia, near Atlanta. Emergency crews responded to the park following reports of the incident, and authorities confirmed that one person had died. Several other people were reported injured.

Goliath is one of the park's largest roller coasters. The steel ride reaches approximately 200 feet at its highest point and speeds of up to 70 mph. Investigators are working to determine how the rider was able to fall from the attraction and whether the incident involved the restraint system, another mechanical issue or other circumstances.

The Cobb County Police Department is investigating the death. Officials have not publicly announced a final cause of the incident, and authorities were continuing to gather witness accounts and examine evidence from the ride.

Following the fatality, Six Flags stopped operations on Goliath while investigators and safety personnel examined the attraction. Riders who were on the coaster when operations were halted were assisted from the ride.

The incident also prompted a broader precautionary response from Six Flags Entertainment Corporation. Similar roller coasters at parks in multiple states were temporarily closed while inspections were conducted, expanding the impact of the investigation beyond the Georgia amusement park.

The closures are intended to allow engineers and safety teams to examine comparable ride systems before operations resume. Authorities had not established that the other rides suffered from the same problem or that a mechanical failure caused the Georgia fatality.

Amusement park rides operate under state safety requirements that vary across the United States, with parks also conducting their own inspections and maintenance programs. A fatal incident involving a major attraction can lead to additional inspections by regulators, manufacturers and park operators before a ride is cleared to reopen.

Investigators are expected to examine the coaster's restraint system, maintenance records, operating procedures and available video as they determine what happened.
