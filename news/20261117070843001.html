Suspect In Custody After Bomb Found At Illinois Ethanol Plant

FBI investigation continues after device forced thousands to evacuate

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 17, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A person of interest was taken into custody on Monday, November 16, in connection with an explosive device discovered at the BioUrja Renewables ethanol plant in Peoria, Illinois, marking a major development days after the threat forced thousands of residents and workers from the surrounding area.

Peoria Mayor Rita Ali said BioUrja leadership informed her that a suspect was in custody. Peoria police initially issued and then briefly retracted a statement about the detention before confirming that the FBI's Chicago office had informed them a person of interest was being questioned. Separately, an unnamed law enforcement official cited by CNN said an arrest had been made and investigators were examining whether anyone else was involved. Authorities had not publicly identified the individual or announced formal charges.

The incident began on Friday, November 13, when authorities responded to a bomb threat at the ethanol facility. Bomb technicians subsequently located an explosive device and rendered it safe. Because the plant handles combustible materials, officials ordered an evacuation within approximately one mile of the facility, covering portions of Downtown Peoria, the Warehouse District and East Peoria.

The evacuation lasted roughly 18 hours, displacing thousands of residents and workers before officials lifted the order around 1 p.m. Saturday. Emergency shelter was provided at Manual High School, while the response also disrupted nearby roads and commercial traffic on the Illinois River.

On Monday, Peoria police assisted federal authorities with court-authorized law enforcement activity at the plant beginning around 7 a.m. The investigation involves the FBI, Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, Illinois State Police and local law enforcement.

Investigators have not publicly disclosed a motive or detailed the construction of the explosive device. Federal authorities also have not said whether additional arrests are expected.
