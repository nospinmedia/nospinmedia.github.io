Russia could launch nuclear-armed satellite within 60 days, intelligence warns

NATO vows space defense as Germany takes reports 'very seriously'

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 24, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Russia could be preparing to launch a nuclear-armed satellite within 60 days, according to Western intelligence reports that have prompted Germany to raise concerns and NATO to pledge that its defenses extend into space.

The findings were first reported November 23 in a joint investigation by German public broadcasters WDR and NDR and newspaper Süddeutsche Zeitung. The outlets cited intelligence assessments exchanged between Germany's foreign intelligence service, the BND, and the Bundeswehr.

According to the reports, warnings circulated among NATO allies in early November indicating Russia could launch a nuclear-equipped spacecraft, reportedly called Sputnik-S, potentially as soon as December 2026. The spacecraft was reportedly assembled several years ago and has since been stored in the Moscow region.

The intelligence has not been publicly verified, and Russia has not announced such a launch.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte, speaking alongside German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, said the alliance would respond to threats extending beyond Earth.

"We will always make sure we defend every inch of NATO territory, and that includes making sure we are safe when it comes to the space element," Rutte said. Germany's government said it was taking the reports "very seriously."

A nuclear anti-satellite weapon would not necessarily be intended to strike Earth. Detonating a nuclear device in low-Earth orbit could generate an electromagnetic pulse and radiation capable of damaging or destroying large numbers of military and commercial satellites, potentially disrupting communications, navigation and reconnaissance systems.

Placing a nuclear weapon in orbit would violate the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, while detonating one there would also violate the 1963 Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty.

Russia rejected the allegations. The Russian Embassy in Berlin called them "unfounded insinuations" and part of a "propaganda campaign." Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov separately said outer space "must be free of any weapon." Moscow has previously denied U.S. assessments that it is developing a space-based nuclear anti-satellite capability.

Western governments have monitored that possibility for years, including concerns surrounding Russia's Kosmos 2553 satellite, launched in 2022 and linked by U.S. officials to testing associated with a potential anti-satellite system. Russia disputes that characterization.

For now, the reported 60-day window remains an intelligence assessment, not a confirmed Russian launch schedule.
