US freezes green-card applications for Microsoft and 7 tech firms

Labor Department blocks new and pending certifications over alleged hiring abuses

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 9, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

The Trump administration suspended Microsoft, Adobe and six major IT services companies from a federal employment-based green-card certification program on October 8, 2026, citing alleged abuses of immigration and hiring rules. The action prevents the U.S. Department of Labor from accepting new or processing pending permanent labor certification applications involving the eight companies, potentially disrupting a pathway to permanent residency for foreign technology workers.

Vice President JD Vance announced the restrictions alongside Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling. The affected companies are:

- Microsoft - Adobe - Cognizant - Infosys - Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) - Wipro - HCL Technologies - Capgemini

The suspension targets the Permanent Labor Certification Program (PERM), which employers commonly use as an initial step toward sponsoring foreign workers for employment-based green cards. Employers must demonstrate that hiring a foreign worker will not adversely affect the wages or working conditions of similarly employed U.S. workers and that qualified American workers are not available for the position.

The H-1B temporary work visa program has not been suspended. Workers holding valid H-1B visas can continue working under their existing authorization. The restrictions instead affect the permanent labor certification process used by the named employers.

Vance singled out Microsoft, alleging that the company laid off approximately 6,000 American workers while benefiting from 6,300 H-1B visas and nearly 3,000 green cards. He accused companies of using ineffective domestic recruitment advertisements to justify hiring foreign workers rather than qualified Americans. These are administration allegations, not established findings of fraud against every affected employer.

Microsoft disputed the characterization of its hiring practices. The company said approximately 80% of its H-1B applications during the previous fiscal year involved extensions or status changes for existing employees rather than new hires. Microsoft also said it pays H-1B employees compensation comparable to other workers performing similar jobs.

Sonderling said the department would neither accept new nor process pending PERM applications involving the companies. Vance indicated that the restrictions would remain in place until the administration was satisfied with changes to their hiring practices.

The announcement came alongside investigations into nine universities, including Harvard, Yale, Stanford, Brown and MIT, over alleged misuse of exchange-visitor visa programs. Federal officials said investigators would examine potential visa abuses and improper financial relationships involving federally funded research.

The restrictions could complicate permanent residency plans for affected foreign employees, although their immediate impact will vary. Indian technology industry representatives said major outsourcing companies have reduced their reliance on employment-based green cards in recent years.
