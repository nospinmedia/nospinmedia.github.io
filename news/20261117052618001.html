17 Killed In Two Mass Shootings In South Africa

Police hunt suspects as motives remain unknown

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 17, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

At least 17 people were killed in two separate mass shootings in South Africa on Sunday, November 15, with police investigating attacks roughly 530 kilometers (330 miles) apart in Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal provinces. Authorities have not established motives for either shooting and have not announced arrests connected to the attacks.

In Jeffsville, an informal settlement in Atteridgeville near Pretoria, police found nine people — six men and three women — dead from gunshot wounds inside a four-room shack. Gauteng police spokesperson Brigadier Brenda Muridili confirmed the deaths. The shooting is believed to have occurred between 10 p.m. and 11 p.m. Sunday, with police alerted Monday morning after a young child entered the shack looking for a relative and discovered the bodies.

Gauteng Police Commissioner Tommy Mthombeni said investigators are examining a possible connection to a 2025 mass shooting in nearby Saulsville that killed 12 people and wounded 13. One person killed in Jeffsville had been a person of interest in that earlier case. Police have not established retaliation or any other motive. Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi visited the crime scene Monday and said the provincial government was coordinating with other departments to support the investigation.

In the separate KwaZulu-Natal attack, eight men were found shot dead inside a bullet-riddled vehicle in Sweetwaters, outside Pietermaritzburg. Provincial police spokesperson Robert Netshiunda said some families had come forward to assist authorities in identifying the victims, although formal identifications were still pending.

National Police Commissioner Fannie Masemola condemned the killings and directed provincial police leadership to devote resources to finding those responsible. Authorities have not said the Jeffsville and Sweetwaters shootings are connected to each other.
