Trump Orders Creation of U.S. Space Academy Commission

Panel has 120 days to design new federal institution

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 29, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

President Trump signed an executive order establishing a Presidential Commission on the United States Space Academy, launching the first formal effort to create a federal undergraduate institution dedicated to educating future leaders in military, civil and commercial space operations. Announced during a ceremony at NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston, the initiative envisions an academy modeled after longstanding federal service academies such as West Point, the U.S. Naval Academy and the U.S. Air Force Academy, with graduates prepared for careers in the U.S. Space Force, NASA and the growing private aerospace industry.

The executive order names NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman as commission chair and directs the panel to deliver recommendations to the president within 120 days. The commission will examine academic programs, governance, admissions standards, campus location, funding requirements and potential service commitments for graduates. Michael Kratsios, Assistant to the President for Science and Technology, and Kevin Hassett, Assistant to the President for Economic Policy, will serve as vice chairs, while NASA Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson will act as executive director. Other members include Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles and Office of Management and Budget Director Russell Vought.

According to the order, the commission will evaluate whether the academy should operate within NASA, function as a joint civilian-military institution or adopt another organizational structure. It will also recommend curriculum requirements focused on engineering, science, space operations, cybersecurity and leadership while considering partnerships with universities, government agencies and private aerospace companies. Trump said multiple states have already expressed interest in hosting the future campus, although no location has been selected.

The announcement reflects the administration's broader emphasis on expanding America's space capabilities amid increasing international competition and rapid commercial growth in the aerospace sector. Any permanent academy would still require congressional action to authorize funding, establish the institution and determine its long-term governance after the commission submits its recommendations later this year.
