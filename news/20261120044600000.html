Trump, Xi and Putin issue joint peace declaration

Nuclear pledge comes without arms-control breakthrough

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 20, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin ended their unprecedented three-way summit in Shenzhen, China, with a four-point joint peace declaration pledging dialogue on nuclear risks but no timetable for an arms-control agreement or breakthroughs on the conflicts dividing the three powers.

The declaration reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought and committed the United States, China and Russia to dialogue aimed at reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation. The leaders also pledged support for reforms of the United Nations, increased global fuel supplies and immunity for state officials in international courts. The statement did not address Russia's war in Ukraine or the U.S. conflict with Iran.

Trump told Reuters afterward that securing a three-way nuclear limit remained "a very hard thing," saying China wanted the United States to reduce its nuclear arsenal by about 2,000 weapons before Beijing would accept comparable limits. Trump said he would provide additional details at a White House press conference Friday, November 20.

Putin said the declaration did not establish ambitious goals and that no country would be forced to comply with its decisions. He also said he would consider attending the December 2026 G20 summit in Miami after discussing the gathering with Trump. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, meanwhile, characterized the Shenzhen meeting as a "photo op" as European leaders stressed that decisions involving Ukraine required participation by Ukraine and Europe.

Separate U.S.-China talks on the sidelines produced additional agreements. Trump and Xi agreed to develop an action plan for "constructive strategic stability" and continue bilateral work on artificial intelligence safety guardrails. They did not finalize an anticipated $30 billion trade framework.

The summit therefore produced a formal joint declaration and additional U.S.-China commitments, but no binding nuclear-arms agreement or resolution of the major conflicts discussed around the meeting.
