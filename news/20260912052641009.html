Court rejects order keeping Michigan coal plant open

Judges say Energy Department exceeded emergency authority

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A federal appeals court has rejected the Trump administration's use of emergency powers to keep a Michigan coal-fired power plant operating beyond its planned retirement, ruling that the Department of Energy exceeded its authority under federal law. The unanimous decision from the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit concerns the J.H. Campbell Generating Plant in West Olive, which Consumers Energy had planned to retire on May 31, 2025.

Energy Secretary Chris Wright used Section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act to require the 1,420-megawatt plant to remain available, arguing that additional generation was necessary to protect electric-grid reliability. Michigan, Illinois and Minnesota joined environmental groups in challenging the federal intervention. Judge Cornelia Pillard, writing for the three-judge panel, concluded that the provision is a narrow emergency power intended as a last resort and that the circumstances surrounding Campbell's planned retirement did not constitute the type of emergency required by the law.

The financial consequences have become a major part of the dispute. Consumers Energy reported approximately $259 million in net costs from operating Campbell beyond its scheduled retirement through June, with those expenses potentially affecting electricity customers across portions of the Midwest. Michigan officials had argued that the plant's retirement was planned years in advance and replacement electricity resources had already been arranged.

The ruling could also affect legal challenges involving other power plants. The administration has issued emergency orders aimed at preventing the retirement of several generating facilities around the country, maintaining that keeping existing plants available is necessary as electricity demand rises. Critics argue the orders improperly override state energy planning and force customers to subsidize aging plants.

The decision does not necessarily mean Campbell will shut down immediately. Consumers Energy said it is reviewing the ruling and complying with the government's latest temporary order, which currently directs the plant to remain available until November 14.
