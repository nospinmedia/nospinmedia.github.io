Officer and man wounded in gunfire near Ontario synagogue

Motive remains unknown after Yom Kippur services are canceled

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 21, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A police officer and a man were wounded in an exchange of gunfire near the Sons of Jacob Synagogue in Belleville, Ontario, on September 20, as members of the Jewish community gathered to observe Yom Kippur. Authorities have not established whether the synagogue or Jewish community was targeted.

The Belleville Police Service said an officer encountered the man near Bleecker Avenue and Victoria Avenue at about 7:10 p.m. Both were injured and taken to a hospital. Ontario's Special Investigations Unit, the civilian agency responsible for investigating serious incidents involving police, said the man was hospitalized in life-threatening condition. Authorities had not publicly disclosed the officer's condition in the initial reports.

Police said there was no continuing threat to public safety. Investigators had not disclosed what led to the encounter or the exchange of gunfire. No one inside the synagogue was injured, but the congregation said its remaining Yom Kippur services were canceled following the shooting.

Ontario Premier Doug Ford condemned the violence and expressed support for the Jewish community and the wounded officer. Police agencies elsewhere in Ontario also increased their visibility around synagogues and other places of worship as a precaution. Toronto Police said there was no known threat or connection to Toronto but that patrols were being increased out of an abundance of caution.

The timing and location of the shooting prompted immediate concern because it occurred during Yom Kippur, the holiest day on the Jewish calendar. However, investigators had not announced a motive or evidence showing that the synagogue, its congregation or the Jewish community was deliberately targeted. The investigation remained active early September 21.
