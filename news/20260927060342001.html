Former Kennedy Center employees allege planned repairs were halted

Whistleblowers raise questions about decisions before safety closure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 27, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Former employees of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts allege that officials halted planned repairs to known water-intrusion problems months before structural concerns led to a temporary closure of the Washington, D.C., landmark.

The whistleblower disclosure was submitted to Congress by attorney David Seide of the Government Accountability Project. Documents provided to Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island indicate that $9.3 million in federal funding had been secured for roof remediation work. The former employees allege that Kennedy Center leadership nevertheless stopped work on the project in April 2026.

The allegations focus in part on Executive Director Matt Floca, who previously served as the Kennedy Center's Vice President of Facilities in 2024. Whistleblowers contend that Floca was involved in identifying the building's repair needs and securing funding before becoming executive director, raising questions about how much management knew about the deterioration before later citing serious safety concerns.

The whistleblowers also allege that $1.4 million previously earmarked for waterproofing repairs was redirected to help fund a $4.4 million project to paint the building's exterior columns. That allegation has become part of the dispute over whether available federal money was appropriately prioritized as structural problems persisted.

In a September 2026 court declaration, Floca cited acute risks to public safety following inspections involving a deteriorating roof terrace canopy and a partial ceiling collapse. Kennedy Center leadership disputes the suggestion that necessary repairs were simply abandoned. Floca has said proposed repair work became substantially more expensive than anticipated and was incorporated into a broader renovation effort as inspections revealed more extensive deterioration.

Whitehouse has requested information about repair decisions and the use of federal funding at the center. The whistleblower allegations have not established that Kennedy Center officials deliberately allowed unsafe conditions to develop, but the previously secured repair funding and allegations about how some of that money was used add new questions about why planned remediation was halted.
