ICE officer shoots father with 5-year-old son in car

Seven shots fired during NYC arrest operation spark protests

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 9, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

A federal immigration officer shot and wounded a 28-year-old Dominican man while his 5-year-old son was inside the vehicle during an enforcement operation in New York City's Marble Hill neighborhood on Thursday, October 8, 2026. The shooting occurred around 4 p.m. near West 225th Street and Marble Hill Avenue, triggering protests and demands for answers from city and state officials.

New York Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch identified the wounded man as Oscar Belgal. According to Tisch, surveillance footage showed Belgal pulling into a parking space when two plainclothes federal officers approached his vehicle. One officer approached the driver's window with his gun drawn, while another moved toward the front of the car. Belgal then began moving the vehicle forward and out of the camera's view.

Tisch said evidence collected at the scene indicated that at least one federal officer fired seven rounds, striking Belgal in the neck below his left ear. The surveillance camera did not capture the shooting itself. Belgal survived and was transported to a hospital with injuries that were not considered life-threatening. His son was physically unharmed and placed in the care of a family member.

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) said officers were conducting a targeted operation to arrest Belgal, whom the agency described as having a prior criminal conviction. According to Tisch, Belgal pleaded guilty to grand larceny following a 2023 arrest and received probation. He also faces an unresolved drug-related charge from 2025, to which he has pleaded not guilty.

DHS confirmed that an ICE officer discharged his weapon but did not explain what prompted the shooting. The agency said federal officers provided immediate medical assistance. Federal authorities are investigating the incident.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani condemned the shooting and said he spent approximately two hours speaking with President Trump and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, unsuccessfully seeking Belgal's release from federal custody. Mamdani said federal authorities subsequently removed Belgal from the hospital. Governor Kathy Hochul also contacted federal officials and demanded accountability.

Protesters gathered near the shooting scene before marching to the hospital, where demonstrators blocked an intersection and confronted police barricades. Officers made arrests following clashes with some protesters.

The NYPD was not involved in the federal enforcement operation, although city officers responded after the shooting.
