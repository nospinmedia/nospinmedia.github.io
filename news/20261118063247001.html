FAA shuts down new routers after nationwide ground stop

Agency falls back on 25-year-old equipment as engineers recreate failure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 18, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has shut down newly installed Juniper routers and reverted to roughly 25-year-old Cisco equipment while investigators determine how a network upgrade triggered Friday's nationwide ground stop, with the change coming just ahead of the busy Thanksgiving travel period.

About 400 of 3,648 planned Juniper routers had been installed as part of the FAA's telecommunications modernization program, with 69 operating when the disruption occurred. The agency has paused further installations and disabled the deployed Juniper equipment while the investigation continues.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said investigators recreated the network-flooding problem traced to a radio-communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where installation of the 69th router preceded the failure. Routers are undergoing additional diagnostics at contractor L3Harris Technologies' facilities in Florida with participation from the FBI.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE), which owns Juniper, said its current findings indicate the router "functioned as it was configured to by the installer," rather than suffering a hardware defect.

The November 13 failure forced the FAA to stop departures nationwide for more than four hours and contributed to more than 9,500 flight cancellations through the weekend.

Congress is now seeking technical answers. Senate Commerce Committee Democrats, including Sen. Maria Cantwell and Sen. Tammy Baldwin, requested a joint briefing with the FAA, L3Harris and Juniper by November 23 on the failure and safeguards for future upgrades.

The Trump administration also sent lawmakers an updated funding request Tuesday seeking $50.1 billion, including $10.1 billion specifically for air traffic control system updates, beyond $12.5 billion approved last year.
