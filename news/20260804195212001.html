DC Circuit Says EPA Wrong To Cancel $20B ‘Green Bank’ Grants

Ruling briefly stayed as government weighs Supreme Court appeal

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 5, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A divided U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit ruled on August 4, 2026, that the Environmental Protection Agency improperly moved to terminate about $20 billion in clean-energy grants awarded under the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022. In an en banc decision, six of ten judges agreed the EPA likely violated the law when it canceled the awards and sought to claw back funds, delivering a major setback to President Trump’s effort to dismantle the program.

The case centers on the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund’s “green bank” awards that had been obligated to nonprofit consortia including Climate United Fund. According to court filings and prior rulings, billions were already parked in Citibank accounts for project lending when Administrator Lee Zeldin froze the money in early 2025 and later issued termination notices, citing alleged mismanagement and potential fraud. A district judge previously found the government failed to provide evidence of fraud and said termination ran afoul of statutory limits; a September 2025 three-judge panel briefly sided with the administration before the full court agreed to rehear the dispute.

Tuesday’s decision means the administration’s cancellation effort cannot proceed on the current record, but access to funds is not immediate: the court’s relief is temporarily stayed for several days to allow the government to seek emergency intervention from the U.S. Supreme Court. Reporting also notes that a tax-and-spending measure enacted last year rescinded unobligated portions of the broader program, a complication the judges flagged while still finding the EPA likely overreached by attempting to unwind already obligated grants.

Why it matters now: the ruling preserves, at least for the moment, a financing pipeline aimed at lowering costs for households and small businesses through community-scale clean energy investments. Reported examples include residential solar, building-efficiency upgrades, and cleaner transportation options targeted to underserved areas. Key specifics: - Program tranche: $20 billion (part of a larger $27 billion initiative) - Named parties: EPA, Lee Zeldin, Climate United Fund, Citibank - Procedural status: en banc ruling with a short administrative stay; potential Supreme Court filing expected within days
