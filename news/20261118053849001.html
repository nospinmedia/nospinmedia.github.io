NSA restricts Fort Meade access as classified network outage continues

Officials point to software patch, not coordinated cyberattack

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 18, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The National Security Agency (NSA) restricted access to its Fort Meade, Maryland headquarters Tuesday evening as technicians continued working to restore a classified internal communications network, reversing an earlier move toward normal operations after the outage began during routine maintenance.

The NSA initially used a "code yellow" status with staggered employee arrival times while dealing with problems affecting NSANet, its classified internal network. After briefly telling employees normal operations could resume, the agency shifted to "code orange" Tuesday evening, restricting access to the Fort Meade campus for most personnel while restoration work continued. The NSA has said its mission-critical capabilities remained operational through redundant systems.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said the outage followed installation of a software patch addressing significant PHP vulnerabilities discovered the previous month. According to Clayton, some systems failed to restart as expected following the update. He said officials had found no indication that the incident resulted from a cyberattack.

The disruption has received heightened attention because it follows an unusual series of technology failures affecting Microsoft 365, U.S. air traffic systems, JPMorgan Chase, United Airlines, Apple services and other major systems.

Secretary of State and national security adviser Marco Rubio said officials have "zero evidence" that the outages are connected or resulted from hackers, foreign governments or another coordinated malicious campaign.

Senate Intelligence Committee Vice Chairman Mark Warner said the recent failures highlight pressure on organizations to rapidly patch older computer systems as security threats evolve, including threats accelerated by artificial intelligence. Lawmakers from both parties have sought briefings about the disruptions and their causes.

President Donald Trump, speaking in Manila on Tuesday, also addressed the recent outages, saying, "Things like that happen," while saying officials had been in contact with affected organizations.

The NSA has not publicly provided a timetable for full restoration of normal access at Fort Meade.
