Italy fines Meta €805 million over access to users' photos

Regulators say Facebook and Instagram failed to obtain specific consent

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 15, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Italian regulators have fined Meta €805 million, about $880 million, after concluding that the company improperly accessed photographs stored on users' devices through Facebook and Instagram without obtaining sufficiently specific consent.

The penalty was issued by Italy's competition authority, the AGCM, following an investigation into how Meta's apps request permission to access photographs. Regulators said users were not given a sufficiently clear choice about how much of their photo libraries the company's services could access.

The case centers on permissions presented when people use features that require photographs, such as uploading an image to Facebook or Instagram. According to the Italian authority, Meta sought broad access to users' photo libraries rather than limiting access to individual photographs selected by the user.

Regulators concluded that this approach amounted to an unfair commercial practice because consumers were not adequately informed about the extent of the access being requested and were not offered a sufficiently specific way to consent.

The distinction is important because modern mobile operating systems increasingly allow users to give an app access to selected photos instead of an entire photo library. Italy's regulator said Meta's implementation did not provide users with the level of choice required under the country's consumer-protection rules.

The €805 million penalty is substantial even by European technology-enforcement standards. The case is focused on Meta's handling of photo permissions and consumer consent in Italy rather than an allegation that the company publicly exposed users' private photographs.

Meta disputes the decision and plans to appeal. The company has argued that its applications follow the permission systems provided by Apple's iOS and Google's Android and that users can control photo access through their device settings. Meta also said it disagrees with the regulator's interpretation of how those permissions should be presented to consumers.

The ruling adds to a series of European regulatory actions involving Meta, although this case is separate from previous investigations involving targeted advertising, competition, children's safety and the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation.

The Italian decision could have consequences beyond the immediate fine if it forces Meta to change how Facebook and Instagram request access to photographs. Other technology companies using similar permission systems could also face scrutiny over whether users are clearly told when an application seeks access to an entire photo library rather than only the images they choose to share.

Meta can challenge the ruling through the Italian courts, meaning the fine and the regulator's findings may still be reviewed before the case is ultimately resolved.
