Mississippi city fires police chief over racist texts

Dismissal follows protests after toddler's death

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The Board of Aldermen in Senatobia, Mississippi, unanimously voted to fire Police Chief Harold "Hal" Vanderford after reports revealed he repeatedly used racist language in private text messages. The board rejected Vanderford's resignation and instead voted to terminate his employment, a decision that comes as the city faces continuing public scrutiny following the fatal police shooting of 1-year-old Kohen Wiley earlier this year.

Investigative reporting by Mississippi Today, The New York Times, and Verité News found that Vanderford exchanged multiple text messages containing racial slurs and derogatory remarks about Black people with other members of the department. City officials had initially accepted the chief's explanation after the messages became public, but mounting public criticism and ongoing demonstrations prompted the board to reconsider. Following the vote, city leaders appointed Angelica Maze as interim police chief while the department searches for a permanent replacement.

The controversy follows the June 14, 2026, shooting in which Sgt. Hunter Foster fired into a vehicle during a response to a reported shoplifting incident outside a Walmart. The gunfire killed Kohen Wiley, who was seated inside the vehicle, and critically injured the driver, Latoya Ferrell. Foster remains on administrative leave while the Mississippi Bureau of Investigation and the Mississippi Attorney General's Office continue reviewing the shooting.

Civil rights attorney Ben Crump, who represents Wiley's family, welcomed Vanderford's dismissal but said additional accountability is still needed, including the public release of body-camera and surveillance footage from the shooting. The case has drawn statewide attention and renewed discussion about police accountability, department leadership, transparency, and community trust as Senatobia works through the aftermath of the incident.
