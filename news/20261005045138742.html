Russia escalates strikes on Kyiv's Dnipro bridges

Three of city's six road crossings face disruption

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 5, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Russia has intensified attacks on bridges crossing the Dnipro River in Kyiv, disrupting transportation between the two halves of the Ukrainian capital as Moscow expands its campaign against infrastructure. Russian forces struck the Northern Bridge again on Sunday, with Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko saying the attack damaged the roadway, wounded two people and damaged a hot-water supply system in the area.

The latest strike followed several days of attacks on Kyiv's river crossings. Russian assaults since Thursday forced the full or partial closure of three of Kyiv's six road bridges, creating traffic backups and disrupting movement across a river that divides the city. An earlier strike on the Northern Bridge damaged its road surface and overhead trolleybus cables, while the Southern Bridge was also repeatedly targeted. More than a third of Kyiv's roughly 3 million residents lived on the eastern, or left, bank before Russia's full-scale invasion, while many workplaces and government institutions are on the western bank.

Russia's Defense Ministry acknowledged striking the Northern Bridge, saying the crossing was used for troop movements and military cargo. Moscow said it would intensify attacks on military facilities, defense-industry sites and supporting infrastructure in Kyiv and elsewhere. Russia and Ukraine both deny intentionally targeting civilians.

The escalation coincided with a visit by German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who announced another €1 billion ($1.1 billion) in German military assistance and €350 million for repairs to Ukraine's energy sector. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia was increasingly targeting infrastructure and vowed Ukraine would respond. He has also said Ukrainian strikes would focus on Russian infrastructure rather than civilians, including continued attacks against oil refineries.

The bridge attacks add transportation infrastructure to an expanding aerial campaign already affecting electricity, telecommunications and other systems as Ukraine approaches another winter of war.
