DOJ investigates Corey Lewandowski over conduct at Homeland Security

DHS watchdog referral centers on government contracts and alleged financial gain

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 23, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

The U.S. Department of Justice is investigating longtime President Trump adviser Corey Lewandowski over allegations that he may have improperly benefited financially from his role at the Department of Homeland Security, according to people familiar with the investigation. Lewandowski has not been charged with a crime and denies wrongdoing.

The investigation follows a criminal referral from the DHS Office of Inspector General, which had been examining contracting practices during the tenure of former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem. Lewandowski served as a senior adviser and was widely described as functioning as Noem's de facto chief of staff before both left the department in March 2026.

Investigators are examining Lewandowski's involvement in government contracts and whether he profited from his position, according to reporting by Reuters and other outlets. Reuters reported that the inquiry includes his role overseeing some contracts, including matters involving the Federal Emergency Management Agency, and whether he exercised authority he legally did not have. CBS News reported that allegations of personal financial gain are connected to contracts established while Noem led DHS.

The investigation remains at an early stage. Reuters reported that the Justice Department had not issued subpoenas as of its report. Lewandowski's attorney, Adam Trigg, said his client has not been contacted by the Justice Department and denied that Lewandowski engaged in wrongdoing while serving in what the attorney described as a voluntary government role. The Justice Department declined to comment to CBS News.

President Trump said Thursday that he had heard about the investigation but had not been briefed on it by Attorney General Todd Blanche. Trump said he expects people working for him to be honest and that anyone found to have acted dishonestly should face prosecution.

Lewandowski has been a prominent Trump political adviser for a decade. He managed Trump's presidential campaign during part of the 2016 election and was involved in later Trump campaigns before joining Noem at DHS.
