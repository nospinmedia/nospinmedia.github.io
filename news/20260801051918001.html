Russia launches new ballistic missile barrage on Kyiv

At least 9 killed as second major attack in 48 hours strikes Ukraine's capital

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 1, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

At least nine people were killed and 30 others were injured, including four children, after Russia launched a large-scale overnight attack using ballistic missiles and drones against Kyiv early August 1, according to Ukrainian officials. The assault marked the second major strike on the Ukrainian capital in less than 48 hours, as emergency crews searched damaged residential buildings for additional victims. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said multiple districts were hit, with fires, collapsed structures and widespread damage reported across the city.

The deadliest damage occurred in Kyiv's Darnytskyi district, where officials said seven people were killed after missile debris struck residential buildings. Two more fatalities were reported in the Solomianskyi district, where a five-story apartment building sustained severe structural damage. Ukraine's State Emergency Service said rescuers evacuated more than 100 residents, while heavy equipment was used to clear rubble and search for people believed trapped inside damaged buildings.

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia employed ballistic missiles alongside attack drones in another coordinated overnight assault designed to overwhelm air defenses. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy renewed appeals for additional Patriot air defense systems and interceptor missiles, arguing that stronger defensive capabilities are essential to protect civilians from increasingly frequent missile attacks. Russian officials said the strikes targeted military-industrial and logistics facilities, while Ukraine maintained that residential neighborhoods and civilian infrastructure suffered significant damage.

The latest bombardment followed another deadly Russian attack earlier in the week and came as Moscow continued intensified aerial operations despite ongoing diplomatic efforts to end the war. Ukrainian officials warned that shortages of advanced air defense interceptors have made it increasingly difficult to defend the capital against repeated ballistic missile attacks.
