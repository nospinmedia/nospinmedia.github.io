FBI arrests man accused of plotting Mall of America mass shooting

Prosecutors say suspect planned to kill 30 to 60 people in support of ISIS

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 8, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Federal authorities arrested Sheikhdoon Abdullahi Mohamud, an 18-year-old from Richfield, Minnesota, after prosecutors alleged he planned a mass shooting at the Mall of America in support of ISIS. Mohamud was arrested October 6 and charged by federal complaint with knowingly receiving a firearm and ammunition to commit a federal crime of terrorism. He made his initial court appearance Wednesday.

According to the criminal complaint, Mohamud exchanged hundreds of messages with an FBI confidential source beginning in December 2024, initially discussing traveling to Somalia to join a designated terrorist organization. Authorities allege that when he lacked the money to travel overseas, he began discussing an attack in Minnesota. In July, prosecutors say he identified the Mall of America as a potential target and later said he wanted to kill 30 to 60 people there.

Investigators say Mohamud eventually selected October 24, when a Hmong Arts Celebration was scheduled at the mall, as the date for the alleged attack. Court documents say he discussed previous mass-casualty attacks, including the Pulse nightclub shooting and the 2025 New Orleans attack, and sent a video in which authorities say he pledged allegiance to ISIS.

On October 6, an undercover FBI employee arranged to sell Mohamud an AK-47-style rifle, four 30-round magazines and approximately 200 rounds of ammunition for about $600, according to the Justice Department. The FBI had rendered the rifle inoperable before the transaction. Authorities allege Mohamud reaffirmed during the meeting that he intended to use the weapon to shoot people at the Mall of America. Agents arrested him shortly after he took possession of the items.

The FBI, Joint Terrorism Task Force and Bloomington Police Department investigated the case. The Mall of America thanked law enforcement for disrupting the alleged threat. The charge remains an allegation, and Mohamud is presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.
