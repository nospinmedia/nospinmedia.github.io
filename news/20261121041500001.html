Russian Missiles Land In NATO Member Poland

Tusk Calls Airspace Breaches An Intentional Provocation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 21, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Two Russian missiles and a suspected jet-powered drone entered Polish airspace and came down in southeastern Poland on Friday, November 20, as Russia carried out a major overnight attack on neighboring Ukraine. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the incidents appeared to be an "intentional provocation," while NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called the incursions a "grave violation" of allied airspace.

Polish officials said one object was identified as a Russian Kh-101 long-range cruise missile. The three objects came down near Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem and Mołodycz, close to Poland's border with Ukraine. Officials reported no injuries or significant damage from the impacts. Polish and allied aircraft were scrambled and air defenses were placed on alert as the objects approached and entered Polish territory.

The timing drew particular attention because European forces were conducting Exercise Koalicja 26.2 near Nowa Dęba, preparing for a possible multinational mission in Ukraine after any future peace agreement. One of the Russian missiles came down about 22 miles from the exercise area. Tusk said Polish officials and NATO partners did not believe the timing appeared accidental. Russia had not immediately commented on the Polish incidents.

The incursions occurred during a much larger Russian bombardment of Ukraine. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia launched more than 400 drones and 110 missiles, killing at least eight people and injuring dozens. The attacks struck infrastructure and other targets across several regions and triggered emergency power restrictions.

Rutte is scheduled to travel to Warsaw on Saturday, November 21, following the incursions. Polish officials said the country had not invoked NATO Article 4, which allows a member to request consultations when it believes its territorial integrity, political independence or security is threatened. Poland instead sought additional allied forces and air-defense capabilities.

The strikes came after Ukraine's recent attacks on the Crimean Bridge and after Russian President Vladimir Putin threatened retaliation following his summit in China with President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping. The sequence adds to concerns about escalation, although Polish and NATO officials have not established that the weapons landed in Poland specifically in retaliation for the Crimean Bridge attack.

The incidents also do not automatically trigger NATO Article 5, which provides for collective defense when an ally is attacked and requires political decisions by member states about the response.
