Jeffries launches speaker bid as Johnson steps aside

House Democrats also prepare new Trump administration oversight

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 5, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The leadership of the U.S. House of Representatives is beginning to shift following the midterm elections, with Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries formally seeking the speakership, Republican Speaker Mike Johnson preparing to leave party leadership and incoming Democratic committee leaders outlining plans for increased oversight of President Trump's administration.

Jeffries asked Democratic colleagues Wednesday to support his bid for speaker when the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027. Democrats won control of the House in Tuesday's elections, putting Jeffries in position to become the first Black speaker in U.S. history if he wins the House vote. No major Democratic challenger has emerged.

Johnson, meanwhile, will not seek to remain the top House Republican after his party lost its majority. He is expected to remain in Congress as a rank-and-file representative from Louisiana. His departure opens a Republican leadership contest, with Steve Scalise and Jim Jordan among those seeking the top GOP position. House Republicans are scheduled to hold leadership elections in November.

Beyond leadership changes, Democrats are preparing to use their new House majority to expand congressional oversight of the Trump administration. Democratic lawmakers have discussed investigations involving administration spending, potential conflicts of interest, actions by Cabinet officials and other executive-branch decisions. Control of House committees will give Democrats subpoena authority when the new Congress takes office.

The election does not immediately transfer control. Republicans retain the current House majority until the existing Congress ends, and Jeffries must still win the formal speaker election. Democrats' investigative plans also remain proposals until committees organize and determine their priorities next year.
