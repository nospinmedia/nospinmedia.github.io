U.S. Declares Scholar Min Zin Wrongfully Detained in China

Case Becomes Diplomatic Priority Ahead of Xi Visit

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 21, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

The U.S. Department of State has formally designated American citizen Min Zin as wrongfully detained by China, elevating his case to one of Washington's highest diplomatic priorities under the Robert Levinson Hostage Recovery and Hostage-Taking Accountability Act. Secretary of State Marco Rubio made the determination following what the department described as an extensive review of the circumstances surrounding Min Zin's detention. The designation transfers responsibility for coordinating his release to the State Department's Office of the Special Presidential Envoy for Hostage Affairs and signals that the United States considers his imprisonment to be politically motivated rather than a legitimate criminal prosecution.

Min Zin, a Myanmar-born political scholar, U.S. citizen, and executive director of the Institute for Strategy and Policy – Myanmar (ISP-Myanmar), was detained on June 3 after traveling to Yunnan Province to attend an academic conference in Kunming at the invitation of the Chinese government. Chinese authorities later accused him of endangering national security through alleged espionage. A senior U.S. official said he was detained shortly after arriving at the airport and has since endured repeated interrogations and prolonged isolation. China has maintained that the case is being handled according to Chinese law and rejected U.S. claims that his detention is wrongful.

The decision comes just weeks before Chinese President Xi Jinping is expected to visit Washington on September 24 for a summit with President Trump, increasing the likelihood that Min Zin's detention will become a prominent issue during bilateral discussions. Human rights advocates and former U.S. officials have urged the administration to press for his release, arguing that his arrest has alarmed academics who travel to China for research and conferences.

Min Zin is now the second American officially classified as wrongfully detained in China, joining Dr. Youlin Chen, a Boston-based seismologist who was detained in 2024 on espionage charges that the United States also considers baseless. The State Department said it will continue seeking the release of both Americans while urging China to free all U.S. citizens it considers arbitrarily detained.
