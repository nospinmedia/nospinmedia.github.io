Grand jury issues no indictments in Nolan Wells' death

Review finds no evidence of criminal or racially motivated wrongdoing

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 22, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

A Mississippi grand jury unanimously declined to indict anyone in connection with the death of Nolan Wells, an 18-year-old Black teenager whose body was found off Horn Island two days after a Fourth of July trip with friends. The grand jury concluded there was no credible evidence of criminal conduct or racially motivated wrongdoing connected to his death.

The Jackson County grand jury reviewed an extensive collection of evidence, including testimony from 43 witnesses, material obtained through 132 subpoenas, physical and digital evidence, GPS information, cellphone records, videos and investigative reports. Its report said the position, condition, discovery time and location of Wells' body were consistent with drowning. The grand jury found no evidence of a fatal injury or disease, although Wells had bruising on the back of his head that investigators determined was not fatal.

Wells disappeared after traveling with friends to Horn Island, a barrier island off the Mississippi Gulf Coast, on July 4. His body was recovered in the water on July 6. Questions surrounding his death attracted national attention after photographs and accounts from the trip circulated online, while Wells' family and civil rights advocates called for a comprehensive and transparent investigation.

The Mississippi Chief Medical Examiner and a forensic expert retained by Wells' family were unable to establish a definitive cause and manner of death. Accidental drowning remained a possible explanation. Civil rights attorney Ben Crump, who represents the family, said unanswered questions remain despite the grand jury's findings.

Jackson County District Attorney Angel Myers McIlrath announced the grand jury's decision Monday. The report said that if evidence later emerges indicating Wells' death was not accidental, it can be presented for further consideration because Mississippi has no statute of limitations for homicide cases.
