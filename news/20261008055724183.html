Trump sends $2.35 billion in payments weeks before midterms

$90 Medicare and $500 health refunds join proposed $5,000 checks

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 8, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Donald Trump is sending $2.35 billion in direct payments to millions of Americans in the weeks before the November 3 midterm elections, while separately promising much larger $5,000 payments if Republicans retain control of Congress. The payments and proposals target several groups, including Medicare beneficiaries and people who purchase insurance through the federal Affordable Care Act marketplace.

The largest payment already announced is a one-time $90 Medicare Part B rebate for 20.8 million people, costing about $2 billion. The White House says the money will come from the Medicare Improvement Fund and help offset healthcare costs. Most of the nation's 70.6 million Medicare recipients will not qualify, including Medicare Advantage enrollees and people whose Part B premiums are paid by Medicaid.

The administration has also sent $500 payments to about 950,000 Healthcare.gov customers. The White House describes those checks as refunds of fees it says were excessive under the Biden administration. Reuters found that 71% of eligible recipients live in states holding competitive U.S. Senate or gubernatorial elections. The administration says the payments are intended to return money to consumers and reduce healthcare costs.

A much larger proposal remains only a campaign promise. On September 9, Trump said every adult U.S. citizen would receive $5,000 if Republicans retain control of the House and Senate. Reuters estimates that would cost approximately $1.2 trillion, or about 16% of the federal budget. Congress would have to authorize the money, and the proposal has so far gained little support on Capitol Hill.

The administration also announced $150 million for Alaska power lines on October 5, the same day Vice President JD Vance campaigned in the state for Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan. Trump has separately announced plans for an Alaska liquefied natural gas pipeline, though details remain unresolved.
