Yemen launches major offensive to retake Houthi territory

Rebels advance toward Taiz after seizing key Red Sea route

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 5, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

Yemen's internationally recognized government has launched a major military campaign to retake territory controlled by the Iran-backed Houthis, dramatically escalating a civil war that had been largely restrained by a 2022 truce. Presidential Leadership Council chief Rashad al-Alimi said government forces would continue operations until the country was liberated from Houthi control.

The offensive follows major Houthi territorial gains. The group, formally known as Ansar Allah, captured the strategic Bab el-Mandeb Strait and about 150 kilometers (90 miles) of Yemen's Red Sea coastline during a rapid advance last month. Bab el-Mandeb is the southern gateway to the Red Sea and a critical shipping route connecting the Mediterranean and Asia.

The fighting is now threatening Taiz, Yemen's third-largest city. The Houthis captured the strategic town of Turbah and have taken villages and positions around Taiz, threatening the main road connecting the city with Aden, the interim seat of Yemen's internationally recognized government. Control of that route could effectively isolate hundreds of thousands of civilians as well as thousands of government troops stationed in the area.

Saudi Arabia is backing the government campaign. Regional and Western officials previously said more than 100,000 Yemeni troops could be mobilized depending on the offensive's scope, with Saudi forces providing air support. The Houthis said they responded to Saudi-led strikes by launching ballistic missiles and drones at Saudi Aramco facilities in Riyadh and Khurais. Saudi Arabia had not immediately confirmed the claimed attacks.

The renewed war is worsening an already severe humanitarian crisis. The World Food Programme said escalating violence has displaced tens of thousands of people and that three in four families in parts of Yemen are going hungry as fighting disrupts essential supplies.
