Musk regrets nearly $500 million in Republican midterm spending

Says he is 'not MAGA' after Democrats take control of Congress

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 19, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Elon Musk says he regrets spending nearly $500 million supporting Republicans in the 2026 midterm elections, acknowledging the limited electoral return and publicly distancing himself from the MAGA movement after Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress.

Speaking with Andrew Ross Sorkin at The New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday, Musk said he felt "pretty foolish" about spending so much. "If I could rewind the clock, I would have spent less money," he said. Musk spent about $445 million on federal races during the election cycle, according to Reuters, making him the largest individual donor of the midterms.

Musk said his main political objective had been helping Republicans preserve their 51-seat Senate majority. He also said he prefers divided government rather than having the White House and both chambers of Congress controlled by one party.

Some of Musk's largest spending was concentrated in Texas, home to Tesla and SpaceX. He poured almost $230 million into races there. Democrat James Talarico defeated Republican Ken Paxton for a U.S. Senate seat, while Democrat Gina Hinojosa defeated incumbent Republican Governor Greg Abbott. Musk said he expects Hinojosa to govern as a moderate and predicted Texas would be fine under her administration.

Musk also rejected the MAGA label. "I'm not MAGA," he said, explaining that if the term means automatically supporting Republican policies, it does not describe him. He characterized himself as politically moderate and said both major parties have strengths and weaknesses.

The remarks mark a shift from Musk's highly visible support for President Trump during the 2024 election, when Musk appeared at Trump rallies and described himself as "dark gothic MAGA." Musk later spent about 180 days leading the Trump administration's Department of Government Efficiency before leaving government in 2025. He subsequently had a public falling-out with Trump over the president's spending legislation and other disputes before the two later reconciled.
