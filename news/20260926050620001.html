Supreme Court revives expanded voter citizenship database

States can resume SAVE checks ahead of midterms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 26, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court on September 25 allowed the Trump administration to resume using an expanded federal database to help state and local election officials check the citizenship status of registered voters ahead of the November midterm elections. The 6-3 emergency decision pauses a lower court ruling that had blocked the expanded system over privacy and accuracy concerns.

The case involves the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) program, operated by the Department of Homeland Security. SAVE has existed for decades, but the federal government expanded it in 2025 after President Trump directed agencies to provide state and local officials with additional tools for checking voter citizenship. The revised system incorporates records from the Social Security Administration and allows officials to conduct bulk searches using Social Security numbers and other identifying information.

A federal district court previously blocked the changes after finding that the government had not complied with federal privacy requirements. The Supreme Court's unsigned opinion concluded that federal law requires DHS to respond to requests from state and local officials seeking citizenship verification and said the lower court's order interfered with federal assistance to election administrators. The ruling is temporary while litigation over the system continues.

Justices Ketanji Brown Jackson, Sonia Sotomayor and Elena Kagan dissented. Jackson wrote that the expanded system uses Social Security records that may contain outdated citizenship information, creating a risk that recently naturalized U.S. citizens could be incorrectly identified as noncitizens. The administration has argued that the expanded database gives states a more effective way to verify voter eligibility.

The decision does not itself remove anyone from a voter roll. States remain responsible for determining how verification results are used under applicable election laws, while the underlying legal challenge to the expanded SAVE system continues.
