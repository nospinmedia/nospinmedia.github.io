Analysis points to direct U.S. hit on Iranian wedding site

Five dead as CENTCOM investigates civilian casualties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 4, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A deadly explosion at a wedding celebration in southern Iran was likely caused by a direct hit from a U.S. munition, according to a Reuters analysis of verified images and video reviewed by four independent weapons experts. The September 1 blast in Kuhestak, a coastal community in Hormozgan province near the Strait of Hormuz, killed five people and injured nearly 70, while the U.S. military has opened a review into reports of civilian casualties.

The experts said damage to the wedding venue was consistent with an air-delivered American weapon striking the building directly rather than debris from another strike or an errant Iranian air-defense weapon. Reuters reported that a hole through the roof, penetration into the building and other visible damage supported that assessment. The analysis does not constitute an official U.S. finding, and the Pentagon has not acknowledged that an American weapon struck the wedding site. U.S. officials confirmed that American forces targeted a communications facility approximately 135 meters from the home.

Among those killed was a 4-year-old child, while another victim, a 22-year-old woman, later died from her injuries. The Iranian Red Crescent reported 67 people were injured. U.S. Central Command initially said it was aware of Iranian reports about the incident and subsequently confirmed it was looking into them. CENTCOM spokesperson Navy Capt. Tim Hawkins said the U.S. military does not target civilians.

Vice President JD Vance also confirmed that the incident is being investigated while cautioning that the facts have not yet been established by the United States. The strike occurred during a renewed wave of U.S. attacks on Iranian targets that CENTCOM said included air-defense systems, radar installations, maritime assets and communications facilities. Iran responded with missile and drone attacks against U.S. positions and regional targets. The wedding incident has drawn particular scrutiny because the independent weapons analysis challenges earlier explanations that the casualties resulted from secondary debris rather than a direct strike.
