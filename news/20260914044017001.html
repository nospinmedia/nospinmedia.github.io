Trump rejects calls to slow AI development

President says US must maintain its lead over China

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 14, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump pushed back on calls to slow the development of advanced artificial intelligence on September 13, 2026, arguing that the United States must preserve its technological advantage over China. Speaking to reporters at his golf resort in Doonbeg, Ireland, Trump said the U.S. currently leads China in AI and declared that "whoever wins AI wins," while saying some guardrails could still be considered.

Trump's comments came one day after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for the AI industry to deliberately pace development of increasingly capable systems so safety measures have more time to catch up. Amodei proposed a framework that includes stronger testing and oversight and said coordination among companies and governments would be necessary. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and xAI founder Elon Musk publicly backed the push for slowing the pace of frontier AI development.

Asked whether AI companies should slow development or face additional regulation, Trump said the issue was being amplified by "very negative forces" raising scenarios he believes will not happen. He nevertheless acknowledged the possibility of safeguards, saying, "We could put guardrails." His response underscored the tension between concerns about increasingly powerful AI systems and fears that slowing American development could allow China to gain ground in a technology viewed as strategically important.

The debate has intensified after current and former Anthropic researchers warned about potentially severe risks from rapidly advancing AI. The administration has generally favored rapid American AI development, although federal officials have also acknowledged security and safety concerns. Chinese President Xi Jinping is scheduled to visit the United States later in September, with AI expected to be among the issues discussed as competition between the two countries continues.
