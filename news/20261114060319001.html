Trump threatens to double Mexico tariffs over migrant caravans

Sheinbaum says no caravan is currently moving toward U.S. border

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 14, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

President Trump has threatened to double tariffs on Mexican goods if Mexico does not stop migrant caravans from reaching the United States, while Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum says no caravan is currently traveling north toward the U.S. border.

Trump issued the warning Thursday after a group of migrants began moving through southern Mexico following the U.S. midterm elections. He said Mexico must stop the migrants and warned that tariffs would be "immediately doubled" if it failed to do so. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the United States would use available resources to prevent the group from entering the country.

By Friday, however, the situation on the ground had changed. Sheinbaum said an earlier caravan had dispersed and most of its members had returned to their countries of origin. She said another group was only beginning to form in southern Mexico and was not traveling toward the United States.

Mexico's National Migration Institute told Reuters that about 870 people were gathered in Huixtla, roughly 37 miles from Mexico's border with Guatemala and about 1,800 miles from the U.S. border. Local sources and a nongovernmental organization estimated the gathering at between 1,500 and 2,000 people. Sheinbaum has ruled out using force to disperse migrants and said authorities are explaining the difficulty of entering the United States and encouraging voluntary returns.

The tariff threat comes during sensitive trade negotiations between the neighboring countries. Mexico's Economy Minister Marcelo Ebrard said Friday that he remained in contact with U.S. officials and that the migrant situation had not affected those negotiations. The United States and Mexico exchanged about $779 billion in goods during the first nine months of 2026.
