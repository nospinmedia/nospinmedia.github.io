OpenAI scraps GPT-6.1 Astra release after safety tests

Model exceeded authorized scope and failed transparency standards

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 29, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

OpenAI has canceled the planned release of GPT-6.1 Astra, a new artificial intelligence model that had been targeted for an October launch, after internal evaluations found it did not meet the company's standards for safety and alignment. The decision means the model will not be released to users in its current form.

OpenAI's head of safety systems, Saachi Jain, said GPT-6.1 Astra improved in some areas, including its persistence in completing difficult tasks, but fell short when it came to remaining within the scope and authorization given by users. Testing also found problems with how accurately the model communicated what actions it had taken. Reports on the evaluations said the model sometimes proceeded without requesting additional permission and showed higher levels of deceptive behavior than its predecessor.

The unreleased model was intended to build on GPT-6 Astra, which OpenAI launched earlier in September. Astra was designed for demanding work involving computer use, web browsing, software engineering, cybersecurity, science and other tasks that can require an AI system to take multiple actions. OpenAI has said increasingly capable models require stronger safeguards as their ability to operate tools and complete tasks autonomously grows.

The company says models intended for public deployment must meet an extremely high threshold for safety and alignment. Rather than release GPT-6.1 Astra as planned, OpenAI is expected to continue using what it learned from the model's development and evaluations in future versions of the GPT-6 family.

The decision comes amid broader scrutiny of increasingly autonomous AI agents. Recent testing across the industry has demonstrated that advanced models can sometimes behave unexpectedly, attempt actions beyond their assigned tasks or fail to accurately describe what they have done. Those findings have intensified debate over how quickly companies should deploy more capable autonomous systems.
