Vermont Reviews Border Patrol Partnership After Traffic Stop

Sheriff Suspends Federal Program Amid Misconduct Investigation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 23, 2026 at 2:36 PM ET

Vermont officials are investigating whether a Chittenden County Sheriff's Department deputy violated the state's Fair and Impartial Policing Policy after a routine traffic stop led to the detention and deportation of a migrant farmworker by U.S. Border Patrol. The incident has prompted Sheriff Dan Gamelin to suspend his department's participation in Operation Stonegarden, a federally funded border security program, while state regulators review the deputy's actions. The case has renewed debate over how local law enforcement agencies should interact with federal immigration authorities.

The investigation centers on a July 19 traffic stop in Berkshire, Vermont, where Sgt. Scott Costella stopped a vehicle for a missing front license plate while working a federally funded Operation Stonegarden assignment. According to records and cruiser camera footage released publicly, Costella contacted U.S. Border Patrol shortly after initiating the stop. Agents responded to the scene, detained Alex Genri Jimenez, a 22-year-old dairy farmworker, and later transferred him into federal immigration custody. Jimenez was subsequently deported to Mexico.

Following public disclosure of the incident, Sheriff Gamelin filed a complaint with the Vermont Criminal Justice Council, requesting an independent review of whether the deputy's actions complied with state policing standards. Vermont's Fair and Impartial Policing Policy generally prohibits state and local officers from assisting with civil immigration enforcement except under limited circumstances related to public safety or criminal investigations. The sheriff also announced that his department would suspend participation in Operation Stonegarden while the matter is under review.

The case has drawn attention to the broader role of federal border security partnerships in northern Vermont, where several law enforcement agencies participate in Operation Stonegarden. Civil rights advocates and immigration organizations have called for additional oversight of those agreements, while state officials say the investigation will help determine whether further policy changes or guidance are needed for local agencies working alongside federal authorities.
