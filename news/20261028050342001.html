Flávio Bolsonaro leaves Brazil after election defeat and court order

U.S. says it will grant visas to Bolsonaro and his family

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 28, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Brazilian Senator Flávio Bolsonaro left Brazil for the United States after losing the presidential election to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, despite a Supreme Court order requiring him to surrender his passports as part of a money-laundering investigation.

Bolsonaro left after failing to comply with a 24-hour deadline to surrender his regular and diplomatic passports. Two sources told Reuters the documents were canceled after the deadline expired. The court order, issued by Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes, also prohibited Bolsonaro from leaving Brazil and cited concerns that he could flee because of his financial resources.

A senior U.S. State Department official said the United States would issue visas to Bolsonaro, his wife and their two daughters and allow them to enter the country. One source close to the family said Bolsonaro had landed in Florida, although his precise whereabouts were initially unclear. Brazilian authorities were also examining how he left the country, with a senior Federal Police official saying he had not departed through a regular migration checkpoint.

Bolsonaro had said just a day earlier that he had no intention of leaving Brazil and would surrender his passports while fighting what he characterized as an attempt to intimidate him. He denies wrongdoing in the money-laundering investigation and has portrayed the legal cases against him as politically motivated.

The departure came two days after Lula defeated Bolsonaro in Brazil's presidential runoff. Bolsonaro has not formally conceded and said he would seek to annul the result over alleged voting irregularities. His Liberal Party, however, acknowledged Lula's victory. Bolsonaro's campaign manager said the departure surprised him because party officials had expected the senator to become a leading opposition figure after receiving roughly 60 million votes.

Lula called Bolsonaro's departure "shameful" and said he should have remained in Brazil to defend himself. President Trump said Wednesday that he approved of Bolsonaro coming to the United States, while criticizing the Brazilian election but saying he had not taken sides.

The episode could add to tensions between Brazil and the United States, which have already clashed over U.S. policies toward the Bolsonaro family and Brazilian officials. Flávio's father, former President Jair Bolsonaro, similarly traveled to the United States after losing the 2022 election before later returning to Brazil.
