Social Security Benefits to Rise 3.6% in 2027

Average Retired Worker Will Receive About $75 More Each Month

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 10:39 AM ET

Social Security benefits will increase 3.6% in 2027, giving millions of retirees and other beneficiaries a larger monthly payment as the annual cost-of-living adjustment responds to higher consumer prices. The increase will add about $75 per month to the benefit received by the average retired worker beginning in January 2027.

The annual Cost-of-Living Adjustment, or COLA, is designed to prevent inflation from eroding the purchasing power of Social Security benefits. The adjustment is calculated using the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers, known as the CPI-W, comparing inflation during the third quarter with the same period a year earlier. September's inflation data completed the calculation used to determine the 2027 increase.

The 3.6% COLA applies to Social Security beneficiaries, including retired and disabled workers and eligible family members. Supplemental Security Income recipients will also receive the adjustment. The increase comes as higher energy costs helped push overall U.S. inflation to 3.7% in September, underscoring the higher expenses many households continue to face.

For an average retired worker, the adjustment translates to roughly $75 more each month, although the actual dollar increase will vary depending on an individual's current benefit. Beneficiaries should receive notices detailing their specific 2027 payment amounts before the new benefits take effect.

The increase does not necessarily mean retirees will have the full additional amount available for other expenses. Many Social Security recipients have Medicare Part B premiums deducted directly from their monthly benefits, and changes in those premiums can reduce the net increase beneficiaries see in their payments.

The annual adjustment is automatic under federal law and does not require congressional approval. It ties Social Security payments to measured changes in consumer prices, meaning years with higher inflation generally produce larger benefit increases while periods of low inflation result in smaller adjustments.

The new COLA will affect household budgets for tens of millions of Americans who rely on Social Security for at least part of their retirement or disability income.
