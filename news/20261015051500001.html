White Powder Threats Target Election Offices in Three States

FBI Investigates Suspicious Mailings Weeks Before Midterm Elections

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Election offices in Ohio, Louisiana and Oklahoma have been targeted with suspicious mailings containing unidentified white powder, prompting evacuations and a federal investigation just weeks before the November 3, 2026, midterm elections. The FBI and U.S. Postal Inspection Service are investigating the incidents, which occurred on October 13 and 14. No injuries or adverse health effects have been reported.

In Louisiana, an employee of the Secretary of State's Elections Division opened a package containing white powder at approximately 12:20 p.m. Tuesday in Baton Rouge. The discovery prompted an evacuation of the building while law enforcement investigated. The employee was taken to an emergency room as a precaution but was not injured. Louisiana State Police said preliminary examination found no evidence of harmful substances. The recovered material was submitted to the FBI crime laboratory for further testing.

The Oklahoma State Election Board also received an envelope containing suspicious white powder Tuesday morning. State Election Board Secretary Paul Ziriax confirmed that the incident did not interfere with election preparations. Officials worked with law enforcement to secure the facility and investigate the mailing.

In Ohio, federal postal investigators intercepted a suspicious letter intended for Secretary of State Frank LaRose before it reached his office. LaRose confirmed the incident Wednesday and said the FBI was handling the investigation. His office notified county election boards to remain vigilant and emphasized that security procedures and contingency plans were already in place.

The FBI said investigators were examining a series of suspicious mailings sent to addresses nationwide, including state election offices. Authorities are working to identify both the substances involved and whoever sent the letters. Investigators have not publicly established whether the incidents were coordinated or identified a suspect.

The National Association of Secretaries of State and National Association of State Election Directors issued a joint statement expressing concern about threats against election personnel. Both organizations emphasized that election workers should be able to perform their duties without fearing for their safety.

The incidents resemble earlier threats against election administrators. During the 2024 presidential election, officials in at least 20 states received suspicious powder-containing letters, prompting federal agencies to issue additional guidance on handling potentially hazardous mail.

Despite the latest incidents, officials in the affected states reported no disruption to preparations for the upcoming elections. Federal investigators have not announced whether the substances are dangerous or whether the mailings constitute a coordinated intimidation campaign.
