Grand Canyon Flash Flood Kills 1

Search Continues for Missing Hikers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 31, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A powerful flash flood swept through Grand Canyon National Park over the weekend, leaving one person dead and about 15 people still unaccounted for as search-and-rescue operations continued on August 31. The National Park Service (NPS) and the Arizona Department of Public Safety recovered the body of an unidentified 46-year-old man near Crystal Rapids along the Colorado River after intense monsoon rainfall sent torrents of water and debris through Bright Angel Canyon and the Phantom Ranch area. Rescue crews also used helicopters to evacuate at least 62 stranded visitors from the inner canyon.

According to park officials, heavy rain fell over the Dragon Bravo wildfire burn scar on the North Rim, where vegetation lost during the fire allowed runoff to rapidly funnel into canyon drainages. The National Weather Service in Flagstaff reported that as much as 1 inch of rain fell in less than 30 minutes, triggering destructive debris flows and rapidly rising floodwaters. Search teams continued looking for hikers, campers, and river runners who had not yet been accounted for as crews assessed conditions throughout the canyon.

The flooding caused widespread damage to critical park infrastructure. Nearly all pedestrian bridges across Bright Angel Creek were destroyed or heavily damaged, and the Transcanyon Waterline, which supplies drinking water and supports fire protection on both rims, was severed. The NPS closed Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel Campground, the North Kaibab Trail, Silver Bridge, Black Bridge, and several additional inner-canyon facilities until further notice. Overnight lodging at affected park locations and some river operations were also suspended while engineers evaluated damage and began planning repairs.

Park officials warned that additional storms remain possible during the Southwest monsoon season and urged visitors to avoid closed areas and monitor weather alerts before entering the backcountry. Search operations remain active as authorities work to locate everyone believed to have been in the flood zone when the waters surged through the canyon.
