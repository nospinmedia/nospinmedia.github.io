Congo Ebola Outbreak Surpasses 9,000 Cases and 4,300 Deaths

Deadly Virus Spreads Across Seven Provinces as Kenya Reports Imported Case

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The Democratic Republic of the Congo is facing its largest recorded Ebola outbreak, with 9,066 confirmed infections and 4,381 deaths, according to government figures released on Tuesday, October 13, 2026. The epidemic has spread across 65 health zones in seven provinces, overwhelming medical resources and raising concerns about further transmission beyond the country's borders.

The latest figures represent a reported case-fatality ratio of approximately 48.3%, meaning nearly half of confirmed patients have died. Health officials reported 88 additional cases in the latest update and warned that transmission continues within communities. Many deaths are occurring outside treatment facilities, making it more difficult to identify infected individuals, trace their contacts, and prevent additional exposure.

The outbreak began in May 2026 and is caused by the Bundibugyo ebolavirus, a relatively rare strain for which no vaccine or treatment has been specifically approved. Unlike the Zaire strain responsible for previous major epidemics, existing approved Ebola vaccines have not been established as effective against Bundibugyo.

Health workers face significant obstacles, particularly in eastern Congo, where armed conflict, displacement, limited medical infrastructure, and community mistrust complicate containment efforts. In some areas, patients delay seeking treatment, while difficulties identifying contacts and conducting safe burials allow transmission to continue.

The epidemic has also crossed international borders. Uganda previously recorded 20 confirmed cases and two deaths before declaring its outbreak over in August. Kenya's first imported case involved a 40-year-old Kenyan citizen who had lived in Congo for seven years and died in Nairobi on October 5. Health reports indicate the man took fever-reducing medication during his journey, temporarily lowering his temperature and allowing him to pass thermal screening while traveling. The case has raised concerns about the limitations of temperature checks when travelers use medication that can mask one of Ebola's most recognizable symptoms.

International spread has not been limited to East Africa. France also treated an imported Bundibugyo Ebola case earlier in the outbreak. The patient received treatment and recovered before being discharged in July 2026, according to European disease surveillance information.

The World Health Organization has assessed the risk within Congo as very high and the risk to neighboring countries as high, while maintaining that the broader global risk remains low. Health authorities continue working to expand testing, improve treatment access, strengthen contact tracing and develop vaccines and treatments targeting the Bundibugyo strain.

Although Congo's outbreak has become the country's largest on record, it remains smaller than the 2014–2016 West African Ebola epidemic, which infected more than 28,600 people and killed over 11,000.
