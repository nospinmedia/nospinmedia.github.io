Vatican official calls Pope Leo’s cancer a ‘game-changer’

Pontiff will proceed with demanding Latin America trip

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 28, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Pope Leo XIV's lung cancer diagnosis is changing perceptions of his young papacy even as he recovers well and prepares for an extensive trip to Latin America, according to the Vatican's top foreign policy official.

Archbishop Paul Richard Gallagher, the Holy See's foreign minister, said October 27 that Leo's illness was "a bit of a game-changer" after the pope's energetic start had created what Gallagher described as a "Superman, a Superpope" image. Gallagher said the diagnosis underscored that the pontiff is human and vulnerable and could affect both how others view him and how he views himself.

Leo, 71, underwent surgery on October 13 to remove the lower lobe of his right lung after doctors discovered a tumor. The tumor was diagnosed as lung adenocarcinoma. Subsequent testing found the cancer was localized, and doctors determined that Leo does not currently require additional cancer treatment. He has been gradually returning to his duties following the operation.

The Vatican has confirmed that Leo still plans to make his scheduled November 6-17 trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru without changes to the itinerary. The journey includes about 31,000 kilometers (19,000 miles) of travel, a dozen flights and two helicopter trips. Leo spent roughly two decades as a missionary priest in Peru and holds Peruvian citizenship.

Gallagher said the medical advice surrounding the pope's recovery has been positive and that Leo wants to make the trip. The archbishop also said he expects the Chicago-born pope to visit the United States in the "not-too-distant future," although no U.S. trip has been formally scheduled.
