Ross Fire grows to 80,000 acres across North Texas

Containment reaches 12% as crews battle sprawling wildfire

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 27, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

The Ross Fire has expanded to approximately 80,000 acres across Palo Pinto and Jack counties in North Texas as firefighters continue a large-scale effort to contain the fast-moving blaze. The wildfire, which began on August 24 near U.S. 180 in Palo Pinto County, was 12% contained as of the latest official update. At least five structures have been destroyed, while the fire has forced evacuations, school closures and road disruptions across several rural communities.

Conditions improved enough on August 26 for officials in Jack County to lift mandatory evacuation orders affecting residents near County Road 2210 and U.S. 281, although emergency managers warned residents to remain prepared because the fire continues burning in neighboring Palo Pinto County. Fire crews from across Texas have joined local departments, while aircraft, heavy equipment and ground teams continue working to strengthen containment lines and protect homes, ranches and critical infrastructure.

Governor Greg Abbott announced earlier this week that the Federal Emergency Management Agency approved a Fire Management Assistance Grant for the Ross Fire. The program allows Texas to seek federal reimbursement for 75% of eligible firefighting costs, including equipment, personnel, field camps and emergency operations required to prevent further loss of life and property. State officials said the grant was approved while the fire was threatening hundreds of homes and rapidly expanding through extremely dry vegetation.

The fire grew from about 50,000 acres on August 25 to approximately 80,000 acres by the following morning, illustrating how quickly conditions deteriorated under hot, dry and windy weather. Ranchers and local volunteers have also worked to relocate cattle and other livestock from threatened areas. Officials continue urging residents to follow local evacuation guidance, conserve water where requested and avoid outdoor activities that could create additional sparks while firefighting operations remain active.
