Riyadh airport attack kills 12, injures 309

American among dead as Trump weighs military response

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 11, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

An attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, killed 12 people and injured 309 others on Saturday, October 10, marking the deadliest strike on the kingdom in more than a decade. The Saudi-led military coalition blamed Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement, while President Trump said the United States was considering joining Saudi military operations against the group.

Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation confirmed that the dead included four Saudi citizens, two Bangladeshis, and one national each from the United States, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Palestine, and Sudan. Several injured passengers and airport workers remained in critical condition as hospitals received casualties transported by ambulance and helicopter.

The attack struck near passenger facilities, damaging parts of the airport and forcing authorities to suspend operations. Approximately 260 flights were canceled and 219 delayed, disrupting international travel through one of Saudi Arabia's busiest aviation hubs.

Saturday's strike was the third attack on the airport within a week. Two earlier attacks killed four people and damaged a Saudi commercial aircraft. The latest assault occurred without an advance warning from Saudi Arabia's threat detection system, raising concerns about the country's ability to protect civilian aviation infrastructure.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed that an American citizen was among those killed and condemned the attacks on civilians. The Saudi-led coalition described Saturday's strike as a war crime and promised a decisive response. Although Saudi and American officials blamed the Houthis, the group had not immediately claimed responsibility for this particular attack.

Asked whether American forces might join Saudi operations against the Houthis, President Trump responded, "We may. We're going to look at it." He subsequently said he intended to speak with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, describing the attack as unacceptable.

The violence follows renewed fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed Yemeni forces, including battles over territory near the strategically important Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a major shipping route connecting the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden.
