Bank of America Hit With $1.2 Billion Fine Over Money Laundering Failures

Federal Regulators Say Bank Failed to Flag Billions in Suspicious Transactions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Federal regulators have imposed more than $1.2 billion in penalties against Bank of America after finding widespread failures in the bank's efforts to detect suspicious financial activity and comply with U.S. sanctions laws. The enforcement action, announced on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, involves more than $5 billion in suspicious transactions that regulators say the bank failed to properly monitor or report.

The U.S. Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) assessed a $1.2 billion penalty for violations of the Bank Secrecy Act, the federal law requiring financial institutions to maintain safeguards against money laundering and other financial crimes. Regulators identified more than $1 billion in transactions associated with national security concerns and priority illicit-finance risks.

Among the activities cited were transactions potentially connected to Chinese money laundering networks and illicit financial operations along the U.S.-Mexico border. Regulators said weaknesses in the bank's monitoring systems prevented potentially important information from reaching law enforcement agencies in a timely manner.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) separately assessed a $750 million civil penalty, the largest in that agency's history. That amount is included within the coordinated $1.2 billion enforcement action rather than added on top of it.

The Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) also announced an approximately $11.6 million settlement involving apparent violations of sanctions programs related to Russia, Iran, narcotics trafficking, and global terrorism. Together, the coordinated actions bring the announced penalties to approximately $1.21 billion.

Bank of America admitted that it willfully violated provisions of the Bank Secrecy Act, including requirements to maintain an effective anti-money laundering program, conduct appropriate reviews of certain foreign banking relationships, and submit suspicious activity reports.

The enforcement action follows a December 2024 regulatory order addressing deficiencies in the bank's compliance operations. Regulators said the problems persisted despite earlier warnings and required corrective measures.

As part of the settlement, Bank of America must retain an independent third party to review previously overlooked transactions and identify suspicious activity that may still require reporting. The bank said it has invested in additional compliance personnel and technology to strengthen its monitoring systems.

The company expects the penalties to reduce its third-quarter 2026 earnings by approximately $1.2 billion, equivalent to about 17 cents per share.

The case highlights the importance of financial institutions identifying suspicious transactions before potentially illicit funds move through the banking system. The regulatory findings concern failures to detect and report activity; they do not establish that every flagged transaction involved proven criminal conduct.
