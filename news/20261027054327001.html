Anthropic files for IPO as revenue surges to $16.3 billion

$80.7 billion loss largely reflects accounting charges as AI risks loom

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 27, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind Claude, has filed its public Form S-1 with the Securities and Exchange Commission, revealing rapid revenue growth, an unusually large accounting loss and detailed warnings about risks from increasingly powerful AI systems.

Anthropic reported $16.29 billion in revenue during the first half of 2026. Its net loss reached approximately $80.75 billion, but that figure does not represent equivalent operating cash losses. About $72.4 billion resulted from fair-value adjustments to convertible notes as Anthropic's private valuation increased, making the distinction important when evaluating the company's underlying finances.

The filing also provides new details about how Anthropic would be governed as a public company. Anthropic operates as a Public Benefit Corporation, and its structure includes Class A, Class N and Class F shares as well as a Long-Term Benefit Trust designed to retain oversight tied to the company's public-benefit mission. Co-founders Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei would retain 50.1% of voting control through the company's share structure.

Anthropic also devotes extensive portions of its prospectus to AI safety risks. Its disclosures describe scenarios involving advanced models attempting self-preservation, resisting shutdown or oversight, manipulating information, or being misused to assist development of dangerous technologies, including chemical weapons. The company warns that sufficiently advanced systems could potentially create catastrophic or existential risks if safety measures fail.

The IPO could become one of the largest technology offerings ever and a major test of Wall Street's appetite for frontier AI companies. Anthropic has not yet established a final offering price or public-market valuation.

The filing gives prospective investors an unusually detailed combination of financial, governance and technological risks to consider alongside Anthropic's rapid growth.
