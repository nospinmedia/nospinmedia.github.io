Trump replaces national cyber director after wave of tech outages

Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies takes over in acting role

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 20, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

President Trump has removed National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross and named Pentagon Chief Information Officer Kirsten Davies as acting head of the Office of the National Cyber Director, reshuffling White House cybersecurity leadership after a week of major technology disruptions across the United States.

Cairncross, an attorney who had served as National Cyber Director since August 2025, oversaw the administration's March 2026 cybersecurity strategy. Sources close to the White House told CNN and CBS News that Trump supported removing Cairncross in part because of concerns that he lacked strong technical cybersecurity credentials. Davies will initially serve in an acting capacity, meaning permanent appointment to the position would require Senate confirmation.

Davies has served as the Defense Department's Chief Information Officer after being confirmed by the Senate in December 2025 and sworn in in January 2026. She previously held cybersecurity leadership positions at Unilever, Estée Lauder and Booz Allen Hamilton. If nominated permanently, her confirmation would come before a Senate controlled by Democrats beginning in January 2027.

The leadership change follows a series of high-profile technology failures. An FAA air traffic telecommunications failure triggered a nationwide ground stop and disrupted more than 18,000 flights. Separate problems affected JPMorgan Chase banking transactions, Microsoft services and classified National Security Agency networks.

Officials have said there is no evidence those disruptions were part of a coordinated cyberattack. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said there was "zero" indication the outages were coordinated or caused by a foreign or malicious cyberattack. The individual incidents have instead been linked to separate technical problems, including software and network failures.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Rubio publicly congratulated Davies following the announcement. As national cyber director, she will be responsible for coordinating federal cybersecurity strategy across government agencies and working with private-sector operators of critical infrastructure.
