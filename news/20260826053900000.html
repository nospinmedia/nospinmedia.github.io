Nevada Sues Federal Government Over Colorado River Plan

State challenges water cuts that could reduce its allocation by more than 70%

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Nevada, the Colorado River Commission of Nevada, and the Southern Nevada Water Authority (SNWA) have filed a federal lawsuit challenging the U.S. Department of the Interior's newly adopted operating framework for the Colorado River, becoming the first basin state to seek judicial review of the federal drought management plan. Filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Nevada, the lawsuit argues the new rules unfairly place the heaviest mandatory conservation burden on Lower Basin states while exempting Upper Basin states from comparable reductions.

The complaint challenges the Record of Decision signed by Interior Secretary Doug Burgum on August 21, 2026, which establishes operating rules for the river during the 2027-2028 period. Nevada officials contend the framework could reduce the state's annual allocation of 300,000 acre-feet by as much as 213,556 acre-feet during severe shortage conditions, leaving Southern Nevada with significantly less water than state officials believe is necessary to meet long-term municipal demand.

Governor Joe Lombardo and water officials argue the federal government failed to adequately consider Nevada's decades of conservation efforts and the economic consequences of the plan. SNWA General Manager John Entsminger said Southern Nevada reduced its consumptive water use to approximately 198,000 acre-feet in 2025 through aggressive conservation and water recycling programs, but warned the proposed reductions could force supplies well below levels needed to support the Las Vegas metropolitan area during prolonged drought conditions.

The lawsuit asks the court to vacate the federal operating plan and require additional environmental and legal review before mandatory reductions are implemented. The case marks the first major legal challenge to the Biden-era replacement framework adopted under the Trump administration and could influence how future shortages are managed across the seven Colorado River basin states as water demand continues to exceed available supplies.
