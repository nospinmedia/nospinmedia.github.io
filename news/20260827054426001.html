ICE arrests reach record monthly high under Trump's second term

More than half of July detainees had no criminal record

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 27, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Newly released government data show U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) arrested 49,571 people in July 2026, the highest monthly total of President Trump's second term. The figures, provided by ICE to the Deportation Data Project at UC Berkeley and UCLA, represent a 15% increase from 43,021 arrests in June and a 70% increase from 29,241 arrests in February. ICE also carried out 33,990 removals during July, another monthly high for the administration.

The data indicate a continued expansion of immigration enforcement even after the administration shifted away from the large, highly publicized operations that drew national attention earlier this year. Since Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin took office in March, ICE has increasingly relied on traffic stops, arrests at immigration offices and airports, and expanded partnerships with local law enforcement through Section 287(g) agreements. Texas and Florida accounted for nearly 20,000 arrests during the month, making them the busiest enforcement states.

For the first time during the current administration, immigrants with no criminal convictions or pending criminal charges made up the majority of those arrested, accounting for 51% of all July apprehensions. People convicted of violent crimes represented about 4% of arrests, according to the data. ICE averaged approximately 1,599 arrests per day during July, remaining below the administration's long-term goal of 2,000 daily arrests while still marking the agency's busiest month of the president's second term.

The Department of Homeland Security has said its enforcement strategy prioritizes individuals who are in the country unlawfully and those who pose public safety risks, while critics argue the latest figures show enforcement is increasingly affecting immigrants without criminal records. Immigration advocates say the growing use of community arrests has expanded enforcement beyond traditional jail transfers, while administration officials contend the broader strategy is necessary to carry out federal immigration law.
