Shein Prices Hong Kong IPO at $26.5 Billion

Fast-Fashion Giant Raises $1.7 Billion

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 31, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Shein finalized pricing for its long-awaited Hong Kong initial public offering on August 31, valuing the fast-fashion retailer at approximately $26.5 billion ahead of its market debut on September 1. The Singapore-headquartered company sold about 280 million Class B shares at HK$48.56 per share, raising roughly HK$13.6 billion (about $1.73 billion). Shares are scheduled to begin trading under stock code 00625, marking the company's first successful public listing after previous attempts in New York and London were abandoned amid regulatory and political hurdles.

The IPO valuation is significantly below Shein's peak private valuation of $98.2 billion in 2022, reflecting slower growth, increased competition, and greater regulatory scrutiny. The company has also faced questions from lawmakers and regulators over its supply chain, including concerns involving sourcing in China's Xinjiang region, while changes to U.S. trade policy—including the end of the de minimis duty exemption for many low-value imports—have increased pressure on its business model.

Regulatory filings showed that Shein recorded a $99 million net loss during the first quarter of 2026, compared with a $395 million profit during the same period a year earlier. Revenue growth also slowed during 2025 as the retailer faced increasing competition from rivals such as Temu, owned by PDD Holdings. Several major investors, including Boyu Capital, General Atlantic, Tencent, and Tiger Global, committed approximately $383 million as cornerstone investors in the offering.

Under Shein's dual-class share structure, founder and Chief Executive Officer Chris Xu (Xu Yangtian) and senior executives will retain roughly 90% of the company's voting power after the IPO, allowing management to maintain control despite becoming a publicly traded company. Investors will be watching the company's first days of trading for signs of confidence in the retailer's long-term growth following years of delayed listing efforts.
