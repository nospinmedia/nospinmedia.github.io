Montana Family Massacre Claims Nine Lives

Gunman kills eight relatives before setting home on fire and taking his own life

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Authorities have identified the gunman and victims in one of Montana's deadliest family killings in decades after 44-year-old Alan Smith fatally shot eight relatives, including four children, during a family dinner outside Billings before setting the home on fire and dying from a self-inflicted gunshot wound. Investigators said officers responded shortly after 6 p.m. Sunday to reports of an active shooter, hearing additional gunfire as they arrived before flames engulfed the residence.

According to relatives and investigators, the victims spanned four generations of the same blended family. Those killed included Smith's parents, his grandmother, his younger sister Megan Ghekiere, and four children: Landon Ghekiere (15), Owen Ireland (13), Charlotte Ghekiere (12), and Chloe Ireland (7). Family members said Smith had recently been asked to move out of his parents' home before returning during the family's weekly dinner gathering. Police said Smith was found outside the home with a self-inflicted gunshot wound and later died at a hospital.

Authorities said the investigation remains ongoing as forensic teams continue examining the scene after the fire destroyed the house. Fire officials said crews were unable to immediately attack the blaze because officers first had to secure the active shooting scene. The subdivision also lacked fire hydrants, complicating firefighting efforts. Family members said Charlotte Ghekiere managed to call 911 during the attack, an act relatives described as heroic.

Researchers at Northeastern University said the killings marked the 22nd mass killing in the United States during 2026, with domestic incidents continuing to account for the majority of such attacks. Investigators have not announced a formal motive, and authorities said additional findings will be released as the investigation progresses.
