Washington voters approve transgender athlete restrictions

Measure limits K-12 girls' sports based on biological sex

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 6, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Washington voters have approved Initiative 638, changing state policy to restrict participation in girls' K-12 school sports based on biological sex. The measure passed with approximately 57% of voters supporting it, according to election results reported after Tuesday's midterm elections.

The initiative requires athletic programs designated exclusively for female students to be limited to students classified as biologically female. Students participating in those programs will be required to provide documentation from a health care provider verifying their sex before competing.

The vote represents a significant reversal for Washington, which in 2007 became the first state to adopt a statewide policy allowing transgender students to participate in school athletics consistent with their gender identity. The Washington Interscholastic Activities Association's existing rules have allowed transgender athletes to compete according to their gender identity without requiring medical or legal documentation.

Supporters of Initiative 638 argued that separating female athletic competition by biological sex protects competitive fairness and opportunities for girls. Opponents argued that the measure discriminates against transgender students and that requiring medical verification raises privacy concerns, particularly for children participating in school athletics.

The measure comes amid a broader national dispute over transgender participation in women's and girls' sports. More than half of U.S. states have adopted laws or policies restricting transgender athletes from participating in female sports, while those restrictions have faced continuing challenges in state and federal courts.

Initiative 638 is expected to take effect in December 2026, but its implementation may face legal challenges. Civil rights and LGBTQ+ advocacy organizations have raised potential concerns involving privacy and equal-protection provisions, meaning courts could ultimately determine whether some or all of the new requirements can be enforced.

The vote makes Washington one of the latest states to resolve the issue through statewide legislation or a direct vote rather than leaving participation rules primarily to athletic organizations and individual school districts.
