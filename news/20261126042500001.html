Macy’s parade postponed for first time due to weather

100th Thanksgiving parade moved to Saturday as coastal storm hits

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 26, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

The 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade has been postponed from Thursday, November 26, to Saturday, November 28, as a powerful coastal storm threatens New York City and much of the East Coast with damaging winds, heavy rain and coastal flooding. It is the first time weather has forced the parade to be postponed.

Macy’s said the parade will begin at 8:30 a.m. Saturday after organizers determined the expected conditions posed a safety risk to participants and spectators. The parade was suspended from 1942 through 1944 during World War II and its balloons were grounded because of weather in 1971, but it had never previously been rescheduled because of severe weather.

NBC will air a rerun of the 2025 parade from 8:30 a.m. to noon Thursday across all time zones and plans to broadcast the rescheduled 100th parade live beginning at 8:30 a.m. Saturday.

The storm has placed more than 75 million people under wind alerts along the East Coast, with gusts potentially reaching 70 mph along portions of the New Jersey coast. New York Gov. Kathy Hochul declared a state of emergency covering New York City, Long Island and Westchester County, while New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill declared a statewide emergency.

The last-minute schedule change is also affecting parade participants. The University of Alabama Million Dollar Band withdrew from Saturday’s parade and is returning to Tuscaloosa to perform at the Iron Bowl football game. Several other visiting marching bands are staying in New York and have sought additional funding to cover unexpected hotel and travel expenses.

Holiday air travel is also being affected. Delta, American, United and Southwest issued travel waivers for affected East Coast airports, while the Federal Aviation Administration warned that strong winds could trigger delays or ground stops at airports including JFK, LaGuardia, Newark, Miami and Fort Lauderdale.
