Trump says FBI is looking into Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang

Separate chip-smuggling probe involving Megaspeed now led by DOJ

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 10, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

President Trump says the FBI is looking into Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a Chinese businesswoman whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect of passing information to Beijing, adding federal scrutiny to an expanding controversy involving the world's leading AI-chip company.

Huang acknowledged having an extramarital affair after reports identified the woman as Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed International. She is not related to Jensen Huang. Trump said he spoke directly with the Nvidia CEO, who told him he had no idea the woman was allegedly involved in espionage. Trump said he believed him. China has rejected the espionage allegations as groundless.

Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation into whether its CEO was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. The company has said it currently has no reason to believe sensitive company information or U.S. national-security information was compromised.

The controversy overlaps with a separate federal investigation of Megaspeed, which U.S. authorities have scrutinized over suspicions that advanced Nvidia AI chips may have been diverted to China in violation of export controls. No public finding has established that Megaspeed violated those rules.

Reporting by Bloomberg and The New York Times found that Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter contacted Commerce Department officials in 2025 while its Bureau of Industry and Security was investigating Megaspeed. Investigators reportedly became concerned after Teter referenced permission from senior Commerce official Jeff Kessler to contact them. Kessler later instructed investigators to handle the matter strictly according to established procedures, and a Commerce official said he did not direct agents to provide Nvidia information or alter investigative decisions.

The Justice Department subsequently asked Commerce to pause its work, and DOJ is now conducting a criminal investigation into Megaspeed. The precise reason for the transfer remains unclear, making it inaccurate to conclude that Nvidia's contact itself caused the handoff.

Three House committee chairmen have also requested a closed-door briefing from Jensen Huang by November 18 about the allegations and Nvidia's protections against foreign espionage.
