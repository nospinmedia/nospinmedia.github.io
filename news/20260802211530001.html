Sen. Bernie Moreno Urges Rep. Max Miller to Resign

Public rebuke lands days before Ohio’s Aug. 5 ballot swap deadline

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 3, 2026 at 11:56 AM ET

Sen. Bernie Moreno on August 2, 2026 publicly urged his former son-in-law, Rep. Max Miller, to resign from Congress, saying on X that Miller “fails basic standards of character,” is a “danger” to his daughter Emily Moreno, and should seek professional help. The rare, high-profile GOP-on-GOP rebuke escalates a scandal that could put Ohio’s 7th Congressional District in play just days before a replacement deadline under state law.

Moreno’s call came roughly an hour after Miller posted an 18‑minute livestream denying he abused his ex‑wife or their 2‑year‑old daughter and vowing to stay in the race: “I’m not dropping out of this race and I’ll win in November.” Miller argued no agency or court has substantiated abuse allegations. Democrats have pressed for a House Ethics Committee probe, with Reps. Teresa Leger Fernández, Emilia Sykes, and Hillary Scholten urging an investigation. Miller faces union ironworker Brian Poindexter in November; he carried the district with just over 51% of the vote two years ago.

The timing is pivotal: Ohio parties have until Wednesday, August 5, 2026 to replace a congressional nominee who withdraws. Behind the scenes, Republicans have discussed potential stand‑ins should Miller exit; names floated include State Rep. Mike Dovilla and former State Sen. Kevin Coughlin. A late substitution could reshape campaign spending and strategy in a district that President Trump won and national parties view as competitive amid a narrow House majority.

Context driving the rift includes allegations in court filings and police reports that Miller scalded and threatened Emily Moreno and that the couple’s toddler suffered a broken collarbone; Miller denies wrongdoing and has filed defamation claims. Moreno’s statement marks his most forceful public break with Miller and underscores GOP risks if the controversy lingers past the ballot deadline and into peak campaign season.
