U.S. Officials Suspect Iran-Linked Hackers Targeted More Than 30 Minnesota Water Systems

Federal Agencies Warn Utilities To Disconnect Vulnerable Controls From Internet

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 31, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Federal and state authorities are investigating a coordinated cyberattack that targeted operational technology at more than 30 community water systems across Minnesota, as U.S. officials examine evidence suggesting the activity may be connected to Iran-linked hackers. Minnesota IT Services said the unauthorized intrusions occurred on July 26 and 27 and involved malicious access to technology used by local utilities to remotely monitor and control water equipment.

U.S. intelligence agencies have preliminarily assessed that Iran was likely responsible, according to reporting by The Washington Post, while Reuters reported that investigators and officials consider Iranian-linked actors the leading possibility. However, neither the FBI nor Minnesota officials have publicly announced a formal attribution, and the investigation remains ongoing.

Most of the confirmed incidents involved internet-connected operational technology, including programmable logic controllers and computer interfaces used to monitor equipment. Officials said attackers in some incidents changed passwords, locked operators out of systems or forced equipment to be disconnected and manually reset. Water and wastewater utilities in at least seven states have reported related incidents to the FBI, with some attacks causing degraded operations, including pressure loss and flooding.

Minnesota authorities said the attacks did not compromise drinking-water safety, and officials were not aware of any communities asking residents to change their water usage. Some affected utilities temporarily shifted to manual operations while compromised systems were examined and restored.

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency warned water utilities nationwide to remove exposed control equipment from the public internet as quickly as possible. Federal agencies have also urged operators to replace default passwords, restrict remote access, use secure network connections and review equipment for unauthorized configuration changes.

Iran-affiliated hackers have previously targeted U.S. water infrastructure and other critical systems. An April federal advisory warned that attackers were exploiting internet-facing industrial controllers, and a July update expanded the list of potentially targeted equipment manufacturers.
