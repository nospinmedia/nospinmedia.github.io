Shell Profits More Than Double To $9.8 Billion As Middle East War Drives Oil And Gas Prices Higher

Strong Trading Results Help Produce Company's Second-Highest Quarterly Profit On Record

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 30, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Energy giant Shell reported $9.84 billion in adjusted earnings for the second quarter of 2026, more than double the $4.26 billion recorded during the same period last year. The result exceeded analysts' average estimate of $8.92 billion and represented the company's second-highest quarterly profit on record, as conflict in the Middle East pushed oil and natural gas prices higher and created profitable opportunities across Shell's global trading operations.

Shell benefited because the market value of the oil and gas it produces increased faster than many of its operating costs. Its large commodity-trading business also earned money from rapidly changing prices and disruptions to normal supply routes. Shell's integrated gas division generated approximately $2.7 billion in adjusted earnings despite a sharp decline in production, while its chemicals and products business, which includes refining and oil trading, produced about $2.3 billion.

The company achieved the results despite damage and reduced production connected to the regional conflict. Operations at the Pearl gas-to-liquids facility in Qatar were disrupted following an attack, and Shell said full repairs could take about a year. The company's refineries nevertheless operated at exceptionally high utilization levels as elevated fuel prices and stronger refining margins increased earnings.

Shell Chief Executive Wael Sawan credited operational performance, global supply flexibility, and the company's trading network for helping it navigate the disruption. Shell also announced another $3 billion share-buyback program, continuing its practice of returning cash to shareholders while reducing debt.

The earnings renewed criticism from consumer and environmental groups, which argued that major energy companies were receiving windfall profits while households and businesses faced higher fuel and energy bills. Shell maintains that global commodity markets determine oil and gas prices and that its international supply network helps keep energy moving during major disruptions.
