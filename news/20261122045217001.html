Russian Strike Kills 2 In Zelenskyy’s Hometown

Injured Rises To 73 As First Responders Are Among Those Wounded

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 22, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A Russian attack on Kryvyi Rih, the hometown of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, killed two people and wounded at least 73 others, including nine children, according to an updated casualty count from local officials.

The attack hit the central Ukrainian city on Friday night, November 20. Officials identified those killed as a 7-year-old boy and a 20-year-old female paramedic. The boy was critically injured and later died in intensive care. Kryvyi Rih Defense Council head Oleksandr Vilkul said the youngest of the nine wounded children was a 3-year-old girl.

Officials said an initial ballistic missile strike was followed by additional attacks after rescuers, medical personnel and utility workers reached the scene. Vilkul described the sequence as a "triple-tap" attack, with jet-powered drones used in the follow-up strikes. Emergency personnel were among those wounded, including three emergency medical technicians and responders from Ukraine's State Emergency Service.

By Saturday, November 21, officials said 29 people, including six children, remained hospitalized and nine patients were in serious condition. The strikes damaged roughly two dozen apartment buildings as well as a hospital, schools, businesses, ambulances and other vehicles, and disrupted utility and transportation services.

Municipal and utility crews subsequently stabilized gas supplies and restored electric public transportation after trolleybus infrastructure was damaged. Authorities also opened a local relief headquarters to distribute construction materials and provide financial assistance to residents affected by the attack.

The strike came amid heightened fighting between Russia and Ukraine and followed recent Ukrainian attacks on the Kerch Bridge, which connects Russia with Russian-occupied Crimea. It also underscores the danger posed by repeated strikes after emergency personnel arrive at an initial attack site.
