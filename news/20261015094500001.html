Federal Investigators Seize Ilhan Omar's Phone, Subpoena Relatives

More Than a Dozen Grand Jury Subpoenas Issued in Immigration Fraud Inquiry

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

Federal investigators have seized the cellphone of Representative Ilhan Omar, a Minnesota Democrat, and issued more than a dozen grand jury subpoenas, including subpoenas involving members of her family, according to a person familiar with the ongoing criminal investigation.

The new details, reported by the Associated Press on October 14 and corroborated by NBC News, reveal additional investigative steps beyond the previously reported search of Omar's Washington, D.C., residence.

According to the Associated Press, the subpoenas were issued as part of a grand jury investigation in Minnesota examining possible fraud involving immigration paperwork connected to Omar's family. The source spoke anonymously because they were not authorized to discuss the investigation publicly.

The precise scope of the inquiry remains unclear. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has previously suggested investigators were examining potential irregularities in the family's immigration documents.

Meanwhile, Jeanine Pirro, the U.S. attorney for Washington, D.C., said Tuesday that multiple investigations involving Omar were underway. She indicated that her office was also examining questions surrounding the congresswoman's financial disclosures.

Those statements do not establish that investigators have uncovered criminal wrongdoing. Authorities have not publicly disclosed evidence proving immigration fraud, and the issuance of subpoenas does not mean the recipients are accused of crimes.

The developments follow Monday's search of Omar's Washington home by Homeland Security Investigations agents operating under a judicially authorized warrant. Federal authorities have provided limited information about the evidence collected or the specific allegations being examined.

The timing has intensified political controversy because Omar is seeking reelection in the November 3 midterm elections, now less than three weeks away.

The Justice Department has historically followed guidelines discouraging overt investigative actions involving political candidates close to elections. Democratic leaders have questioned whether those longstanding safeguards were respected.

Omar has rejected allegations concerning her immigration history and described the investigation as politically motivated. She maintains that the administration is using federal law enforcement to intimidate a political opponent.

President Trump has denied directing the search, saying the investigation is a Justice Department matter. Administration officials have defended examining potential violations of federal law.

For now, the investigation remains an evidence-gathering process. No criminal charges against Omar were identified in the reporting, and neither the cellphone seizure nor the subpoenas establish guilt.
