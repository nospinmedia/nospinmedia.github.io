U.S. envoys arrive in Moscow for talks with Putin

Visit follows prisoner-swap agreement but no breakthrough on ending Ukraine war

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 12, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Moscow on Thursday, November 12, for talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin, carrying forward a renewed U.S. effort to negotiate an end to Russia's nearly five-year war against Ukraine.

The Moscow visit follows two days of U.S.-mediated negotiations in Abu Dhabi involving Russian and Ukrainian delegations. Those talks ended Wednesday without a major breakthrough on the central disputes blocking a peace agreement, but the two sides agreed in principle to a large-scale prisoner exchange. The final number of prisoners had not been confirmed when the talks concluded. Kushner said negotiators intended to finalize the numbers Thursday and described the planned exchange as potentially the largest humanitarian prisoner release of the war.

The Abu Dhabi meetings were the first direct negotiations between Russian and Ukrainian delegations since talks in Geneva in February. Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky and Ukrainian negotiator Rustem Umerov both confirmed the prisoner-exchange agreement, while officials from all three countries said negotiations would continue.

Major disagreements remain over territory, postwar security guarantees and Europe's role in future negotiations. Ukraine continues to reject Russia's demand that it surrender territory in the Donbas that Moscow does not currently control. Kushner said territorial questions remain among the most difficult issues because of deep mistrust between the two sides.

European governments, the European Union and NATO sent representatives to Abu Dhabi, but they did not participate in the direct trilateral negotiations. Ukrainian officials have argued that Europe must be involved in discussions affecting the continent's future security. Russia has resisted European participation in the negotiating format, although Moscow has not ruled out some future involvement.

Witkoff and Kushner are expected to brief Putin on the Abu Dhabi discussions and explore whether progress can be made on unresolved issues. After Moscow, the U.S. envoys plan to travel to Dublin, Ireland, to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who is attending a gathering of European leaders there. Kushner said the meeting would allow them to brief Zelenskyy and explore ideas developed during the negotiations.

The diplomatic activity comes as fighting continues in Ukraine, including missile and drone attacks, underscoring the gap between renewed negotiations and conditions on the battlefield.
