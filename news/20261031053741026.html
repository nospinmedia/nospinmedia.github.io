U.S. pressure campaign squeezes Iran's economy

Treasury report says inflation hit 90% as oil exports stalled

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 31, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

A new U.S. Treasury Department assessment says the American naval blockade, economic sanctions and efforts to restrict Iran's other trade routes have severely damaged the country's economy, strengthening the Trump administration's argument that economic pressure should be given more time before further military action is considered.

The internal assessment, obtained by Axios, says Iran's annual inflation rate reached about 90% in September, compared with roughly 35% in early 2025. The Iranian rial has lost about a quarter of its value since the pressure campaign began in August, with the unofficial exchange rate reaching a record 2.687 million rials per U.S. dollar in October. The Treasury figures were also reported by The Hill.

The report says Iran has loaded no oil onto tankers since August 25 as the blockade and sanctions restrict exports. Maritime-tracking firm Kpler separately reported that Iran has sold the remaining oil it had stored aboard vessels at sea. Treasury's campaign, called Operation Economic Outcast, also targets Iran's access to banking networks, commercial aviation, international trade routes and cryptocurrency exchanges that could provide alternatives to the conventional financial system.

Iran has attempted to move more trade over land, but the Treasury assessment says those routes are encountering bottlenecks. Thousands of trucks have faced delays of as long as 20 days at the Turkish border, while additional vehicles have backed up along other neighboring routes. Restrictions on Iranian airlines and banks in several countries have added to the economic pressure.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has argued within the administration that the campaign should be allowed more time to work. The Treasury assessment does not conclude that Iran's government is close to collapse, but says the measures are reducing its revenue and increasing economic pressure as the United States simultaneously pursues negotiations with Tehran.
