Iran warns U.S. military bases in Europe could be targeted

Tehran says installations used in renewed attacks would face retaliation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 12, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Iran has warned that U.S. military bases in Europe and other distant locations could be targeted if they are used to support renewed American attacks against the country, expanding Tehran's stated potential response beyond the Middle East.

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said Wednesday that any American military installation involved in an attack on Iran would be considered a potential target. "If the US launches another war against Iran, every military base involved in the aggression — from West Asia to Europe — will be a legitimate target," Rezaei wrote on social media.

Rezaei made similar comments during an interview with Lebanon-based Al Mayadeen, saying Iran would not limit retaliation to nearby American installations. He said that if U.S. aircraft departed from a base in a European country or another distant location to attack Iran, Tehran would consider that base a target.

Another senior Iranian military official, Kioumars Heydari, deputy chief of the Armed Forces General Staff, issued a similar warning Wednesday. He told Iranian state television that U.S. military bases and interests were within range of Iranian missiles and drones and said Iran had prepared potential targets without the geographic restrictions applied during earlier fighting.

The statements are conditional threats of retaliation, not announcements that Iran has selected specific European bases for attack. Rezaei did not publicly identify individual European installations that Iran would target. Iranian media and commentators have discussed possible U.S. and allied facilities, but those lists should not be treated as an official Iranian operational target list.

The warning comes as the U.S. military has repositioned aircraft from RAF Mildenhall in eastern England because of concerns about a potential Iranian threat, adding a concrete European security dimension to Tehran's statements.

The warnings come amid continuing tensions between Iran and the United States following military conflict earlier this year. Tehran and Washington remain divided over sanctions, Iran's nuclear program, the U.S. naval blockade and security arrangements surrounding the Strait of Hormuz. Rezaei said Iran has submitted conditions for ending the confrontation and is awaiting an American response.

President Trump said earlier this week that the military conflict was "effectively over," while indicating that the U.S. naval blockade would remain in place. Iranian officials, however, have continued warning that any renewed American military action would trigger retaliation.
