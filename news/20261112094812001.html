Brooklyn building collapse kills 2 and injures 17

Rescuers search unstable rubble as investigators examine nearby construction

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 12, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

At least two people were killed and 17 others were injured after a three-story mixed-use building collapsed in Brooklyn on Wednesday afternoon, triggering a large search-and-rescue operation and investigations into what caused the structure to fail.

The collapse happened shortly after 4 p.m. Wednesday, November 11, at 1321 Flatbush Avenue in Brooklyn's Flatbush neighborhood. The occupied building, which contained a business on the ground floor and apartments above, fell toward an excavation site next door. Part of an adjoining building was also damaged, leaving rooms and stairways exposed.

FDNY Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore said the injured included four people in critical condition, 12 civilians in stable condition and one firefighter with minor injuries. Seven children were among those hurt. More than 250 fire and police personnel responded, using search dogs, drones, power tools and other equipment to work through unstable piles of brick and timber. Several people were pulled from the wreckage alive.

The cause of the collapse remains under investigation. Construction workers had been performing excavation work on the neighboring property at 2625 Bedford Avenue when the building fell. City records show developers proposed a nine-story mixed-use building for that site, although officials said those plans had not been approved and a three-story project had been authorized. Investigators have not established that the construction caused the collapse.

The Brooklyn District Attorney's Office said it is working with the New York City Department of Buildings and law enforcement to determine whether any criminal conduct contributed to the collapse. City building inspectors are also examining nearby structures for additional safety concerns. Mayor Zohran Mamdani was among city officials responding to the disaster.

For residents displaced by the collapse, New York City Emergency Management and the American Red Cross opened a temporary reception center at 1401 Flatbush Avenue. The neighboring construction site had previously received a city violation in April involving demolition work performed despite a stop-work order; records show that violation was later resolved.
