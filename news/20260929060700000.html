$15 billion steel plant planned for eastern Iowa

Project expected to create 1,750 permanent jobs and begin production in 2030

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 29, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

President Trump and Mesabi Metallics have announced plans for a $15 billion steel manufacturing complex in Lee County, Iowa, a project expected to become one of the largest steel plants in the United States. The company plans to begin production in 2030, creating an estimated 1,750 permanent jobs and supporting as many as 6,000 construction jobs while the facility is built.

The plant is expected to initially produce about 7.5 million tons of steel annually, with later expansion potentially increasing capacity to approximately 10 million tons per year. Mesabi Metallics plans to use iron ore from its newly opened mine in Minnesota, creating a domestic supply chain connecting mining operations there with steel production in Iowa.

Mesabi Metallics is a Minnesota-based company owned by India's Essar Group. The company has already invested more than $2.5 billion in its Minnesota mining operations, including a new iron ore mine and pellet facility. Ore from Minnesota is expected to travel to the Iowa plant using rail and the Mississippi River, where it will be processed into steel using electric arc furnaces along with recycled scrap.

The White House projects the Iowa development will generate about $95 billion in economic activity over its first decade. The administration has pointed to its tariffs on imported steel as a factor encouraging domestic investment, although that is the administration's assessment of what drove the project. The U.S. Export-Import Bank has also committed up to $10 billion in financing connected to Mesabi's broader expansion.

The announcement comes weeks before the November 3 midterm elections, with competitive congressional races underway in Iowa. Republican state officials praised the investment and its expected jobs, while Iowa Democratic leaders said they want more information about potential state incentives, protections for taxpayers, labor participation and impacts on local communities. State lawmakers could be called into a special session to consider incentives, although federal officials said the project is not dependent on local incentives.
