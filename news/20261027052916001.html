Microsoft offers voluntary buyouts to 60,000 workers

More than a quarter of workforce eligible as AI spending grows

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 27, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, more than a quarter of its global workforce, as the technology company seeks to control costs while spending heavily on artificial intelligence infrastructure.

The time-limited program is available to eligible employees at level 70 and below in participating teams in the United States, Canada, United Kingdom and India. Employees in areas including AI experiences, Copilot, finance, human resources, Xbox and Windows + Devices are among those eligible, while marketing, commercial business and security are excluded, according to Reuters. Microsoft employed about 223,000 full-time workers worldwide as of June 30, making the potential pool roughly 27% of its workforce.

Chief People Officer Amy Coleman told employees the voluntary program gives workers considering leaving Microsoft an opportunity to do so with support while allowing participating organizations to adjust their workforces as needs change. Microsoft has not said that all 60,000 eligible employees are expected to accept the offers.

The program comes as Microsoft continues spending heavily on data centers, processors and other infrastructure needed for AI services. The company has forecast approximately $175 billion in capital expenditures for calendar year 2026, while investors increasingly scrutinize whether enormous AI investments across the technology industry will generate sufficient returns.

Microsoft has already made other workforce reductions this year. It previously offered voluntary buyouts to thousands of U.S. employees, and in July announced approximately 4,800 job cuts, equal to about 2.1% of its global workforce, as it restructured its Xbox gaming operations.

The latest offer differs from a traditional layoff because participation is voluntary. Microsoft said eligible employees would receive details about the program directly, and described the initiative as building on lessons from an earlier voluntary retirement program.
