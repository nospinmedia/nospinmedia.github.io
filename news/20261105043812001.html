Voters decide abortion rights in several states

Virginia and Missouri preserve protections while Idaho rejects expansion

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 5, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Voters in several states decided major abortion policy questions in the 2026 midterm elections, producing different outcomes as states continue to set reproductive health policy following the end of the federal constitutional right to abortion.

In Virginia, voters approved Question 1, adding reproductive freedom protections to the state constitution. The amendment protects decisions involving prenatal care, contraception, fertility treatment, miscarriage care and abortion. It allows Virginia to regulate abortion after fetal viability while providing an exception when a physician determines an abortion is necessary to protect the pregnant patient's life or physical or mental health.

Missouri voters rejected Amendment 3, leaving in place reproductive-rights protections voters approved in 2024. The defeated proposal would have substantially restricted abortion while also placing restrictions involving gender-transition procedures for minors into the state constitution. Missouri's existing constitutional protection generally permits abortion until fetal viability.

Abortion was also on the ballot in Nevada and Idaho. Nevada voters considered constitutional protection for reproductive freedom after previously approving an abortion-rights amendment in 2024, a second vote required for a constitutional amendment there. Idaho voters considered Proposition 1, a statutory initiative that would have expanded abortion access to approximately fetal viability rather than amending the state constitution. Idaho voters rejected the proposal.

The measures illustrate how abortion policy continues to be decided state by state through legislatures, courts and direct votes. More than 140 ballot measures in 40 states appeared before voters this election, addressing subjects ranging from reproductive rights to election rules and other state policies.
