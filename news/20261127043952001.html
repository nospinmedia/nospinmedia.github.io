Two Italian technicians killed in drone attack in Kuwait

Kuwait and Italy blame Iran while Tehran denies involvement

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 27, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

Two Italian civilian technicians working on Kuwait's air-defense system were killed Thursday in a drone attack that Kuwait and Italy blamed on Iran, while Tehran denied responsibility and offered to participate in an investigation.

The technicians worked for Rheinmetall Italia and were performing maintenance on detection antennas for Kuwait's Skyguard air-defense system when an explosion occurred at the site, according to Italy's Foreign Ministry. A Kuwaiti army captain and a Pakistani national were also injured.

Kuwait's Defense Ministry said its armed forces detected and engaged several drones inside Kuwaiti airspace. Officials initially reported four people wounded by falling debris at what they described as a "vital facility," before confirming that the two Italians died from their injuries. The incident also caused material damage.

Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said the technicians were working at an air-defense surveillance station when it was targeted. Italy said it would lodge a diplomatic protest with Iran, while Tajani urged restraint and said Gulf countries not involved in the wider conflict should not be attacked.

Iran has denied carrying out the strike. Iranian military spokesman Abolfazl Shekarchi rejected allegations that Iranian forces conducted the attack. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the incident "suspicious," expressed condolences over the deaths and said Tehran was prepared to cooperate with Kuwait and Italy in an investigation.

The attack drew condemnation beyond Kuwait and Italy. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned the strike and called for increased international pressure on Tehran. The United Arab Emirates and Jordan also condemned the attack, describing it as a violation of Kuwait's sovereignty.

The incident comes amid the broader U.S.-Israel-Iran conflict, which began in late February 2026 and has produced attacks and counterattacks across the region. Kuwait hosts U.S. military forces but has sought to avoid becoming directly involved in the conflict.

Responsibility for Thursday's attack remains disputed despite the accusations from Kuwait and Italy and Iran's denial.
