James O'Keefe and Cam Higby charged over Minnesota voting stunt

Prosecutors say they used other voters' identities at polling places

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Conservative activists James O'Keefe and Cam Higby have been charged with felonies in Minnesota over an undercover voting stunt conducted during the state's August 11 primary election. Prosecutors allege the two intentionally misrepresented their identities while applying for ballots at Minneapolis-area polling places.

O'Keefe and Higby visited multiple polling locations wearing disguises they described as “Muslim garb” while recording interactions with election workers. Their stated goal was to demonstrate what they considered vulnerabilities in Minnesota's voter vouching system, which allows a registered voter in a precinct to verify the residence of another eligible voter who lacks conventional proof of address.

The Hennepin County Attorney's Office charged each man with felony unlawful voting and identity theft. County Attorney Mary Moriarty said the men provided names belonging to registered Minnesota voters while presenting themselves as those individuals. Prosecutors said some of the names belonged to deceased people, while others belonged to living voters who had not given permission for their identities to be used.

Importantly, prosecutors said O'Keefe and Higby did not obtain ballots or sign the affidavits required to complete the vouching process. Minnesota law nevertheless prohibits intentionally misrepresenting one's identity when applying for a ballot. Moriarty said election judges noticed the men's behavior and participated in the subsequent investigation.

Higby has denied wrongdoing, describing the prosecution as “lawfare” and saying the project was intended to expose weaknesses in election procedures. O'Keefe and Higby have characterized their activities as undercover journalism.

Minnesota's vouching system requires the person providing the verification to sign an affidavit under penalty of perjury stating that they personally know the voter they are vouching for.
