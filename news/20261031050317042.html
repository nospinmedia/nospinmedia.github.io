Tech sell-off sends Wall Street to worst day since June

Nasdaq falls 2.9% as investors question massive AI spending

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 31, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

A technology-stock sell-off intensified Friday, pulling major U.S. indexes sharply lower as investors questioned whether enormous investments in artificial intelligence will produce returns quickly enough to justify their costs.

The S&P 500 fell 1.8% for its worst day since June, while the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.9% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 572 points, or 1.1%. The decline capped a third consecutive losing week for the S&P 500. Bloomberg reported that the Nasdaq 100 fell far enough during Friday trading to enter its third technical correction of 2026, defined as a decline of at least 10% from a recent peak.

Technology companies bore the brunt of the selling. Intel fell 28.6% after issuing a profit forecast below analysts' expectations, while Nvidia dropped 4.2%. Amazon also declined after reporting weaker-than-expected profit and disclosing substantial spending tied largely to artificial intelligence infrastructure. Alphabet had already suffered a steep decline a day earlier as investors scrutinized its spending and cash flow. NBC News estimated that Friday's technology sell-off erased more than $1 trillion in market value.

The retreat comes as investors take a harder look at the cost of the AI expansion. Major technology companies are committing hundreds of billions of dollars to data centers, computing equipment and other infrastructure while markets increasingly demand evidence that those investments will generate sufficient profits. BBC News reported that Meta, Alphabet, Oracle, Amazon and Microsoft have collectively committed about $800.6 billion in spending during 2026. Higher interest rates are adding to concerns because they can increase financing costs for further data-center construction.

Despite the decline, the broader U.S. stock market remained about 3.1% below its all-time high, according to the Associated Press.
