Zelenskyy Ends Pause on Strikes Against Russian Oil Refineries

Ukraine Accuses U.S. of Withholding Intelligence as Trump Backs Russian Diesel Deal

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Ukraine will resume attacks on Russian oil refineries after a three-day pause intended to accommodate demands from President Trump. The announcement comes amid growing tensions between Kyiv and Washington over American intelligence support, restrictions affecting Starlink satellite communications and a controversial agreement allowing Russian diesel exports.

Speaking to the Financial Times in London, Zelenskyy confirmed that he had instructed Ukrainian forces to suspend attacks on Russian energy infrastructure for three days. The pause was intended to demonstrate cooperation with Washington's efforts to reduce global fuel prices and establish an energy ceasefire. However, Zelenskyy said Russian forces continued attacking Ukrainian energy facilities and civilian areas during that period.

Zelenskyy said more than 100 Ukrainian civilians had been killed in Russian attacks over the preceding week. He argued that Ukraine could not continue observing a unilateral pause while Russia maintained its military campaign.

The Ukrainian president also accused Washington of withholding critical early-warning intelligence about incoming Russian ballistic missiles. He said Ukrainian military officials had been warned that intelligence sharing could be restricted unless Kyiv stopped targeting Russian refineries. According to Zelenskyy, American officials subsequently described the disruption as a technical problem.

President Trump has denied that the United States cut off intelligence sharing with Ukraine. The conflicting accounts have not been independently resolved.

Zelenskyy also criticized disruptions to Starlink, the satellite communications network used extensively by Ukrainian forces. He said reduced service had interfered with frontline operations and attacks against Russian military targets. Elon Musk responded that restrictions affecting the military-grade Starshield network were controlled by the U.S. government and military rather than determined independently by his company.

The dispute follows Trump's agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin to release additional Russian diesel supplies onto international markets. The arrangement includes an initial 300,000 metric tons, followed by larger shipments planned for November and December. Washington has argued that increasing available diesel supplies could help ease fuel prices.

Zelenskyy warned that additional energy agreements with Moscow could provide revenue supporting Russia's military operations. However, analysts have questioned how much the proposed diesel exports would contribute to Russian government finances or relieve global fuel shortages.

The Ukrainian president said he would consider another suspension of refinery attacks if Ukraine received additional American-made Patriot missile interceptors to defend against Russian strikes. He emphasized that cooperation with the United States remains essential to Ukraine's defense, despite the worsening diplomatic relationship.

The renewed refinery campaign could further disrupt Russian fuel production and complicate international efforts to stabilize energy markets. It also highlights a fundamental disagreement between Washington and Kyiv over whether economic pressure on Moscow should take priority over efforts to reduce global fuel prices.
