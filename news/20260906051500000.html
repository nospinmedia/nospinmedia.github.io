Putin pauses Kyiv strikes as U.S. envoys head to Ukraine

Three-hour Kremlin talks end without a peace breakthrough

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Russian President Vladimir Putin ordered a 72-hour pause in Russian strikes on Kyiv as U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner traveled from Moscow toward Ukraine following more than three hours of talks at the Kremlin. The diplomatic push produced no announced breakthrough toward ending Russia's war in Ukraine, but both Moscow and Washington described the discussions as substantive as the U.S. delegation prepared to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Kyiv on September 6.

Kremlin foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov said the American delegation presented a series of ideas for a possible settlement during its meeting with Putin. The discussions also included potential economic cooperation between the United States and Russia. A White House official said the two sides discussed plans for next steps that are expected to be announced in the coming weeks. Witkoff and Kushner left the Kremlin at about 11 p.m. Moscow time after roughly three hours of negotiations.

The temporary pause is narrowly focused on the two capitals rather than the broader war. Russia agreed not to strike Kyiv for three days, while Zelenskyy said Ukraine would refrain from strikes on Moscow during the U.S. envoys' diplomatic mission. Fighting and aerial attacks continued elsewhere. Overnight Russian attacks included 108 drones and six missiles, according to Ukrainian authorities, while Russia reported intercepting 258 Ukrainian drones across multiple regions and surrounding areas. Casualties were reported on both sides.

Major disagreements over a permanent settlement remain unresolved. Russia continues to demand Ukrainian territorial concessions and an end to Kyiv's ambitions to join NATO, while Ukraine has rejected surrendering territory to Moscow. The diplomatic effort comes as fighting continues along an approximately 1,250-kilometer front, more than four years after Russia launched its full-scale invasion on February 24, 2022.
