Analysis finds major errors in DOJ California voter list

42% of flagged names aren't on state's current voter rolls

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 1, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

More than 4 in 10 names on a federal list of people identified as possible noncitizen voters in California cannot be found on the state's current voter rolls, according to a new analysis submitted in federal court. The findings challenge a list the Department of Justice has used in its effort to prevent potentially ineligible ballots from being counted.

Two data analysts with the UCLA Voting Rights Project examined 20,930 names after a federal judge ordered the Justice Department to provide the list to lawyers challenging the government's actions. They reported that 8,880 names, or 42%, did not correspond to anyone in California's current voter file. The analysis also identified at least 22 U.S. citizens on the federal list.

The researchers said that applying the government's methodology indicated nearly 7,500 naturalized U.S. citizens were highly likely to be incorrectly flagged as ineligible. They also said the federal list lacked voter identification numbers, making it more difficult to determine whether people were registered or whether some entries were duplicates.

The findings were submitted by plaintiffs including the Coalition for Humane Immigrant Rights and the UCLA Voting Rights Project, which are challenging the federal government's voter-list efforts. Xavier Becerra, a senior adviser to the project and Democratic candidate for California governor, co-signed the filing.

The Justice Department disputes the analysis. A federal prosecutor in California called the report bogus, while a DOJ spokesperson said the department is seeking to protect legitimate votes from being canceled out by ballots cast by ineligible voters. DOJ previously said its work with the Department of Homeland Security identified more than 20,000 people it considers likely noncitizens registered to vote in California.

The dispute comes just days before the November 3 midterm elections, with federal courts already rejecting attempts to require California to separate ballots associated with people on the list.
