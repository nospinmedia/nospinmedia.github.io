UEFA Says It Has No Confidence in FIFA’s Infantino

World Cup equity plan scrapped after boycott threat

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 3, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

UEFA said on August 1, 2026 it has lost confidence in FIFA President Gianni Infantino, hours after FIFA withdrew a proposal to sell a minority stake in a new World Cup commercial subsidiary to private investors. The move escalates a governance crisis at the heart of global soccer, with Europe signaling that accountability measures are now on the table.

According to reporting, Infantino’s plan would have created a $20 billion company, FIFA Forward Enterprise, with private investors owning 20%. The collapse followed mounting resistance: on Thursday, July 30, UEFA’s 55 national associations authorized a boycott of all FIFA competitions if the deal proceeded; North America’s CONCACAF and the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) also opposed the proposal. On Friday, July 31, senior adviser Carlos Cordeiro resigned, and FIFA’s chief operating officer Kevin Lamour criticized the project as “the **project of one person,” urging leaders to make “the right decisions.” UEFA’s statement denounced “secret schemes on fast-track timescales, cooked up by faceless individuals,” and said “no option should be off the table.”

The immediate impact is twofold: the private equity venture is shelved, and pressure intensifies on FIFA’s leadership ahead of the next presidential contest. Reporting notes a candidate filing deadline of November 18 for the 2027 FIFA presidential election, with the vote to be held in Rabat, Morocco. UEFA says it will work with its member associations and other confederations in the “coming days and weeks” to ensure similar proposals cannot recur, signaling potential reforms around consultation, transparency, and fiduciary oversight.

Context from prior coverage indicates this is the third time a major Infantino initiative has unraveled after backlash, following a $25 billion SoftBank-linked plan in 2018 and the biennial World Cup push in 2021. Reporting also identifies an “anchor investor” as a New York firm launched by Joshua Kushner. UEFA’s latest action, paired with public dissent inside FIFA, marks the sharpest challenge yet to Infantino’s decade-long tenure, with confederations now debating how—and how quickly—to pursue formal leadership reviews.
