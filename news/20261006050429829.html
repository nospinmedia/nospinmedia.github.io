Flydubai co-pilot intended to crash jet into Ben Gurion Airport

Investigators probe how previously flagged pilot reached cockpit

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 6, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Investigators believe the co-pilot accused of attacking the captain of a Flydubai passenger jet intended to crash the aircraft into Ben Gurion International Airport near Tel Aviv, according to officials familiar with the investigation. Hamam Al-Hammani, an Omani national, had previously been identified as a security risk, raising new questions about how he was later hired and cleared to fly an Israel-bound commercial aircraft.

The attack occurred aboard Flydubai Flight FZ1073, traveling from Dubai to Tel Aviv. Investigators say Al-Hammani attacked the captain with an emergency crash axe and attempted to take control of the aircraft. The wounded captain managed to unlock the cockpit, allowing passengers and crew members to overpower the co-pilot. Other qualified pilots aboard the flight then took control and safely landed the aircraft in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia.

According to officials cited by ABC News, Al-Hammani had been suspended as a trainee co-pilot by Oman Air in 2024 after extremist materials were discovered and he was considered a security risk. Investigators are now examining how he subsequently obtained employment with Flydubai and was assigned to a flight bound for Israel. Officials familiar with the investigation have said they believe he intended to strike Ben Gurion Airport, although authorities continue investigating his motive and whether anyone else was involved.

The incident has intensified scrutiny of airline employee screening and the security procedures used for foreign crews flying into Israel. Authorities in the United Arab Emirates are leading the investigation with assistance from Israeli officials, while other countries are examining the suspect's background. The case represents a particularly difficult aviation-security problem because the alleged threat originated from inside the cockpit rather than from a passenger attempting to gain access to it.
