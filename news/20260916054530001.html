Inquiry finds hospital failed to protect babies from Lucy Letby

UK moves toward cot cameras after preventable deaths and injuries

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 16, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A public inquiry has found a "complete failure" to protect babies at Countess of Chester Hospital in England, where former neonatal nurse Lucy Letby was convicted of murdering seven babies and attempting to murder seven others. The report, published September 15, concluded that some deaths and injuries could have been prevented if hospital staff and managers had acted sooner on concerns about Letby.

The inquiry, led by Lady Justice Kathryn Thirlwall, described dysfunctional management, poor governance and a failure to understand basic safeguarding responsibilities. Consultants had repeatedly raised concerns about Letby's presence during unexplained collapses, but senior managers failed to escalate those warnings appropriately. After Letby was removed from the neonatal unit in July 2016, she filed a formal grievance alleging victimization and discrimination. The inquiry found that senior clinicians who had raised concerns were then subjected to what Thirlwall described as a "deplorable" grievance process, and three consultants were ultimately told to apologize to Letby.

The report also sharply criticized how hospital leaders presented the rise in neonatal deaths. Thirlwall described efforts to reassure the hospital board about mortality figures as an "exercise in spin," reflecting wider concerns that protecting the hospital's reputation took priority over confronting the possibility that babies were being deliberately harmed. Police were not contacted until May 2017, nearly a year after Letby had been removed from clinical duties.

The inquiry concluded that two babies would not have died and five others would not have been harmed if Letby had been removed sooner. It also found another baby might have survived and two others might have avoided harm if an earlier insulin poisoning had been recognized.

In Parliament, Health Secretary Yvette Cooper apologized to the affected families and said the government would urgently develop plans for "cot cams" across neonatal units. Thirlwall recommended fitting all neonatal cots and incubators with livestreaming baby monitors, with NHS England directed to produce an implementation roadmap by March 31, 2027. The government also plans stronger safeguarding measures and tougher accountability for NHS managers.
