Musk's America PAC launches midterm spending campaign

FEC filings show $800,000 directed to key races

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 4, 2026 at 12:20 PM ET

Elon Musk's America PAC has begun its first major spending effort of the 2026 midterm election cycle, reporting more than $800,000 in independent expenditures through newly filed Federal Election Commission (FEC) disclosures. The filings cover spending between August 18 and August 31 and show the super PAC directing resources toward competitive Republican Senate and House campaigns ahead of the November elections.

Most of the reported spending was concentrated on Senate contests. According to the filings, the PAC spent approximately $247,595 supporting Ken Paxton in Texas, $170,224 backing Susan Collins in Maine, and $105,675 in Ohio in support of Jon Husted. Additional expenditures targeted Senate races in New Hampshire, Iowa, Michigan, and Alaska. Nearly $90,000 more was directed to House campaigns, including races involving Scott Perry, Ryan Mackenzie, and Rob Bresnahan in Pennsylvania, along with Tom Kean Jr. in New Jersey, Mike Lawler in New York, and Derrick Van Orden in Wisconsin.

The reported expenditures were categorized as independent spending, primarily for direct mail and printed campaign materials. Under federal election law, super PACs may raise and spend unlimited amounts advocating for or against candidates, provided they do not coordinate their activities with the campaigns they support. The latest disclosures mark the committee's first significant reported spending of the current general election season.

America PAC was one of the largest outside political spenders during the 2024 election cycle, and political observers expect outside groups from both parties to increase advertising and voter outreach as Election Day approaches. Campaign finance reports filed with the FEC provide the public with information about how political committees allocate resources across competitive races, offering an early indication of where outside organizations believe control of Congress could be decided.
