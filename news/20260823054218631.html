Texas Judge Rejects Karmelo Anthony's Request for New Trial

Murder Conviction and 35-Year Sentence Remain in Place

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 23, 2026 at 5:38 PM ET

A Texas judge has denied Karmelo Anthony's request for a new trial, leaving intact his murder conviction and 35-year prison sentence for the fatal stabbing of Austin Metcalf during a high school track meet in Frisco. Visiting Judge Michael Chitty issued the ruling after a two-day evidentiary hearing, concluding that the defense had not demonstrated legal grounds to overturn the verdict returned by a Collin County jury in June 2026. Anthony, now 19, was convicted of murdering the 17-year-old Metcalf during an altercation under a team tent at a Frisco Independent School District track meet in April 2025.

Anthony's appellate attorney, Russell Wilson, argued that his client did not receive a fair trial because of courtroom access restrictions and an unwritten agreement between the original defense team and prosecutors to exclude certain evidence concerning both teenagers' prior conduct. The defense contended those decisions influenced Anthony's choice not to testify in support of a self-defense claim. Prosecutors, led by Collin County District Attorney Greg Willis and Bill Wirskye, argued the trial was conducted fairly and that the jury reached its verdict after considering the evidence presented in court.

The hearing also followed the recusal of original trial judge John Roach Jr., who stepped aside after giving a television interview following the verdict. During the post-trial proceedings, attorneys discussed evidence that had been excluded from the original trial, including text messages involving Anthony and school disciplinary records relating to Metcalf. Judge Chitty ultimately ruled that none of the issues raised warranted setting aside the conviction or ordering a new trial.

Anthony's legal team said it plans to appeal the decision to Texas's Fifth Court of Appeals, where appellate judges will review whether any legal errors affected the outcome of the trial. Until then, the conviction and sentence remain in effect while the appellate process moves forward.
