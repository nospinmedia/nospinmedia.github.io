Mexico fireworks explosion kills 10, injures 64

Prosecutors investigate deadly blast at religious festival

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 7, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

At least 10 people were killed and 64 others injured when fireworks exploded during a religious festival in central Mexico, causing part of a structure to collapse onto people gathered for the celebration. The explosion occurred late September 5 in Santa María Canchesdá, a community in the municipality of Temascalcingo in Mexico State, where residents were celebrating festivities honoring Our Lady of the Light.

The blast occurred near the Nuestra Señora de la Luz church as fireworks were being used during the celebration. Witnesses said sparks from a traditional pyrotechnic bull known as a torito may have reached fireworks stored nearby, but authorities have not established the official cause. The explosion destroyed part of the structure where pyrotechnic material was kept, bringing down a roof and walls and trapping victims beneath debris. Emergency crews worked through the night using heavy equipment to search the rubble.

Authorities initially reported eight deaths at the scene before two additional victims died from their injuries, bringing the toll to 10. Dozens of injured people were taken to hospitals throughout the region, including the General Hospital of Atlacomulco. Two Catholic priests were among those hospitalized. Emergency personnel from state and local civil protection agencies, police and the Mexican Red Cross participated in the response.

The Mexico State Attorney General's Office opened an investigation into the explosion, including the circumstances surrounding the storage and handling of the fireworks. Mexico has a long tradition of using elaborate pyrotechnics during religious and community celebrations, but deadly accidents have repeatedly raised concerns about manufacturing and storage practices. In Tultepec, another Mexico State community known for fireworks production, an explosion at a pyrotechnics market killed 42 people in 2016, while another series of explosions there killed 24 people in 2018.
