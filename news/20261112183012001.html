House petition forces vote on new Epstein files bill

Bipartisan measure would allow lawsuits seeking unreleased DOJ records

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 12, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A bipartisan effort to force another House vote over the government's handling of Jeffrey Epstein records reached a key threshold Wednesday, after a discharge petition secured the required 218 signatures.

Republican Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky and Democratic Rep. Ro Khanna of California are leading the effort behind the Epstein Files Transparency Act II, which seeks additional disclosure of Justice Department records related to Epstein. A discharge petition allows House members to bypass leadership and eventually bring legislation to the floor once a majority of representatives sign it.

The petition reached 218 signatures with support from all 214 House Democrats and four Republicans: Massie and Reps. Nancy Mace and Ralph Norman of South Carolina and Brian Fitzpatrick of Pennsylvania.

The legislation follows an earlier Epstein transparency law enacted in 2025. Massie and other supporters of the new measure argue that the Justice Department has not fully complied with that law, saying millions of records remain unreleased and that documents already disclosed contain excessive redactions.

The new bill would add a significant enforcement mechanism. It would allow members of Congress, state attorneys general and Epstein survivors to sue the Justice Department over alleged failures to comply with disclosure requirements and seek access to unredacted records. Existing legal protections for victims and other sensitive information would still affect what could legally be made public.

Reaching 218 signatures does not mean the legislation has passed. Under House discharge procedures, the petition must remain on the calendar for at least seven legislative days before a signer can seek floor action. That makes December 2 or 3 the earliest possible window under the current schedule, although the actual timing of a vote could come later.

The successful petition is notable because discharge petitions are designed specifically to overcome resistance or inaction from House leadership. In this case, a small group of Republicans joined the entire Democratic caucus to reach the threshold necessary to advance the legislation independently of leadership.

Even if the House approves the measure, it would still need to pass the Senate and be signed by President Trump, or overcome a presidential veto, before becoming law.
