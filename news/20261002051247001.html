U.S. pushes Europe to release emergency diesel reserves

Trump weighs export ban as American diesel reaches $6.38 a gallon

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 2, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The Trump administration is pressing France, Germany and other European allies to release diesel from emergency reserves as the United States considers restricting its own diesel exports in an effort to bring down sharply higher fuel prices.

The administration has told France and Germany to draw down emergency diesel inventories or potentially face a U.S. export ban, according to people familiar with the discussions. One European source told Reuters that Washington has asked the European Union to release 120 million barrels of diesel over six months. The European Commission, France, Germany, Italy, Ireland and Britain have discussed possible releases as governments respond to tight global supplies.

The pressure comes as diesel costs have become a major concern in the United States. The average U.S. diesel price reached $6.38 per gallon Thursday, according to AAA, about 70% higher than when the war with Iran began. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called on European partners to accelerate existing commitments and make additional supplies available, arguing that American farmers, truckers and businesses should not bear the burden of disruptions to global energy markets.

Europe faces its own difficult calculation. The region has become more dependent on American fuel since banning Russian petroleum imports following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, while the conflict with Iran has disrupted supplies from the Middle East. Russia's diesel export ban has added further pressure. European governments must weigh releasing emergency stocks to increase supply now against preserving those reserves in case the fuel shortage worsens.

President Trump has said the United States could restrict diesel exports as his administration seeks to lower domestic prices ahead of the November midterm elections. The U.S. normally exports roughly 1.2 million to 1.5 million barrels of diesel per day, meaning restrictions could reduce supplies available elsewhere even as Washington asks European countries to put more fuel onto the global market.
