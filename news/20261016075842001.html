Suspicious Mailings Expand to Election Officials and Members of Congress

FBI and Postal Inspectors Investigate Letters Containing White Powder and Threats

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 16, 2026 at 10:37 AM ET

Federal authorities are investigating a widening series of suspicious letters sent to election officials and members of Congress, expanding an investigation that initially involved election offices in several states. Some envelopes contained white powder or threatening messages, prompting hazardous-material responses and temporary evacuations.

The FBI and U.S. Postal Inspection Service are jointly investigating the mailings and working to determine whether they are connected. Authorities have said substances tested from several of the letters have not been found to be hazardous, but additional envelopes remain under investigation.

The latest recipients include congressional offices as well as state and local election officials. Offices associated with Democratic Sens. Chris Murphy of Connecticut and Jacky Rosen of Nevada were among those receiving suspicious mail. Similar letters have been reported at government offices in multiple states as authorities work to identify their origin and sender.

The investigation expands on earlier reports of white-powder letters targeting election offices ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. Election officials in Michigan, Wisconsin and Pennsylvania were among the initial recipients. The new congressional mailings indicate the campaign is not limited to election administrators.

Law enforcement agencies have urged recipients not to open suspicious envelopes and to isolate any questionable mail while contacting authorities. Even when a substance turns out to be harmless, sending threatening communications or materials intended to appear dangerous can result in federal criminal charges.

No injuries or illnesses linked to the mailings had been reported. Investigators had also not publicly identified a suspect or announced a motive. Authorities were examining the envelopes, their contents, postal markings and other evidence to determine whether the incidents were coordinated.

The mailings come as election offices nationwide prepare for increased activity ahead of the midterms, adding another security concern for officials responsible for administering voting and counting ballots.
