Hunter Biden Says Joe Biden's Cancer Has Spread Further

Former president experiencing significant pain as treatment continues

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 10, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

Hunter Biden has disclosed new details about former President Joe Biden's prostate cancer, saying the disease has spread further and is causing his father significant pain. In an interview with the BBC broadcast on August 7, 2026, Hunter Biden said the cancer has "metastasized into his bones and further" and described the 83-year-old former president's condition as very painful and debilitating in several respects.

Biden's office first announced his diagnosis in May 2025, when doctors said he had an aggressive form of prostate cancer with a Gleason score of 9 that had already spread to his bones. At the time, his office said the cancer was hormone-sensitive, allowing doctors to manage it with treatment. Hunter Biden's latest comments do not represent the first disclosure that the cancer had reached Biden's bones; the new information is that his son says the disease has since spread further and is now causing substantial pain.

Hunter Biden said watching his father's illness has been difficult for the family and added that he wishes his father would complain more about what he is experiencing. Despite the illness, he said Biden remains engaged with public affairs and continues speaking about issues important to him. The former president has made occasional public appearances since leaving office in January 2025 and continues working on his next memoir.

That memoir, Promise Me, America, is scheduled for publication on November 17, 2026, shortly after the midterm elections. Biden has largely remained outside daily political life while continuing to discuss his presidency and public service. Neither his office nor his son announced a new treatment plan in connection with the latest disclosure, and the family's comments did not provide additional medical details about where the cancer has spread beyond the previously confirmed bone metastases.
