Hawaii Braces for Tropical Storm Moke

Flooding Threat Grows After Hurricane Lala Soaked Islands

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 23, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Tropical Storm Moke is expected to bring heavy rainfall, dangerous surf, and the risk of flash flooding to parts of Hawaii as it passes south of the island chain. The Central Pacific Hurricane Center said the storm was packing maximum sustained winds of about 45 mph while moving west at roughly 14 mph. Although forecasters do not expect the storm's center to make landfall, its outer rain bands could produce 5 to 10 inches of rain across windward and southeastern portions of Hawaii Island, with isolated totals reaching 15 inches.

State and county officials have expanded emergency preparations because the ground remains saturated from Hurricane Lala, which affected the islands just over a week earlier. Governor Josh Green requested a federal emergency declaration for Hawaiʻi County, Maui County, and the City and County of Honolulu to speed access to disaster resources if conditions worsen. Emergency managers warned that already saturated soils significantly increase the risk of flash floods, mudslides, rockslides, and rapidly rising streams.

Local officials have also taken additional preparedness measures ahead of the storm. Hawaiʻi County Mayor C. Kimo Alameda declared a local state of emergency, while the Hawaiʻi National Guard mobilized personnel and specialized high-water vehicles to support flood response operations and clear drainage channels. Authorities urged residents in flood-prone areas to monitor forecasts closely, prepare emergency supplies, and avoid traveling across flooded roadways as rainfall intensifies.

The approaching storm follows extensive damage caused by Hurricane Lala, which brought exceptional rainfall, road closures, and millions of dollars in infrastructure and residential losses across Hawaii Island. Officials said even a weaker tropical system can produce significant impacts when it arrives over terrain already saturated by a previous storm. Forecasters expect the greatest hazards from Moke to be flooding, high surf, and localized landslides rather than destructive winds.
