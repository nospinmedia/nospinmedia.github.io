ICE officer faces Minnesota charges over shooting into occupied home

Agent also accused federally of lying to FBI about January encounter

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 18, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

ICE officer Christian Castro made his first appearance in Minnesota state court September 17 on charges stemming from the January 14 shooting of Julio Cesar Sosa-Celis during an immigration enforcement operation in Minneapolis. Castro faces four felony counts of second-degree assault with a dangerous weapon and one misdemeanor count of falsely reporting a crime. He is presumed innocent unless proven guilty.

Minnesota's criminal complaint alleges Castro pursued another Venezuelan man to a north Minneapolis duplex and struggled with him outside. Sosa-Celis came outside during the encounter, and the two men then ran into the home. Prosecutors allege Castro stood outside, drew his service weapon and fired once through the front door, striking Sosa-Celis in the leg. Multiple people, including children, were inside, and investigators said the bullet ultimately lodged in a wall of the downstairs apartment. Castro's attorneys have maintained that he acted in self-defense.

The case also produced a separate federal prosecution. A federal grand jury indicted Castro on six counts of making materially false statements to the FBI. Federal prosecutors allege Castro falsely told an FBI agent that men had attacked him with a broom and snow shovel and that he fired while still on the ground. Video evidence described in the state complaint and federal indictment portrays a substantially different sequence. The federal charges are also allegations, and Castro is presumed innocent.

Castro's return to Minnesota followed a months-long extradition dispute after his May 29 arrest in Texas. Minnesota officials sued Texas Gov. Greg Abbott after Abbott did not sign the state's extradition warrant. Castro later returned to Minnesota after surrendering on the federal charges, allowing the state case to proceed.

A judge set bail at $400,000, or $200,000 with conditions. Those conditions include electronic home monitoring, no firearms, surrender of his passport and no contact with witnesses, including members of his ICE team. His next state-court appearance is scheduled for November 3.
