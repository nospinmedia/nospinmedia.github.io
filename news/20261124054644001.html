Rep. Jimmy Gomez to resign after ethics panel finds evidence of sexual misconduct

California Democrat disputes findings but will leave Congress Dec. 1

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 24, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Democratic Rep. Jimmy Gomez of California will resign from Congress effective December 1 after the bipartisan House Ethics Committee found "substantial evidence" that he made unwelcome sexual advances and engaged in unwelcome sexual contact with a female congressional staffer.

Gomez has represented California's 34th Congressional District since 2017 and is serving his fifth term. The committee released a 19-page report on Monday detailing two encounters involving a woman who worked in a congressional leadership office.

According to the committee, one incident occurred at a bar in 2024, where investigators found substantial evidence that Gomez engaged in nonconsensual sexual contact with the staffer. The report described a second encounter inside Gomez's congressional office in which he was allegedly "forceful" with her. Investigators concluded that the conduct violated House rules and said it could constitute misdemeanor sexual abuse under District of Columbia law. The committee's finding is not a criminal conviction.

The report said the encounters caused the staffer significant distress and contributed to her decision to leave Capitol Hill.

Gomez disputes the committee's conclusions. He acknowledged an extramarital relationship but maintains that his interactions with the woman were consensual. In announcing his resignation, Gomez apologized to his family and constituents for "personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage," while accusing the committee of selectively quoting his testimony and not giving him an adequate opportunity to respond to its conclusions.

Gomez was already scheduled to leave Congress in January after losing his November reelection bid to fellow Democrat Angela Gonzales-Torres. He said he selected December 1 as his resignation date to give his congressional staff time to close out constituent casework.

Because Gomez had already lost reelection and little time remains in the 119th Congress, the Ethics Committee did not recommend a specific disciplinary sanction, leaving any additional action to the full House.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, Minority Whip Katherine Clark and Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar called the findings "serious and deeply troubling" and said Gomez's resignation was appropriate.

Attorneys for the former staffer said the findings validated her account and demonstrated that Gomez abused his position of power. Gomez continues to reject the committee's determination that the encounters were nonconsensual.
