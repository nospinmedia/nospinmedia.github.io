SpaceX plans $74 billion AI expansion in Texas and Arizona

Two supercomputing campuses face local votes over infrastructure and environmental concerns

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 13, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

SpaceX plans to invest more than $74 billion in two large artificial intelligence supercomputing campuses in Texas and Arizona, significantly expanding the company's computing and energy infrastructure in the Southwest.

The larger project calls for approximately $50 billion in Cochise County, Arizona, where an application filed under Tombstone Resources L.L.C. proposes a 747-acre campus with as many as nine computing buildings constructed in four phases. The facility could eventually scale to 6 gigawatts of computing capacity and support about 5,500 construction jobs and 1,000 permanent positions.

The Cochise County Planning and Zoning Commission will consider the proposal December 9, with construction potentially beginning in March 2027 if approvals are obtained. SpaceX is negotiating with Sulphur Springs Valley Electric Cooperative and Arizona Electric Power Cooperative on power arrangements that the company says could eventually reduce electric bills for cooperative members by as much as 20%. Local residents have raised concerns about the project's environmental and infrastructure effects.

In South Texas, SpaceX plans to invest more than $24 billion at Starbase, supporting more than 3,000 construction jobs and eventually more than 500 permanent positions. The company plans to generate electricity using 35 natural gas turbines, initially providing about 748 megawatts of capacity.

SpaceX has also committed $220 million toward Brownsville-area water and wastewater infrastructure, including $40 million already paid to the Brownsville Public Utilities Board. Planned projects would provide 8 million gallons per day through potable reuse and another 12 million gallons through brackish-groundwater treatment.

The Starbase City Commission will hold a December 9 public hearing on a proposed property-tax abatement for the Texas project. Environmental groups have raised concerns about emissions from the planned gas turbines, while Arizona residents have raised concerns about wildlife and the project's location near habitat used by protected sandhill cranes.
