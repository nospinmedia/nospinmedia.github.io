New Orleans port sues after tanker overturns cruise ship

Judge authorizes seizure as terminal damage tops $1 million

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 15, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The Port of New Orleans has sued the owner and manager of the tanker that struck a docked river cruise ship on the Mississippi River, seeking more than $1 million in damages as investigators examine why the tanker apparently lost steering before the collision.

The tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the American Heritage, operated by American Cruise Lines, at the port's Thalia Street Terminal around 12:30 p.m. Friday. The impact caused the much smaller cruise vessel to roll onto its side and partially sink while passengers and crew were still aboard.

All 197 passengers and crew members aboard the American Heritage survived, as did the tanker's crew. Thirty-two people, including two police officers who were thrown into the Mississippi River during the rescue, were taken to hospitals. Officials reported no critical injuries, and eight people remained hospitalized Saturday.

About 100 people ended up in the river during the collision and evacuation. Passengers and officials credited life jackets, emergency responders and nearby vessels with helping prevent deaths in an incident New Orleans officials later described as a "miracle on the Mississippi."

The Port of New Orleans filed its lawsuit in federal court against the Atlantic Asphalt, its owner Nave Shipping SA, and its manager Thome Ship Management Pte Ltd. The port alleges negligence and says the collision significantly damaged the Thalia Street Terminal. Its claim includes cleanup, inspections, repairs and other expenses, with officials warning that the total could increase as the damage is fully assessed.

A federal judge also authorized the seizure of the Atlantic Asphalt, issuing a warrant that allows federal officials to take control of the tanker while the case proceeds. The port has asked that the vessel ultimately be sold if necessary to satisfy its financial claims. No sale has been ordered.

The tanker has been separated from the American Heritage and was reported stable at anchor while inspections continued. Thome said the tanker's crew was safe and that the company was cooperating with authorities.

The National Transportation Safety Board has recovered the tanker's voyage data recorder and is interviewing witnesses. Investigators are examining reports that the Atlantic Asphalt lost steering before striking the stationary cruise ship. The NTSB expects to issue a preliminary report within about 30 days, while a complete investigation could take 18 to 24 months.

The American Heritage remained on its side Saturday. Crews must remove fuel and stabilize the vessel before it can be moved, a process officials said could take weeks.
