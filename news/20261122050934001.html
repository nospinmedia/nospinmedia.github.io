FBI Most-Wanted Cartel Boss Captured In Mexico

11 Killed As Arrest Sparks Gunfights, Roadblocks And Burning Vehicles

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 22, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Mexican forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, the alleged cartel leader known as "El Chapo Isidro" and one of the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives, after an operation in Sinaloa that left 11 people dead and triggered hours of gunfire, roadblocks and burning vehicles.

The operation began on Friday night, November 20, in Guasave, where Mexican military and National Guard forces moved to arrest Meza Flores. Officials said suspected cartel gunmen attacked security forces with firearms and explosives. Mexico's Defense Ministry reported that four Mexican soldiers and seven suspected cartel gunmen were killed. No civilian deaths were reported.

Authorities detained eight people, including Meza Flores and two alleged senior associates. Officials identified his cousin Germán N., also known as "Serafín," as the organization's alleged second-in-command and his brother-in-law José N. as an alleged financial operator. Meza Flores was later transported to Mexico City.

The arrests triggered retaliatory violence around Guasave and nearby highways, where armed groups erected roadblocks and set vehicles and businesses on fire. Residents sheltered inside businesses as gunfire continued and military helicopters operated overhead.

The 44-year-old Meza Flores has long been associated with the Beltrán Leyva Organization and more recently allied with the "Los Mayos" faction amid internal conflict within the Sinaloa Cartel. The FBI placed him on its Ten Most Wanted list in February 2025, while the U.S. State Department offered up to $5 million for information leading to his arrest or conviction.

The U.S. Department of Justice said the capture followed a Homeland Security Task Force investigation involving American and Mexican authorities. FBI Director Kash Patel also confirmed the arrest following the investigation.

Mexican Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch called the detentions "an important step toward restoring peace in Sinaloa" and said security forces would remain deployed to protect civilians.
