Leaked grand jury report faults Hope Florida funding transfer

No criminal charges filed over $10M Medicaid settlement diversion

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 28, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A leaked Leon County grand jury report concluded that $10 million from a $67 million Medicaid settlement with Centene Corporation was improperly redirected through the Hope Florida Foundation, a nonprofit initiative associated with Florida First Lady Casey DeSantis, before ultimately reaching organizations that financed opposition to Amendment 3, the failed 2024 ballot measure to legalize recreational marijuana. Although the grand jury sharply criticized how the money was handled, it declined to seek criminal indictments.

According to the report, Florida revised the settlement agreement so that $57 million was paid directly to the state while $10 million was sent to the Hope Florida Foundation. The foundation then awarded two $5 million grants to Save Our Society From Drugs and Secure Florida's Future. Investigators found those organizations later transferred millions of dollars to Keep Florida Clean, a political committee chaired by James Uthmeier, who served as Governor Ron DeSantis' chief of staff at the time and is now Florida's attorney general. The committee led the campaign opposing Amendment 3.

The grand jury described the funding arrangement as an improper diversion of public settlement money but said investigators could not establish sufficient evidence to pursue criminal charges against specific individuals. Jurors wrote that witnesses frequently denied responsibility or said they could not recall key decisions surrounding the transfer of funds, making it impossible to prove criminal intent beyond the legal standard required for indictments.

Governor Ron DeSantis rejected the report's conclusions, calling the leak politically motivated and demanding an investigation into how the sealed document became public. Democratic lawmakers and government watchdogs have renewed calls for additional investigations and legislative reforms governing state settlement funds and nonprofit oversight. The report is expected to intensify scrutiny of the Hope Florida program as state officials continue reviewing its financial practices.
