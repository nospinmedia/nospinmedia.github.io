Russian Court Jails Opposition Politician Lev Shlosberg For 11 Years

Supreme Court Upholds Yabloko Ballot Ban Ahead of Sept. 18–20 Vote

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 8:08 AM ET

On August 17, 2026, a court in Pskov sentenced opposition politician Lev Shlosberg, 63, the deputy chairman of Yabloko, to 11 years and 1 month in a penal colony for statements opposing Russia’s war in Ukraine. He was convicted under laws criminalizing “discrediting” the armed forces and disseminating “false information” about the military. Shlosberg has remained in Russia despite a sweeping crackdown since February 2022.

Prosecutors tied the charges to an online debate in which Shlosberg called for ending the war and to his reposting of a British tabloid front page on his Telegram channel. He has been held in pretrial detention since December 2025. In his final courtroom remarks, Shlosberg said Russia had moved “from hopes for freedom to the almost complete destruction of human and civil rights,” and warned the country would “shudder” when the war’s true death toll is known. Yabloko called the punishment “barbaric” and said an appeal is planned.

In a separate decision the same day, Russia’s Supreme Court upheld removing Yabloko—the only registered party openly opposing the war—from the ballot for the September 18–20 State Duma elections, denying the party’s appeal. Russian media reported several dozen detentions near the court. Party leaders Nikolai Rybakov and Grigory Yavlinsky criticized the ruling and vowed to continue legal challenges. The twin rulings narrow space for organized anti-war participation ahead of the vote.

Rights groups reacted quickly. Amnesty International condemned Shlosberg’s conviction as an arbitrary reprisal for peaceful expression and urged his immediate release. Russia’s wartime speech laws on “discrediting” the military and spreading “false information” carry penalties of up to 15 years, and have been increasingly used against political figures and activists. AP reporting identified Shlosberg’s case as part of a broader pre-election clampdown; additional outlets confirmed the sentence length, his age, location, and the Supreme Court’s decision affecting Yabloko’s ballot access.
