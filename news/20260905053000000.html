Labor watchdog details misconduct under Chavez-DeRemer

Report finds toxic workplace and misuse of government resources

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Former Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer oversaw a workplace described by employees as "toxic, intimidating and humiliating" and repeatedly violated department policies, according to a new investigation by the Department of Labor's Office of Inspector General. The watchdog found that Chavez-DeRemer directed government employees to handle personal tasks, improperly mixed personal and official travel, violated workplace alcohol policies and failed to properly report several gifts.

Investigators interviewed 53 current and former Labor Department employees and reviewed more than 500 documents, images and videos. Among the findings, Chavez-DeRemer directed her personal aide and executive assistant to travel to her home during the workday in a government vehicle and organize her bedroom closet, including arranging clothing, shoes and purses. Staff members were also sent to retrieve packages and make personal purchases. The report found that senior officials routinely used threatening, demeaning and abusive communications with employees and that Chavez-DeRemer failed to stop the conduct.

The watchdog also found that Chavez-DeRemer maintained an "inappropriately close and unprofessional relationship" with a senior member of her security detail. Investigators examined hotel-door records, key-fob records and video footage documenting interactions between the two, but did not find direct evidence of a romantic or sexual relationship. Chavez-DeRemer and the security agent did not participate in interviews with investigators.

The report additionally detailed unauthorized alcohol use on federal property, personal activities incorporated into government-funded travel and unreported gifts that included rodeo tickets, an alligator-hide wallet and cowboy hats. During one personal trip to Oregon, investigators found that Chavez-DeRemer visited a strip club with members of her security detail and instructed her driver to give money to a performer despite his objections.

Chavez-DeRemer resigned as Labor secretary in April 2026. Her attorney, Nick Oberheiden, disputed the significance of the findings and said the report confirmed that she had not violated any laws.
