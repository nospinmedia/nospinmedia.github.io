UAE Suspends Iran Trade After Missile Scare

Two ballistic missiles fell into waters near Gulf state

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 19, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The United Arab Emirates suspended all trade activities, commercial exchanges and financial transactions with Iran until further notice after its defense ministry said two ballistic missiles launched from Iran were detected on August 18, 2026. The missiles fell into the sea, with one landing inside UAE territorial waters and the other outside them. Iran denied responsibility for the launches.

The UAE Ministry of Defence said its assessment indicated the missiles were targeting maritime traffic near the Strait of Hormuz. The incident was the first reported missile threat involving Iran and the UAE since a May 4 strike on the port of Fujairah. Emergency alerts were sent to residents during Tuesday's incident before authorities later issued an all-clear. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei rejected the UAE's accusations as baseless.

The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs said the suspension was imposed because of regional escalation that it said threatened regional and international peace and security. The decision cuts off a significant commercial relationship between the two countries at a time when shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is already heavily disrupted. Direct cargo shipping between the UAE and Iran had only resumed in late June through Dubai's Jebel Ali Port after being suspended in early March.

The missile incident comes amid renewed tension surrounding the Strait of Hormuz. Iran says the waterway will remain closed until the United States meets conditions contained in a June interim agreement, while President Trump said Tuesday that no talks with Tehran were taking place or scheduled. The Strait carried roughly one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas flows before the current conflict, making continued disruption a major concern for energy markets and commercial shipping. UAE stock markets fell in early trading Wednesday following the missile scare and trade suspension.
