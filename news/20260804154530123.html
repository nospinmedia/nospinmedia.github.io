WestJet, CUPE Reach Tentative Deal To End Cabin-Crew Strike

495 flights canceled; 250,000 trips disrupted as operations restart

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

WestJet and CUPE Local 8125 said on August 3, 2026 they reached a tentative collective agreement, ending a brief nationwide walkout by roughly 4,400 flight attendants that upended travel over Canada’s civic holiday weekend. The union said cabin crew are returning to work while the draft deal proceeds to a ratification vote, allowing the airline to begin restoring service.

The stoppage triggered widespread cancellations at the peak of summer travel. According to the airline, 495 flights were canceled on August 2, and at least 250,000 travelers had trips scrapped over the weekend. Disruptions were concentrated on WestJet’s mainline network; the carrier had earlier indicated regional WestJet Encore and partner-operated code shares were not covered by the action. Passengers were urged to check flight status as schedules are rebuilt.

The dispute centered on compensation for ground duties such as boarding, safety checks, and delays before takeoff. Union leaders said the tentative agreement addresses pre-flight and other duty time that had been a flashpoint for members. WestJet, which maintains that cabin crew are paid under a broader “credit hour” system rather than a strict clock, previously outlined a compensation framework that, per the company, included a 13% wage increase effective in October, additional 2.5% annual raises over the next three years, retroactive pay to January 2026, and pay recognition for all hours worked. Full contract terms remain confidential pending membership review and a ratification vote.

What’s next for travelers is recovery: the airline said teams are working to re-establish normal operations and rebook affected customers. Given the scale of cancellations and crew/aircraft positioning needs, some residual delays are likely as the network stabilizes. For readers, the key near-term steps are to monitor rebooking notices, verify airport departure times, and consider alternative routings if flexibility is available.
