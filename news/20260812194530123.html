ICE Plans Up To $20M Purchase Of Electric-Shock Gloves

Potential no-bid contract draws civil rights concerns

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) plans to spend up to $20 million on gloves capable of delivering electric shocks to help officers gain compliance from combative individuals, according to a Department of Homeland Security procurement notice published on August 10, 2026. The notice says a solicitation for a potential no-bid contract could be published as early as Friday, with ICE seeking to purchase thousands of the devices by March.

The gloves, known as the G.L.O.V.E. (Generated Low Output Voltage Emitter), are manufactured by Compliant Technologies LLC of Lexington, Kentucky. They function as ordinary patrol gloves until activated by an officer, then deliver an electric shock through direct skin contact. The manufacturer says the brief pain stimulus is intended to quickly gain compliance without causing lasting injury and requires officers to complete training with recertification every two years. The company also advises the gloves should not be used as punishment or on children, pregnant people, the elderly, or other high-risk individuals.

The proposal immediately drew criticism from civil rights advocates, who questioned introducing the devices into civil immigration enforcement. Jenn Rolnick Borchetta of the American Civil Liberties Union warned the concealed nature of the gloves could increase the risk of misuse. Supporters, including John Peters of the Institute for the Prevention of In-Custody Deaths, argued the devices could shorten dangerous confrontations and reduce injuries by ending struggles more quickly.

Department of Homeland Security officials said they were preparing a response to questions about the planned procurement but had not announced deployment policies, oversight procedures, or when officers might begin carrying the gloves if the procurement moves forward. The procurement notice represents an early step in the purchasing process rather than a finalized contract.
