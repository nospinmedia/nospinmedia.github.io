FBI Reports Sharp Drop In U.S. Violent Crime During 2025

Murder rate ties lowest level since the mid-1950s

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) released its 2025 Crime in the Nation report on August 14, 2026, showing a broad decline in violent and property crime across the United States. According to the bureau, the murder rate fell to 4.1 per 100,000 people, tying the national rate recorded in 1955 and 1956, while the total number of homicides declined 18.1% compared with 2024. President Trump highlighted the findings during remarks at the Nassau County Police Academy in Garden City, New York, alongside FBI Director Kash Patel.

The FBI reported that violent crime overall declined 9.3% during 2025. Robbery fell 18.5%, aggravated assault dropped 7.2%, and rape decreased 7.6%. Property crime also continued its downward trend, falling 12.4%, including a 22.7% decline in motor vehicle theft and a 15.8% decrease in burglary. The bureau also reported 10,606 hate crime incidents, a 7.0% decline from the comparable 2024 dataset.

The report draws on submissions from more than 17,000 state, local, tribal and campus law enforcement agencies participating in the National Incident-Based Reporting System (NIBRS), representing approximately 96.2% of the U.S. population. FBI officials said the nationwide decline in violent crime represents one of the largest year-over-year improvements since the bureau began compiling national crime statistics, while noting that participation in the reporting system remains voluntary and local crime trends can differ from national averages.

Criminologists cautioned that crime trends are influenced by numerous factors and cannot be attributed to a single policy or event. The FBI's annual report provides a nationwide benchmark that policymakers, researchers and local agencies use to compare long-term public safety trends while continuing to monitor changes at the state and community level.
