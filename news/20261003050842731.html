U.S. adds just 29,000 jobs in September

July and August gains revised down by another 60,000

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 3, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

U.S. employers added just 29,000 jobs in September, substantially below economists' expectations, while the unemployment rate edged up to 4.2%, according to the latest federal employment report released October 2. Economists surveyed by FactSet had forecast a gain of about 90,000 jobs, making the latest reading another sign of restrained hiring across much of the economy.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics said employment changed little across all major industries during September. Health care continued its upward trend, adding 17,000 jobs, but at a slower pace than its average monthly gain of 33,000 over the previous 12 months. Overall nonfarm payroll growth has averaged just 45,000 jobs per month during the past year.

The report also contained significant downward revisions to previous months. July's payroll change was revised from a gain of 21,000 to a loss of 10,000, while August was revised from 162,000 jobs to 133,000. Together, those revisions removed 60,000 jobs from previously reported employment gains.

The unemployment rate increased from 4.1% in August to 4.2% in September, while the number of unemployed people stood at 7.1 million. The labor force participation rate was 61.8%. The unemployment rate has remained within a relatively narrow range of 4.1% to 4.3% since March.

Wage growth was modest. Average hourly earnings for private nonfarm workers increased 5 cents, or 0.1%, to $37.81 in September. Over the past 12 months, average hourly earnings have risen 3.0%.

The September report shows a labor market with very limited payroll growth rather than a broad collapse in employment. The combination of only 29,000 new payroll jobs, downward revisions totaling 60,000 jobs for July and August, and slower wage growth provides policymakers and businesses with fresh evidence about the pace of the U.S. labor market.
