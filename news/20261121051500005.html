COP31 Climate Talks Go Into Overtime Over Fossil Fuel Fight

Nearly 200 Countries Remain Divided As Key Language Disappears From Draft Deal

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 21, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The COP31 climate summit has gone into overtime after nearly 200 countries failed to reach agreement on a final deal, with negotiations deadlocked over whether the conference should explicitly call for countries to move away from fossil fuels.

The two-week United Nations climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, had been scheduled to conclude on Friday, November 20. Delegates approved several technical matters but were unable to reach consensus on the broader political agreement. COP31 President and Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum told delegates that negotiators were close to an agreement but had not yet reached one.

A major dispute centers on language addressing oil, coal and natural gas. An earlier draft prepared by summit cohost Australia called for a faster phaseout of fossil fuels. A revised version released Thursday removed references to fossil fuels after objections from Saudi Arabia and other major oil and gas producers. Several countries objected strongly to the change, arguing that a climate agreement should directly address the fuels responsible for much of the world's greenhouse-gas emissions.

The European Union warned that the revised language risked undermining the purpose of the summit, while Panama and other countries demanded a clear commitment to transition away from fossil fuels. The dispute is particularly difficult because COP agreements generally require consensus, allowing individual countries or blocs to prevent adoption of a final text.

Climate finance is another point of contention. The revised draft removed a proposed $120 billion target for helping poorer countries adapt to climate change. Developing nations have argued that wealthier countries need to provide more financial assistance as they face increasing costs from extreme weather and other climate-related effects.

Negotiators are also divided over proposed electricity targets and the role of carbon capture technology, which supporters view as a way to reduce industrial emissions while critics argue could prolong reliance on fossil fuels.

A new draft agreement was expected on Saturday, November 21, as negotiations continued beyond the summit's scheduled closing date.
