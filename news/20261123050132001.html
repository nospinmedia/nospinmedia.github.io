U.S. intelligence tracked Nvidia-linked woman in contact with Chinese spy agency

Officials say investigation has not established that she spied for China

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 23, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

U.S. intelligence tracked Alice Huang, a businesswoman linked to former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, as she maintained contact with an officer of China's Ministry of State Security, according to reporting based on people familiar with the intelligence.

The contacts reportedly occurred from the spring of 2025 through fall 2026, overlapping with at least part of Alice Huang's relationship with Jensen Huang. Investigators have not publicly concluded that she passed Nvidia information to China or acted as a spy, and no espionage charge has been announced.

Alice Huang previously led Singapore-based Megaspeed International, an Nvidia customer under investigation over whether restricted artificial-intelligence chips were diverted to China. The Commerce Department opened an export-control investigation in 2024 and sought Nvidia sales records in 2025 before the Justice Department took over the criminal investigation.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton confirmed on November 22 that federal authorities are investigating the allegations, while noting that Attorney General Todd Blanche and the Justice Department have not reached a conclusion on whether Alice Huang acted as a spy. Investigators are also examining Megaspeed associates and whether restricted Nvidia chips were routed through Southeast Asian data centers to Chinese companies or military-linked customers.

The New York Times reported that President Trump was briefed at least twice on intelligence involving Alice Huang and her relationship with Jensen Huang, including in 2025 and May 2026, but Jensen Huang did not receive a defensive intelligence briefing. On November 21, President Trump publicly questioned whether Alice Huang was a spy, said he had not been told she was a Chinese intelligence asset and called for Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang. The board has rejected reinstatement.

The investigation has also prompted congressional scrutiny of enforcement surrounding U.S. restrictions on advanced AI-chip exports to China.
