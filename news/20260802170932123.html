Peru Tourist Flight Near Nazca Lines Kills 13

7 Italians among dead as probe opens, airport halt considered

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 3, 2026 at 8:10 PM ET

A sightseeing flight over the Nazca Lines in southern Peru crashed on August 1, 2026, killing all 13 people on board, local authorities said. The dead include 11 foreign tourists and 2 crew members. Officials confirmed the nationalities of the tourists as 7 Italians, 2 Spaniards, and 2 Germans, and identified the crew as pilot Américo Salazar and copilot Irenka Guanilo del Carpio.

Nazca’s municipal government said the aircraft was operated by Aerodiana, which offers overflights using Cessna Grand Caravan aircraft and has run the route for about 14 years. Authorities said the plane went down in a field outside Nazca, near the UNESCO World Heritage geoglyphs. There is an unresolved detail on the flight’s origin: municipal officials said it departed from Ica, while Peru’s National Police told ABC News the flight took off from Pisco Airport and crashed at about 12:55 p.m. local time. That discrepancy had not been reconciled as of August 2.

Peruvian officials said the cause is under investigation. President Keiko Fujimori expressed condolences and said a temporary suspension of the local airport is being considered pending formal reports from the relevant ministries. Aerodiana was not immediately available for comment. The Nazca Lines—a major draw for international visitors—are typically viewed from small planes or towers; officials did not indicate any damage to the archaeological site.

The accident underscores persistent safety concerns around low-capacity sightseeing flights in remote desert conditions. While initial reports mentioned weather in the broader region in recent days, investigators have not determined whether atmospheric factors, mechanical failure, or human error played any role. Until findings are released, authorities are urging caution and are reviewing procedures for tour flights operating around Nazca.
