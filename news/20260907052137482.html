Israeli strikes kill 11 in southern Lebanon

Two children and two medics among dead in Kfar Roummane

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 7, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

Israeli strikes killed 11 people, including two children and two emergency medics, in the southern Lebanese town of Kfar Roummane early September 7, according to Lebanon's state-run National News Agency. The attacks came during another night of Israeli operations in southern Lebanon as renewed fighting puts additional strain on a U.S.-brokered ceasefire reached in June.

Nine people were killed when an Israeli strike hit a residential building in Kfar Roummane in the Nabatieh district shortly after midnight, while five people were pulled wounded from the rubble. Lebanon's state news agency said no evacuation warning had been issued before that strike. A separate Israeli drone strike then hit a vehicle in the same town, killing two paramedics affiliated with the Islamic Risala Scout Association, an emergency organization associated with Lebanon's Amal Movement.

Israeli strikes were also reported around Nabatieh al-Fawqa, Arab Salim and Deir al-Zahrani. Before striking a building in Deir al-Zahrani, the Israeli military issued an evacuation warning identifying the structure and surrounding area. The Israel Defense Forces said its operations were targeting Hezbollah infrastructure and personnel. The Israeli military did not immediately provide details establishing the identities of those killed in the Kfar Roummane residential strike.

The overnight attacks followed another escalation on September 6, when Israeli strikes in Lebanon killed at least seven people after Israel said Hezbollah launched two explosive drones toward Israeli troops. The violence has continued despite the June ceasefire intended to reduce cross-border hostilities. Israeli forces continue operating within a security zone in southern Lebanon, while Lebanese authorities have called for Israeli withdrawal and the restoration of government control over the area. More than 4,300 people have been killed in Lebanon since the current round of intensified fighting began in March, according to Lebanese authorities cited in current reporting.
