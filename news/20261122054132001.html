ICE Arrests 2 Travelers While Boarding Flight At Seattle Airport

Airline Says It Had No Warning As Confrontation Delays Flight Nearly 3 Hours

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 22, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

Masked U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers arrested two travelers while passengers were boarding an Alaska Airlines flight at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport on Saturday, November 21, triggering a confrontation on the jet bridge and delaying the flight nearly three hours.

The arrests occurred around 8:05 a.m. as passengers boarded Alaska Airlines Flight 1306 for Los Angeles. Video showed officers struggling with at least one traveler while other passengers reacted to the confrontation. A woman could be heard shouting that her daughter was being taken and saying, "We have a work permit."

ICE said the operation targeted Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, a Mexican national whom the agency says had overstayed his visa. Officers also arrested Sofia Hernandez Mejia, another Mexican national ICE says had overstayed her visa, after she allegedly threw hot coffee at officers. ICE said she could face charges related to assaulting or obstructing a federal officer. The allegations have not been adjudicated in court.

Alaska Airlines said it received no advance notice of the operation and described the incident as "distressing" for passengers and employees. Travelers were rebooked and replacement crew members were requested before the Los Angeles-bound flight departed around 11 a.m.

The Port of Seattle, which operates the airport, said it also had no advance knowledge of the operation and described what occurred as "outrageous." The Port said it was reviewing the incident and its protocols with federal authorities.

Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson called video of the arrests "horrific" and "chilling." He said his office is investigating how federal agents accessed the jet bridge and whether their masks complied with a state law he signed in March 2026 restricting facial coverings worn by law enforcement officers.

Democratic Rep. Pramila Jayapal, whose district includes parts of the Seattle area, called the operation "totally unacceptable" and said her office was gathering additional information.
