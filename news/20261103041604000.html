Supreme Court rejects California ballot challenge

About 20,000 disputed ballots can be counted

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 3, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court rejected the Trump administration's emergency request to require California election officials to separate roughly 20,000 mail ballots that the federal government said were associated with people whose citizenship status was in question.

The court's action leaves in place lower-court decisions that declined to require the ballots to be segregated. The justices issued the order without an accompanying opinion, allowing California to continue processing the ballots under its existing election procedures. The decision came immediately before the November 3 midterm elections.

The dispute reached the Supreme Court after the Department of Justice sought an emergency order requiring California officials to identify and set aside ballots linked to a federal list of approximately 20,000 registered voters. California disputed the federal government's characterization of the list and opposed changing its ballot-handling procedures so close to Election Day.

A federal district court had previously rejected the government's request, and the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals declined to overturn that decision. The administration then asked the Supreme Court to intervene on an emergency basis. The latest order ends that effort for purposes of the current election and means the disputed ballots will not be separated under the requested federal order.

The litigation centered on allegations involving potential noncitizen voting and the federal government's authority to require changes to state election procedures. The Supreme Court's order did not resolve those broader legal questions or determine whether any individual ballot was cast unlawfully; it rejected the requested emergency intervention before the election.
