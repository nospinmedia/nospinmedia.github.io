Drone Strike Damages U.S.-Owned Gas Vessel At Egyptian Port Near Suez Canal

Attack Raises New Concerns Over Security Of Global Energy Shipping Routes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 31, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Egyptian authorities confirmed that a drone attack caused fires aboard two liquefied natural gas vessels at the Mediterranean port of Damietta, including the U.S.-owned floating storage and regasification vessel Energos Winter, marking a significant escalation in regional threats to critical energy infrastructure. The fire later spread to the GasLog Salem before emergency crews brought both blazes under control. Officials reported no deaths or injuries, and an investigation into the attack remains underway.

Egypt's Cabinet said the strike occurred on July 29 and confirmed that a drone caused the fires after an initial investigation. Maritime security firms identified the Energos Winter as the vessel first struck, while Egyptian authorities have not publicly attributed responsibility to any country or armed group. The vessel plays an important role in Egypt's natural gas network by converting imported liquefied natural gas back into pipeline-quality fuel for the domestic market.

The attack has renewed concerns about the security of the Suez Canal and the adjacent SUMED Pipeline, two of the world's most important energy transportation corridors. Since fighting disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb, Saudi Arabia and other regional exporters have increasingly relied on these Egyptian routes to move crude oil and liquefied natural gas toward European markets. Analysts cautioned that while the Damietta attack did not interrupt canal operations, additional incidents could increase insurance costs, delay shipments and place additional pressure on global energy markets.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly said Egypt has secured alternative fuel supplies to meet domestic electricity demand should the damaged vessel remain out of service. Petroleum Minister Karim Badawi said the country continues operating multiple floating regasification units and maintains additional capacity through regional energy partnerships while repairs and damage assessments continue.

The strike occurred amid heightened regional tensions following recent attacks involving Iran, Israel, U.S. forces and Iran-backed groups across the Middle East. Although no organization has claimed responsibility for the Damietta attack, the incident underscores growing concerns that commercial energy infrastructure beyond the Persian Gulf could become increasingly vulnerable as the conflict expands.
