Trump threatens to block Bombardier jet sales in U.S.

Canadian plane maker told to build aircraft in America

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 8, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump threatened to block Canadian aircraft manufacturer Bombardier from selling planes in the United States unless the company begins manufacturing them in the country, adding another point of conflict to the escalating U.S.-Canada trade dispute.

Trump announced the threat Monday on Truth Social, writing, "NO MORE SELLING BOMBARDIER IN THE UNITED STATES!" and saying the Montreal-based manufacturer would have to build in America if it wanted continued access to the U.S. market. Trump also criticized the quality of Bombardier aircraft and accused Canada of unfairly restricting American companies, including rival business-jet manufacturer Gulfstream Aerospace. The White House had not publicly detailed how a Bombardier sales prohibition would be implemented.

The U.S. is an especially important market for Bombardier. More than half of the company's revenue comes from the United States, according to Trump and industry reporting, and roughly half of the approximately 5,100 Bombardier business jets operated by customers worldwide are based there. Bombardier also has an extensive U.S. footprint, employing about 3,500 people, working with approximately 2,800 American suppliers and spending more than $2.5 billion annually with U.S. suppliers.

This is not Trump's first threat against the Canadian aircraft maker. In January, he said the United States would decertify Bombardier Global Express jets and threatened a 50% tariff on Canadian-made aircraft because Canada had not certified several newer Gulfstream models. Neither threatened U.S. action ultimately occurred, and Canadian regulators subsequently certified several Gulfstream aircraft in February.

The latest threat came immediately before Canada's retaliatory tariffs on approximately $20 billion in American goods took effect Tuesday. Those tariffs followed U.S. duties imposed on Canadian products after negotiations between the neighboring countries broke down, broadening a trade dispute that now reaches industries ranging from agriculture and manufacturing to aerospace.
