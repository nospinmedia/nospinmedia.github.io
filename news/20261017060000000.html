Ilhan Omar Challenges Legality of Federal Search of Her Home

Attorneys Demand Return of Property as DHS Secretary Calls Investigation a Strong Case

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 17, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Rep. Ilhan Omar is challenging the legality of the federal search of her Washington-area home, demanding the return of seized property and accusing investigators of violating her constitutional rights during an immigration fraud investigation.

Attorneys Amy Jeffress and Joshua Matz sent a letter to federal prosecutors on October 16 disputing the basis and execution of the search. They said there is no legitimate basis to suggest Omar violated citizenship laws and asked authorities to return property taken from her home and preserve audio and video recordings of the operation.

Federal agents searched the Minnesota Democrat's residence on October 12 under a warrant approved by a federal magistrate judge. Investigators seized items including Omar's phone, passport and documents. The investigation is examining her immigration and naturalization history, including possible false statements on government documents. Omar has not been charged with a crime.

Her attorneys also allege investigators failed to follow restrictions imposed by the judge overseeing the warrant, including safeguards intended to protect congressional materials that could fall under the Constitution's Speech or Debate Clause. Federal authorities dispute allegations that the search was improper and maintain that the operation was judicially authorized.

The investigation appears broader than the search of Omar's home. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said agents executed multiple warrants that day and described the investigation as a strong case that authorities expect to present to a grand jury.

Separately, sources familiar with the investigation said federal agents collected discarded trash before the raid while seeking DNA evidence connected to Omar and members of her family. Collecting garbage left outside for pickup can generally be conducted without a search warrant.

Omar maintains that the investigation is politically motivated. Administration officials deny that characterization and say federal authorities are enforcing immigration laws.
