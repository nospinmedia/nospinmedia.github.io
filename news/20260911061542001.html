Missouri's congressional district ping-pong match continues

U.S. Supreme Court sends November election back to 2022 map

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 11, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

Missouri's congressional map has flipped again. The U.S. Supreme Court on Thursday blocked a federal judge's order requiring the state to use its newer 2025 congressional districts, putting Missouri back on course to conduct the November 2026 election using boundaries adopted in 2022.

The latest decision follows an extraordinary series of conflicting court actions. On September 3, the Missouri Supreme Court ruled that the Republican-drawn 2025 map could not take effect before voters consider it in a statewide referendum, returning the state to its 2022 districts. On September 8, Justice Brett Kavanaugh declined an initial emergency request from Missouri Secretary of State Denny Hoskins, leaving that state-court decision intact.

The situation changed again shortly afterward when U.S. District Judge Stephen Clark issued a competing federal order requiring election officials to use the 2025 map. Clark cited concerns about changing congressional boundaries after Missouri had already conducted its August primary under the newer districts.

The conflicting instructions became so serious that the Missouri Supreme Court ordered Hoskins to appear at a contempt hearing after he directed local election authorities to follow Clark's federal order and use the 2025 districts. Hoskins avoided sanctions after agreeing to comply with the state court and proceed with the 2022 map if the federal order was stayed.

That stay arrived Thursday. Kavanaugh referred the latest emergency request to the full U.S. Supreme Court, which blocked Clark's order without providing an explanation. No justice publicly dissented.

The repeated changes affect more than 1 million Missouri voters who participated in a primary conducted under the newer districts but will now vote in November using the 2022 boundaries. The disputed 2025 map reshapes the Kansas City-area district represented by Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver and could change Missouri's congressional delegation from its current 6–2 Republican advantage to 7–1.

The 2025 map will still appear before Missouri voters in November, but the 2022 boundaries are currently set to govern this year's congressional races.
