Judge keeps Minnesota's AI 'nudification' ban in effect

xAI fails to secure injunction as First Amendment challenge continues

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

A federal judge has refused to temporarily block Minnesota's first-in-the-nation ban on AI 'nudification' technology, leaving the law enforceable while xAI continues its constitutional challenge. U.S. District Judge Donovan Frank denied xAI's request for a preliminary injunction on September 4, finding that the company had not demonstrated the irreparable harm required for emergency relief. The law, House File 1606, took effect on August 1 and allows Minnesota's attorney general to seek civil penalties of up to $500,000 per violation.

The statute prohibits operators of websites, applications, software and other services from allowing users to create realistic images or videos depicting intimate parts of identifiable people that were not present in the original material. xAI, whose products include the Grok Imagine image and video generator, sued Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison in July, arguing the law is an overbroad, content-based restriction that violates the First Amendment.

Frank did not resolve that constitutional question. Instead, his 14-page order focused on whether xAI had established the immediate harm necessary for a preliminary injunction. The judge noted that the law was signed on May 7, but xAI waited until shortly before its effective date to seek emergency relief. xAI also told the court it had already implemented additional geographically targeted controls for Minnesota users. The company said those restrictions increased moderation of image-editing prompts in the state by more than 10%, but Frank found its claims of future lost users and business too speculative to establish irreparable harm.

The court also concluded that the balance of harms and public interest weighed strongly in Minnesota's favor, citing evidence presented to lawmakers about nonconsensual sexual imagery involving adults and children. Frank emphasized, however, that the constitutional issues surrounding the emerging technology remain complex and will receive further consideration as the lawsuit proceeds.

xAI said in a court filing that it will appeal the preliminary-injunction ruling to the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals. The underlying lawsuit challenging the law remains unresolved.
