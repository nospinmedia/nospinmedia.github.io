Supreme Court takes up Trump southern border asylum restrictions

Justices will review presidential authority and limits on court injunctions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 14, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court agreed Friday to consider President Trump's restrictions on asylum at the southern border, taking up a case that could define both presidential authority over border entry and the power of lower courts to block federal immigration policies.

The case, Mullin v. Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services (RAICES), No. 26-239, challenges Proclamation 10888, which President Trump issued in January 2025. The proclamation invoked Section 212(f) of the Immigration and Nationality Act to suspend entry for migrants covered by the order and prevent them from applying for asylum and certain other humanitarian protections.

The administration argues that federal law gives the president broad authority to suspend entry and deny asylum to migrants covered by the proclamation. Immigration advocates contend that federal law allows people who reach the United States to seek asylum regardless of how they entered and requires their claims to be considered under procedures established by Congress.

U.S. District Judge Randolph Moss in Washington, D.C., ruled against the administration, finding that the president could not override the asylum system created by Congress. A divided panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit upheld that decision. However, a partial stay has allowed the asylum restrictions to remain in effect while the litigation continues.

The Supreme Court will also consider a separate question involving 8 U.S.C. § 1252(f)(1): whether the statute limits lower courts' ability to issue classwide injunctions against federal immigration enforcement policies. That issue could affect remedies available in immigration lawsuits beyond this particular asylum dispute.

Arguments are expected in spring 2027, with a decision expected by the end of June.
