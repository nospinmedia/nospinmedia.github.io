David Crowley Wins Wisconsin Democratic Governor Primary

Late Milwaukee absentee ballots secure November matchup with Tom Tiffany

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Milwaukee County Executive David Crowley won Wisconsin's Democratic primary for governor after late-counted absentee ballots from Milwaukee County lifted him past state Rep. Francesca Hong in one of the closest statewide races of the 2026 election cycle. The result finalizes the Democratic ticket in a key presidential battleground state and sets up a general election against Republican U.S. Rep. Tom Tiffany on November 3, 2026.

The race remained too close to call on election night after a human error delayed the reporting of roughly 28,000 Milwaukee County absentee ballots. Once those votes were added early Wednesday, Crowley moved ahead and Hong conceded the race. Crowley's victory capped an unusual campaign in which he suspended his gubernatorial bid in July 2026, then re-entered days later after Gov. Tony Evers endorsed him following Lt. Gov. Sara Rodriguez's withdrawal from the race.

Crowley campaigned on his executive experience leading Wisconsin's largest county and argued he was the strongest Democratic candidate to compete in a closely divided swing state. Tiffany, who won the Republican primary with the endorsement of President Trump, will campaign on conservative priorities including public safety, taxes, and energy policy. The general election is expected to attract significant national attention as both parties compete for control of the governor's office.

If elected, Crowley would become Wisconsin's first Black governor. The Democratic primary also highlighted ideological divisions within the party, with Hong running a progressive campaign that energized many liberal voters while Crowley emphasized electability and governing experience. Both candidates called for Democratic unity following the primary as attention shifts to the November election.
