Justice Department investigates five TV networks over Trump coverage

Antitrust probe follows broadcasters' boycott of White House press pool

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 11, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The U.S. Department of Justice announced an antitrust investigation on October 10, 2026, into television networks that temporarily suspended their shared coverage of President Trump after his administration barred three news organizations from the White House. The investigation examines whether the broadcasters' coordinated decision violated federal competition laws.

The dispute began on September 19, when the White House revoked access credentials for journalists from CNN, MS Now and Politico. Administration officials defended the restrictions, arguing that access to the president is a privilege rather than an automatic right.

In response, the television press pool, which includes ABC, CBS, CNN, Fox and NBC, suspended its shared presidential coverage. The broadcasters normally rotate responsibility for recording presidential events and distribute footage to other news organizations, allowing outlets to provide coverage without maintaining separate camera crews at every appearance.

A Justice Department spokesperson confirmed Saturday that the department was examining whether the collective suspension constituted an unlawful agreement among competing businesses. Federal antitrust laws can prohibit coordinated boycotts that improperly restrict competition.

The department emphasized that opening an investigation does not establish that the networks violated the law. No findings or enforcement actions were announced.

The broadcasters subsequently resumed their pooled coverage after a federal judge temporarily restored White House access to the excluded organizations. CNN, MS Now and Politico had challenged the restrictions in court, arguing that the administration improperly penalized them for their reporting.

A federal judge extended the temporary protections while considering whether to issue a longer-lasting injunction. Further court proceedings are expected as the dispute over press credentials continues.

The Committee to Protect Journalists criticized the Justice Department's investigation. Its Americas regional director, Jose Zamora, warned that antitrust enforcement should not be used to intimidate news organizations defending press access.

The case raises competing legal questions about federal competition law, coordinated business decisions and constitutional protections for independent journalism. The government maintains that antitrust rules apply to competing businesses, while press advocates argue that collective action defending journalistic access deserves protection.
