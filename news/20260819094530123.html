Iran's Bahrain strikes disrupted U.S. Navy logistics

Damage forced supply operations 2,200 miles to Diego Garcia as USS Lincoln deployment strains mount

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 19, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Iranian missile and drone attacks that heavily damaged the U.S. Navy's base in Bahrain forced a major change in how American warships in the region are supplied, according to new reporting that helps explain some of the shortages and strains reported aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln.

The New York Times, in reporting republished by GV Wire, says the Navy began using Diego Garcia as its main shipment point shortly after Naval Support Activity Bahrain was heavily damaged early in the war with Iran. The island base, south of the Maldives, is roughly 2,200 miles from the Gulf of Oman, where U.S. carrier strike groups have been operating. A U.S. military official told the Times that Diego Garcia took over the shipment role after the Bahrain attacks. The loss of the much closer logistics hub has contributed to reported supply problems aboard carriers supporting operations against Iran.

The damage to Bahrain was substantial. A separate Wall Street Journal investigation using satellite imagery, social-media footage and Defense Department records found that Iranian attacks between late February and June hit the command headquarters at NSA Bahrain, at least a dozen other buildings and two satellite-communications terminals. The military has said no one was killed at the base and that the strikes did not significantly disrupt operations, although most personnel were evacuated and only a smaller staff remained.

The logistics disruption comes as the USS Abraham Lincoln approaches nine months at sea. The carrier, with roughly 5,000 sailors and Marines, has gone more than 200 days without a port call. Families and lawmakers have raised concerns about basic-supply shortages, water and plumbing problems, mail disruptions and the mental-health effects of the extended deployment. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has disputed portrayals of deteriorating conditions, saying the reports have been misrepresented and that the Navy is providing crews with what it can under wartime conditions.

Sen. Richard Blumenthal has called for an inquiry into conditions aboard the Lincoln and warned that the prolonged deployment raises broader questions about fleet readiness, maintenance and service-member well-being. The Navy is now sending the Japan-based USS George Washington toward the Middle East to relieve the Lincoln, a move that will also temporarily reduce the U.S. carrier presence in the western Pacific.

The Bahrain-to-Diego Garcia shift illustrates a broader vulnerability exposed by the war: striking shore infrastructure can complicate U.S. naval operations even without directly hitting an aircraft carrier. The Pentagon has not publicly released a complete assessment of how the Bahrain damage has affected regional logistics, but independent reporting now establishes a direct connection between the damaged hub, the longer supply route and problems supporting U.S. carriers at sea.
