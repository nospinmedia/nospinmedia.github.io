Man accused of destroying $60 million U.S. military plane with ax

Prosecutors describe elaborate early-morning breach at Irish airport

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 18, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Federal prosecutors have charged an Irish man with carrying out an elaborate attack that permanently disabled a $60 million U.S. Air National Guard C-130H Hercules at Shannon Airport in Ireland, with court records describing months of preparation and extensive efforts to prevent anyone from entering the aircraft.

Daithi O'Corrain, 48, also known as David Curran, allegedly breached the airport perimeter at about 4:40 a.m. on April 11, 2026. The aircraft, assigned to the Missouri Air National Guard, had been parked overnight on a taxiway while on a military mission.

Prosecutors say O'Corrain barricaded himself inside, covered windows with crew hammocks, secured doors using straps, brackets and screws, and packed door tracks with ball bearings and epoxy. He allegedly cut wiring and hydraulic lines, destroyed cockpit instruments, punctured air ducts and crimped a liquid-oxygen line.

Authorities also say he brought gallium, a metal that can damage aluminum, and a heat gun used to keep it liquefied. When the flight crew returned, prosecutors allege O'Corrain climbed through a roof hatch and attacked the aircraft's exterior with an ax. The plane was rendered permanently inoperable.

An online fundraiser organized by O'Corrain's supporters describes the attack as a protest against U.S. military use of Shannon Airport and U.S. policy in the Middle East. Prosecutors have charged him with destruction of government property, but the indictment itself does not establish a political motive. The allegations have not been proven in court.

The investigation involved the FBI, Air Force Office of Special Investigations and Ireland's An Garda Síochána. O'Corrain is currently detained in Limerick Prison on related Irish charges while U.S. authorities seek to bring him to New York. The federal charge carries a maximum sentence of 10 years in prison.
