Senate passes bipartisan bill to speed energy and infrastructure permits

Measure would require data centers to cover more of their power-grid costs

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 20, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The lame-duck U.S. Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) on Thursday, November 19, advancing a major permitting overhaul intended to accelerate energy and infrastructure projects while requiring large data centers to shoulder more of the costs they impose on the power grid. The bill passed 62–33 and now moves to the House of Representatives.

The legislation was negotiated by Republican Sens. Shelley Moore Capito of West Virginia and Mike Lee of Utah with Democratic Sens. Martin Heinrich of New Mexico and Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island. Fifteen members of the Democratic caucus joined nearly all Republicans in supporting the measure. The vote occurred during the post-election lame-duck session, before the newly elected Congress takes office in January 2027.

BAAJA establishes stricter deadlines for federal environmental reviews, which can currently take five years or longer for some major projects. It also limits the federal government's ability to revoke previously issued permits and creates provisions allowing developers to recover between 50% and 100% of construction costs when a permit is determined to have been wrongfully revoked.

The legislation also addresses the rapidly increasing electricity demands of AI and other large data centers. It directs the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) to establish rules requiring data centers to pay their full grid and transmission costs rather than shifting those expenses to other electricity customers.

Independent estimates cited by lawmakers project that the data-center provisions could require technology companies to contribute roughly $55 billion toward transmission costs over 10 years and save residential electricity customers approximately $19 billion over 14 years. Estimates also project as many as 105,600 jobs through 2040 and roughly a 20% reduction in power-sector carbon emissions.

Supporters say the legislation could accelerate construction of power generation, transmission lines and other infrastructure while protecting consumers from costs created by rapidly expanding data centers. Some environmental organizations and Tribal Nations have opposed the measure, arguing that faster permitting could weaken environmental safeguards and review protections.
