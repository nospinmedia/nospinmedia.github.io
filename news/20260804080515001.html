Five States Vote Today As Michigan’s Senate Primary Takes Center Stage

Stevens–El‑Sayed showdown sets November matchup against Rogers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 7:24 AM ET

Voters in Michigan, Kansas, Missouri, Virginia, and Washington are casting primary ballots on Tuesday, August 4, 2026, in contests that will shape control of Congress and key state offices. The day’s marquee race is Michigan’s open U.S. Senate seat, where Democrats are choosing between U.S. Representative Haley Stevens and epidemiologist Abdul El‑Sayed to succeed retiring two‑term Senator Gary Peters.

The Michigan Democratic clash pits party establishment against the progressive wing: Stevens is backed by Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer and, in recent weeks, by retiring Senator Peters; El‑Sayed, a former Detroit health official, has campaigned on policies such as Medicare for All with support from national progressives including Bernie Sanders. On the Republican side, former U.S. Representative Mike Rogers is unopposed for his party’s nomination, setting up a high‑stakes November contest in a state both parties view as pivotal.

Election administration details are straightforward but matter to turnout: in Michigan, polls are open from 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. (local time), and voters in line at closing may still cast ballots. State officials say expanded pre‑processing of absentee ballots should speed up result reporting after polls close. Elsewhere, Virginia’s primaries were moved to August 4 this year by state action amid a redistricting fight, Kansas and Missouri are holding their customary first‑Tuesday‑in‑August primaries, and Washington is conducting its August top‑two primary, which advances the two highest vote‑getters to November regardless of party preference.

The outcome in Michigan will help define Democrats’ message heading into the general election: whether to elevate a center‑left negotiator with statewide endorsements (Stevens) or a progressive insurgent promising sweeping change (El‑Sayed). The race narrowed after State Senator Mallory McMorrow exited on July 5, 2026, simplifying the field but intensifying scrutiny of policy contrasts, fundraising, and coalition strength ahead of November.
