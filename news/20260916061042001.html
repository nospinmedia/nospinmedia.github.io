NBC and Telemundo news helicopter crashes in Los Angeles

Reporter, pilot and person on ground killed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 16, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A news helicopter used by NBC Los Angeles and Telemundo 52 crashed and burst into flames in Chatsworth, Los Angeles, killing three people as its crew covered another deadly accident nearby. The helicopter went down shortly before 7 p.m. Tuesday, September 15, near a large storage facility in the San Fernando Valley.

NBC identified the two people aboard the helicopter who died as aerial reporter Eliana Moreno and pilot George Marciniw, both employees of Angel City Air, which operated NewsChopper4. The Los Angeles Fire Department said at least one other person was killed on the ground. Two additional people were taken to a hospital, with their conditions not immediately released.

The impact sparked a fire that damaged at least four vehicles and two storage containers before firefighters extinguished it. More than 50 firefighters responded. The helicopter was so badly damaged that emergency crews initially could not determine how many people had been aboard.

The crew had been covering a separate collision about a mile away involving an SUV and a Los Angeles Metro bus. The SUV slammed into the middle of the bus roughly two hours before the helicopter crash, killing at least two people and injuring six others. Several television news helicopters were operating over the area when NewsChopper4 went down. Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass said the two crashes resulted in the loss of five Angelenos and described the night as deeply painful for the city.

The National Transportation Safety Board and Federal Aviation Administration are investigating the helicopter crash, with investigators expected at the scene Wednesday. No cause had been announced. A review of federal accident records and news reports found at least eight fatal U.S. news-helicopter crashes since 2000, resulting in 16 deaths before Tuesday's crash.
