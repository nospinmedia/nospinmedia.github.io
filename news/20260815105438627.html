Army Halts Apache Training Flights Nationwide

Stand-down follows fatal Texas crash that killed two soldiers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The U.S. Army temporarily halted all AH-64 Apache helicopter training missions nationwide on August 14, 2026, following a fatal crash in Central Texas that killed two soldiers. The Army said the stand-down will remain in effect until investigators have a better understanding of what caused the accident, making the order a significant safety action affecting training across the Apache fleet.

The pause follows an August 12 crash near Salado, Texas, about 30 miles from Fort Hood. The Army identified the soldiers killed as Chief Warrant Officer 2 Deontre T. Huey, 34, of Killeen, Texas, and Warrant Officer Seth L. Olmstead, 25, of Belton, Texas. They were the only two people aboard the Apache when it crashed in a grassy field. Both were assigned to Bravo Company, 1st Battalion, 227th Aviation Regiment, 1st Air Cavalry Brigade, 1st Cavalry Division. No cause has been announced.

A safety investigation team from the U.S. Army Combat Readiness Center at Fort Rucker, Alabama, is examining the crash. The Army said the training stand-down is intended to remain in place while investigators work toward identifying the root cause. Similar Army-wide aviation pauses have followed previous fatal accidents, including a 2023 grounding that required aviation units to complete additional safety training after 12 soldiers were killed in separate helicopter crashes in Alaska and Kentucky.

The Apache has been a central part of Army aviation since the first aircraft entered service in 1984 and remains a primary U.S. attack helicopter. The current order applies to Apache training missions; reporting on the stand-down indicated the Army did not announce a corresponding halt to combat missions. The temporary pause could affect pilot training and unit schedules until commanders receive enough information from the investigation to safely resume normal training operations.
