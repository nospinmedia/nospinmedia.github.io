Judge bars DOJ threats against election officials

Order blocks demands to reject or segregate disputed ballots

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 31, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

A federal judge has temporarily barred the U.S. Department of Justice from threatening state and local election officials with prosecution or pressuring them to reject or segregate ballots involving people the government alleges may not be U.S. citizens.

U.S. District Judge Trina Thompson issued the temporary restraining order late October 30, days before the midterm elections. The order prevents federal officials from demanding that election administrators remove people from statewide voter-registration rolls, reject ballots from specified voters or segregate ballots based on the federal government's list of alleged noncitizens. It also blocks threats of criminal prosecution against election officials who refuse to comply with those demands.

The dispute has been especially prominent in California, where the Justice Department sought to have ballots associated with roughly 20,000 people set aside while their citizenship was examined. California officials have challenged the reliability of the federal list and warned that eligible U.S. citizens could be included. The administration has argued that federal law prohibits noncitizens from voting and that enforcing those restrictions is a Justice Department responsibility.

Thompson found that federal officials likely violated the Voting Rights Act of 1965, including its prohibition against attempting to intimidate, threaten or coerce people who assist others in voting. She also concluded that the government was likely acting beyond its legal or constitutional authority. The order requires the Justice Department to notify the court about pending search-warrant applications or subpoenas related to the dispute.

The temporary order is scheduled to remain in effect until November 16, with another hearing set for November 13. It follows separate court rulings rejecting the Justice Department's attempt to force California to segregate the disputed ballots.
