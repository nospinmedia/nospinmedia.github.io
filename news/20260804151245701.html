France Wildfire Exposes 400+ WWII Shells In Le Porge

Demining wraps Aug. 3 as residents return to fire‑damaged homes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 5:44 PM ET

A massive blaze in southwestern France has unearthed and ignited long‑buried munitions, with authorities recovering more than 400 pieces of World War II ordnance in the coastal village of Le Porge. After several days of bomb‑disposal work, residents were allowed to return on Monday, August 3, 2026, as teams declared streets safe enough for reentry. Officials said the extreme heat from the wildfire likely set off the caches, compounding risks for firefighters and residents.

Local leaders described a harrowing night when more than 100 blasts echoed across the town and nearby pine forest. Fire crews initially thought they were hearing exploding gas cylinders, but commanders later said the detonations were consistent with buried shells triggered by intense heat. The recovered stockpile includes German and French munitions—mainly mortar rounds—with some high‑explosive anti‑tank shells. Mayor Martial Zaninetti said shrapnel and at least one projectile struck homes; fire commander Thomas Mimiague noted the blasts were probably linked to abandoned wartime stores.

Damage to property is extensive: more than 180 homes in Le Porge were destroyed or severely damaged by the wildfire, according to local authorities. While the controlled return of residents signals progress, officials cautioned that ordnance specialists will continue sweeping charred lots and forest margins to ensure no additional explosives remain hidden beneath ash or debris. The episode highlights a secondary hazard in Europe’s historic battle zones, where modern fires can reanimate munitions buried for decades.

The Gironde wildfire has been among France’s most serious in years, forcing large‑scale evacuations across communities west of Bordeaux. As temperatures remain elevated, emergency managers warn that flare‑ups and spot fires can still threaten hard‑hit neighborhoods, even after main fire lines are contained. Residents are urged to report any suspicious metallic objects and to avoid disturbing debris until bomb‑disposal teams clear each site.
