FBI investigates suspected breach of its networks

Hackers claim they stole personal data belonging to thousands of agents

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 23, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The FBI is investigating suspected unauthorized activity affecting its computer systems after the cybercriminal group Shiny Hunters claimed it stole personal information belonging to thousands of FBI agents. The bureau has confirmed that it is investigating activity involving FBIjobs.gov, but it has not independently confirmed the hackers' claims about the amount or type of information obtained.

The FBI's Special Agent Applicant Portal was unavailable Tuesday while the bureau dealt with the incident. Shiny Hunters claimed it began accessing FBI data Monday evening and subsequently listed the bureau as a victim on the group's dark-web site. The group demanded that the FBI remove or revise a public advisory describing Shiny Hunters' alleged cybercrime and extortion tactics, while claiming that its objective in this incident was not financial.

Shiny Hunters has previously targeted major organizations in the technology, finance, retail and healthcare sectors. In a public advisory issued earlier this year, the FBI described the group as targeting large companies and stealing substantial amounts of customer information. The FBI has not said how the suspected attackers may have accessed FBIjobs.gov, whether other bureau systems were affected or whether information claimed by the hackers is authentic.

The incident follows other recent attacks targeting federal law-enforcement systems and information. In August, a cybercriminal group leaked files apparently stolen from the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, including material involving targets of previous investigations and analyses of phone communications. Federal law-enforcement agencies have also experienced previous breaches involving sensitive investigative systems and data. The FBI investigation into the latest incident remains active, and the hackers' claims should be treated as unverified until investigators establish what information, if any, was actually accessed.
