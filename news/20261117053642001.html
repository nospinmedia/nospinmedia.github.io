Ukraine Targets Crimean Bridge For Second Straight Night

Bridge damaged as attacks intensify across Russia and Ukraine

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 17, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Ukraine targeted the Crimean Bridge linking Russia with occupied Crimea for a second consecutive night, prompting Russian-installed authorities to suspend traffic across the strategically important road-and-rail crossing early Tuesday, November 17. Russia's Investigative Committee confirmed the bridge was damaged and opened a criminal investigation into the attack.

Russian-installed Crimean leader Sergei Aksyonov told motorists to avoid traveling toward the bridge. The 19-kilometer (12-mile) crossing over the Kerch Strait connects Crimea with Russia's Krasnodar region and has served as an important supply route for Russian forces. It is also a prominent symbol of Moscow's 2014 annexation of Crimea, which is internationally recognized as Ukrainian territory.

The strike followed an attack on the bridge the previous night that Russian officials said caused minor damage. Russian authorities also reported two people killed in a separate drone strike in Crimea. Russia's Defense Ministry said air defenses intercepted 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight across Russian regions, occupied Crimea and nearby waters. That figure could not be independently verified.

The wider Ukrainian attack also reached deep inside Russia. Drones targeted the Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat refinery in Bashkortostan, roughly 1,200 kilometers (746 miles) from the Ukrainian border. Bashkortostan's emergency authorities said air defenses repelled more than 50 drones and claimed the refinery suffered no critical damage, while independently analyzed eyewitness footage showed multiple fires and black smoke at the facility.

Russia simultaneously continued attacks across Ukraine. Zaporizhzhia Regional Governor Mykhailo Semikin said a combined morning attack on industrial and energy infrastructure in Zaporizhzhia killed at least two people and injured 18 others. Strikes were also reported in Kyiv, Cherkasy and Dnipropetrovsk.

The escalating exchanges come as both countries await another round of U.S.-mediated negotiations over the war.
