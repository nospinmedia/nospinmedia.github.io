Treasury Expands Iran Sanctions Campaign

U.S. warns global partners to cut financial ties or face penalties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 25, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

The U.S. Treasury Department has launched Operation Economic Outcast, a broad new sanctions campaign intended to further isolate Iran by targeting its remaining financial and commercial connections around the world. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced the initiative on August 24, saying the administration is expanding the use of secondary sanctions while immediately designating nearly 60 individuals, companies and vessels tied to Iran's oil trade, military procurement networks and cyber operations.

The administration also expanded the sectors that could trigger secondary sanctions to include digital assets, technology, gold, commercial aviation and maritime shipping. Bessent warned that foreign governments, financial institutions and businesses facilitating Iran's access to international markets could ultimately lose access to the U.S. financial system and dollar-based transactions if they continue supporting sanctioned activities. He said the administration will provide a limited period for organizations to end prohibited business before broader enforcement begins, adding that "the clock just started ticking."

Administration officials said the strategy is designed to increase economic pressure after months of conflict with Iran and to reduce the revenue streams supporting Tehran's military, weapons procurement and regional operations. The State Department simultaneously announced sanctions targeting entities and vessels linked to illicit oil shipments, weapons-related procurement and cyber activities, describing the measures as part of a coordinated government effort. Officials emphasized that no specific country is exempt from potential secondary sanctions, although the initial announcement stopped short of immediately penalizing major foreign financial institutions.

Iranian officials rejected the new measures, saying the country has prepared for additional sanctions and intends to continue operating despite the expanded restrictions. Analysts noted that the campaign's ultimate impact will depend largely on whether foreign governments, banks and companies choose to reduce business with Iran during the administration's compliance window or risk future U.S. penalties.
