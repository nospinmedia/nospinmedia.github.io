President Trump awards Rudy Giuliani nation's highest civilian honor

Medal recognizes former NYC mayor despite disbarments and $148M defamation judgment

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 25, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

President Trump awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom to former New York City Mayor Rudy Giuliani on Tuesday, honoring a longtime political ally whose career spans celebrated leadership after the September 11 attacks and serious legal and professional consequences stemming from his efforts to overturn the 2020 election.

Trump presented the nation's highest civilian honor to the 82-year-old Giuliani during a private Oval Office ceremony on November 24. The White House highlighted Giuliani's record as a federal prosecutor who pursued organized crime and his leadership of New York City following the September 11, 2001, terrorist attacks.

Giuliani became internationally known as "America's Mayor" following 9/11 and was named TIME's Person of the Year for 2001. Trump has called him the "greatest Mayor in the history of New York City" and an American patriot.

Giuliani later became Trump's personal attorney and played a leading role in efforts to challenge the 2020 presidential election, advancing false claims of widespread election fraud. He was subsequently disbarred in New York and Washington, D.C. over his election-related conduct.

A federal jury also ordered Giuliani to pay $148 million to Georgia election workers Ruby Freeman and Shaye Moss after finding him liable for defamation for falsely accusing them of election fraud. The women testified that the accusations resulted in racist threats and harassment.

Trump granted Giuliani a federal pardon in November 2025 covering potential federal offenses related to efforts to overturn the election. Separate state criminal election-interference cases against Giuliani in Georgia and Arizona were dismissed; Arizona prosecutors retain the option to refile charges. The pardon did not erase Giuliani's civil judgment or professional disciplinary actions.

Giuliani said he was honored to receive the medal. Critics included Democratic Rep. Jerry Nadler of New York, who said Giuliani "led efforts to undermine American democracy by attempting to overturn the 2020 election" and was "clearly unfit for one of America's highest honors."

The award recognizes a figure with sharply contrasting chapters in his public life: a prosecutor and mayor widely praised for his leadership after 9/11, and a political attorney later disbarred and held liable for false accusations against election workers.
