UK Links Suspected Plot Against U.S. Bomber Base to Iran

Prime Minister Says Revolutionary Guard Almost Certainly Directed Threat

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 13, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Monday, October 12, 2026, that a suspected plot involving RAF Fairford, a British military airbase used by American bombers, was highly likely to have originated in Iran and was almost certainly directed by the country's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC).

The assessment represents one of Britain's strongest public accusations connecting Iranian authorities to the security incident. The investigation began after counterterrorism officers arrested five men near the airbase in Gloucestershire, England, on September 27. Authorities initially suspected terrorism and explosives offenses, although investigators subsequently reported finding no viable explosive device.

RAF Fairford has served as an operating location for U.S. Air Force B-1 bombers, including aircraft involved in military operations against Iran. Concerns about a possible attack prompted the United States to withdraw its bombers from the installation in early October as a precaution.

According to reporting by Reuters, British authorities believe an individual based in Iran played a significant role in the suspected operation. Investigators are examining possible connections between the suspects and foreign intelligence networks, including concerns that the installation could have been targeted by drones.

British police have arrested seven people in connection with the investigation. The suspects were subsequently released on bail while inquiries continued. Authorities have not publicly established that an attack was imminent or that the suspects possessed a functioning explosive device.

Following the government's latest assessment, British officials announced additional measures targeting individuals linked to Iranian intelligence activities. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband summoned Iran's ambassador to protest what London described as hostile activity associated with Tehran.

Iran has denied involvement in the suspected plot. The British government's attribution remains an intelligence assessment rather than a finding established through criminal proceedings.

The allegations come amid continuing military tensions between Iran and the United States. American bombers operating from British facilities have participated in strikes against Iranian targets, making the security of those installations an important concern for both governments.

The investigation also raises questions about how Britain and its allies should respond to suspected foreign-directed operations while criminal proceedings and intelligence assessments remain incomplete.
