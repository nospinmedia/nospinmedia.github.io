Jury says Apple owes $5.7 billion in patent case

Taptic Engine in iPhones and Apple Watches found to infringe haptics technology

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 29, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A federal jury in San Diego found that Apple infringed two patents owned by Taction Technology and awarded the California company more than $5.7 billion in damages. The dispute centers on Apple's Taptic Engine, which produces the tapping and vibration feedback used in iPhones and Apple Watches.

The jury returned its verdict on September 25, finding infringement of claims from U.S. Patent Nos. 10,659,885 and 10,820,117. The patents cover tactile-transducer technology that produces vibrations users perceive through touch. Jurors found that Apple's infringement was not willful, while rejecting Apple's arguments that the patents were invalid. The award totaled approximately $5.72 billion.

Apple disputes the verdict and says it will appeal. The company maintains that its Taptic Engine is fundamentally different from Taction's technology and says testing presented during the trial supported that position. The verdict is not necessarily the amount Taction will ultimately receive: large patent awards can be reduced or overturned during post-trial proceedings and appeals.

The lawsuit dates to 2021, when San Diego-based Taction accused Apple of using its patented haptic technology without permission. A federal judge ruled for Apple on noninfringement grounds in 2023, but the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit vacated that decision in 2025 and sent the case back for further proceedings, eventually leading to the jury trial.

The size of the verdict has also drawn attention to litigation financing. Burford Capital, which helped finance Taction's case, said it could be entitled to approximately $1.4 billion if the award ultimately survives the remaining legal process. Burford cautioned that there is substantial uncertainty because the verdict remains subject to post-trial proceedings and Apple's planned appeal.
