Big Bear bald eagle Jackie dies at 14

Ojai Raptor Center confirms; CDFW necropsy seeks cause

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 7:43 AM ET

Jackie, the widely watched 14-year-old bald eagle from Big Bear Lake, died in intensive care at the Ojai Raptor Center early on August 10, 2026, after weeks of treatment for an illness whose cause remains unknown. The center identified her as Patient 26-519 and said staff worked around the clock as her condition declined.

Rescuers brought Jackie in on July 17, 2026, after she was found grounded and weak near Big Bear following an altercation with two subadult eagles. Veterinarians later documented severe anemia, including a critically low packed cell volume that dropped below 6%, and administered blood transfusions, continuous oxygen, and other supportive care from a team of nine specialists. While initial concern focused on potential trauma, testing did not find an injury that explained the rapid deterioration.

Jackie’s remains were transferred to the California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW) for a necropsy, conducted in coordination with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS). Officials hope laboratory analysis will determine whether toxins, infection, or another factor caused the anemia. Results have not yet been released, and authorities have not suggested a broader public safety risk at this time.

Key verified details: - July 17, 2026: Rescue near Big Bear Lake after an eagle scuffle - Severe anemia documented; packed cell volume below 6% - August 10, 2026: Death in ICU at Ojai Raptor Center - Necropsy led by CDFW, with USFWS coordination

Jackie became a symbol of regional recovery when she hatched in 2012, the first documented bald eagle to emerge in modern times in Big Bear Valley. She and her longtime mate Shadow drew millions of viewers through the Friends of Big Bear Valley live nest cam, boosting public awareness of protections under the Bald and Golden Eagle Protection Act. Supporters and local conservation groups are mourning her loss as they await necropsy findings that could inform raptor health monitoring in Southern California.
