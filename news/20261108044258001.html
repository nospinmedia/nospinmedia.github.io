Six killed in Kraków market square shooting

Three more bodies found at gunman’s home

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A gunman killed six people in Kraków, Poland, before being shot and arrested by police, while authorities later found three additional bodies at his family home as investigators examined possible links to far-right ideology.

Police said the 25-year-old Polish citizen, a resident of the Świętokrzyskie Voivodeship, drove into Kraków’s historic Main Market Square on Saturday, November 7, and began firing at people from his vehicle. The six people killed were four women and two men, including a British citizen. At least 15 people were wounded, including two police officers, while several foreign nationals were among the injured.

During the attack, gunfire shattered the cab windows of a passing tram, although no passengers aboard were injured. Two carriage horses were also struck by gunfire and both survived.

Police stopped the gunman’s vehicle near the intersection of Dietla and Stradomska streets, where officers exchanged fire with him and took him into custody. Authorities said the apprehension occurred within about three minutes of the initial emergency call. The suspect used an AR-15 rifle, had a firearms license and was wearing military-style clothing. Additional weapons and ammunition were recovered from his vehicle.

Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said evidence indicated the suspect held radical, ultranationalistic views and appeared fascinated by far-right ideology. Authorities have described the shooting as an act of terror, while investigators continue examining the attack and the suspect’s motives.

Police subsequently discovered three bodies at the suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski. Authorities identified them as his 53-year-old father, 52-year-old mother and 21-year-old sister. Investigators believe the three were killed by gunfire before the Kraków attack.

Poland’s security services are assisting with the investigation, and President Karol Nawrocki declared November 8 and 9 days of national mourning.
