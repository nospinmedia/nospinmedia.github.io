Grand jury declines to indict former Trump aide Cassidy Hutchinson

DOJ had sought charges related to her testimony about January 6

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 28, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A federal grand jury in Washington, D.C., has declined to indict former White House aide Cassidy Hutchinson after the Justice Department sought criminal charges related to testimony she provided about the events surrounding January 6, 2021.

The grand jury returned what is known as a "no true bill," meaning prosecutors presented potential charges but jurors determined there was not sufficient basis to issue an indictment. The decision represents a significant setback for the Justice Department's investigation of Hutchinson, who became one of the most prominent witnesses before the House committee that investigated the January 6 attack on the U.S. Capitol.

The investigation examined whether Hutchinson made false statements in connection with her accounts of events inside the White House and whether she improperly retained government records after leaving her position, according to reporting on the case. The exact evidence presented to the grand jury has not been made public, and grand jury proceedings are generally conducted in secret.

Hutchinson served as an aide to White House Chief of Staff Mark Meadows during President Trump's first administration. She attracted national attention in June 2022 when she testified publicly before the House January 6 committee about conversations and events she said she witnessed during the final weeks of Trump's first term and on the day of the Capitol attack.

Some portions of Hutchinson's testimony were later disputed by other people who were present during the events she described. Hutchinson defended her testimony and subsequently provided additional information to congressional investigators. She also described pressure she said she experienced from people associated with Trump while preparing to cooperate with the committee.

The Justice Department later began examining whether aspects of her accounts or handling of government material could support criminal charges. A grand jury's refusal to indict is unusual because prosecutors control which evidence and witnesses are presented during the secret proceedings and only need to establish probable cause, rather than prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

The decision does not amount to a finding that every statement Hutchinson made was accurate. It means the grand jury declined to approve the criminal charges presented by federal prosecutors. Hutchinson has not been charged with a crime in connection with the investigation.
