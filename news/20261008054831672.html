Houthi attacks on Saudi airports kill three and injure 36

Saudi-led coalition strikes more than 80 targets in Yemen

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 8, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

Houthi-claimed attacks on two airports in Saudi Arabia killed three people and injured 36, as renewed fighting in neighboring Yemen intensified around one of the world's most important shipping routes. Saudi civil aviation authorities said one person was killed and eight wounded at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, while two people were killed and 28 wounded at Abha International Airport in southern Saudi Arabia.

Saudi authorities identified those killed at Abha as female residents from Morocco and Algeria. A Sudanese resident was killed in Riyadh. The attacks occurred on Tuesday and Wednesday and caused material damage, according to Saudi aviation officials. Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said the group targeted the Riyadh airport with several drones.

The Saudi-led coalition responded Wednesday by striking more than 80 Houthi military sites across the Yemeni governorates of Saada, Hodeidah, Jawf and Marib, saying attacks on Saudi civilian sites would not go unpunished. Coalition forces also said they intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile north of Riyadh and tracked its launch platform in Houthi-controlled Sanaa.

The violence extended to Aden International Airport in Yemen, where the country's Transport Ministry said the Houthis launched ballistic missiles and explosive-laden drones. One missile landed near the runway minutes before a flight from Cairo was scheduled to arrive. No casualties were reported there.

The escalation comes as Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces conduct a counteroffensive against Houthi forces around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the narrow passage connecting the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden. Control of the area has become increasingly important for global shipping and energy transportation. The latest fighting has displaced more than 200,000 people, according to the International Organization for Migration, worsening Yemen's long-running humanitarian crisis.
