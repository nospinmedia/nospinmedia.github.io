Dow surges more than 1,500 points after midterms

Oil plunges as Wall Street reacts to shift in Congress

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 5, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

U.S. stocks surged Wednesday following the midterm elections, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbing more than 1,500 points as investors reacted to Democrats winning control of Congress and a sharp decline in oil prices.

The Dow gained 1,569.40 points, or 3.03%, to close at 53,356.12, its strongest percentage gain since April 2025. The S&P 500 rose 1.58% to 7,688.62, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.44% to finish at 25,736.51. The rally was broad, with hospital operators, retailers serving lower-income consumers and other companies sensitive to household spending among the strongest performers.

Energy markets moved sharply in the opposite direction. Brent crude fell $8.98, or 8.7%, to $94.62 per barrel, while U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate dropped 8.3% to $88.12. Both settled at their lowest levels since late August.

The moves followed Democrats' victories in the 2026 midterm elections, which ended Republican control of Congress. Investors were assessing how divided government could affect federal spending, trade policy and the ongoing war with Iran. Oil traders also increased bets that the change in Congress could raise pressure for a negotiated end to the conflict, which has contributed to higher energy prices.

Lower oil prices also helped ease pressure in the bond market, where Treasury yields declined. However, market movements can reflect multiple factors, and a single trading session does not establish how the election results will affect stocks, energy prices or the broader economy over the longer term.
