FBI, Coast Guard investigate cyberattacks on U.S.-bound tankers

Malicious activity found aboard two ships headed for Texas

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 17, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The FBI and U.S. Coast Guard boarded two foreign-flagged energy tankers headed toward the United States after investigators detected signs of cyberattacks on the vessels' computer networks. Federal teams boarded the ships on August 21 and August 24, and the Coast Guard has since confirmed that investigators found malicious cyber activity aboard the vessels.

One of the ships was the VL Prosperity, a 1,093-foot Liberian-flagged crude oil supertanker capable of carrying roughly 2.3 million barrels of oil. The vessel had departed Egypt for Galveston, Texas, before federal investigators boarded it. Coast Guard cyber personnel, law enforcement officers, a vessel inspector and members of the FBI's Cyber Action Team examined information-technology and operational systems and worked with the crew and ship operator to remove the threat.

Authorities reported no operational disruption, vessel instability, danger to crew members or environmental damage from either incident. The Coast Guard has not publicly identified who carried out the attacks and is investigating whether the two incidents are connected and whether a foreign government or other actor was responsible.

Iranian state media claimed hackers had gained access to the VL Prosperity's propulsion, navigation and cargo systems and disrupted communications, but U.S. authorities have not verified those claims or attributed the attack to Iran. Coast Guard Cyber Command chief Rear Adm. Amy Grable said investigators confirmed malicious cyber activity but found no evidence that the vessel had become unsafe to navigate.

The incidents highlight concerns about cybersecurity aboard increasingly connected commercial ships. Modern vessels can use networked systems for navigation, propulsion, steering, ballast and other critical machinery. Grable warned that compromised systems could potentially contribute to collisions, pollution incidents or blocked waterways. She said approximately $5.4 trillion in commerce moves through U.S. ports annually, making disruptions to maritime operations potentially costly even when vessels themselves remain safe.

Who was responsible for the cyberattacks on the two U.S.-bound tankers remains under investigation.
