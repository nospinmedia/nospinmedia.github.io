Trump calls for DOJ investigation of Democratic lawmakers

Demand follows impeachment talk and plans for administration probes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

President Trump called for the Department of Justice to investigate Democrats after lawmakers discussed impeaching administration officials and incoming congressional leaders outlined plans for investigations and subpoenas following the midterm elections.

In a Truth Social post on Saturday, November 7, Trump accused Democrats of threatening administration officials and Republican donors, calling their actions "extortion at the highest level." He said Democrats should be held accountable and directed the Justice Department to examine what he characterized as criminal conduct. Trump also wrote that imprisonment should be directed at what he called the "EXTORTIONISTS." No evidence establishing criminal conduct by the lawmakers was provided in the post.

The comments followed statements by Democratic Reps. Hank Johnson of Georgia and Deborah Ross of North Carolina supporting possible impeachment proceedings against Attorney General Todd Blanche. Johnson said he believed Blanche should be impeached and removed from office. Ross also supported impeaching Blanche but said lawmakers should start with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Republican Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky has separately said he intends to pursue impeachment proceedings against Hegseth.

Trump did not name Johnson or Ross in his post. He specifically identified Democratic Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan, accusing her of threatening people based on Republican political views. Reporting linked Trump’s criticism to October remarks in which Slotkin discussed creating legal deterrence for people considering work in the Trump administration. Slotkin disputed Trump’s characterization and criticized his response.

The confrontation comes after Democrats won control of Congress in the 2026 midterm elections, giving them expanded authority beginning in January to conduct investigations, demand documents, call administration officials to testify and hold hearings.

A presidential request for an investigation does not establish that a crime occurred or require prosecutors to bring charges. Any criminal investigation would require the Justice Department to determine whether sufficient legal and factual grounds exist.
