Armed Group Citing Black Panthers Appears at Philly ICE Protest

What’s confirmed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished January 16, 2026 at 9:37 AM ET

A group identifying itself as the Black Panther Party for Self-Defense appeared at an anti-ICE protest in Philadelphia on Jan. 8, 2026, drawing national attention after members were seen carrying firearms during the demonstration. The event matters now because it adds a new and unusual dimension to the wave of protests sparked by the fatal shooting of Renee Good during a federal immigration operation in Minneapolis.

According to reporting, the group joined hundreds of demonstrators outside a federal facility in Philadelphia as part of coordinated nationwide protests against U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). Several members openly carried rifles and handguns, which is legal under Pennsylvania law. Organizers said the group’s presence was intended to deter violence and signal opposition to federal enforcement actions, while police monitored the protest without intervening.

Authorities did not report arrests or direct confrontations between the armed demonstrators and law enforcement. Officials said officers maintained standard crowd-control positions and did not coordinate with protest groups. There is no evidence that the armed participants marched alongside police or acted in cooperation with them, though they occupied the same public space during the demonstration.

Journalists and historians have emphasized that the Philadelphia group is a contemporary organization that invokes the name and symbolism of the historic Black Panther Party, which was active in the 1960s and 1970s. The original party’s relationship with law enforcement, particularly in cities like New York, was marked by sustained conflict rather than cooperation. That historical backdrop has fueled online speculation, some of it inaccurate, about recent protests.
