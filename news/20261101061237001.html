D.C. and Virginia sue to block Trump's proposed 250-foot arch

Administration says construction could begin as early as Nov. 5

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 1, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The District of Columbia and Virginia have sued the Trump administration in federal court seeking to stop construction of President Donald Trump's proposed 250-foot triumphal arch near Arlington National Cemetery. The lawsuit adds to existing legal challenges as the administration prepares to seek final approval for the project.

D.C. Attorney General Brian Schwalb and Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones argue that the project violates federal laws governing monuments and construction in the nation's capital. Their lawsuit cites the Commemorative Works Act, which generally requires congressional authorization for commemorative works on federal land in Washington, and the Height of Buildings Act, along with environmental and historic-preservation requirements.

The planned site is at Memorial Circle, near the Arlington Memorial Bridge connecting Washington with Virginia. The proposed arch would stand 250 feet tall, about 100 feet taller than the Statue of Liberty from its base to the top of its torch. The lawsuit says a planned three-year construction period could disrupt traffic and emergency services and create environmental impacts around the Potomac River.

The administration argues that a 1925 law authorizing construction associated with the Arlington Memorial Bridge provides legal authority for the project. Plans from that period contemplated monumental columns near the Virginia entrance to the bridge, and the government contends the proposed arch can proceed under that earlier authorization.

There are now three lawsuits seeking to halt the project. U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan has consolidated related challenges and instructed the parties to submit an agreed-upon briefing schedule as she considers a request for a temporary restraining order.

The administration plans to seek approval from the National Capital Planning Commission on November 5 and has said construction could potentially begin that day. The White House says the arch would serve as a gateway to Arlington National Cemetery and honor American veterans.
