Russian strikes across Ukraine kill at least 32

Five children among dead after apartment block is destroyed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 8, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Russian missile and drone strikes across Ukraine killed at least 32 people and injured about 100, with the heaviest losses in the northern city of Pryluky, where a missile destroyed a five-story residential building. Ukraine's emergency service said the death toll in Pryluky had risen to 22, including five children, as rescuers continued searching the rubble Thursday.

The attack on Pryluky, in the Chernihiv region about 93 miles east of Kyiv, also injured roughly 55 people. Emergency crews pulled 15 survivors from the wreckage. Additional deaths were reported in Kyiv, Oleksandria and Kremenchuk. In Kyiv, the death toll rose to four after two more bodies were recovered from an apartment building struck during the barrage.

President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Russia launched 70 missiles, many of them high-velocity ballistic weapons, along with dozens of Shahed attack drones during the overnight assault. He described it as one of Russia's most "vile strikes" and said ballistic missiles remain Ukraine's greatest air-defense challenge. Ukraine's air force said it intercepted just over 80% of 46 jet-powered drones, an improvement from recent interception rates of roughly 50% to 60%.

Russia's Defense Ministry acknowledged carrying out what it called a "massive strike," but said its targets were Ukrainian military-industrial facilities. Moscow said those included sites connected to defense manufacturer Fire Point, as well as port and ship-repair infrastructure. Ukrainian officials, however, reported extensive civilian casualties and damage to residential buildings, energy infrastructure and industrial facilities.

The attack coincided with Russian President Vladimir Putin's 74th birthday. Zelenskiy renewed his appeal for additional Patriot air-defense systems, which Ukraine relies on to counter high-speed ballistic missiles. The European Union also released a new €1.2 billion ($1.34 billion) tranche of assistance that Ukraine's government said would be directed toward defense needs.
