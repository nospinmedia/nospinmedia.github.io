Kilmar Abrego Garcia leaves U.S. for Spain after prolonged deportation fight

Trump administration had sought to remove him to several African countries

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 25, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Kilmar Abrego Garcia, the Salvadoran man mistakenly deported to El Salvador by the Trump administration in 2025 and later returned to the United States under court pressure, has voluntarily departed for Spain, ending for now a prolonged legal battle over where the government could send him.

The 31-year-old left the Washington, D.C., area on a commercial flight to Madrid Tuesday night. His attorneys said he ultimately plans to settle in Mexico. Abrego Garcia paid for his own flight, according to the Department of Homeland Security.

His departure was voluntary in the sense that he selected where to go rather than being forcibly placed on a government deportation flight, but he was not choosing between Spain and simply remaining indefinitely in Maryland. The Trump administration continued seeking his removal and had proposed sending him to several African countries, including Uganda, Eswatini, Ghana and most recently Liberia.

Abrego Garcia is not a U.S. citizen. He is a Salvadoran national who had lived in Maryland with his family. In 2019, an immigration judge granted him withholding of removal to El Salvador after finding he could face danger from gangs there. That protection did not grant citizenship or permanent legal status, but it prohibited the government from deporting him to El Salvador.

Despite that order, immigration authorities arrested him in Maryland in March 2025 and sent him to El Salvador's CECOT prison. Government attorneys subsequently acknowledged in court filings that the removal resulted from an administrative error. Following federal court rulings, including action by the U.S. Supreme Court, the administration returned him to the United States in June 2025.

The administration nevertheless maintained that he could legally be removed to a third country and most recently sought to send him to Liberia, which had agreed to accept him. Abrego Garcia ultimately arranged to leave for Spain instead.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said DHS was "delighted" that Abrego Garcia had self-deported. DHS said he received no financial incentives and will not be permitted to return to the United States. The department also repeated its allegation that Abrego Garcia was affiliated with the MS-13 gang, an allegation his attorneys have consistently denied and which has not been established in criminal court.

Abrego Garcia said, "I am leaving happy. I am leaving with my freedom."
