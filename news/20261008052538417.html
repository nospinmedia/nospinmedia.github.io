ICE leadership pushes return to 3,000 arrests a day

DHS denies agents are operating under arrest quotas

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 8, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement leadership instructed agents nationwide Tuesday to increase immigration arrests by roughly 1,000 people per day, returning to an earlier administration goal of 3,000 daily arrests, according to two sources familiar with the verbal directive cited by CNN. Agents were also told to try to reach approximately 19,000 arrests per week, accounting for typically lower enforcement activity on weekends.

The reported directive would represent another significant increase in enforcement. ICE had recently been averaging around 2,000 arrests per day, according to CNN. Earlier reporting from ABC News documented a previous shift toward a 2,000-per-day goal after officials had pushed for 3,000 daily arrests, while federal detention data reviewed by The Washington Post showed arrests climbing sharply during 2026. The administration has previously struggled to consistently reach the 3,000-per-day level.

The Department of Homeland Security disputes describing the numbers as quotas. In a statement responding to the latest report, DHS said there are "NO quotas on arrests" and said ICE is using lawful tools to find, arrest and deport people who are in the country illegally. The new instruction was described by CNN's sources as a verbal directive rather than a publicly released ICE policy.

The renewed enforcement push comes weeks before the 2026 midterm elections and as some Republican lawmakers in competitive districts have raised concerns about immigration operations. Separate reporting has documented economic and political pressure in areas affected by increased enforcement, including communities where businesses say immigration arrests have contributed to labor shortages. Those political concerns do not establish that the new arrest target was adopted because of the election; administration officials have consistently described increased immigration enforcement as a central policy objective.

ICE is also seeking additional temporary detention capacity. A recent agency solicitation cited an "exceptionally high operational tempo with increased arrests" as it sought more holding space at ICE field offices.
