Russia frees American Stephen Hubbard after more than four years

74-year-old released after President Trump personally appealed to Putin

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 22, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

Russia released Stephen Hubbard, a 74-year-old retired American teacher, on October 21 after more than four years in custody on charges that he fought as a mercenary for Ukraine. Hubbard had been designated as wrongfully detained by the U.S. government.

Russian President Vladimir Putin announced the release during a meeting with U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff in St. Petersburg. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Putin acted after a personal request from President Trump and also took Hubbard's poor health into consideration. Trump said he raised Hubbard's case directly with Putin during a telephone conversation the previous week.

Hubbard, a Michigan native who had lived in Ukraine for years, was captured by Russian forces in eastern Ukraine in April 2022, shortly after Russia launched its full-scale invasion. Russian prosecutors accused him of signing a contract with the Ukrainian military and fighting against Russian forces. Hubbard and his family denied the accusations. A Russian court convicted him following a closed trial in 2024 and sentenced him to nearly seven years in prison.

Trump said the United States did not exchange a Russian prisoner for Hubbard. The Kremlin described the release as being made on humanitarian grounds. Hubbard was taken to Pulkovo Airport in St. Petersburg and departed Russia with Witkoff. He was expected to receive medical treatment after returning to the United States.

The release occurred as Witkoff met Putin for talks focused on ending the war in Ukraine. Putin also agreed in principle during those discussions to resume three-way negotiations involving Russia, Ukraine and the United States. Neither government announced that Hubbard's freedom was exchanged for concessions in those negotiations.

Hubbard was the last American in Russian custody formally designated by the State Department as wrongfully detained, although other U.S. citizens remain imprisoned in Russia. His release follows the freeing of U.S. Marine veteran Robert Gilman in August.
