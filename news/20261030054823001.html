DoorDash agrees to buy Instacart for $17 billion

Deal would combine two major delivery platforms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 30, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

DoorDash has agreed to acquire Instacart in a deal valued at about $17 billion, a combination that would significantly expand DoorDash's presence beyond restaurant delivery and into the online grocery market. The companies announced the agreement on October 29, but the transaction still requires shareholder and regulatory approval and is not expected to close until late 2027 or the first half of 2028.

DoorDash is offering Instacart shareholders $65 per share, about a 26% premium to Instacart's closing price on October 22, before reports of the negotiations emerged. DoorDash has expanded rapidly from restaurant delivery into groceries, convenience items and other retail products, while Instacart specializes in grocery delivery and technology used by retailers.

The companies say Instacart will retain its name and app after the acquisition. Its approximately 600,000 independent-contractor shoppers would be able to continue fulfilling Instacart orders. Instacart customers would also gain access to more than 50,000 additional stores available through DoorDash. The companies plan to allow customers who currently pay separately for DashPass and Instacart+ to eventually use one membership across both services.

The proposed acquisition also raises competition questions. DoorDash says combining its logistics network with Instacart's grocery technology and retailer relationships would help local stores compete with larger companies including Amazon and Walmart. Critics and antitrust specialists have warned that reducing the number of independent delivery platforms could also mean fewer choices for grocers, workers and consumers.

The Federal Trade Commission is expected to examine the transaction. Reuters reported that the agency has recently focused on affordability issues and has previously scrutinized business practices involving delivery platforms, including Instacart.

For consumers, the eventual impact will depend partly on whether the combined company delivers lower costs and broader selection or whether reduced competition gives it greater influence over delivery prices and services.
