Child dies from measles in New York

First state measles-associated death in more than 30 years

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 3, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

A child in Lewis County, New York, has died in the state's first known measles-associated death in more than 30 years, health officials announced Monday as New York responds to a growing outbreak of the vaccine-preventable disease.

The New York State Department of Health and Lewis County Public Health did not disclose the child's age, vaccination status or other medical details, citing the family's privacy. Officials therefore have not established publicly whether the child had received the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine.

The death comes during a significant increase in measles cases. New York reported 178 cases statewide as of October 24, including 170 outside New York City and eight in the city. Gov. Kathy Hochul declared a state disaster emergency in October that expanded access to vaccination and other outbreak-response resources.

Health officials emphasized that vaccination provides strong protection against both infection and serious illness. According to the CDC and New York health officials, one MMR dose is about 93% effective at preventing measles, while two doses are about 97% effective. Breakthrough infections can occur, but the CDC says vaccinated people who develop measles are more likely to experience milder illness and fewer complications than people without pre-existing immunity.

Vaccination coverage is also a concern in the area where the death occurred. State data show 78.7% of children in Lewis County had received at least one MMR dose by age 2 as of January 2026. Public-health authorities generally seek coverage around 95% to limit sustained measles transmission in communities.

Measles can cause pneumonia, brain swelling, hospitalization and death, particularly among young children and other vulnerable people. New York officials are urging residents to check their vaccination status and contact healthcare providers about immunization.
