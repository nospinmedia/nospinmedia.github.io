Nearly 1,500 migrants reach Canary Islands in single day

Saturday arrivals set highest daily total since 2020 as El Hierro faces surge

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 16, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Nearly 1,500 migrants reached Spain's Canary Islands in a single day, setting the archipelago's highest daily total since 2020 and adding to a sharp November surge along the Atlantic migration route from West Africa.

Spain's Maritime Rescue Service said 1,488 people aboard 14 boats were rescued Saturday. Twelve of the boats arrived on El Hierro, the small westernmost Canary Island that has faced particularly heavy pressure from the recent increase in crossings. The other two reached Gran Canaria and Tenerife.

One vessel carried 240 people, including 12 women and 36 children. Authorities said most of those rescued were from Senegal, The Gambia and Guinea-Conakry.

During a Sunday visit to the port of La Restinga, Territorial Policy Minister Ángel Víctor Torres announced that Spain would deploy another maritime rescue vessel, the Guardamar Calíope, to the area. The deployment will place three of Spain's five Guardamar vessels in waters around El Hierro. Torres also scheduled an Interministerial Immigration Commission meeting for Tuesday, November 17.

El Hierro council president Alpidio Armas said 1,606 migrants remained in reception centers on the island Sunday. Officials planned to transfer about 900 people off El Hierro that day and continue transfers at rates of up to 600 per day during the week.

Canary Islands President Fernando Clavijo also called on Spain's central government to release €15.18 million in previously pledged funding for migrant reception and transfers, along with another €1 million for the Red Cross.

Despite the November surge, government figures show 13,327 migrants arrived by sea in the Canary Islands through October, 5.5% fewer than during the same period in 2025.
