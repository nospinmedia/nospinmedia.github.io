Democrats prepare immigration fight after midterm victory

Miller promotion sets up clash with incoming House majority

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

Congressional Democrats are preparing to challenge President Trump’s immigration policies through legislation and oversight after winning control of the House, setting up a confrontation with a White House where immigration strategist Stephen Miller has been promoted to chief of staff.

President Trump announced on Friday, November 6, that Miller would replace Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff. Miller had served as deputy chief of staff and has been a central figure in developing and implementing the administration’s immigration and deportation policies. Wiles, who became the first woman to serve as White House chief of staff, is leaving the position following Republican losses in the midterm elections.

Democrats are now preparing to use their incoming House majority to scrutinize immigration enforcement. Incoming committee leaders have indicated that oversight of federal enforcement operations will be among their priorities when the new Congress takes office. That could include hearings, document requests and investigations into how the administration has carried out its immigration agenda.

Incoming House Oversight Committee Chair Robert Garcia is among the Democrats preparing for increased scrutiny of the administration. The party’s new House majority will provide Democrats with subpoena and investigative powers they did not control during the previous Congress, although passing major immigration legislation would still require navigating the Senate and a presidential veto.

Immigration also emerged as a significant issue among voters. An AP Voter Poll conducted during the midterms found roughly 6 in 10 voters believed the Trump administration’s immigration enforcement actions had gone too far.

Miller’s promotion therefore comes as the administration prepares to defend its immigration policies against a Congress with greater Democratic oversight authority. The appointment itself does not change existing immigration law, but it places one of the administration’s principal immigration strategists in the White House’s top staff position.
