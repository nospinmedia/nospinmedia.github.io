Peltola officially wins Alaska Senate race with first-round majority

Victory gives Democrats 56 seats and makes history for Native American women

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 20, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Democrat Mary Peltola has officially won Alaska's U.S. Senate race, defeating Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan with a majority of first-choice votes and flipping a seat Republicans had held since 2015. The result gives Democrats a 56-seat Senate majority when the new Congress takes office in January 2027.

Peltola received approximately 50.1% of first-choice votes, compared with 45.8% for Sullivan, a margin of about 4.3 percentage points. Because Peltola crossed the 50% threshold in the first round, Alaska's ranked-choice system did not require elimination rounds or redistribution of voters' subsequent choices.

NSM previously reported Peltola's victory while covering the Justice Department's investigation into allegations surrounding independent candidate Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a retired teacher who shared the incumbent senator's surname. Peltola's margin over Sen. Sullivan was approximately 12,500 votes, while Daniel Sullivan Jr. received fewer than 5,000 votes, meaning even assigning all of those votes to the incumbent would not have changed the outcome.

The Justice Department moved on Thursday, November 19 to drop its appeal seeking to block certification of the election, saying the civil case had become moot. Alaska is scheduled to officially certify the results on November 25. Federal prosecutors have said the separate criminal investigation into allegations surrounding the recruitment of the second Sullivan remains ongoing. Peltola has denied involvement in any effort to recruit him.

Peltola, who is Yup'ik, will also make history when she enters the Senate. She and Minnesota Sen.-elect Peggy Flanagan, a member of the White Earth Band of Ojibwe, are set to become the first two Native American women to serve in the U.S. Senate.

Peltola previously made history as the first Alaska Native elected to Congress when she won a U.S. House seat in 2022. She lost that seat in 2024 before returning to statewide politics to challenge Sullivan.
