Netanyahu orders West Bank outposts dismantled

Move follows U.S. pressure over settler violence

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 7, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has ordered authorities to dismantle unauthorized Israeli settler outposts in the occupied West Bank, according to officials and current reporting. Two sources familiar with the directive said the move followed pressure from the United States over increasing violence by Israeli settlers against Palestinians. Israeli media reported that roughly 100 outposts could be affected, although no timetable for their removal has been announced.

Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, a prominent supporter of West Bank settlements, publicly confirmed that Netanyahu had instructed authorities to remove unauthorized sites established in Areas A and B. Smotrich said he opposed the directive. Under arrangements stemming from the Oslo Accords, Area A is under Palestinian civil and security administration, while Area B is under Palestinian civil administration with Israel retaining overriding security authority. Israel had already approved action against unauthorized Israeli construction in Area B earlier this year.

The directive follows increased U.S. criticism of settler violence. U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee visited the Palestinian town of Turmus Ayya on September 5, where he met Palestinian Americans affected by attacks. Huckabee condemned violent extremists among the settlers as terrorists and said those responsible should face consequences. The visit came amid growing concern about attacks on Palestinian communities and property across the West Bank.

A separate Israeli Supreme Court ruling on September 6 ordered Israeli authorities to enable three Palestinian families to return safely to their homes in the village of Jalud after they were driven out by settlers. The court criticized security authorities for failing to adequately protect the residents. The West Bank currently contains 146 recognized Israeli settlements and approximately 340 outposts, according to figures cited in current reporting; most countries consider Israeli settlements in the territory illegal under international law, while Israel disputes that position.
