EPA repeals major power plant carbon limits

Coal and gas rules rolled back as broader repeal is proposed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 15, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency has finalized the repeal of most of the 2024 Carbon Pollution Standards governing greenhouse gas emissions from coal- and natural gas-fired power plants, reversing one of the Biden administration's major climate regulations. EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin announced the action September 14 at the G20 Energy Abundance Ministerial in Houston, Texas.

The repealed requirements included emission guidelines for existing coal-, oil- and gas-fired steam generating units, carbon-capture standards for substantially modified coal plants and carbon-capture standards for new baseload natural-gas turbines. The 2024 rules relied in part on carbon capture and storage capable of reducing approximately 90% of carbon dioxide emissions at certain plants. EPA now says that level of carbon capture has not been adequately demonstrated at the scale and on the timetable required by the regulation.

EPA estimates the repeal will produce approximately $310 billion in savings over about two decades. The figure is the agency's estimate of avoided regulatory and compliance costs. The Biden administration had previously estimated that its standards would generate as much as $370 billion in climate and public-health benefits over roughly two decades.

The action does not yet eliminate every federal greenhouse gas standard for power plants. Alongside the finalized rollback, EPA issued a separate proposal to rescind the remaining standards and the findings underpinning regulation of greenhouse gas emissions from fossil-fuel power plants under Section 111 of the Clean Air Act. Until that additional proposal is finalized, some earlier standards remain in place.

The legal basis for regulating power-plant emissions has been contested for years. EPA's new proposal cites Supreme Court decisions including West Virginia v. EPA and Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo in arguing that Congress did not provide the agency sufficient authority for the broader regulatory approach. Environmental organizations including the Environmental Defense Fund and Earthjustice dispute EPA's interpretation and say the rollback will increase greenhouse gas emissions and associated climate and health risks.
