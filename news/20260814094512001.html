Judge Dismisses DOJ Antisemitism Lawsuit Against Harvard

Court Says Government Failed To Show Ongoing Title VI Violations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 2:47 PM ET

A federal judge dismissed the Department of Justice lawsuit accusing Harvard University of violating Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 by failing to adequately address antisemitic harassment on campus. In a four-page order issued on August 13, 2026, U.S. District Judge Richard G. Stearns granted Harvard's motion to dismiss, concluding the government did not plausibly allege ongoing noncompliance required to pursue enforcement that could have placed billions of dollars in federal research funding at risk.

The lawsuit, filed in March 2026 under President Donald Trump, alleged Harvard demonstrated deliberate indifference toward antisemitic incidents that followed the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack on Israel and the subsequent campus protests. Judge Stearns wrote that most of the government's examples came from the 2023–2024 academic year and that the three incidents cited from March 2025 were "too isolated and episodic" to establish a continuing institutional violation. The court also emphasized that the complaint contained no factual allegations of actionable misconduct after the government's formal June 30, 2025 notice to the university, a requirement the judge said is central to Title VI enforcement.

The ruling narrows the government's path for seeking to suspend or recover federal funding based on past campus conduct, reinforcing that Title VI is designed to secure present and future compliance rather than punish institutions for historical actions alone. The Justice Department said it disagrees with the decision and is evaluating whether to appeal. Harvard pointed to policy changes, revised disciplinary procedures, and ongoing efforts to address antisemitism and other forms of campus harassment.

Universities across the United States continue to face heightened scrutiny over campus speech, student safety, and discrimination policies following the October 7, 2023 attacks and the war that followed. Legal experts say the decision could influence how future federal civil rights cases are structured, particularly regarding what constitutes an ongoing violation under Title VI.
