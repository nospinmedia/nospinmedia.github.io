U.S. Measles Cases Surpass 4,000 in 2026

Nationwide Total Far Exceeds All of Last Year as Outbreaks Continue

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 10, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

The United States has recorded more than 4,000 measles cases in 2026, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, pushing this year's total far beyond the number reported during all of 2025 as outbreaks continue in multiple parts of the country.

The milestone represents a major resurgence of a disease that was declared eliminated from continuous domestic transmission in the United States in 2000. Most people infected during the current surge have been unvaccinated or have an unknown vaccination status, according to federal health data. Children have accounted for a substantial share of cases, while some patients have required hospitalization because of complications from the highly contagious virus.

Measles spreads through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes and can remain infectious in an enclosed space for up to two hours after that person leaves. The virus can cause fever, cough, runny nose, red eyes and a characteristic rash. More serious complications can include pneumonia, brain swelling and, in rare cases, death.

The MMR vaccine, which protects against measles, mumps and rubella, remains the primary tool for preventing infection. The CDC says two doses are approximately 97% effective at preventing measles, while one dose is approximately 93% effective. Public health officials have urged people to verify their vaccination status, particularly before traveling internationally or entering communities experiencing outbreaks.

The national increase comes as New York has declared a disaster emergency in response to its own growing measles outbreak, expanding efforts to improve vaccination access and contain transmission. That outbreak is part of the broader national resurgence rather than the sole driver of the country's case total.

The continuing spread also threatens the United States' longstanding measles elimination status. Elimination does not mean that no cases occur; it means the disease has not maintained continuous transmission within the country for at least 12 months. Federal and state health officials continue tracking outbreaks to determine whether sustained transmission could jeopardize that designation.

The growing national total underscores how quickly measles can spread when vaccination coverage falls below levels needed to reliably interrupt transmission.
