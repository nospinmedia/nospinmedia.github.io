Ex-Fauci Adviser Pleads Guilty To Hiding COVID-19 Records

Felony conspiracy carries up to 5 years; FOIA evasion admitted

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 20, 2026 at 2:52 PM ET

Dr. David M. Morens, a longtime senior adviser to Anthony Fauci at NIAID, pleaded guilty to a felony conspiracy charge for concealing federal records related to COVID-19, admitting he worked to evade FOIA requirements during the pandemic. The 78-year-old entered the plea in U.S. District Court before Judge Paula Xinis in Greenbelt, Maryland.

Prosecutors said Morens conspired beginning in April 2020 to keep agency communications off official systems and out of public view, including by using personal email, deleting messages, and sharing non-public NIH materials with outside researchers. The charge—conspiracy to commit offenses and to defraud the United States—carries a maximum penalty of 5 years in prison. Sentencing is set for November 12, 2026.

The case centers on how a senior federal official handled transparency obligations amid intense scrutiny of pandemic-era research and grant oversight. Court filings describe how Morens conducted official business on Gmail, discussed avoiding record retention, and circulated internal drafts while corresponding with outside scientists. Investigators also highlighted communications tied to the suspended EcoHealth Alliance grant, "Understanding the Risk of Bat Coronavirus Emergence," which included subawards to the Wuhan Institute of Virology. A House investigation previously publicized emails in which Morens outlined methods to keep sensitive exchanges from disclosure; those findings align with the conduct admitted in court.

Prosecutors emphasized that the scheme sought to thwart federal transparency and records laws, including FOIA and the Federal Records Act. While the plea establishes criminal responsibility for Morens, the proceeding does not allege wrongdoing by Fauci or conclude broader questions about research funding decisions. The court will determine the sentence after reviewing the advisory guidelines and the parties’ submissions.
