139 Migrants Die in Djibouti Boat Disaster

15 Children Among Dead as 84 Remain Missing and Search Continues

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 12, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

At least 139 migrants, including 15 children, have died after an overcrowded boat carrying 305 people capsized off the coast of Djibouti while attempting to reach Yemen. Another 84 people remain missing, according to the United Nations' International Organization for Migration (IOM), making the disaster one of the deadliest recorded along the migration route connecting the Horn of Africa with the Arabian Peninsula.

The vessel overturned near Guhere, north of the Djiboutian port town of Obock, during the night of October 8–9, 2026. The boat was carrying migrants across the Gulf of Aden toward Yemen, a dangerous crossing frequently used by people seeking employment and economic opportunities in wealthier Gulf countries.

The IOM reported that 82 people survived, all Ethiopian nationals. Survivors were taken to a migration assistance center in Obock, where humanitarian workers provided medical treatment and psychological support. Some had swallowed seawater during the sinking, while others were experiencing severe emotional distress.

According to Alexandre Coissac, the IOM's chief of mission in Djibouti, the vessel sank shortly after departure, primarily because it was overcrowded. Survivors described the boat beginning to sink backward almost immediately after they boarded.

The confirmed death toll increased from an initial 109 to 139 as additional bodies were recovered. Authorities warned that the number could rise further because dozens of passengers remained unaccounted for. Some victims were buried by fellow migrants before authorities could formally identify them, complicating efforts to establish the final death toll and notify families.

The tragedy highlights the dangers of the Eastern Migration Route, which connects Ethiopia and Somalia with Djibouti, Yemen, and other destinations across the Arabian Peninsula. Migrants frequently travel aboard overcrowded vessels operated by smugglers, facing risks from unsafe boats, exploitation, and regional conflict.

According to the IOM, more than 900 migrants died or disappeared along the Eastern Route in 2025. Renewed violence in Ethiopia's Tigray region and continuing instability in Yemen have added pressure to an already dangerous migration corridor.

IOM Director-General Amy Pope called for expanded safe and legal migration pathways, arguing that preventing similar disasters requires international cooperation rather than relying solely on emergency rescue operations.
