Trump administration plans $2.8 billion bomb sale to Israel

Package includes 40,000 heavy bombs and bunker-penetrating warheads

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 16, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The Trump administration is planning a $2.8 billion weapons sale to Israel that would provide tens of thousands of heavy munitions, according to U.S. officials familiar with the proposed package. Congress has been informally notified about the pending transaction, which is not yet final and would largely be financed through U.S. Foreign Military Financing assistance.

The package includes 20,000 Mk84 bombs and 20,000 BLU-117 bombs, both 2,000-pound munitions, along with 20,000 I-2000 penetrator warhead bodies designed for use against hardened underground targets. The I-2000 components are warhead bodies rather than an additional 20,000 complete bombs, an important distinction in describing the size of the package. The heavy munitions can be used against fortified targets, while their destructive power has also prompted scrutiny over potential civilian harm when used in densely populated areas.

The proposed transaction comes after the Biden administration withheld one shipment of 2,000-pound bombs in 2024 amid concerns about their potential use in densely populated areas of Gaza. President Trump lifted that hold after returning to office. The Trump administration subsequently approved additional arms sales to Israel, including packages totaling $6.67 billion in January 2026.

The informal notification now places the proposal before leaders of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee and House Foreign Affairs Committee, an early stage of the U.S. arms-sale review process. Committee leaders traditionally review proposed transactions before the State Department submits a formal notification to Congress. The review comes as some rank-and-file lawmakers have raised concerns about continued U.S. weapons transfers to Israel. The weapons have not yet been transferred, and major arms packages can take considerable time to complete and deliver.

The proposal comes as Israel continues military operations following years of conflict in Gaza and Lebanon and expanded hostilities involving Iran. Israel says its military operations are necessary for its defense, while the use of heavy explosive weapons in populated areas has faced sustained criticism over civilian casualties.
