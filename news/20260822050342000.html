Federal Officials Order Historic Colorado River Water Cuts

Arizona, California and Nevada Face Mandatory Reductions Through 2028

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 22, 2026 at 12:57 PM ET

The U.S. Department of the Interior has finalized new operating rules requiring Arizona, California, and Nevada to reduce their combined annual use of Colorado River water by 1.25 million acre-feet during 2027 and 2028. The decision follows years of negotiations that failed to produce a unanimous agreement among the seven Colorado River Basin states before existing guidelines expired. Officials said the reductions are necessary to protect the river system after more than two decades of drought and historically low reservoir levels.

Under the finalized framework, Arizona will reduce annual deliveries by 760,000 acre-feet, the largest share of the cuts. California must reduce its allocation by 440,000 acre-feet, while Nevada will cut 50,000 acre-feet each year. Under existing treaty obligations, Mexico will also reduce deliveries by 250,000 acre-feet. The Upper Basin states—Colorado, New Mexico, Utah, and Wyoming—will not face mandatory reductions under the current plan but have committed to voluntary conservation efforts totaling at least 700,000 acre-feet.

Interior Secretary Doug Burgum signed the official Record of Decision on August 21, 2026, completing the environmental review process required under the National Environmental Policy Act. Federal officials said the operating plan is designed to stabilize water storage in Lake Mead and Lake Powell, while helping prevent Lake Powell from falling below the critical elevation of 3,510 feet, a level that could threaten hydroelectric power generation at Glen Canyon Dam and limit downstream water releases.

The Colorado River supplies water to roughly 40 million people, irrigates millions of acres of farmland, and supports major cities across the Southwest. Officials described the new operating rules as an interim stabilization measure while basin states continue negotiating a longer-term agreement expected to govern river operations through 2036.
