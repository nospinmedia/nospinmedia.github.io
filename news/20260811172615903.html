Judge Orders Anonymous Jury in Luigi Mangione Murder Trial

Court cites high-profile case ahead of September proceedings

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 2:49 PM ET

A New York judge ruled on August 11, 2026, that jurors in the upcoming state murder trial of Luigi Mangione will remain anonymous, citing the extraordinary public attention surrounding the case. Justice Gregory Carro also approved the use of an overflow courtroom so members of the public and news media can watch the proceedings when the primary courtroom reaches capacity.

Mangione, 28, has pleaded not guilty to charges including second-degree murder, criminal possession of a weapon, and criminal possession of a forged instrument in connection with the December 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson outside a Manhattan hotel. Jury selection is scheduled to begin on September 8, 2026.

Anonymous juries are used in a limited number of high-profile criminal cases when courts determine that protecting juror identities may reduce the risk of harassment, intimidation, or outside influence. While Justice Carro did not publicly identify specific threats, the court concluded that the level of public attention justified additional safeguards while preserving public access through the overflow courtroom.

Prosecutors have indicated they plan to present surveillance video, forensic evidence, digital records, and a 3D-printed firearm they allege is connected to the shooting. Defense attorneys continue to deny Mangione's guilt and have challenged portions of the prosecution's evidence as the case moves toward trial.

The ruling establishes procedures for one of the country's most closely watched criminal trials, balancing courtroom transparency with measures intended to protect the integrity of the jury process before testimony begins later this year.
