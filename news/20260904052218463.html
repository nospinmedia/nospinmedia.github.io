ICE officer federally indicted over Minneapolis shooting account

Christian Castro faces six false-statement counts

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 4, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

A federal grand jury has indicted U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer Christian Castro, 52, on six counts of making false statements to investigators about his role in the shooting of a Venezuelan man during the federal immigration crackdown in Minnesota. Castro was taken back into custody in Texas on September 3, one week after his release from a county jail amid a dispute over Minnesota's efforts to extradite him on separate state charges.

The federal case stems from the January 14 shooting of Julio Cesar Sosa-Celis in Minneapolis. Minnesota prosecutors allege Castro fired his service weapon through the closed front door of an occupied home, striking Sosa-Celis in the leg. Castro had reported that Sosa-Celis and others attacked federal officers with a snow shovel and broom before the shooting. Video evidence contradicted that account, and federal charges originally brought against Sosa-Celis and another man were dismissed in February after prosecutors cited newly discovered evidence. Castro has not publicly responded to the federal allegations.

Castro already faces Minnesota charges including four counts of second-degree assault and one count of falsely reporting a crime. Texas authorities arrested him in May, but Texas Gov. Greg Abbott declined to approve Minnesota's extradition request while questioning whether its legal requirements had been satisfied. A federal judge later rejected Minnesota's attempt to compel the extradition, and Castro was released after spending 90 days in a Texas jail. The Minnesota arrest warrant remains active.

The federal indictment is the first known criminal case brought by the Justice Department against an immigration agent for alleged wrongdoing connected to the Minnesota enforcement operation. DHS has suspended Castro. Reuters reported that a separate federal civil-rights investigation remains open and that no decision has been made on additional charges. Federal prosecutor Matt Evans, who had worked on the investigation, was fired this week after reporting that he objected to Justice Department officials blocking efforts to pursue other federal charges; Reuters reported that Evans is now under investigation for alleged obstruction of the grand jury process.
