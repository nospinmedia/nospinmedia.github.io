DOJ expands Alaska 'Decoy Dan' investigation after election

Peltola's 12,458-vote margin exceeded second Sullivan's entire vote total

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 19, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

A federal investigation into allegations that Democrats helped recruit a second candidate named Dan Sullivan for Alaska's U.S. Senate race has expanded to more than 200 subpoenas, even though the disputed candidate did not receive enough votes to have changed the election outcome.

Democrat Mary Peltola received 144,302 first-choice votes, defeating Republican incumbent Sen. Dan S. Sullivan, who received 131,844, by 12,458 votes. Independent candidate Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., whose candidacy is at the center of the investigation, received 4,760 votes.

Even if every one of Daniel J. Sullivan Jr.'s 4,760 votes had instead gone to the Republican incumbent, Dan S. Sullivan would have finished with 136,604 votes — still 7,698 behind Peltola. The numbers therefore show that the second Sullivan's candidacy could not by itself have changed the winner.

Recruiting or encouraging another person to run for office is not inherently illegal. The federal investigation instead concerns allegations that the effort involved potentially unlawful coordination, deception or other conduct surrounding Daniel J. Sullivan Jr.'s candidacy. No one involved has been charged with a crime, and Peltola has denied involvement and said she has not been contacted by investigators.

The Justice Department investigation began before Election Day and now spans multiple states. Prominent Democratic election attorney Marc Elias, whose firm represents Peltola's campaign, previously received a target letter and has characterized the investigation as politically motivated. The Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee has also denied involvement in recruiting Daniel J. Sullivan Jr.

The Justice Department separately tried to delay certification if the incumbent lost by fewer votes than Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. received. U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed that lawsuit on November 1, finding problems with standing and the requested remedy. Alaska officials are targeting November 25 to certify the election.

The criminal investigation can continue regardless of whether the alleged conduct affected the result, but the vote totals establish that the disputed candidacy did not determine who won the Senate seat.
