Lula Launches Reelection Campaign In Brazil

President opens race against Flávio Bolsonaro ahead of October vote

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 11:13 AM ET

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva officially launched his campaign for a fourth presidential term on August 16, opening Brazil's general election season with a rally in São Bernardo do Campo, the industrial city where his political career began. Speaking before thousands of supporters, Lula pledged to strengthen Brazil's economy, protect the country's strategic mineral resources, expand public services, and improve conditions for workers, including those in the growing gig economy.

The campaign sets up a closely watched contest against conservative Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, who also formally launched his campaign on August 16 with a rally on Copacabana Beach in Rio de Janeiro. The election follows years of political polarization after Jair Bolsonaro, Brazil's former president and Flávio Bolsonaro's father, was convicted in connection with an attempted coup and remains under house arrest.

Brazil's first-round presidential election is scheduled for October 4, with a runoff planned later in the month if no candidate wins an outright majority. As Latin America's largest economy and democracy, Brazil's election is expected to influence regional politics, trade, environmental policy, and international relations well beyond its borders.

The campaign is expected to focus heavily on economic growth, public security, labor policy, inflation, and Brazil's role in global affairs. Polling over the coming weeks will provide a clearer picture of whether either candidate can expand support beyond their traditional political bases before voters head to the polls.
