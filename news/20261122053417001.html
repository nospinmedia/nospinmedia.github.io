Denmark Says Russia Plotted Assassination On Its Soil

Armed Suspects Were Arrested Minutes Before U.S.-Ukrainian Rapper's Concert

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 22, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Denmark has accused Russian intelligence of orchestrating a plot to assassinate dual U.S.-Ukrainian citizen Albert Vasiliev, known professionally as rapper Kyivstoner, saying police stopped armed suspects just minutes before he was scheduled to perform near Copenhagen.

Danish authorities say the plot was disrupted in September, when police intercepted three men linked to Denmark's criminal underworld near a concert venue in Hellerup, north of Copenhagen, about five minutes before the concert. Officers recovered loaded firearms and knives, and one suspect had purchased a ticket to the performance. Two Danish men now face charges of attempted murder and assisting a foreign intelligence service. Both deny wrongdoing.

Denmark's Security and Intelligence Service, known as PET, announced on November 20 that it assesses Russian intelligence was behind the operation. Denmark subsequently summoned Russian Ambassador Vladimir Barbin and ordered Russia's defense attaché to leave the country within seven days.

Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said Denmark "will not be intimidated" and pledged to work with allies to strengthen sanctions against Russia and increase support for Ukraine. Russia rejects Denmark's allegations and says Copenhagen has not presented evidence establishing Moscow's involvement.

The diplomatic response has since expanded. Denmark announced it would halt non-essential visas for Russian citizens and urged other European governments to tighten entry restrictions. Copenhagen is pushing for stronger controls on Russian tourist visas, non-biometric passports and entry by current and former Russian combatants across Europe.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa condemned the alleged assassination plot on November 21, describing it as an attack on European Union soil and signaling that European governments would respond.

The case adds to European concerns about Russia allegedly recruiting local criminal networks to conduct covert operations while creating distance between those carrying out the acts and Russian intelligence services.
