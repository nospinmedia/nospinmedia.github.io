Ousted Ukrainian Defense Minister Calls for Wartime Elections

Former official urges voting despite martial law restrictions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 19, 2026 at 9:30 AM ET

Ukraine's former defense minister Mykhailo Fedorov called for the country to establish a way to hold presidential elections despite the ongoing war with Russia, arguing that democratic institutions should continue to function even under wartime conditions. In a video address released Tuesday, Fedorov urged officials to create a legal and secure process that would allow civilians, frontline troops and displaced Ukrainians to vote while preserving national security.

The appeal represents one of the most significant public political challenges to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy since Russia's full-scale invasion began in February 2022. Under Ukraine's constitution and martial law provisions, national elections are suspended while the country remains under martial law. As a result, Zelenskyy has remained in office beyond the end of his original five-year presidential term in May 2024, a situation supported by existing Ukrainian law during wartime.

Fedorov was removed as defense minister in July 2026 following disagreements with senior military leadership. His call for elections came just hours before Ukraine's parliament considered confirming acting Defense Minister Yevhenii Khmara to permanently lead the ministry. While Fedorov argued that a democratic renewal process should begin despite the conflict, he did not present a detailed plan for conducting nationwide voting under current battlefield conditions.

Ukrainian officials have not announced any plans to change the country's election laws, and holding a nationwide vote would require resolving major legal, logistical and security challenges, including protecting polling locations, enabling displaced citizens to participate and ensuring voting access for military personnel serving on the front lines. For now, Fedorov's proposal remains a political appeal rather than a government policy initiative.
