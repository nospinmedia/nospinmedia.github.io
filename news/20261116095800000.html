Vulcan rocket fails during flight with 40 Amazon satellites aboard

First total ULA mission failure triggers investigation and grounding

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 16, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

A United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket failed about 10 minutes after liftoff Sunday, destroying its payload of 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites and triggering an investigation into the first total mission failure in ULA's 20-year history.

The Vulcan Centaur lifted off from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida at about 12:40 p.m. Eastern Time. The flight initially appeared normal, with six solid-fuel boosters separating after helping propel the rocket through the lower atmosphere. ULA later confirmed that an anomaly prevented the mission from reaching orbit.

The exact cause remains unknown. Reporting from CBS News and Spaceflight Now indicated that the rocket's first stage appeared to burn longer than expected and separate later than planned. The anomaly occurred while the hydrogen-fueled Centaur upper stage was firing, but ULA has not determined what caused the failure.

The loss marks the first outright mission failure for United Launch Alliance, the joint venture formed by Boeing and Lockheed Martin in 2006. Vulcan had flown only four previous missions and was returning after a nine-month pause while ULA and its partners investigated problems involving solid rocket booster nozzles on earlier flights. Those boosters appeared to operate normally Sunday.

The failure also affects Amazon Leo, formerly known as Project Kuiper, Amazon's planned satellite internet competitor to SpaceX's Starlink. Amazon had 398 satellites in orbit before Sunday's mission and had purchased 38 Vulcan launches from ULA as part of its deployment strategy.

ULA said it will analyze flight data, determine the cause and implement corrective action before Vulcan returns to flight. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman offered NASA engineering assistance with the investigation.
