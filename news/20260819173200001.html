Pennsylvania Tightens Rules for AI Data Centers

Shapiro order requires local approval and grid safeguards

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 20, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro signed an executive order on August 18, 2026, establishing new statewide requirements for AI data center development. The order removes proposed data centers from the state's PA Permit Fast Track program and requires developers to meet the administration's Governor's Responsible Infrastructure Development (GRID) standards before receiving state permits or incentives. Shapiro said the changes are intended to address growing concerns about electricity demand, water use, transparency and local community impacts.

Under the executive order, developers must obtain formal municipal approval, enter enforceable agreements meeting GRID requirements, provide dedicated power generation while covering associated infrastructure costs, commit to community benefits and local workforce development, and comply with new transparency requirements that prohibit state agencies from signing nondisclosure agreements related to data center projects. Developers that fail to comply could also lose eligibility for Pennsylvania's sales tax exemption for qualifying computer data center equipment.

State officials said Pennsylvania has received proposals for more than 100 potential data center projects, but only five currently hold the permits necessary to begin operating. The administration argues the new policy is designed to discourage speculative projects while ensuring viable developments do not shift electricity costs or infrastructure burdens onto local communities. The order does not impose a statewide moratorium on AI data centers but establishes stricter conditions before projects can move forward.

Because the changes were enacted through an executive order rather than legislation, future administrations could revise or rescind them, while lawmakers continue debating whether to codify similar standards into state law. Pennsylvania's approach is likely to be closely watched by other states facing rapid growth in AI infrastructure and increasing concerns over electric grid capacity, environmental impacts and local control of large-scale development.
