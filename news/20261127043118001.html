Zelenskyy warns Russia is preparing massive new strike

155-drone barrage follows warning as Ukraine seeks more air defenses

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 27, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says intelligence indicates Russia is completing preparations for another massive attack on Ukraine's power grid and other critical infrastructure, as a new overnight barrage of 155 Russian drones underscored the continuing threat.

Russia launched the 155 drones overnight into Friday, November 27, according to Ukrainian authorities. Air defenses shot down or suppressed 110, while strikes were recorded at 27 locations. The attack came hours after Zelenskyy warned that Moscow was preparing a larger assault in the coming days targeting Ukraine's winter energy system and other essential infrastructure.

Zelenskyy issued his warning after a security coordination meeting on Thursday, November 26, saying Russia was preparing attacks against electrical substations, transmission lines, logistics infrastructure and essential municipal services. He urged Ukrainians to pay close attention to missile and air-raid warnings and said Kyiv was pressing Western partners for additional Patriot and NASAMS air-defense interceptors.

Ukraine was already repairing damage from an earlier Russian attack involving ballistic missiles and 113 drones overnight into Thursday. Ukrainian authorities said 87 of those drones were intercepted while utility crews worked to restore electricity across six regions.

The military developments come alongside renewed diplomatic efforts. President Trump said Thursday that he had urged Russian President Vladimir Putin to "pull back all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe" in hopes of reaching an agreement between Russia and Ukraine. Trump's comments followed recent discussions in China involving Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping, as well as a Wednesday meeting with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte at Mar-a-Lago.

Zelenskyy has also said Ukraine could appeal directly to the U.S. Congress for a new military aid package and tougher sanctions if U.S.-led diplomatic efforts fail to produce an acceptable peace agreement by the end of the year. U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff have been involved in negotiations, with Kushner describing talks as being in "a little bit of a deadlock" over territorial issues.
