Houthi attacks wound 73 across southern Saudi Arabia

Fires force some energy operations to temporarily halt

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 8, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A wave of attacks by Yemen's Houthi movement struck southern Saudi Arabia early Tuesday, wounding 73 civilians, including women and children, as renewed fighting in Yemen spills across the border. Saudi officials said civilian, economic and energy facilities were targeted in one of the most significant escalations since years of relative calm between the two sides ended this summer.

Saudi-led coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki said attacks hit sites in Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan and Najran. He described the strikes as a dangerous escalation and said the coalition would take measures to counter further attacks. Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry separately condemned the strikes and said the country reserved the right to defend its territory and infrastructure.

The Saudi Ministry of Energy said several energy facilities and installations in the kingdom's southern region were attacked Tuesday morning. Fires broke out at multiple locations and forced a temporary halt to some operations while emergency teams worked to contain the fires, secure the sites and assess damage. Officials did not immediately provide a detailed assessment of the effect on Saudi oil production.

Houthi media claimed the group targeted Abha International Airport, King Khalid Air Base and Saudi Aramco facilities. Those specific targeting claims came from the Houthis and were not independently confirmed by Saudi authorities. The attacks came after the Houthis accused Saudi Arabia of carrying out a strike on a detention facility in Yemen's al-Jawf province.

The escalation follows weeks of renewed fighting between the Houthis and forces aligned with Yemen's internationally recognized government, ending a roughly four-year period of relative calm in the country's civil war. Fighting has intensified along several fronts, while Houthi attacks have also targeted Saudi oil infrastructure and commercial shipping in the Red Sea.
