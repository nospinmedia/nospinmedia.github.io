Nancy Mace's Ex-Fiancé and Two Others Charged in Sexual Assault Case

South Carolina Arrests Follow Allegations Congresswoman Detailed in 2025 House Speech

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The former fiancé of U.S. Representative Nancy Mace and two other men have been charged with sexual misconduct-related offenses in South Carolina, following allegations the Republican congresswoman publicly described during a speech on the House floor last year.

The South Carolina Law Enforcement Division announced the charges Wednesday, October 14, in connection with an alleged sexual assault involving a 23-year-old woman in October 2018.

Brendan Patrick Bryant, 53, Mace's former fiancé, faces a voyeurism charge. John Douglas Osborne, 46, faces first-degree assault and battery and two counts of third-degree criminal sexual conduct. Eric Bernard Bowman, 46, faces voyeurism, obstruction of justice and unlawfully publishing the identity of an alleged sexual assault victim.

According to investigators, the woman became severely intoxicated at a residence on Sullivan's Island, near Charleston, before losing consciousness.

Court documents allege that Osborne sexually assaulted her while Bryant and Bowman watched and photographed or recorded the incident. Investigators say another woman witnessed what happened through a home security camera system and preserved evidence.

The allegations remain unproven, and all three defendants are entitled to the presumption of innocence.

Additional charges against Bowman concern alleged actions during the investigation. Authorities say he reset a cellphone after investigators obtained a warrant seeking the device. He is also accused of publishing an alleged victim's identity on social media.

The arrests follow a nearly hourlong speech Mace delivered in February 2025, when she accused several men, including Bryant, of sexual misconduct involving women and described discovering disturbing material on his cellphone.

Bryant denied the allegations at the time. Following Wednesday's charges, his attorney again rejected the accusations and said the case should be contested in court.

The investigation began before Mace's congressional speech. State authorities were examining allegations involving the men after receiving information from federal law enforcement.

Following the arrests, Mace highlighted legislation she has introduced to strengthen protections for sexual assault survivors, including measures addressing retaliation and lawsuits brought against people who report alleged abuse.

Mace is leaving Congress after an unsuccessful campaign for South Carolina governor. The criminal proceedings will continue independently of her political career.

The case also highlights the distinction between public accusations and criminal prosecution. Investigators must establish sufficient evidence to support charges, while prosecutors must ultimately prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.
