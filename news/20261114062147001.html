Microsoft restores Outlook and Teams after worldwide outage

Server problems disrupted email and communications for more than a day

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 14, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Microsoft says its affected services have recovered after a worldwide outage disrupted Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 functions for more than a day, interfering with communications at businesses, government agencies and other organizations around the world.

The disruption began Thursday and primarily affected features connected to Exchange Online, Microsoft's cloud-based email and scheduling service. Users reported being unable to send email, manage calendars or hold virtual meetings. Microsoft attributed the outage to server problems and said it had found no evidence of malicious activity. The company also ruled out artificial intelligence as the cause.

The scale of the disruption became apparent as workdays began across different regions. Downdetector, operated by Ookla, recorded more than 7.4 million outage reports worldwide, with reports at one point reaching nearly 20,000 per minute. Organizations running Microsoft services on their own servers were less affected than customers relying on Microsoft's cloud infrastructure.

The outage affected organizations across government, healthcare, transportation and finance. Britain's National Health Service and Metropolitan Police reported disruptions, while Australia's federal police and intelligence agency were affected. Airlines warned customers of communication delays, and some financial firms turned to telephone calls and even fax machines to confirm transactions when email was unavailable.

Microsoft deployed software fixes more broadly as engineers worked to stabilize the affected systems. The incident was the company's second Microsoft 365 service disruption this year and renewed attention on how heavily businesses and public agencies depend on a small number of cloud and software providers.

The outage was caused by Microsoft's own server problems rather than the separate nationwide U.S. air traffic control failure that occurred Friday.
