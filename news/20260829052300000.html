Federal Judge Rejects Trump's Latest Hush-Money Appeal

Third attempt to move case to federal court fails

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 29, 2026 at 12:19 PM ET

President Trump lost his latest effort to move his New York hush-money criminal case into federal court after U.S. District Judge Alvin K. Hellerstein ruled that the request was untimely and failed to present any new legal basis for removal. The decision marks the third time Hellerstein has rejected Trump's attempt to transfer the prosecution from New York state court, leaving his 34 felony convictions in place while the case continues through the appellate process.

Hellerstein wrote that Trump's latest filing was "neither new nor legally sufficient" and did not establish the good cause required under federal removal law to revisit a case that has already gone through trial, sentencing and appeal in state court. The judge also rejected arguments that evidence presented during the trial involved protected presidential conduct under the U.S. Supreme Court's 2024 presidential immunity ruling, concluding that the conduct at issue involved private actions rather than official presidential duties. He stated that payments intended to avoid personal embarrassment do not fall within the outer perimeter of presidential responsibilities.

Trump was convicted in May 2024 on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records related to reimbursements for a $130,000 payment made to adult-film actress Stormy Daniels before the 2016 presidential election. Prosecutors argued the records were falsified to conceal the true purpose of the payments, while Trump has consistently denied wrongdoing and has argued the case was politically motivated. He received an unconditional discharge rather than jail time or probation, allowing him to appeal without additional sentencing conditions.

Following Friday's ruling, Trump's attorneys filed a notice of appeal with the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, continuing their effort to challenge the conviction in federal court. Separately, Trump's direct appeal of the underlying state conviction remains pending in New York's appellate courts, where his legal team continues to seek reversal of the verdict.
