North Korea fires about 10 short-range ballistic missiles

Launch comes as tensions rise along border with South Korea

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

North Korea fired about 10 short-range ballistic missiles toward waters off its east coast on October 29, according to South Korea's military, as tensions continue to rise between the two Koreas following a deadly land-mine incident along their heavily fortified border.

South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said the missiles were launched at about 12:30 p.m. local time from the Wonsan area on North Korea's eastern coast and traveled approximately 250 kilometers. Japan said the missiles appeared to have fallen outside its Exclusive Economic Zone. South Korea said it strengthened surveillance for additional launches while sharing information with the United States and Japan.

The launch came less than a week after South Korea proposed military talks with Pyongyang intended to reduce the risk of accidental clashes along the Military Demarcation Line. Relations have deteriorated further since a September 21 land-mine explosion injured three South Korean soldiers in the Demilitarized Zone. A South Korean and United Nations investigation concluded that North Korea had planted the mines, while Pyongyang has denied responsibility and accused Seoul of fabricating the allegations.

North Korea has also threatened to resume anti-South propaganda broadcasts and has increased visible military activity near the border. South Korea's presidential office convened an emergency security meeting following Thursday's missile launches and called on Pyongyang to stop actions that violate United Nations Security Council resolutions.

Thursday's barrage was North Korea's first ballistic missile launch since October 12, when it fired multiple missiles toward its eastern waters. South Korean military officials said some of those earlier missiles traveled more than 900 kilometers.
