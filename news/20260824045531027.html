Hawk Fire surges toward Reno as 42,000 ordered out

Nevada mobilizes National Guard as blaze destroys homes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 24, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

A fast-moving wildfire has destroyed homes and forced mass evacuations around Reno, Nevada, after spreading across more than 23 square miles with zero containment. The Hawk Fire, which began Saturday, August 22, swept across the Peavine Peak area of the Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest, where shifting winds and extremely dry vegetation drove flames toward neighborhoods on the northwest side of the city.

Authorities ordered about 42,000 residents in the expanding "GO NOW" evacuation zone to leave, while another 45,000 people were placed under evacuation warnings. The mandatory zone reached the edge of the University of Nevada, Reno, and officials warned residents that changing winds could continue to move the fire unpredictably. At least six people — three first responders and three civilians — were injured. Officials have described the fire as human-caused, although they have not said whether it was accidental or intentional and the investigation remains underway.

Nevada Gov. Joe Lombardo declared a state of emergency in Washoe County and mobilized the Nevada National Guard. Two Guard helicopter crews were assigned to aerial firefighting operations, while 60 troops were deployed to help law enforcement protect evacuated neighborhoods. More than 800 fire personnel were responding to the blaze. Nearly 10,000 customers remained without power Sunday, portions of U.S. Route 395 were closed, and two hospitals evacuated patients as a precaution.

Washoe County opened the Reno-Sparks Convention Center for evacuees, with separate locations established for animals. Officials said an unknown number of homes had been destroyed as the fire moved so quickly Saturday night that flames jumped over fire trucks and complicated access to hot spots. Fire crews continued facing extreme fire behavior as gusts pushed flames through dense, dry fuels.
