States renew challenge to USPS mail ballot rule

New lawsuit seeks emergency block before midterm voting begins

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 27, 2026 at 7:42 PM ET

A coalition of 23 states, the District of Columbia, and Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro filed a new federal lawsuit in Boston seeking to block the U.S. Postal Service's newly finalized mail ballot rule ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. The challenge was filed immediately after a federal judge lifted an earlier injunction following a U.S. Supreme Court procedural ruling that concluded previous lawsuits had been brought before the Postal Service finalized its regulations. The new complaint argues the finalized rule exceeds the agency's legal authority and unlawfully interferes with state administration of elections.

The Postal Service rule implements portions of President Trump's Executive Order 14399, requiring states to submit lists of eligible mail voters and use standardized ballot envelopes containing unique barcodes before USPS will deliver mailed ballots. The lawsuit argues the requirements violate the Administrative Procedure Act, federal election laws and constitutional protections by imposing new federal conditions on state-run elections without congressional authorization. State officials also contend the late changes would force expensive redesigns of ballot materials and election procedures only days before some jurisdictions begin mailing ballots.

U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani vacated her earlier injunction after the Supreme Court determined the previous legal challenge was premature because the regulations had not yet been finalized. While lifting the injunction, Talwani stated the order could still prove unconstitutional and scheduled expedited proceedings on the newly filed lawsuit. A hearing on the states' request for a preliminary injunction is expected in early September, with election officials warning that overseas and military ballots are scheduled to begin mailing within days.

Voting rights organizations, including the American Civil Liberties Union and the League of Women Voters, filed related legal challenges seeking to block the finalized rule. The Trump administration argues the new requirements improve election security and standardize mail ballot handling nationwide, while the plaintiffs contend they create unnecessary barriers that could disrupt voting before the November election.
