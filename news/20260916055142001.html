FBI arrests Pennsylvania man accused of preparing ISIS-inspired attack

Agents seize rifle, 190 rounds and about 30 knives

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 16, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Federal authorities say an FBI investigation stopped a 21-year-old Pennsylvania man who had armed himself and was preparing for an alleged ISIS-inspired mass-casualty attack. Jonathan Hunter Kramer, of Valencia, Pennsylvania, was arrested after agents approached him outside a hotel in Cranberry Township, about 25 miles north of Pittsburgh, as he returned with rifle magazines and ammunition.

According to federal court documents, investigators seized a semiautomatic rifle, five rifle magazines — including two that were loaded — a rifle scope and bipod, 190 rounds of rifle ammunition, pepper spray, an entrenchment tool, a prayer rug and approximately 30 knives during searches of Kramer's hotel room and residence. Authorities said Kramer had purchased a disposable phone before acquiring a long black bag and checking into the hotel with cash. He later went to a sporting goods store, where FBI agents observed him at the gun counter.

The Justice Department said Kramer had been known to the FBI since an earlier 2023 investigation. As a juvenile, he was charged and adjudicated delinquent by Pennsylvania authorities for his role in a plot to commit a mass-casualty incident and for possessing child sexual abuse material. After his release from juvenile detention in March 2026, an anonymous tipster alerted the FBI to renewed concerning behavior.

Investigators allege Kramer subsequently used the online identity "Hamza Al Rashid," claimed affiliation with ISIS, offered to share explosive manuals and discussed preparing for a "mission." Federal officials said he had pledged allegiance to ISIS and was making final preparations for an attack, although authorities have not publicly identified a specific intended target.

Kramer is charged with receiving a firearm and ammunition while knowing or having reasonable cause to believe they would be used to commit a felony or federal terrorism offense, including providing or attempting to provide material support to a designated foreign terrorist organization. He made an initial court appearance on September 14, and prosecutors requested that he be detained without bond. The allegations have not been proven in court, and Kramer is presumed innocent unless convicted.
