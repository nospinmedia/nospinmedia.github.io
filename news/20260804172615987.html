Senate Panel Advances Todd Blanche 12–10

Rescinded **$1.8B** fund wins holdouts; floor vote expected next

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 5, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The Senate Judiciary Committee voted 12–10 along party lines on August 4, 2026, to advance acting attorney general Todd Blanche’s nomination to lead the Department of Justice, clearing a key hurdle toward a full Senate vote. The move followed Blanche’s written order on August 2, 2026, rescinding a proposed $1.8 billion “Anti‑Weaponization Fund,” a concession that unlocked support from Republican holdouts.

Blanche’s directive came after weeks of negotiations and addressed a settlement tied to the IRS leak of presidential tax records. According to the written terms described to senators, audit‑immunity provisions for President Trump, his sons, and the Trump Organization remain in place but apply retroactively to prior tax years and not to future filings. Committee Democrats uniformly opposed advancing the nomination, arguing Blanche’s past service as the president’s personal attorney raises unresolved questions about prosecutorial independence and whether the order truly prevents payouts to political allies. Republicans Sen. John Cornyn and Sen. Thom Tillis backed the nomination after the fund’s formal rescission, saying the change met their conditions.

If confirmed, Blanche would assume permanent leadership of an agency now overseeing high‑profile investigations and policy shifts initiated since Pam Bondi’s removal in April 2026. Senate leaders have signaled they intend to bring the nomination to the floor before the August recess, though Democrats maintain that keeping audit immunity for the president and his businesses underscores ongoing conflicts. Separately, critics note that existing federal claims processes still allow individuals who believe they were wronged by the government—including some tied to January 6 cases—to seek compensation; Democrats say Blanche’s order does not foreclose that avenue.

Key facts: - Vote: 12–10 (party line) on August 4, 2026 - Trigger for deal: Rescission of the $1.8 billion Anti‑Weaponization Fund on August 2, 2026 - GOP holdouts flipped: Sen. John Cornyn (TX), Sen. Thom Tillis (NC) - Audit immunity: Remains for President Trump, his sons, and the Trump Organization for prior tax years; future returns not covered
