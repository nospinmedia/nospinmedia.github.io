U.S. gas reaches record $4.15 on Labor Day

Hormuz turmoil and tight fuel supplies keep pump prices elevated

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 7, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

American drivers are paying a record price for gasoline this Labor Day, with AAA reporting a national average of $4.1505 per gallon on September 7. The previous Labor Day record was $3.82 per gallon, set on September 3, 2012, and AAA says the national average had never previously exceeded $4 on the holiday. The current price is also substantially higher than the roughly $3.16 motorists were paying around Labor Day last year.

The increase comes as instability surrounding the Strait of Hormuz continues to keep crude oil costs elevated. AAA said volatility around the waterway pushed crude prices into the $90-per-barrel range heading into the holiday weekend. The usual late-summer decline in gasoline demand has therefore provided less relief than drivers typically see as the peak vacation season ends.

Refining economics are adding pressure as well. The U.S. Energy Information Administration reported that gasoline refining margins, commonly measured through crack spreads, have remained unusually high because of tight global gasoline supplies. Since May, the New York Harbor gasoline crack spread has averaged around $1 per gallon, well above the approximately 60-cent-per-gallon peak seen during 2025. Higher crude costs combined with elevated refining margins have contributed directly to higher retail prices.

Diesel prices have climbed even more sharply. The national average reached a record $5.85 per gallon ahead of Labor Day, surpassing the previous high of $5.81 set in June 2022. Fuel prices could normally begin easing after the holiday as driving demand falls and refiners transition toward less-expensive winter gasoline, but continued disruption in global oil markets could limit that seasonal decline.
