Inflation remains a major concern one week before midterms

Prices have risen faster than wages over the past year

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 27, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Persistent inflation and rising household costs are shaping the final week of the 2026 midterm elections, with Republicans defending their economic record while Democrats focus campaigns on affordability and purchasing power.

Annual inflation stood at 3.7% in September, compared with about 3% when President Trump took office in January 2025. Consumer prices have also risen faster than wages over the past 12 months, reducing purchasing power even as other measures of the economy remain relatively strong.

Energy costs have become particularly visible. Filling a basic Ford F-150 now costs roughly $100, about $30 more than a year ago, according to an Associated Press analysis. Average gasoline prices reached approximately $4.32 per gallon in mid-October, nearly $1.30 higher than a year earlier. Housing affordability has also been pressured by mortgage rates above 7%.

The economic picture is mixed. The stock market has reached record levels, median incomes have increased and unemployment remains slightly above 4%. At the same time, job creation has slowed and consumers continue reporting pressure from groceries, fuel, housing and other everyday expenses.

Trump has emphasized those stronger economic indicators and says the country is experiencing a ‘golden age.’ He has argued that energy and interest-rate pressures will ease after the war with Iran ends. Republican candidates are also emphasizing economic growth while portraying Democratic policies as likely to increase costs.

Democratic candidates are focusing heavily on affordability, including higher fuel and grocery prices, and arguing that administration policies have contributed to household financial pressure. Trump's tariffs and higher energy costs associated with the Iran war are among the factors economists have identified as contributing to recent price increases.

The competing messages leave voters evaluating an economy with continued growth alongside higher inflation and significant increases in several highly visible household expenses.
