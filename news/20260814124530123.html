Record Deluge Near Tokyo Kills 4

JMA Issues Highest Rainfall Alert As Thousands Stranded

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 10:32 AM ET

Record-breaking rainfall across eastern Japan prompted the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) to issue its highest-level Level 5 heavy rain emergency warning for parts of Chiba Prefecture, killing at least four people and disrupting transportation during the busy Bon holiday travel period. Flooding stranded thousands of travelers at Narita International Airport, while rail service to and from the airport was suspended overnight.

Authorities said four people were found dead in flooded areas of Ichikawa, Sakura, and Kashiwa, while at least one person remained missing as rescue crews continued search operations. The Ground Self-Defense Force (GSDF) transported approximately 4,000 stranded passengers from Soga Station after widespread rail disruptions left commuters unable to continue their journeys. TEPCO reported power outages affecting about 23,000 households Friday morning, down from a peak of roughly 68,000 earlier in the storm.

The JMA reported record hourly rainfall totals, including 11.5 centimeters (4.5 inches) in Chiba City and 9.7 centimeters (3.8 inches) in Sakura. Some parts of Chiba Prefecture received as much as 360 millimeters (14 inches) of rain within 24 hours, overwhelming drainage systems, flooding homes and roads, and triggering landslide concerns. Nearly 230,000 residents were advised to evacuate or seek safer locations as emergency crews responded across the region.

By Friday morning, the JMA had downgraded its highest-level emergency warning as the heaviest rainfall shifted away from the region, but flood and landslide advisories remained in effect because of saturated ground conditions. Authorities warned that transportation disruptions, power restoration, and recovery efforts could continue even after the rain subsides.
