Alibaba raises record $10.2B in Hong Kong share sale

AI funding plan sends stock sharply lower

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 24, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Alibaba Group raised HK$80 billion ($10.2 billion) through a record primary share offering in Hong Kong, marking the largest follow-on equity sale ever completed by a Hong Kong-listed company. The company issued 710 million new shares at HK$112.70 each, an 8.4% discount to its previous Hong Kong closing price, prompting investors to sell the stock over concerns about dilution and the cost of its expanding artificial intelligence strategy.

Alibaba's Hong Kong-listed shares fell as much as 10% before trimming losses during Monday trading. Despite the market reaction, the offering was reported to be nearly three times oversubscribed, attracting roughly $28 billion in orders from institutional investors, including sovereign wealth funds and long-only investment firms. The company said all net proceeds will be invested in its "full-stack" AI strategy, including advanced chips, computing infrastructure and development of its Qwen large language models.

The fundraising follows Alibaba's recent quarterly earnings, which showed capital expenditures increased 75% year over year as the company accelerated investment in AI infrastructure. Those spending increases contributed to a 75% decline in quarterly net profit, although executives said stronger demand for AI services has shortened the expected payback period on those investments. Alibaba has also continued expanding its cloud footprint, including the recent opening of a third data center in South Korea, as competition intensifies among global AI providers.

The transaction ranks as the third-largest follow-on share sale globally in 2026, behind offerings by Alphabet and Intel. While analysts said the strong investor demand demonstrates confidence in Alibaba's long-term AI ambitions, the steep discount and immediate share-price decline highlight continued concerns over shareholder dilution and whether massive AI spending will generate sufficient returns in an increasingly competitive technology market.
