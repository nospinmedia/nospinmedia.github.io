Women Make Up 26% of U.S. AI Hires

C-suite share just 13% at AI companies

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 20, 2026 at 3:02 PM ET

Women captured only 26% of new U.S. artificial intelligence hires in 2025, according to new August 18, 2026 data from LinkedIn Corp.—a striking gap in one of the fastest-growing, best-paid corners of the labor market. Researchers warn that without targeted action, the high-wage trajectory of AI work could entrench unequal access to leadership and long-term earnings for half the workforce.

The analysis spans roughly 15,000 companies across 27 countries and shows steep drop-offs for women at senior levels: just 13% of executive roles in AI are held by women, versus about 19% in non-AI sectors. Overall, women account for 27% of AI roles at AI companies, compared with 31% at non-AI employers. Pay patterns mirror the gap. The increasingly common “member of technical staff” role—sitting between advanced research and full-stack engineering—had a median listed pay of $223,000 this year, and 82% of 2025 hires into that job went to men. By contrast, data-annotation roles, which carry a median of $51,000, are the only tracked AI job to reach gender parity at 50%.

The figures matter because AI jobs command significant salary premiums and are multiplying quickly. LinkedIn’s platform data indicates AI job postings have surged in recent years and that typical AI roles pay more than twice comparable non-AI positions, concentrating opportunity where women remain underrepresented. As AI systems are embedded in products, finance, healthcare, and public services, leadership diversity can influence how those technologies are designed, tested, and governed.

LinkedIn said it derived the findings from member profiles and posted job listings, providing a snapshot of hiring and compensation trends across the rapidly evolving AI workforce. Sarah Steinberg, LinkedIn’s head of global public policy partnerships, said expanding pathways into technical and leadership roles will be important if companies want broader representation as AI employment continues to grow.
