Federal Probe Examines Rep. Ilhan Omar's Decades-Old Immigration Records

Investigators Explore Whether Evidence Could Support Denaturalization or Deportation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 16, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Federal investigators are examining decades-old immigration records involving Democratic Rep. Ilhan Omar of Minnesota, including documents connected to her family's arrival in the United States and her later citizenship, as authorities investigate whether any information submitted to the government was false.

The review is part of a broader criminal investigation that has already included the seizure of Omar's cellphone and subpoenas issued to relatives and other people familiar with her family's immigration history. Investigators are examining records dating to the 1990s, when Omar came to the United States as a refugee from Somalia with members of her family.

According to people familiar with the investigation cited by Reuters, authorities are looking at whether discrepancies or false statements in immigration paperwork could provide grounds for a future denaturalization case. Such a proceeding would require the government to establish legally sufficient grounds to revoke Omar's U.S. citizenship. No such case has been filed, and investigators have not publicly announced evidence establishing immigration fraud.

Omar became a U.S. citizen in 2000 and was first elected to Congress in 2018. She has denied wrongdoing and said allegations questioning her immigration history and citizenship are false. Her attorneys and supporters have also challenged the investigation's timing and accused the Trump administration of targeting a political opponent. The administration and federal investigators have defended the inquiry as a legitimate law-enforcement matter.

The investigation has intensified in recent days. Federal agents searched Omar's Washington-area residence, seized electronic evidence and obtained grand jury subpoenas as prosecutors sought additional information about her immigration history. The latest reporting provides new detail about the government's objective: determining whether the evidence could support action against Omar's citizenship and, ultimately, possible removal from the United States.

The investigation remains ongoing. Omar has not been charged with a crime, and the existence of an investigation does not establish that fraud occurred or that she could legally be denaturalized or deported.
