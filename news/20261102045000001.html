Democrats hold midterm advantage as voters seek change

Trump opposition and economic concerns shape final days

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 2, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Democrats enter the final days before the 2026 midterm elections with an advantage in national congressional polling, as surveys show dissatisfaction with President Trump, economic concerns and a desire for political change are motivating many voters.

Final NBC News polling found President Trump's job approval at 40% and disapproval at 60%, with opposition to the president playing a significant role in voters' decisions about Congress. The findings come as both parties make their closing arguments before Election Day and control of the House and Senate remains at stake.

Separate national polling from NPR/PBS News/Marist, conducted October 19-20, found Democratic congressional candidates leading Republicans 52% to 44% among likely voters. That survey carried a margin of error of 3.8 percentage points. Among likely voters, 40% approved of President Trump's job performance and 59% disapproved.

The Marist survey also found that a plurality of likely voters described their congressional vote as primarily a vote against President Trump. Democrats and independents were more likely than Republicans to say they wanted change, while enthusiasm remained high across both major parties. Overall, 84% of likely voters said they were enthusiastic or very enthusiastic about voting.

Economic concerns are also prominent. Recent Fox News polling found widespread dissatisfaction with economic conditions, with majorities describing gasoline, grocery, housing and health care costs as serious problems. Fox News interviews with voters around the country found motivations extending beyond the economy to immigration enforcement, abortion, gun rights, foreign policy and concerns about the direction of the country.

National generic-ballot polling measures overall voter sentiment rather than individual congressional districts, meaning it cannot by itself determine which party will control Congress after Tuesday's elections.
