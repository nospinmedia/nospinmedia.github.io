U.S. and Iran hold mediated talks in New York

Negotiations focus on ending war and reopening Strait of Hormuz

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 23, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The United States and Iran held hours of mediated talks Tuesday on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly in New York, marking a new diplomatic step after weeks of escalating military threats. U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff said mediators shuttled between American and Iranian delegations throughout the day, while Iranian state media confirmed contact between Witkoff and Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. Accounts differed over how much of the discussion occurred directly between the two sides.

President Donald Trump said Witkoff and Jared Kushner participated in a roughly three-hour meeting with Iranian officials and described the discussions as "very good" and "productive." Witkoff said mediators had completed a round of discussions that he hoped would prove constructive and that mediation would continue. No agreement or cease-fire was announced, and reporting from New York indicated there had been no breakthrough in the talks.

A central issue is the Strait of Hormuz, the strategically important shipping route whose disruption during the war has affected global energy markets. Iran has tied reopening the waterway to several demands, including easing U.S. military pressure, lifting the naval blockade of Iranian ports and releasing frozen Iranian assets. A senior Iranian official told Reuters before Tuesday's discussions that Tehran could reopen the strait within seven days if the United States eased military pressure and lifted the blockade. Iran said it had submitted its latest proposal to Washington through mediators on September 16.

Regional governments are playing a major role in the diplomatic effort. Qatar says it is working with Pakistan to reduce tensions and facilitate dialogue between Washington and Tehran, while Oman has also participated in diplomacy concerning maritime access through Hormuz. Qatar's prime minister and foreign minister met Araghchi in New York on Tuesday as regional governments pressed both sides toward de-escalation.

The diplomacy is unfolding alongside continued threats from both governments. Hours before the talks, Trump warned at the U.N. that the alternative to an agreement could be substantially greater military action against Iran. Iranian military officials responded by warning that additional U.S. attacks would bring more severe retaliation. The discussions therefore represent renewed diplomatic contact, but the two countries remain far apart and further talks are expected.
