Chinese Humanoid Robot Runs 100 Meters in 9.39 Seconds

Beijing Games Highlight Rapid Advances in Robotics

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 23, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

A humanoid robot developed by the Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center completed the 100-meter dash in 9.39 seconds during the opening of the World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing, a time faster than Usain Bolt's human world record of 9.58 seconds set in 2009. The robot, known as Tiangong Ultra, showcased advances in robotic balance, locomotion, and actuator technology. After crossing the finish line, the machine was unable to stop on its own and came to rest against protective crash cushions, underscoring that the demonstration highlighted engineering progress rather than human-like athletic performance.

The sprint was one of several headline events during the opening competition, which brought together more than 2,000 humanoid robots across 51 events at Beijing's National Speed Skating Oval. Another humanoid robot, Lightning, developed by Honor, recorded an official 9.47-second run during the preliminary heats after earlier demonstration trials had shown faster unofficial performances. Other robots competed in events including obstacle courses, high jumping, football, and coordinated movement challenges designed to test speed, balance, and physical artificial intelligence.

The games are being held alongside the 2026 World Robot Conference, where Chinese companies and research institutes are showcasing rapid advances in humanoid robotics. Although several robots stumbled, overheated, or crashed into safety barriers after completing events, researchers said the demonstrations illustrate significant improvements in mobility, dynamic balance, and autonomous control. Developers say technologies refined through these competitions could eventually support applications in manufacturing, logistics, emergency response, healthcare, and hazardous environments.

The event also reflects intensifying international competition in advanced robotics as governments invest heavily in artificial intelligence and automation. China has identified humanoid robotics as a strategic technology sector, while several Western governments continue to scrutinize Chinese robotics companies over national security concerns. Analysts say the Beijing demonstrations show that robot mobility is advancing rapidly, even as engineers continue working to improve reliability, endurance, and safe operation outside controlled environments.
