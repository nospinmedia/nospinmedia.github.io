Trump launches $25 million fraud whistleblower program

Tips leading to convictions could earn rewards of up to $30,000

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 21, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

President Trump and Vice President JD Vance have announced a $25 million program offering cash rewards to people who report suspected fraud involving federal programs and taxpayer money.

Under the Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program, or WHIP, people can submit tips through the federal government's fraud reporting website. The White House said tips that lead to a fraud conviction or significant federal enforcement action can qualify for rewards of up to $30,000. The Justice Department will administer the program, while the Treasury Department has committed $25 million to fund it.

During the announcement, Trump said successful whistleblowers could receive a 1% commission based on the amount of fraud uncovered. The Justice Department, however, described awards as ranging from $10,000 to $30,000, depending on the case. Trump also said he would resolve disputes when multiple people claim responsibility for providing the same information.

The administration presented the program's first $10,000 award to Nick Shirley, a conservative YouTuber whose videos alleged fraud involving Minnesota childcare centers receiving government funding. State officials disputed some of Shirley's broader allegations, but federal investigations followed. One woman featured in his reporting, Fahima Mahamud, pleaded guilty in July to wire fraud and conspiracy involving inflated reimbursement claims for meals allegedly provided to children.

The program is part of a broader federal anti-fraud effort. Trump created a government fraud task force in March 2026 and placed Vance in charge of the initiative. Administration officials say the new rewards are intended to encourage members of the public to provide information that can help investigators identify misuse of federal funds.
