Stocks suffer worst losing streak since 2023 as investors retreat

S&P 500 slides for sixth day as AI concerns and rate fears hit markets

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 20, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

U.S. stocks fell sharply Thursday, November 19, extending a global retreat as investors pulled back from technology and other riskier assets. The S&P 500 fell 2.0%, or 147.80 points, to 7,175.83, its sixth consecutive decline and longest losing streak since April 2024, leaving the index 8.2% below its October record high.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 1,101 points, or 2.2%, to 49,097.06, while the technology-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell 2.3% to 23,879.20 and is now 13.5% below its record high, placing it deeper into correction territory. Globally, the MSCI All Country World Index recorded a seventh consecutive decline, its longest losing streak since September 2023.

The selling reflected several concerns hitting markets simultaneously. Investors have increasingly questioned whether valuations of artificial intelligence companies and the enormous spending required for new data centers have moved too far ahead of expected profits. Markets were also digesting the sudden resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and the possibility that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates again. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 5.04% to 5.05% as investors moved money toward government bonds.

Anthropic initially stood out against the broader decline. The Claude maker priced its record-setting IPO at $83 per share, climbed as high as $115 during Thursday trading and finished its first session roughly 15% to 17% above its IPO price. Shares then fell about 5% to 7% in early Friday trading as the wider market weakness reached the newly public company.

That contrast does not conflict with Anthropic's historic IPO or the wealth it created for employees. Individual stocks can rise sharply even while major indexes fall, and Anthropic's first-day performance showed investors were still willing to pay heavily for selected AI companies despite growing caution toward the broader sector.

The market decline has direct implications for retirement accounts, 401(k)s and investment portfolios if losses persist, making the duration and causes of the selloff important beyond Wall Street.
