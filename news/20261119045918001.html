House Democrat orders Trump allies to preserve records

Incoming oversight chair prepares investigations into finances, DHS and Epstein files

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 19, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The Democrat expected to lead the House Oversight Committee next year has sent 12 preservation letters covering 13 recipients as the party prepares investigations into Trump family business dealings, alleged government misconduct and the administration's handling of records related to Jeffrey Epstein.

Rep. Robert Garcia of California, who is expected to become committee chair when Democrats take control of the House in January, sent the notices Wednesday to President Trump's family members and current and former administration officials. Preservation notices require recipients to retain potentially relevant records but do not establish wrongdoing.

Recipients include Donald Trump Jr., Eric Trump, Jared Kushner, Vice President JD Vance, Stephen Miller, Susie Wiles, Attorney General Todd Blanche, Kristi Noem, Corey Lewandowski and Kimberly Guilfoyle. One letter was jointly addressed to Federal Bureau of Prisons Director William Marshall III and Tanisha Hall, warden of the federal prison camp in Bryan, Texas, accounting for the difference between reports of 12 letters and 13 recipients.

Garcia's planned investigations span several areas. His letters seek records involving the Trump family's World Liberty Financial cryptocurrency interests and Kushner's private investments. Notices to Noem and Lewandowski concern alleged misuse of funds and contracting involving the Department of Homeland Security, while Guilfoyle's concerns allegations involving access to administration officials.

Other requests concern the administration's handling of Epstein-related records and the transfer of Ghislaine Maxwell to the minimum-security Bryan facility. Garcia is also seeking records about financing for the White House ballroom, whose broader East Wing project has been estimated at more than $600 million, including taxpayer funding and private donations. Democrats have specifically raised questions about Nvidia's multimillion-dollar contribution and subsequent administration decisions involving advanced chip sales to China.

The administration disputes Garcia's allegations and the legitimacy of some planned inquiries. White House counsel Will Scharf warned that investigations could cross into criminal conduct, while U.S. Attorney Aaron Reitz has issued his own preservation notices to several congressional Democrats over their planned investigations. Garcia says Congress has broad constitutional oversight authority.
