Skydiving plane crashes in Missouri

12 killed in mass casualty disaster

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished June 15, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

A skydiving aircraft crashed near Butler Memorial Airport in Missouri, killing at least 12 people in one of the deadliest U.S. aviation incidents in recent months. Authorities confirmed that those on board included a group of skydivers and the pilot, with no survivors reported.

The crash occurred shortly after takeoff on June 14, 2026, with witnesses describing the aircraft descending rapidly before impact and becoming engulfed in flames. The plane, identified as a Pacific Aerospace P750XL, was operating as part of a commercial skydiving operation when it went down in a rural area adjacent to the airport.

Emergency crews responded quickly, securing a wide debris field and beginning recovery efforts. Officials confirmed that the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) have launched a full investigation into the cause of the crash.

Early findings have not identified a definitive cause, and investigators are expected to examine maintenance records, flight data, pilot history, and environmental conditions. Authorities cautioned that determining the cause may take weeks as evidence is analyzed.

Skydiving operations typically involve repeated climbs and descents, placing unique operational demands on aircraft. While incidents are rare, they can result in high fatality counts due to the number of occupants during group flights.

Officials are continuing victim identification and notifying families as the investigation remains active.
