Lockerbie Bombing Trial Delayed Over New Evidence

Judge postpones landmark U.S. case days before jury selection

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 25, 2026 at 10:33 AM ET

A federal judge has postponed the long-awaited U.S. criminal trial of Abu Agila Mohammad Mas'ud Kheir Al-Marimi, the former Libyan intelligence officer accused of helping build the bomb that destroyed Pan Am Flight 103 over Lockerbie, Scotland, in 1988. Jury selection had been scheduled to begin on August 26, but U.S. District Judge Dabney Friedrich delayed the proceedings after prosecutors disclosed new evidence to the defense only days before trial.

In a written order, Judge Friedrich said the newly disclosed material, the age and complexity of the case and evidence located overseas justified postponing the trial to ensure both sides have adequate time to prepare. Defense attorney Laura Koenig told the court the late disclosure created ethical and constitutional obligations requiring additional review before the case could proceed. The judge scheduled a September 1 status conference to determine a revised trial schedule.

The case centers on the December 21, 1988, bombing of Pan Am Flight 103, which exploded over Lockerbie 38 minutes after departing London Heathrow Airport for New York City. The attack killed 270 people, including 259 passengers and crew and 11 people on the ground. Among the victims were 190 Americans, including 35 Syracuse University students returning home for the holidays. The bombing remains the deadliest terrorist attack ever carried out on British soil.

Mas'ud was taken into U.S. custody in December 2022 after being transferred from Libya and is charged with destruction of an aircraft resulting in death. If convicted, he faces a potential sentence of life in prison. The case marks the first time allegations related to the Lockerbie bombing have been tried in a U.S. federal court. Previously, former Libyan intelligence officer Abdelbaset al-Megrahi was convicted by a Scottish court in 2001 for his role in the attack.
