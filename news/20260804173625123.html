Trump Tells Exxon and Chevron to Cut Gas Prices Now

Oval Office remarks cite “too much money” as U.S. average hits $4.09

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

President Trump on August 3, 2026 publicly demanded that major U.S. oil companies lower retail gasoline prices “immediately,” saying they had made “too much money” during the recent price run-up. Speaking in the Oval Office and reiterating the message on Truth Social, he singled out ExxonMobil and Chevron while predicting fuel costs would fall sharply once the Iran conflict ends. The national average price for regular gasoline stood at $4.09 per gallon on August 4, 2026, according to AAA.

Trump sharpened his criticism of Chevron chief executive Mike Wirth, urging companies to “cut the retail price, the consumer price,” and adding, “I’m not happy about it.” He argued that consumers should be seeing quicker relief at the pump following a recent dip in crude prices.

Recent earnings underscore the political pressure. In the April–June quarter, ExxonMobil reported net income of about $14.53 billion, while Chevron posted roughly $12.07 billion. Those results coincided with months of constrained global supply and elevated refining margins before a pullback in benchmark crude on Monday. While the White House pressure is direct, industry analysts note that price movements at the pump often lag crude because retailers work through existing inventories and adjust to wholesale costs and local taxes.

One constraint on immediate pump-price control: major oil companies directly own fewer than 5% of U.S. gas stations; most locations are independently operated and set prices based on local competition and wholesale supply dynamics. In a related step earlier this summer, Trump said the Department of Justice would look into potential price gouging at the pump on June 24, 2026.
