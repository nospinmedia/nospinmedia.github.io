Tom Homan Says Ilhan Omar Could Be Denaturalized if Immigration Fraud Is Proven

Border Czar Says He Has Been Told Investigators Have a Strong Case

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 19, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

White House border czar Tom Homan says Rep. Ilhan Omar could ultimately lose her U.S. citizenship and face deportation if federal investigators prove she committed fraud during the naturalization process, adding a new potential consequence to the investigation surrounding the Minnesota Democrat.

Asked on Fox News whether the investigation could result in Omar being denaturalized and then deported, Homan answered "Of course." He stressed, however, that such an outcome would depend on evidence establishing immigration fraud.

"If you lie during the naturalization process, there is a process to denaturalize somebody," Homan said. He described that process as lengthy and said it has been used previously. Homan also said he had been told investigators "have a strong case," while acknowledging that he did not know the evidence in detail and would not get ahead of the federal investigation.

Federal authorities recently searched Omar's Washington, D.C., residence, seized her cellphone and issued more than a dozen grand jury subpoenas, including subpoenas involving relatives. The investigation is examining allegations concerning immigration paperwork submitted when Omar's family came to the United States as refugees from Somalia in the 1990s. No charges have been announced, and the allegations under investigation have not been proven.

Omar became a U.S. citizen in 2000. She has denied wrongdoing and characterized the federal investigation as politically motivated. Her attorneys have also challenged the legality of the search of her home and demanded the return of seized property.

Former President Barack Obama criticized Homan's remarks Sunday, saying Americans should be concerned about the federal government targeting an elected official. The administration maintains that Omar is subject to the same laws and investigative processes as anyone else.

The latest comments move the controversy beyond whether Omar's decades-old immigration records are being investigated by publicly identifying denaturalization and deportation as possible outcomes if investigators establish fraud.
