Man wounded in 2024 Trump assassination attempt dies at 76

Jim Copenhaver died from complications stemming from his gunshot wounds

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 25, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

James "Jim" Copenhaver, one of two spectators wounded during the July 13, 2024 assassination attempt on President Trump in Butler, Pennsylvania, has died at age 76. Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday said Copenhaver died from complications stemming from the wounds he suffered in the shooting.

Copenhaver was sitting in bleachers near the stage when gunman Thomas Matthew Crooks opened fire from the roof of a nearby building. Copenhaver was struck twice. One bullet hit him in the arm, while another traveled through his abdomen, damaging his colon and leaving him with nerve damage. He underwent treatment before being released from the hospital about a week after the attack.

Former Buffalo Township fire chief Corey Comperatore, 50, was killed during the shooting while shielding his family. Rally attendee David Dutch was also wounded, and Trump was struck in the ear. Crooks, 20, was killed by a Secret Service counter-sniper after firing eight shots toward the rally stage.

Copenhaver's family described him as a devoted husband, father and grandfather and noted his military service. President Trump offered condolences following his death, calling Copenhaver a "true American Patriot" and praising the courage he showed following the shooting.

Copenhaver's death comes while litigation over security failures at the rally remains active. In June 2026, Copenhaver and Dutch filed separate federal lawsuits against the United States, alleging negligence by the U.S. Secret Service caused their life-altering physical and emotional injuries. Investigations after the attack documented major security failures, and the Secret Service has acknowledged that the Butler rally represented an operational failure.

Copenhaver's death more than two years after the shooting adds another lasting consequence to an attack that prompted congressional investigations, independent reviews and changes within the Secret Service.
