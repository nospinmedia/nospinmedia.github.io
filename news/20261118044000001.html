Apple blames major service outage on internal update

Company says disruption was not a cyberattack

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 18, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Apple says an internal update — not a cyberattack — caused the widespread service disruption that knocked multiple Apple services offline Tuesday and generated thousands of outage reports across the United States.

The problems affected services including iCloud, the App Store, Apple Music and AppleCare, while users also reported trouble with Apple Pay. Some people were unexpectedly signed out of their Apple Accounts or encountered problems signing back in, downloading applications and accessing other services. Apple's system-status pages showed multiple disruptions as the problems unfolded.

Outage tracker Downdetector recorded roughly 2,300 U.S. reports involving Apple Pay around 12:20 p.m. Eastern Time, while reports involving iCloud peaked at roughly 1,800. Downdetector figures are based on user reports and do not represent a complete count of everyone affected.

Apple said the problems resulted from an internal update and explicitly said they were not caused by a cyberattack. The company initially said many systems had returned while engineers worked to restore the remaining services. By approximately 5:46 p.m. Eastern Time Tuesday, Apple told CBS News its services were "green across the board," and its public system-status page showed the earlier consumer disruptions resolved.

The incident came during an unusual run of unrelated technology failures affecting major organizations. Recent disruptions have included a Microsoft outage affecting services such as Outlook and Teams, a Federal Aviation Administration network failure that triggered a nationwide ground stop, a United Airlines technology failure and problems originating at JPMorgan Chase that disrupted some banking services. Companies and officials have said those incidents were not linked to cyberattacks.

Apple's explanation makes Tuesday's outage another case in which a software or infrastructure problem, rather than malicious activity, caused a significant disruption to widely used digital services.
