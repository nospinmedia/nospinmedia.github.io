Judge holds DHS in contempt over asylum seeker's deportation

19-year-old was sent to El Salvador twice despite court orders

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 26, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A federal judge held the Department of Homeland Security in contempt on September 25 after the government deported a 19-year-old asylum seeker to El Salvador in violation of multiple court orders. U.S. District Judge Stephanie Gallagher, who was appointed by President Trump, ordered the government to return the man, identified in court records as E.L.A., to the United States.

Court records show E.L.A. was improperly deported to El Salvador twice. After his first removal, he returned to the United States on August 7, submitted himself to immigration custody and remained detained for weeks. He was then deported to El Salvador a second time despite court protections barring his removal. Government attorneys previously acknowledged the second removal was improper and attributed it to a communication error.

Gallagher ordered the government to facilitate E.L.A.'s return, restore his property and documents, and refrain from executing another final removal order until a final determination is made on his asylum claim or the settlement governing his case expires. The government must also provide his attorneys with at least five business days' notice and identify its legal authority before attempting another removal.

The dispute stems from the federal class-action case J.O.P. v. Department of Homeland Security, which began in 2019 and involves people who entered the United States as unaccompanied minors and later sought asylum. According to court records cited by ABC News, at least 107 people potentially covered by the settlement were removed before their asylum claims were decided, including 54 confirmed class members and 53 others the government identified as potentially protected.

This is the second time Gallagher has found the government in contempt in the same litigation over deportations of protected asylum seekers. Both sides must report to the court by October 2 on whether E.L.A. wants to return and, if so, plans for bringing him back.
