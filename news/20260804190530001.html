House Ethics Panel Urges Censure of Rep. Chuck Edwards

25-page report finds rule violations; Democratic Women’s Caucus calls for resignation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 5, 2026 at 5:30 AM ET

The bipartisan House Committee on Ethics on August 3, 2026, recommended that the House censure Rep. Chuck Edwards (R‑N.C.) after a 25‑page report concluded he violated the House Code of Official Conduct by creating a hostile environment and failing to adhere to rules prohibiting sexual harassment and unwanted advances toward staff. The panel said it will bring a privileged resolution to the floor for a vote.

Investigators, led by Chair Michael Guest (R‑Miss.) and Ranking Member Mark DeSaulnier (D‑Calif.), described a persistent pattern of intrusive personal attention toward two female aides. According to the report, both staffers received numerous gifts and special treatment that went well beyond professional norms, including: - Designer purses and jewelry totaling over $1,000 - Personalized handguns - A KitchenAid mixer and a robotic vacuum - A laptop, cellphone, flowers, and shoes - One‑on‑one dinners, tickets to performances, vacations, and casino trips

The committee said it found no evidence that Edwards explicitly propositioned staff or engaged in sexual activity, but concluded his conduct would reasonably be interpreted as unwelcome and inappropriate and that it harmed the aides’ careers. The panel also noted that it—not the full House—cannot impose punishment; a censure requires a simple majority vote of the chamber. With the House out until August 31, 2026, any floor action is expected after lawmakers return.

Reaction split quickly. In a statement to the committee, attorneys for Edwards argued the recommendation is “extraordinarily harsh” and rests on vague standards, asserting he was cleared of direct sexual‑misconduct and quid‑pro‑quo violations. Separately, the Democratic Women’s Caucus, led by Teresa Leger Fernández with vice chairs Emilia Sykes and Hillary Scholten, on August 4, 2026, urged Edwards to resign, saying censure alone carries too few consequences and pointing to the aides’ departures from congressional employment.
