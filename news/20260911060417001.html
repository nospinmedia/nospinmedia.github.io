Houthis seize strategic Yemeni port of Mokha

Advance expands rebel reach toward vital Red Sea shipping chokepoint

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 11, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

Iran-aligned Houthi forces have seized the strategic Yemeni port city of Mokha and made additional gains along the country's western coast, forcing government-aligned troops to retreat from positions near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The offensive represents the Houthis' most significant territorial advance since a 2022 ceasefire sharply reduced large-scale fighting in Yemen's civil war.

Mokha sits roughly 50 miles north of Bab el-Mandeb, the narrow passage connecting the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden and carrying about 12% of global trade under normal conditions. The Houthis already control substantial portions of northern and western Yemen, including the major port of Hodeidah.

The offensive has extended beyond Mokha. Houthi forces have also taken Hays and the strategically important Khalid ibn al-Walid military base, while fighting and government withdrawals have affected additional positions along the Red Sea coast and nearby islands. The gains move Houthi-controlled territory closer to a maritime corridor the group has previously targeted with missiles, drones and attacks on commercial vessels.

Reuters also reported a potentially significant Iranian role in the offensive. Iranian, Yemeni and regional sources told the news organization that Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps provided weapons, funding and tactical guidance to the Houthis. Those sources said Abdolreza Shahlaei, a senior commander in the IRGC's Quds Force, played a leading role in directing the operation. Iran denies directing Houthi military operations, meaning the reported command role remains an attributed claim rather than an independently established fact.

Tareq Saleh, vice chairman of Yemen's internationally recognized Presidential Leadership Council and commander of the National Resistance Forces, acknowledged deaths and injuries among government-aligned units and ordered his forces to regroup after the loss of Mokha.

The territorial gains do not mean the Houthis control the entire Bab el-Mandeb Strait, and commercial vessels continue to transit the waterway. But the combination of new coastal territory and reported Iranian assistance raises the stakes around a shipping route already affected by years of regional conflict.
