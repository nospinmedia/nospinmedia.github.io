U.S. Removes Syria From Terrorism Sponsor List

47-year designation ends after congressional review

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 25, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The United States has formally removed Syria from its list of State Sponsors of Terrorism, ending a designation that had been in place since 1979. The action took effect on August 24 after the required 45-day congressional review period expired without blocking the decision. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the move recognizes commitments made by the Syrian government under President Ahmed al-Sharaa to distance the country from international terrorism and is intended to support Syria's political and economic recovery.

At the same time, the State Department revoked the Specially Designated Global Terrorist designation for Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS), formerly known as the al-Nusrah Front, while the Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) removed the group from its Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) sanctions list. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the coordinated actions are designed to remove major legal barriers that have limited investment and financial activity in Syria while maintaining targeted sanctions on individuals and entities that continue to pose security concerns.

The removal of Syria's terrorism designation lifts restrictions affecting U.S. foreign assistance, defense exports and certain financial transactions that automatically applied to countries on the terrorism sponsor list. Administration officials said the decision follows a broader sanctions relief effort announced earlier this year after President Trump notified Congress of his intent to rescind the designation. The administration said additional sanctions unrelated to the terrorism designation remain in place, meaning businesses and financial institutions must still comply with other U.S. restrictions.

Syrian officials welcomed the decision, saying it removes a major obstacle to reconnecting the country's banking system with international markets and attracting foreign investment needed for reconstruction after years of conflict. Following Syria's removal, Cuba, Iran and North Korea remain the only countries designated by the United States as State Sponsors of Terrorism.
