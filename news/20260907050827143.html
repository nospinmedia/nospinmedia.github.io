Amazon cargo jet overruns Miami runway, killing 5

Investigators examine why Boeing 767 failed to stop

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 7, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Federal investigators are examining why an Amazon Air cargo jet overran a runway at Miami International Airport on September 6, crossed a roadway and struck multiple vehicles, killing at least five people and injuring five others. Three of the injured were reported in critical condition. The National Transportation Safety Board is leading the investigation into the crash, which temporarily shut down one of the nation's busiest airports.

The aircraft was 21 Air Flight 7598, a Boeing 767-300 freighter operating for Amazon and arriving from San Juan, Puerto Rico. The jet landed at about 2 p.m. before continuing beyond the end of the runway, crossing a busy road and colliding with vehicles before catching fire. The pilot and copilot became trapped in the cockpit and were rescued, while emergency crews also extricated people from vehicles. More than 60 Miami-Dade Fire Rescue units responded as firefighters battled the blaze and dealt with leaking fuel.

Investigators are now examining several factors, including where on the runway the aircraft touched down and whether wind conditions contributed to the overrun. The NTSB has not determined a cause. The 32-year-old aircraft landed on Runway 30, which is more than 9,000 feet long. Flight-tracking data indicated it was traveling about 112 knots, or 129 mph, when it landed. Investigators are also expected to examine the aircraft, crew actions, weather and airport conditions as part of the inquiry.

The crash caused major disruption at Miami International, with more than 160 flights canceled and about 325 delayed before operations resumed. Amazon said it was supporting those affected and cooperating with authorities, while Boeing and aircraft operator 21 Air also said they would assist investigators. The accident is the second fatal crash involving an Amazon Prime Air Boeing 767-300 freighter since 2019.
