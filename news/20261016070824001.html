Elon Musk Gives $158 Million to Senate Republican Super PAC

Group Is Spending Heavily to Support Ken Paxton in Texas Senate Race

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 16, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Elon Musk contributed $158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund, a super PAC working to elect Republican senators, during the third quarter of 2026, according to newly released federal campaign finance filings. The contributions come as the group spends heavily to support Republican Ken Paxton in the closely watched U.S. Senate race in Texas.

Federal filings show Musk gave the Senate Leadership Fund $75 million on September 9 and another $83 million on September 23. His contributions accounted for more than half of the approximately $273 million the organization reported raising during the quarter.

The Senate Leadership Fund is the leading super PAC aligned with Senate Republicans and can raise and spend unlimited amounts of money independently of candidates' official campaigns. The group has directed substantial resources toward Texas, where Paxton is running against Democrat James Talarico in the November 3 midterm election.

Musk's latest contributions add to his already extensive political spending during the 2026 election cycle. He has also continued financing America PAC, the political action committee he established in 2024. Campaign finance filings show Musk contributed more than $80 million to America PAC during the third quarter as it funded voter outreach and independent political activity in congressional races.

The newly disclosed contributions provide additional detail about Musk's political spending previously reported this election cycle. Earlier campaign finance records showed hundreds of millions of dollars flowing from Musk into Republican-aligned political organizations and his own PAC. The $158 million disclosed for the Senate Leadership Fund represents a specific portion of that broader spending rather than an entirely separate political effort.

Super PACs are prohibited from coordinating their independent expenditures directly with candidates or their campaigns, but they can spend unlimited amounts advocating for or against candidates.
