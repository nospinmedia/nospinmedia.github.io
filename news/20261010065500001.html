Trump Forms Committee to Investigate Federal Reserve Governor

Lisa Cook Faces November Hearing as President Renews Effort to Remove Her

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 10, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

President Trump has established a formal White House committee to investigate Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, renewing his effort to remove her from the central bank over allegations involving mortgage applications.

The committee was created through a presidential memorandum signed on October 7, 2026, and publicly reported on Friday, October 9. Its purpose is to determine whether allegations that Cook made false statements on mortgage documents constitute sufficient legal grounds for her removal from the Federal Reserve Board of Governors.

The investigation represents another development in a legal confrontation over presidential authority and the independence of the Federal Reserve, which makes decisions affecting interest rates, inflation, employment, and the broader economy.

Under federal law, Federal Reserve governors can be removed by the president only for cause. Unlike many executive branch officials, they are protected from dismissal solely because of disagreements with the president's economic policies.

Trump previously attempted to remove Cook over allegations that she improperly identified more than one property as her primary residence when applying for mortgages. Cook has denied wrongdoing, and the allegations have not resulted in criminal charges.

The U.S. Supreme Court ruled earlier in 2026 that Cook could remain in her position while the legal dispute continued, reinforcing the statutory protections governing the removal of Federal Reserve officials.

The newly established committee consists of three senior administration officials: the Assistant to the President for Economic Policy, the Chair of the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, and the Director of the Office of Government Ethics.

According to the White House memorandum, the committee will hold a closed-door hearing on November 5, 2026, where Cook will have an opportunity to respond to the allegations, present evidence, and receive assistance from legal counsel.

The hearing will be transcribed, although it will not be open to the public. Cook may submit additional written arguments through November 10, after which the committee will prepare findings and recommend whether grounds exist for her removal.

Cook's attorney, Abbe Lowell, has rejected the allegations and challenged the administration's attempts to remove her. Her legal team maintains that the president cannot dismiss a Federal Reserve governor without satisfying the legal standard established by Congress.

The administration argues that the investigation is necessary to determine whether Cook's alleged conduct demonstrates sufficient cause for removal. Critics question whether the process threatens the central bank's independence by allowing the White House to exert pressure on officials responsible for monetary policy.

The committee's findings will not automatically remove Cook. Any subsequent presidential action could face additional legal challenges.
