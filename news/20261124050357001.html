Virginia and Oregon sue Musk companies over Grok sexualized images

States allege AI safeguards failed as millions of explicit images were generated

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 24, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

The attorneys general of Virginia and Oregon have filed separate consumer-protection lawsuits against companies controlled by Elon Musk, alleging the Grok artificial-intelligence system generated large numbers of nonconsensual sexualized images, including material depicting children, while the companies misrepresented the effectiveness of their safety protections.

Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones filed his case in Richmond City Circuit Court, while Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court. The lawsuits target SpaceX, X and SpaceX Super Intelligence, formerly known as xAI, which operates Grok.

Virginia's lawsuit invokes the Virginia Consumer Protection Act, while Oregon alleges violations of its Unlawful Trade Practices Act. Both states contend the companies made misleading representations about Grok's safeguards while its image-generation tools were being used to create nonconsensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material.

The complaints cite research by the Center for Countering Digital Hate estimating Grok generated approximately 3 million sexualized images during a period spanning late December 2025 and early January 2026, including an estimated 23,000 images appearing to depict children. Those figures are estimates from outside research cited by the states rather than independently established company totals.

Virginia's complaint also cites data reported to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. According to the complaint, xAI and X Corp. submitted an average of 192,070 CyberTipline reports per month from January through June 2026, compared with an average of 115,736 per month before Grok Imagine launched on X — an increase of approximately 66%.

The states are seeking court orders and financial remedies. Virginia seeks injunctions, consumer restitution, disgorgement of allegedly improperly obtained profits and civil penalties of up to $2,500 per willful violation if the alleged violations are established. Oregon is similarly seeking penalties and other remedies under state law.

The companies have said sexual content involving minors and nonconsensual explicit imagery of real people is prohibited and abhorrent. They maintain that abusive users deliberately attempted to circumvent safeguards and say protections have been strengthened, offending accounts have been acted against and law enforcement has been notified where appropriate.

The lawsuits mark the first civil enforcement actions by U.S. states seeking to hold the companies responsible under state consumer-protection laws for the alleged safety representations and Grok-generated sexualized imagery. The allegations have not yet been adjudicated, and the courts will determine whether the companies violated either state's law.
