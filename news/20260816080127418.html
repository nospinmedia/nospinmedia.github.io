Indiana Floods Kill 5 As White River Reaches Record Levels

FEMA aid approved as rescues and evacuations continue

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 16, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

At least five people have died following days of severe storms and historic flooding across Indiana, prompting President Donald Trump to approve a federal emergency declaration on August 15, 2026. The declaration authorizes FEMA to assist state and local agencies as emergency crews continue water rescues, evacuations and recovery operations across central and eastern Indiana.

The National Weather Service reported that more than 11 inches of rain fell over parts of the state in two days, causing the White River to rise above previous record crests at several monitoring locations, including Anderson and Noblesville. Floodwaters inundated neighborhoods from Hamilton County into northern Indianapolis, where Mayor Joe Hogsett declared a local disaster emergency.

The Indianapolis Fire Department said crews completed at least 95 water rescues and rescued 45 pets, while officials reported approximately 400 voluntary evacuations in northern Indianapolis and more than 350 evacuations in Delaware County. Authorities said the victims included a 58-year-old driver whose vehicle entered floodwaters, an 18-year-old recovered from the Mississinewa River, and a 4-year-old boy killed when a tree struck a home in Jennings County.

More than 150,000 electric customers were without power Saturday in parts of northern Indiana, and numerous roads and bridges remained closed as floodwaters continued moving downstream. Emergency managers warned that saturated ground and swollen rivers could prolong flooding even after rainfall ends, increasing the risk of additional road closures and delaying recovery efforts.

Federal resources are now supporting local response operations while damage assessments continue across affected communities. Officials urged residents to avoid driving through flooded roadways, follow evacuation orders where issued and remain alert as rivers continue to fluctuate during the recovery phase.
