Putin praises Musk as Russia builds its own Starlink rival

Moscow says more than 30 satellites are already in orbit

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Russian President Vladimir Putin praised Elon Musk as a “genius” while acknowledging that Russia remains far behind SpaceX's Starlink as Moscow develops its own low-Earth-orbit satellite internet network.

Putin made the comments on October 28 while attending an exhibition at the Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow. Russian Digital Development Minister Maksut Shadayev told Putin that Russia has launched more than 30 satellites as part of its effort to build a domestic alternative to Starlink.

Putin contrasted that early deployment with the thousands of satellites operated by SpaceX. He said Russia was proud to have begun building its system independently but now needed to expand it significantly, describing the challenge as moving from quality to quantity.

The Russian president also said Moscow remains in “close contact” with American partners, including Musk, over satellite operations. Putin said coordination is necessary to prevent satellites from interfering with one another and to maintain order in orbit. His comments described practical cooperation involving satellite operations despite broader political tensions between Russia and the United States.

Russia's developing constellation is known as Rassvet. Like Starlink, it is intended to use satellites in low Earth orbit, where shorter distances from the ground can provide faster communications than traditional geostationary satellite systems.

The comparison carries particular significance because Ukraine has relied heavily on Starlink and the military-focused Starshield system for battlefield communications and drone operations during its war with Russia. Ukraine recently reported speed restrictions that disrupted some military operations before saying the services had returned to full functionality. Neither Musk nor the U.S. government accepted responsibility for imposing those restrictions.

Russia has pursued satellite internet capabilities for both civilian and military uses as governments increasingly view low-orbit communications networks as strategically important infrastructure.
