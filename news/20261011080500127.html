WHO finds no confirmed plague cases after Russian lab worker's death

Cause of 28-year-old researcher's fatal pneumonia remains unknown

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 11, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The World Health Organization (WHO) says no cases of plague have been registered following the mysterious death of a 28-year-old laboratory worker at a Russian research institute specializing in dangerous infectious diseases. In an assessment released October 10, 2026, the agency reported no evidence of ongoing disease transmission but emphasized that the cause of the woman's fatal pneumonia remains unknown.

The researcher, identified by Russian media as Darya Shipilova, died on October 2 after developing severe pneumonia. She worked at the Irkutsk Anti-Plague Research Institute in Siberia, a facility involved in studying plague and other infectious diseases.

Her death prompted Russian authorities to place approximately 200 people who had contact with her under medical observation. Initial reports raised concerns about possible exposure to pneumonic plague, a potentially deadly bacterial infection that can spread between people through respiratory droplets.

Russia's public health regulator, Rospotrebnadzor, subsequently informed WHO that testing of the deceased worker and her contacts had not detected the dangerous pathogens being investigated. Officials also reported that the monitored contacts remained free of symptoms and that no additional linked cases of severe pneumonia had been identified.

WHO said the available information does not indicate an ongoing outbreak. However, the agency stressed that the absence of a confirmed diagnosis remains an important uncertainty.

Russian authorities have described Shipilova's illness as pneumonia of unknown cause. They maintain that testing found no microorganisms associated with her laboratory work and that postmortem examinations did not detect plague.

The investigation has attracted international attention because of conflicting early reports about a possible laboratory accident. Those reports have not been independently confirmed.

WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus also requested clarification about reports suggesting another institute employee had developed unexplained pneumonia. Russian officials rejected those reports as false.

The United States has called for greater transparency as international health officials continue monitoring the situation.

The central question remains what caused Shipilova's death. Although current findings offer reassurance about the absence of a wider outbreak, investigators have not established a definitive explanation for her illness.
