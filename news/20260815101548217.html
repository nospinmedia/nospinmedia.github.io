U.S. Retail Sales Fall 0.6% In July

Biggest monthly decline since May 2025 as online and auto spending slips

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

U.S. retail sales fell 0.6% in July 2026, the largest monthly decline since May 2025, as Americans reduced spending on vehicles, online purchases and several other categories. The U.S. Census Bureau reported on August 14 that retail and food-services sales totaled an estimated $763.6 billion, down from $768.1 billion in June but still 5.0% higher than a year earlier. Economists had expected a small monthly increase rather than the decline that occurred.

Sales at motor vehicle and parts dealers fell 1.8%, while nonstore retailers, which include online sellers, dropped 2.2%. Spending at gasoline stations declined 0.9%, and electronics and appliance stores fell 0.5%. Several categories moved higher, including clothing and accessories stores, up 1.9%, while food services and drinking places increased 0.5%. Retail sales excluding motor vehicles and gasoline declined 0.2%.

The July pullback followed a 0.2% increase in June and stronger spending earlier in the spring, when larger tax refunds helped support household purchases. Some economists said timing effects may have contributed to July's weakness. Amazon Prime Day was held in late June this year, shifting some online purchases into the previous month, while spending associated with the World Cup also helped lift June activity. Even with those distortions, analysts said the unexpectedly broad decline warrants attention because consumer spending remains a major driver of U.S. economic growth.

The Census figures are adjusted for seasonal, holiday and trading-day differences but are not adjusted for inflation, meaning price changes can affect the reported dollar totals. The report arrives as policymakers assess signs of softer economic activity alongside still-elevated inflation, giving the Federal Reserve another data point to consider when evaluating growth and future interest-rate decisions.
