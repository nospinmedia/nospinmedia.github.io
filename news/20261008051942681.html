Trump administration proposes $70,000 fee for international student work program

Colleges would pay before recommending each student for OPT

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 8, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

The Trump administration is proposing a dramatic new fee structure for Optional Practical Training, or OPT, the program that allows international students with F-1 visas to work in the United States in jobs related to their fields of study. Under a proposed Department of Homeland Security rule, an eligible school would have to pay $70,000 for each student it recommends for OPT for the first time.

The proposal places the payment obligation on Student and Exchange Visitor Program-certified educational institutions, not directly on students. Schools would have to pay before a designated school official could enter an OPT recommendation into the federal SEVIS system. A subsequent OPT recommendation for a student who previously participated in the program would trigger another $30,000 fee. That could include a STEM OPT extension, which can allow qualifying graduates to remain employed for an additional 24 months.

The proposed rule applies to both pre-completion and post-completion OPT. DHS says the fees are intended to combat fraud, strengthen oversight of the immigration system and protect American workers. The agency's own analysis acknowledges that the expense could substantially change participation. DHS modeled scenarios in which schools become less willing to recommend students for OPT because of the cost, particularly when a $70,000 payment would support only one year of employment authorization.

The change is not yet in effect. DHS has proposed a 30-day public comment period on the rule itself. If a final rule is ultimately issued, the new fee system would take effect 60 days after its publication. Students already participating in previously approved OPT, or who receive their school recommendation before the effective date, generally would not trigger the initial fee under the proposal.

OPT has become an important route into the U.S. workforce for international graduates, particularly those in science, technology, engineering and mathematics fields. The administration's proposal would shift a cost currently measured in hundreds of dollars into tens of thousands of dollars for participating schools, potentially forcing institutions to decide which students they are willing to support for employment authorization.
