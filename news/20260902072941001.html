Utah Judge Orders Capital Murder Trial in Charlie Kirk Case

Prosecutors May Pursue Death Penalty

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 2, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A Utah judge ruled there is sufficient evidence for Tyler James Robinson, 23, to stand trial on capital murder charges in the fatal shooting of conservative political activist Charlie Kirk. Following a preliminary hearing in Fourth District Court in Provo on Sept. 1, Judge Tony Graf Jr. determined prosecutors had established probable cause on all felony counts, allowing the case to proceed toward trial. Robinson subsequently entered a plea of not guilty.

The ruling follows a preliminary hearing where prosecutors presented forensic evidence, ballistic analysis and digital evidence they contend links Robinson to the shooting at an event held on Sept. 10, 2025, near Utah Valley University in Orem. Prosecutors argued that firing from an elevated position into an area containing thousands of people met Utah's legal aggravating factors for capital murder because it allegedly placed numerous bystanders at risk in addition to the intended victim.

Defense attorney Staci Vissar argued the evidence did not support seeking the death penalty, contending the shooting was directed at a single individual and did not satisfy the statutory requirements for capital punishment. Judge Graf ruled that prosecutors had presented sufficient evidence at this stage of the proceedings to allow those issues to be decided at trial rather than dismissing or reducing the charges during the preliminary hearing.

The ruling does not determine Robinson's guilt or innocence but permits prosecutors to continue pursuing the death penalty under Utah law if he is convicted. A trial date is expected to be scheduled in the coming weeks. Family members of Charlie Kirk, including his widow Erika Kirk, attended the hearing as the case moved into its next phase.
