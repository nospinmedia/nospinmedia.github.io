Germany commits €1.35 billion more to Ukraine

Military aid and energy repairs target growing Russian attacks

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 5, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz announced more than €1.35 billion ($1.5 billion) in new support for Ukraine during an unannounced visit to Kyiv, combining additional weapons funding with money to repair energy infrastructure damaged by Russian attacks.

Germany will provide €1 billion in military assistance, including support for air defenses, combat and interceptor drones, long-range capabilities and satellite technology. Another €350 million will help repair and strengthen Ukraine's energy sector ahead of winter as Russia intensifies attacks on power, transportation and other infrastructure. Merz traveled to Kyiv with Economy Minister Katherina Reiche and executives from German defense and energy companies.

The visit also produced a series of commercial and defense agreements between German and Ukrainian organizations. The two governments reported agreements involving weapons production, energy and other industrial cooperation, including plans to expand production of drones designed to intercept the jet-powered attack drones increasingly used by Russia. German and Ukrainian companies also discussed production and procurement of air-defense equipment and other military technology.

Merz's visit unfolded while Kyiv was experiencing renewed Russian attacks. A Russian drone struck a bridge across the Dnipro River during the visit, part of a broader campaign against infrastructure in the Ukrainian capital. Standing alongside Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Merz said Germany would not be intimidated and directly urged Russian President Vladimir Putin to end the war.

Germany has become one of Ukraine's most important military and financial supporters since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022. The latest package also underscores Europe's growing role in sustaining Ukraine's defenses as the war enters another winter and Russian attacks place increasing pressure on the country's energy and transportation networks.
