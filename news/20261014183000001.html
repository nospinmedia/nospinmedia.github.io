Trump Reconsiders Livestream of Fort Hood Shooter's Execution

Officials Lean Against Broadcast After President Learns Nidal Hasan Is Paralyzed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The Trump administration is reconsidering plans to livestream the execution of former Army Maj. Nidal Hasan, with officials now leaning against broadcasting the firing-squad execution after President Donald Trump learned that Hasan is paralyzed and uses a wheelchair.

Hasan is scheduled to be executed on December 3, 2026, at Fort Leavenworth, Kansas, for the 2009 Fort Hood mass shooting that killed 13 people and wounded more than 30 others. Hasan was convicted by a military jury in 2013 and sentenced to death. He was shot by responding police during the attack and was left paralyzed from the waist down.

The Pentagon's earlier plan to livestream the execution represented an extraordinary departure from modern U.S. execution practices and generated significant public debate. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had supported making the firing-squad execution available for public viewing, but administration officials are now reconsidering that portion of the plan.

According to reporting from CNN and CBS News, Trump had not previously been aware of the extent of Hasan's disability. After being briefed that Hasan is paraplegic and would need to be transferred from his wheelchair and secured for the execution, the president began questioning whether the event should be broadcast publicly. Officials familiar with the discussions said the administration was increasingly leaning against a livestream, although no final decision had been announced.

The reconsideration concerns the broadcast, not Hasan's death sentence itself. Preparations for the December execution remain underway, and Trump has not indicated that he is considering commuting Hasan's sentence.

The proposed livestream has drawn criticism from lawmakers, religious organizations and civil liberties advocates who argue that broadcasting an execution could transform capital punishment into a public spectacle. Vice President JD Vance has also said he would not watch such a broadcast. Supporters of allowing public access have argued that transparency would permit Americans to see how the federal government carries out a punishment imposed for one of the deadliest attacks on a U.S. military installation.

Legal experts have said challenges specifically aimed at stopping a livestream could face significant hurdles because courts have traditionally given government officials broad authority over execution procedures and media access. Separate legal challenges to Hasan's execution could continue before the scheduled date.

The development is an update to NSM's earlier report that the Pentagon planned to livestream Hasan's firing-squad execution. The execution remains scheduled, but whether the public will be permitted to watch it online is now uncertain.
