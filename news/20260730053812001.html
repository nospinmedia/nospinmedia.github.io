Senate Committee Postpones Todd Blanche Attorney General Vote After GOP Senators Withhold Support

Republican Concerns Over IRS Settlement Stall Confirmation Process

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 30, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The Senate Judiciary Committee postponed a scheduled vote on Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche's nomination to permanently lead the Department of Justice after key Republican senators withheld their support over concerns about a controversial Justice Department settlement tied to President Trump's lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley delayed the markup as negotiations continued in an effort to secure enough Republican votes to advance the nomination.

Republican Senators John Cornyn of Texas and Thom Tillis of North Carolina said they wanted written assurances that a proposed $1.776 billion 'anti-weaponization' fund would be permanently abandoned and that provisions related to tax audit protections would be narrowly limited. Blanche previously testified that the Justice Department no longer intended to pursue the compensation fund, but the senators said they wanted those commitments formally documented before supporting his nomination. Without unanimous Republican support on the committee, Blanche's nomination could not advance.

Blanche has also faced additional political scrutiny following meetings with survivors of Jeffrey Epstein's abuse, which were requested by Tillis during the confirmation process. While those meetings addressed separate concerns, the immediate reason for postponing the committee vote centered on unresolved questions surrounding the IRS settlement and related legal protections.

The delay represents a rare public disagreement within the Republican majority over one of President Trump's Cabinet nominees. Justice Department officials continued discussions with Senate Republicans in hopes of resolving the dispute before the Senate begins its August recess. Until the committee votes, Blanche will continue serving as acting attorney general while negotiations over the nomination remain ongoing.
