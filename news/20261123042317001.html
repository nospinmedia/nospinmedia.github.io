COP31 climate talks collapse without agreement

Fossil fuel and finance disputes leave major issues unresolved

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 23, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

The COP31 U.N. climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ended without an overarching agreement after negotiators failed to bridge major divisions over fossil fuels, climate finance and how to respond to warming beyond the 1.5°C threshold.

COP31 President Murat Kurum, Turkey's environment minister, formally closed the summit on November 22 more than 49 hours after its scheduled Friday deadline. Kurum and COP31 Negotiations President Chris Bowen, Australia's climate minister, announced that countries had failed to adopt the proposed Antalya Pact cover decision, making COP31 the longest U.N. climate summit on record.

The European Union, Norway, Belize, Vanuatu, the Marshall Islands and Panama were among those objecting to the proposed agreement, including over its treatment of transitioning away from fossil fuels. Russia and Saudi Arabia opposed language targeting fossil fuels. India criticized the draft's treatment of responsibility for historical and ongoing emissions, while Pakistan and Ghana expressed frustration that rejection of the overall pact also cost other negotiated provisions. The United States government was absent from COP31 for a second consecutive year, with the White House publicly criticizing the summit.

Climate finance was another major divide. Developing countries had sought a $120 billion target, but that figure was dropped from the failed draft in favor of an unquantified call to triple adaptation finance. The text estimated developing countries' adaptation needs at roughly $305 billion annually and said current national climate pledges would still put the world on a trajectory of about 2.6°C of warming.

Although the overarching pact failed, negotiators adopted some narrower measures, including progress on a Just Transition Mechanism supporting workers and communities during the clean-energy transition. A proposed goal backed by more than 80 countries to raise electricity's share of global final energy use to 35% by 2035 was contained in the failed Antalya Pact and was not adopted as a standalone COP decision.

Unresolved agenda items will carry forward under Rule 16 of the UNFCCC rules of procedure to COP32 in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, in November 2027. U.N. climate chief Simon Stiell also said he would seek advice on possible reforms to the consensus-based negotiating process.
