Virginia state Sen. Louise Lucas charged in federal marijuana conspiracy

Prosecutors allege she used political influence to benefit criminal enterprise

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 23, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas, one of the state's most senior Democratic lawmakers, has been indicted on federal charges accusing her of leading a criminal enterprise involving marijuana distribution, money laundering, bribery and fraud. Lucas surrendered to authorities Thursday and was released on her own recognizance. She has not been convicted of any crime.

An eight-count federal indictment charges Lucas and six other defendants, including family members and business associates. Prosecutors allege Lucas directed an enterprise that acquired and distributed 100 kilograms or more of marijuana and laundered proceeds from marijuana sales. The government alleges the broader scheme operated for about five years and involved approximately $4.5 million.

Lucas, 82, has served in the Virginia Senate since 1992 and is currently the chamber's president pro tempore and chair of the powerful Senate Finance and Appropriations Committee. Prosecutors allege she used her government position to protect the marijuana operation from scrutiny and influence legislation in ways that could financially benefit herself and her associates.

The indictment also alleges Lucas invested money in an entity seeking a recreational marijuana license and agreed to use her political influence to help direct a license to the business. Prosecutors say she failed to disclose that ownership interest on required financial disclosure forms and later accepted a $100,000 partial repayment while continuing to work toward securing the license.

The charges include allegations involving racketeering conspiracy, marijuana trafficking, money laundering and honest-services fraud through bribery. Some defendants also face firearms and other charges. The allegations have not been proven in court, and each defendant is presumed innocent unless convicted.

The investigation began before President Trump's current administration. Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel rejected suggestions that the prosecution was politically motivated. Virginia Democratic officials have emphasized Lucas's right to due process, while some have questioned the timing of the indictment shortly before the November 3 midterm elections.
