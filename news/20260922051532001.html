British Columbia sues OpenAI over school shooting

Province says warning police could have prevented massacre

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 22, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The government of British Columbia has sued OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman in California, alleging the company failed to alert law enforcement after its safety systems flagged concerning ChatGPT conversations linked to the teenager who later carried out the mass shooting in Tumbler Ridge.

The lawsuit, filed Monday in federal court in San Francisco, stems from the February 10, 2026 attack at Tumbler Ridge Secondary School and a nearby home. Shooter Jesse Van Rootselaar, 18, killed eight victims, including five students and an education worker at the school as well as her mother and half-brother at their home, before dying by suicide. More than 25 people were injured.

British Columbia alleges that OpenAI's safety team had previously flagged Van Rootselaar after conversations involving gun violence and disabled one of her accounts, but did not notify police. The province says she subsequently created another account and continued using ChatGPT. The allegations have not been proven in court, and OpenAI has maintained that it uses safeguards intended to prevent harmful use and cooperates with authorities when credible threats are identified.

The province is seeking damages for costs associated with the attack and community recovery, including expenses connected with replacing Tumbler Ridge Secondary School, as well as a court order requiring changes to how OpenAI handles conversations that could indicate threats of violence. British Columbia Attorney General Niki Sharma said the province is pursuing accountability for what it alleges was a failure to warn law enforcement.

The provincial case is separate from litigation already brought by survivors and victims' families against OpenAI and Altman. Those lawsuits similarly allege the company failed to respond adequately to warning signs in the shooter's ChatGPT activity. OpenAI has sought dismissal of the private lawsuits and argued that claims arising from the shooting should be heard in British Columbia rather than California.
