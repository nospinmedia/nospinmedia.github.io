DNA links Ted Bundy to Utah teenager’s 1974 murder

New testing gives authorities evidence to close 52-year-old cold case

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 28, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

New DNA testing has formally linked serial killer Ted Bundy to the 1974 murder of 17-year-old Melissa Ann Smith, allowing Utah authorities to close a case in which Bundy had been suspected for more than five decades but never conclusively tied through forensic evidence.

Smith disappeared from Midvale, Utah, on October 18, 1974, after leaving a pizza restaurant. Her body was discovered nine days later near Summit Park, east of Salt Lake City. Investigators determined that she had been sexually assaulted and killed. Smith was the daughter of Midvale Police Chief Louis Smith, and Bundy quickly emerged as a suspect as investigators began connecting a series of disappearances and killings of young women in the region.

Authorities announced the breakthrough on October 27, 2026 — exactly 52 years after Smith's body was found. Investigators used Y-STR DNA testing, which examines genetic markers passed through the paternal line, to compare evidence preserved from Smith's case with a DNA profile recently developed from another killing attributed to Bundy.

That profile came from evidence in the case of Laura Ann Aime, a 17-year-old whose body was found in Utah in November 1974. Authorities announced earlier this year that new DNA testing had formally connected Bundy to Aime's killing. Investigators then used that genetic profile to reexamine evidence preserved from Smith's case and found a match consistent with Bundy's paternal line.

The new finding is significant because Bundy was never prosecuted for Smith's murder. Although investigators had long believed he was responsible, authorities said they previously lacked the forensic evidence necessary to formally attribute the killing to him. Bundy was executed in Florida in 1989 after being convicted of murders there, and he confessed to killing 30 women across several states before his execution. Investigators have long suspected that the actual number of his victims may have been higher.

Utah investigators said the DNA breakthrough could also help with other unresolved cases from the period. Among them is the 1974 disappearance of 16-year-old Nancy Wilcox, who vanished from Holladay, Utah, and whose body has never been found.

The Smith investigation involved cooperation among multiple Utah law enforcement agencies and relied on biological evidence preserved for more than half a century, illustrating how newer forensic techniques can provide answers in cases that were once considered unsolvable.
