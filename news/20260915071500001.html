NATO fighter jets shoot down drone over Lithuania

Aircraft likely entered from Belarus as investigators examine wreckage

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 15, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

NATO fighter jets shot down an unidentified drone over Lithuania shortly after midnight on September 15, marking the first time allied aircraft have destroyed an airborne target over Lithuanian territory. The drone was intercepted near Pratkūnai, in the Kaišiadorys district near Kaunas, after triggering air alerts around Vilnius and surrounding areas.

Lithuania's National Crisis Management Centre said preliminary tracking indicated the drone entered the country from Belarus and traveled west for roughly half an hour. Vilmantas Vitkauskas, head of the center, said its route passed south of Vilnius through the Lentvaris and Trakai areas before reaching the southern part of Kaišiadorys district near the Kaunas Reservoir. Officials stressed that investigators have not yet determined the drone's origin, operator or intended mission.

The interception was carried out by Italian NATO fighter aircraft operating from Šiauliai Air Base as part of the alliance's Baltic air-defense mission. Lithuanian authorities recovered wreckage from the rural area where the drone was destroyed, and military police, civilian police and other specialists began examining the debris to determine what type of aircraft it was and whether it carried explosives.

The incident occurred near the Kruonis Pumped Storage Hydroelectric Plant, an important Lithuanian energy facility. Officials have not established that the plant was the drone's target. Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda thanked NATO personnel for their rapid response and said heightened readiness was essential as Russia continues its war against Ukraine.

NATO aircraft have patrolled the Baltic states since Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia joined the alliance in 2004. Allied jets have intercepted other stray drones in the region, including Ukrainian drones over Estonia and Latvia earlier this year, as electronic warfare and long-range attacks increasingly create risks of unmanned aircraft crossing national borders.

The Lithuanian incident therefore remains under investigation, with authorities cautioning against attributing the drone to Russia, Belarus or Ukraine until its wreckage and flight data have been analyzed.
