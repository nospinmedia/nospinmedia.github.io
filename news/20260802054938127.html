Ukrainian drones strike major Russian logistics hub deep behind front lines

Separate attack kills two as Moscow says hundreds of drones targeted Russia

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 2, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Ukraine launched one of its largest long-range drone operations of the war overnight, striking a Wildberries distribution warehouse in Russia's Samara region while separate attacks killed two people in the city of Engels, Russian officials said. The strike on the logistics facility triggered a large fire, sending thick smoke over the area as emergency crews worked to contain the blaze. Wildberries, Russia's largest online retailer, confirmed that one of its warehouses had been damaged and temporarily suspended operations at the site.

Russia's Ministry of Defense said its air defenses intercepted or destroyed 635 Ukrainian drones across multiple regions during the overnight assault, describing it as one of the largest drone attacks since the war began. Regional officials said the two fatalities occurred when drone debris struck a residential area in Saratov region, while authorities continued assessing damage at military, industrial and commercial sites elsewhere in western Russia.

The warehouse strike drew particular attention because of the facility's strategic role in Russia's logistics network. Ukrainian officials have previously argued that some commercial infrastructure supporting Russia's supply chains can also provide services benefiting the country's military. However, Ukraine did not immediately comment on this specific overnight operation, and Russian authorities have not publicly presented evidence linking the targeted warehouse to military operations.

The attack came as both sides continued expanding the range and scale of long-distance drone warfare. Ukraine has increasingly targeted energy facilities, logistics centers and industrial sites hundreds of miles inside Russian territory, while Russia has continued launching large missile and drone attacks against Ukrainian cities and infrastructure. Analysts say both countries are relying more heavily on domestically produced drones as the conflict evolves into a sustained contest of long-range precision strikes.

Russian emergency services continued damage assessments Sunday, while investigators worked to determine the full impact of the attacks. Additional casualty and damage figures could change as inspections continue.
