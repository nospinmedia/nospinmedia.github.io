CDC Widens Taco Bell Lettuce Parasite Probe To 9 States

1,947 Illnesses And 98 Hospitalizations Reported So Far

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 3, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Federal health officials with the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention expanded a multistate investigation into a Cyclospora outbreak linked to shredded iceberg lettuce served at Taco Bell restaurants, adding Illinois, Kansas, Oklahoma, and Pennsylvania to the advisory for a total of 9 affected states. Authorities report at least 1,947 laboratory-confirmed illnesses and 98 hospitalizations tied to the outbreak, with case counts expected to keep rising as additional tests are completed.

Traceback findings identify shredded iceberg lettuce supplied to Taco Bell restaurants, with Taylor Farms de Mexico initiating a voluntary recall of product harvested June 29–July 16, 2026. Taco Bell said all potentially affected lettuce was removed from its U.S. locations by July 17, 2026. Because Cyclospora infections often incubate for two to fourteen days, public health officials warn that newly confirmed cases may continue to appear into early August 2026 even though the implicated product is no longer being served.

- Affected states: Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, West Virginia, Illinois, Kansas, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania - Confirmed illnesses: 1,947 - Hospitalizations: 98 - Implicated product: shredded iceberg lettuce used in Taco Bell menu items - Agencies involved: CDC, Food and Drug Administration, state health departments

The Food and Drug Administration and state partners are coordinating inspections and supply‑chain reviews to verify the scope of distribution and ensure recalled product remains out of commerce. Health officials advise anyone who experiences prolonged watery diarrhea, stomach cramps, bloating, or fatigue after eating fresh lettuce to contact a clinician for evaluation and stool testing; effective treatment is available by prescription.

Investigators initially focused on clusters in Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, and West Virginia, then broadened the probe as patient interviews and supply records linked additional restaurants to common lettuce shipments. Officials emphasize that current concerns center on exposures during the recalled harvest window and that routine consumption of lettuce outside that period is not implicated.
