Unitree Robotics Surges 629% in Shanghai IPO Debut

Humanoid robot maker sets new valuation benchmark for physical AI

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 19, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Shares of Chinese robotics company Unitree Robotics, officially known as Hangzhou Yushu Technology Co., surged as much as 629% during their debut on Shanghai's STAR Market on August 19, 2026, making the company mainland China's first publicly traded pure-play humanoid robotics manufacturer. The sharp gain lifted the company's market value to roughly 445 billion yuan (about $66 billion), highlighting strong investor enthusiasm for companies developing so-called "physical AI" technologies.

The company raised approximately 6.1 billion yuan (about $904 million) through its initial public offering after attracting record demand from retail investors. Founded in 2016 by Wang Xingxing, Unitree manufactures humanoid and quadruped robots used in research, industrial applications and technology development. The company reported 1.7 billion yuan in revenue during 2025, reflecting growing commercial interest in robotics hardware.

The debut comes as governments and technology companies invest heavily in embodied artificial intelligence, which combines AI software with robots capable of performing physical tasks. Supporters see humanoid robots as a potential next frontier for manufacturing, logistics, healthcare and service industries, while critics caution that widespread commercial adoption remains years away and that current market valuations may reflect investor optimism more than proven long-term demand.

Strategic investors, including Meituan, Tencent and DeepSeek, also benefited from the company's market debut through their existing ownership stakes. The listing coincided with the opening of the World Robot Conference in Beijing, reinforcing China's broader effort to become a global leader in advanced robotics and artificial intelligence. Even as investor enthusiasm remains strong, analysts say the long-term commercial success of humanoid robots will depend on continued technological advances, lower production costs and broader real-world adoption.
