Judge Dismisses Child Sex Abuse Charges Against Actor Timothy Busfield

Ruling Cites Prosecutorial Misconduct in New Mexico Grand Jury Proceedings

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 17, 2026 at 2:48 PM ET

A New Mexico judge has dismissed child sexual abuse charges against actor Timothy Busfield, ruling that prosecutors failed to properly handle the grand jury proceedings that resulted in his indictment.

District Judge Joseph Montaño dismissed four counts of criminal sexual contact of a minor on October 16 after Busfield's attorneys challenged the way prosecutors presented the case to the grand jury. The judge concluded that the prosecution failed to act fairly and impartially during the process.

The ruling centered on how grand jurors were instructed and whether they were allowed to hear testimony they had requested. Busfield's defense argued that prosecutors withheld potentially exculpatory information and improperly restricted testimony that could have affected the grand jury's decision to indict him.

Busfield, an Emmy-winning actor and director known for roles in thirtysomething, The West Wing and Field of Dreams, has denied the allegations. His attorneys have maintained that the accusations were false and challenged the prosecution's evidence and handling of the case.

The dismissal does not necessarily end the case. The judge dismissed the indictment without prejudice, meaning prosecutors retain the legal ability to seek new charges if they decide to present the case again through proper procedures.

The ruling therefore does not determine whether the underlying allegations are true or false. Instead, it invalidates the existing indictment because of problems the judge identified in the grand jury process.

Prosecutorial misconduct findings can carry consequences beyond an individual case because grand jury rules are intended to ensure that prosecutors present evidence fairly while jurors determine whether sufficient probable cause exists to bring criminal charges.
