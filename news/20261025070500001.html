Obama calls Trump presidency ‘chaos and cruelty’ on campaign trail

Former president urges voters to use midterms as a check on White House

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 25, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Former President Barack Obama delivered some of his sharpest criticism yet of President Trump while campaigning for Democratic candidates in Ohio and Michigan, telling voters that the November 3 midterm elections give them an opportunity to put a check on the administration.

Speaking Saturday in Cleveland and Detroit, Obama accused Trump and his Republican allies of fostering what he described as “chaos and cruelty and corruption and incompetence.” In Michigan, he called the midterm election a “circuit breaker” that voters could use to bring greater accountability to the federal government.

Obama also accused Trump of corruption and criticized his handling of the economy and rising living costs. He mocked Trump's renovations to the White House, joking that the president appeared more focused on remodeling than problems facing Americans.

The former president also criticized Trump's handling of the conflict with Iran, arguing that instability around the Strait of Hormuz has increased Iran's leverage over global energy supplies. He additionally took aim at Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, mocking what Obama portrayed as the administration's focus on appearances and physical fitness within the military.

In Ohio, Obama campaigned alongside Democratic gubernatorial candidate Amy Acton and condemned a controversial advertisement from Republican candidate Vivek Ramaswamy featuring Acton's estranged mother. The advertisement challenges Acton's account of a childhood that she has described as involving homelessness and abuse. Republican Ohio Gov. Mike DeWine has also called for the advertisement to be withdrawn, while Trump and Vice President JD Vance have defended it.

Obama later traveled to Detroit to campaign for Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, who is running against Republican Mike Rogers. Obama defended El-Sayed against attacks involving his progressive politics, name and Muslim faith, comparing some of the rhetoric to false claims about Obama's own birthplace during his presidential campaigns.

Trump was also campaigning Saturday and dismissed Obama's Detroit appearance while defending Ramaswamy's advertisement. With Republicans holding narrow majorities in both chambers of Congress, both parties are deploying their most recognizable political figures as Election Day approaches.
