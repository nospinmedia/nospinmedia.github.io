8-year-old Haitian boy returns home after nearly two weeks in federal custody

Child was detained during scheduled immigration appointment in San Diego

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 18, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

An 8-year-old Haitian boy has been reunited with his family in San Diego after spending nearly two weeks in federal custody following a scheduled immigration appointment earlier this month.

The third-grader, identified in local and advocacy reporting as Edher, was taken into custody on Wednesday, November 4. He had been living with his uncle and cousins after his father was deported from the United States last year and attended Fay Elementary School in San Diego's City Heights neighborhood.

Edher accompanied his uncle to a scheduled immigration check-in at the Edward J. Schwartz Federal Building when federal authorities took him into custody. The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) said his uncle could not immediately provide documentation establishing legal guardianship, requiring officials under federal anti-trafficking rules to designate the boy an unaccompanied alien child.

DHS said agents acted "out of an abundance of caution and to ensure the child's safety." Edher was transferred to the Office of Refugee Resettlement (ORR) and placed at a facility in Texas while officials worked to determine an appropriate guardian.

After his uncle formally established legal guardianship, Edher was released and returned to San Diego on Monday, November 16, after approximately 12 days in federal custody.

U.S. Rep. Juan Vargas of California welcomed the release but said, "I'm so grateful that he's home, but this should have never happened in the first place." Haitian Bridge Alliance Executive Director Guerline Jozef, whose organization advocated for the family, said, "His freedom should never have required a public outcry."

The reunion does not resolve Edher's immigration status. The Trump administration's move to end Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for Haitians affects approximately 350,000 people living in the United States.
