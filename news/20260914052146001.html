Judge says DHS unlawfully controlled FEMA staffing

50% cut was never completed, but court remedy is still pending

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 14, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

A federal judge has ruled that the Department of Homeland Security unlawfully took control of FEMA's staffing decisions when it pushed the disaster agency toward a roughly 50% workforce reduction, even though that sweeping cut was never fully carried out. U.S. District Judge Susan Illston said DHS exceeded authority Congress preserved for FEMA after Hurricane Katrina, making the ruling primarily about who has legal control over the agency's workforce going forward.

The dispute grew out of DHS restrictions on renewals for Cadre of On-Call Response and Recovery, or CORE, employees, who make up a large share of FEMA's disaster-response workforce. Earlier this year, FEMA backed away from a blanket policy of allowing thousands of CORE appointments to expire, offered renewals to some workers and rehired some employees who had already been separated. In June 2026, Illston declined to immediately block the reductions because the agency was no longer systematically refusing renewals.

The broader lawsuit continued, however, and Illston has now found that DHS acted unlawfully by directing staffing decisions that federal law places with FEMA. Court records showed FEMA regional and program offices had recommended maintaining staffing near 23,000 employees, while a later plan submitted to DHS projected only 11,383 employees. The Government Accountability Office separately found no indication that the proposed reduction was based on an analysis of FEMA's actual workforce needs.

The ruling does not mean thousands of layoffs are now being abruptly stopped. Illston specifically noted that the original 50% reduction was not currently being implemented in the same form. However, she found that DHS continued restricting FEMA's authority by limiting the length of some CORE renewals. She also sanctioned the government over deleted Signal messages related to the staffing decisions and said the missing messages will be presumed unfavorable to the defendants in later proceedings.

Illston has not yet ordered a final remedy. The parties must attempt to agree on what relief remains necessary, with unresolved issues due back to the court by October 9, 2026.
