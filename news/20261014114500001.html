ICE Agent Accused of Deliberately Striking Man With SUV in Houston

Surveillance Video Prompts Criminal Investigation as Victim Faces Multiple Surgeries

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

A Texas prosecutor has opened a criminal investigation after surveillance footage appeared to show a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agent intentionally striking a fleeing man with a government vehicle in Houston, Texas. The collision left 48-year-old Miguel Parra Hernandez with a broken leg and injuries requiring multiple surgeries. Harris County District Attorney Sean Teare said the footage raises serious concerns about the agent's actions and could support criminal charges.

The incident occurred on Saturday, October 10, 2026, during an immigration enforcement operation. Surveillance footage shows Parra Hernandez exiting a white truck and running as a dark SUV pursues him. The vehicle then strikes Parra Hernandez, who is seen lying on the ground moments later. The video has become central to the investigation into whether the driver deliberately used the vehicle as a weapon.

Teare said the most apparent potential charge was aggravated assault with a deadly weapon, explaining that a vehicle can qualify as a deadly weapon when intentionally used to injure someone. His comments reflected a preliminary assessment of the evidence, not a final determination of criminal responsibility. No charges against the agent had been announced.

ICE disputes the suggestion that the collision was intentional. The agency said officers were attempting to apprehend individuals during an enforcement operation when Parra Hernandez ran in front of an ICE vehicle and was struck. ICE said agents were searching for Jose Mendoza-Cervantes, who had an active deportation warrant, and said Parra Hernandez had been in the same truck. Kim Bruno, an attorney representing Parra Hernandez, disputed that account, saying her client does not know Mendoza-Cervantes and was not traveling with him.

The investigation has also produced a dispute over potentially important evidence from the government SUV. Teare said federal agents drove the ICE vehicle away from the scene before local investigators could download information from its Crash Data Retrieval system. His office has raised concerns about preservation of evidence as investigators seek information that could help establish what happened immediately before the collision.

According to immigrant rights advocates assisting the family, Parra Hernandez has lived in the United States for 34 years and was traveling to work when the encounter occurred. He underwent surgery for injuries below his knee and was awaiting another procedure to reconstruct his ankle after swelling subsided.

The case places local prosecutors in the unusual position of investigating the actions of a federal immigration officer while the two sides publicly dispute important elements of the encounter. Authorities have not announced whether criminal charges will be filed, and the investigation remains ongoing.
