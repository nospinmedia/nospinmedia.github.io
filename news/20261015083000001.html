Musk Says U.S. Military and Trump Control Starshield Restrictions

Ukraine Reports Disrupted Drone Defenses as Questions Remain Over Who Ordered Limits

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Elon Musk says the U.S. military and ultimately President Trump control restrictions on SpaceX's military satellite communications service, Starshield, following complaints from Ukrainian officials that sudden connectivity limits disrupted critical drone operations.

The controversy centers on restrictions affecting satellite terminals installed aboard Ukrainian military drones. According to Ukrainian officials, connections began failing when the equipment traveled above certain speeds, interfering with both offensive missions and drones used to intercept incoming Russian attacks.

Serhiy Beskrestnov, an adviser to Ukraine's president, said Ukrainian forces received no advance warning before the limitations took effect. He reported that Starshield terminals stopped operating at speeds exceeding approximately 100 miles per hour, or 160 kilometers per hour.

However, some Ukrainian military units reported losing connections at substantially lower speeds. Ukraine's 7th Rapid Reaction Corps said drone operators experienced interruptions lasting 10 to 20 seconds while traveling approximately 68 to 75 miles per hour. Those disruptions reportedly forced the cancellation of several missions.

Responding to criticism, Musk distinguished between commercial Starlink service and the military-focused Starshield system.

Musk stated that Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the U.S. government, with operational restrictions determined by military authorities and ultimately the president. He also emphasized that commercial Starlink remains under SpaceX's control and is intended for civilian and defensive activities rather than offensive military operations.

The distinction matters because Starshield was developed specifically for government and military applications. Although it uses technology and satellite infrastructure associated with Starlink, it offers enhanced security features, dedicated terminals and services designed for defense operations.

Musk's statement establishes his position on who has authority over Starshield restrictions. It does not independently establish that President Trump personally ordered the specific speed limitations affecting Ukrainian drones.

As of October 15, no publicly confirmed presidential directive or Pentagon order identifying who authorized these particular restrictions has been established in the available reporting. The White House had not immediately responded to Reuters' request for comment.

The operational consequences extend beyond offensive missions. Ukrainian forces use satellite-connected drones to identify and intercept Russian aerial threats, meaning connectivity disruptions can also affect defensive operations intended to protect troops and civilians.

The controversy has also drawn international attention. Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski publicly questioned Musk about the restrictions. Poland helps finance satellite communications services used by Ukraine, giving Warsaw a direct interest in their reliability.

The incident raises broader questions about accountability when private companies develop and operate technology that governments depend on for military operations. Starshield's relationship with the Pentagon makes those questions particularly important when service restrictions affect an active battlefield.

For now, the central issue remains unresolved: who specifically authorized the new restrictions, what prompted the decision, and whether Ukrainian forces were supposed to receive advance notice.
