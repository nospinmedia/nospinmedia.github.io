Trump says deportation policy may have been too tough

President raises possibility of immigration deal after midterm losses

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 9, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

President Donald Trump says his administration's deportation policies may have been "too tough" and indicated he could work with the incoming Democratic-controlled Congress on an immigration agreement, days after Republicans lost control of both the House and Senate in the midterm elections.

In a 60 Minutes interview broadcast Sunday, Trump said his immigration approach had both helped and hurt Republicans politically. He defended efforts to reduce illegal border crossings and remove people he described as dangerous criminals, but acknowledged concerns about deporting undocumented immigrants who have lived and worked in the United States for years. Trump specifically pointed to people working in industries such as landscaping and hotels, including some who pay taxes, and said there could be a way to "work a deal for them."

Trump said he would "love to do something on immigration" with Democrats and suggested some form of legal status could be achievable. His comments represent a potential opening for negotiations, but they do not amount to an announced change in administration policy. The White House has not detailed a new immigration plan or said that current enforcement operations will be reduced.

At the same time, the administration is signaling continued aggressive enforcement. Trump replaced outgoing White House chief of staff Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller, who has played a central role in the administration's immigration strategy. Multiple U.S. officials told CBS News that Immigration and Customs Enforcement is unlikely to substantially scale back its crackdown following the midterm results. Miller previously pushed ICE toward a target of 3,000 arrests per day.

Immigration was a significant issue in the election. CBS News exit polling found 58% of voters believed the administration's actions on immigration had gone too far, although voters still narrowly trusted Republicans over Democrats to handle immigration, 43% to 41%.

Trump's comments therefore create an unresolved question between possible legislative compromise and the administration's continuing enforcement strategy.
