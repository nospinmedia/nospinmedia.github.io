Maduro and wife face new US torture charges

Prosecutors allege electric shocks, sexual assault and abuse of American prisoners

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 9, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

Former Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, face new federal charges alleging they oversaw a system of torture targeting political opponents and American citizens. Prosecutors unsealed a 30-page superseding indictment in Manhattan on October 8, 2026, accusing the couple of participating in a conspiracy involving years of physical and psychological abuse.

According to the indictment, Venezuelan military and intelligence agencies subjected detainees to electric shocks, asphyxiation, sexual assault, severe beatings, prolonged isolation and starvation. Prosecutors also described threats against prisoners' families, including children. The allegations involve political dissidents, military personnel suspected of disloyalty and American citizens detained in Venezuela.

Federal prosecutors allege Maduro personally authorized torture, received updates about interrogations and instructed security officials to intensify their treatment of prisoners. During political unrest in 2014, he allegedly proposed abducting several military officers suspected of disloyalty and extracting information through torture. Prosecutors say Flores supported that proposal.

The indictment also describes alleged abuse involving American detainees. One U.S. citizen arrested in 2022 was reportedly injected with substances that caused seizures. Another American detained in 2023 was allegedly beaten and tortured by Venezuelan intelligence officers. Prosecutors say an officer told that prisoner his American citizenship made him valuable for a potential prisoner exchange.

Maduro and Flores have been held in federal custody in Brooklyn, New York, since U.S. military forces captured them in Caracas in January 2026. They previously pleaded not guilty to charges involving narcoterrorism, cocaine trafficking and weapons offenses.

The new conspiracy to commit torture charge carries a maximum sentence of 20 years in prison. The existing narcotics and weapons charges carry substantially longer potential sentences, including life imprisonment.

During a separate hearing Thursday, Flores requested release on bail because of concerns involving her heart condition. Her attorney proposed strict conditions, but the judge denied the request, citing the seriousness of the allegations and her access to substantial financial resources. Her arraignment on the new charge was postponed to allow the defense additional time to review the indictment.

The couple's trial has been tentatively scheduled for June 2027. The charges remain allegations, and both defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty.
