Eisenkot calls West Bank settler arson attacks 'Jewish terrorism'

Israeli opposition leaders warn escalating violence threatens security

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 23, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Israeli opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot condemned overnight arson attacks by Israeli settlers against Palestinian communities in the occupied West Bank, calling them "acts of Jewish terrorism" as Israeli security forces investigated the violence.

The attacks occurred between November 21 and November 22 across several Palestinian communities. Roughly a dozen Palestinian-owned vehicles were burned, including seven in Kafr Malik and two in Deir al-Sudan. Reports also described homes and an agricultural structure being set on fire. Hebrew graffiti included the phrases "This is the first revenge" and "Greetings from Susya," referring to a recently evacuated Israeli settler outpost.

The Israel Defense Forces and Israel Border Police said troops were deployed to Deir al-Sudan, Deir Abu Mashal, Kafr Malik and At-Tuwani. Security officials condemned the violence, saying such attacks harm public order, divert forces from their primary missions and interfere with ongoing counterterrorism operations.

Eisenkot, a former Israeli military chief who currently holds President Isaac Herzog's mandate to try to form a government following Israel's inconclusive October election, called for authorities to use available measures against those responsible, including administrative detention and operations by the Shin Bet domestic security service.

He also blamed National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich for policies he said had brought the West Bank to "the brink of unchecked anarchy." Ben-Gvir rejected Eisenkot's call for administrative detention and accused him of politicizing the attacks during coalition negotiations.

The violence followed the killing earlier in the week of Fadda Fuhaidat, 72, a Palestinian woman shot outside her home near Jericho. U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee had also publicly condemned violent settlers as "terrorists" in September amid concerns about escalating attacks.
