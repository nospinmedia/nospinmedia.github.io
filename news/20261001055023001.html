Swiss glaciers lose 5.5% of their ice in one year

Five-year decline approaches 20% as Alpine melt accelerates

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 1, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Switzerland's glaciers lost 5.5% of their remaining ice volume in 2026, the second-largest percentage decline on record, after low winter snowfall and intense summer heat left the Alpine ice increasingly exposed. The new measurements from the GLAMOS Swiss glacier monitoring service and the Swiss Academy of Sciences show that nearly one-fifth of Switzerland's glacier volume has disappeared in just five years.

The largest percentage loss on record occurred in 2022. This year's decline was smaller in absolute volume than some previous extreme years because substantially less ice remains. Switzerland's total glacier volume is now roughly one-third of what it was in 2003, when a major heatwave caused a 3.5% decline.

Glaciologists said unusually low winter snowfall provided less insulation for the glaciers before summer heatwaves accelerated melting. Average ice thickness fell between about 2.5 and 4 meters across monitored glaciers, while some glacier tongues lost as much as 10 meters. The Aletsch, Rhone, Allalin and Clarident glaciers were among those experiencing their greatest melt on record this year.

Between July and September, Switzerland's glaciers released about 2.2 trillion liters of water — roughly four times the country's annual water consumption. That meltwater temporarily increases river flows and hydropower resources, but researchers warn that continued glacier retreat will eventually reduce the amount of stored ice available to supply water during warm and dry periods.

The consequences extend beyond Switzerland. The country is sometimes described as Europe's water tower because its mountains feed major river systems including the Rhine, Rhone, Danube and Po. Switzerland also produces more than half of its electricity from hydropower, making long-term changes in Alpine water storage important for energy production as well as ecosystems and downstream communities.

Matthias Huss, head of GLAMOS, said Alpine glaciers cannot survive long term under current warming conditions. He said stabilizing the climate by reducing carbon dioxide emissions could slow further losses, although preserving Switzerland's remaining glaciers may already be extremely difficult.
