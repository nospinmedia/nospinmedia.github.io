Trump arrives in Ireland for talks and Irish Open

President heads to tournament at his own Doonbeg golf resort

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

President Trump arrived in Ireland on September 12 for a two-day trip combining meetings with Irish leaders and attendance at the Amgen Irish Open, which is being held at his Trump International Golf Links resort in Doonbeg, County Clare. The trip is not classified as an official state visit, although Trump's schedule includes several presidential engagements before he travels west to the golf tournament.

Trump was scheduled to meet Irish President Catherine Connolly at Áras an Uachtaráin and Taoiseach Micheál Martin at Farmleigh House in Dublin's Phoenix Park. He is also expected to address invited guests at the U.S. ambassador's residence. Discussions come as the United States and Ireland navigate economic ties, trade issues and differences over international affairs, including the continuing conflict in the Middle East.

After the Dublin meetings, Trump is due to travel to County Clare to attend the Irish Open at the golf resort he owns. Tournament play is expected to pause temporarily for his arrival, and Trump is scheduled to present the winner with the trophy on September 13 before returning to the United States. Reporting ahead of the visit described the tournament as a central reason for the timing of the trip.

Irish authorities mounted an extensive security operation in Dublin and around Doonbeg. An Garda Síochána brought in officers from outside the Clare division, while U.S. Secret Service personnel were deployed at the resort. Security measures include restricted access, armed units, airport-style screening for spectators and a specially prepared landing area for Marine One near the golf course. Demonstrations opposing Trump and U.S. policies are also planned in Dublin, Doonbeg and near Shannon Airport.

The visit comes during a turbulent period for Trump's administration, with the continuing Iran conflict contributing to disruptions in global energy markets and higher U.S. fuel prices. The president remains able to carry out official duties while traveling, and the White House has combined the Irish diplomatic meetings with his planned appearance at the privately owned golf property.
