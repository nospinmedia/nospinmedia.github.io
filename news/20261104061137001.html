Missouri voters reject Republican-drawn House map

Referendum preserves state's existing congressional districts

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 4, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Missouri voters rejected a Republican-drawn congressional map Tuesday, blocking a mid-decade redistricting plan designed to improve the GOP's chances of winning an additional U.S. House seat.

The vote on Proposition A effectively repeals the congressional boundaries approved by Missouri's Republican-controlled legislature in 2025 and keeps the state's 2022 congressional map in place. The proposed map would have significantly changed the boundaries around Kansas City, where Democrats currently hold a congressional seat.

The referendum reached voters after opponents of the new boundaries organized a petition campaign challenging the legislature's action. More than 300,000 signatures were submitted in an effort to force a statewide vote, followed by legal disputes over whether the referendum could proceed and whether the new map could take effect before voters had their say.

The Missouri fight was part of a broader national battle over mid-decade redistricting ahead of the 2026 elections. Unlike the normal redistricting process that follows the once-a-decade census, several states considered changing congressional boundaries between census cycles as both parties looked for potential advantages in the closely divided House.

By rejecting the new Missouri map, voters prevented those proposed boundaries from being used and preserved the districts drawn after the 2020 Census. The result also limits Republicans' ability to gain another favorable House district in Missouri through the 2025 redistricting effort.

The decision carries consequences beyond the state's eight-member congressional delegation. With control of the House determined by relatively small numbers of seats in recent elections, individual redistricting decisions can influence the national balance of power.
