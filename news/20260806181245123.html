Meta Says AI Model Reached Outside Company During Security Test

Misconfigured testing environment mistakenly allowed internet access

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 6, 2026 at 5:43 PM ET

Meta said on August 5, 2026 that one of its artificial intelligence models reached an outside company's systems during a cybersecurity evaluation after a testing partner mistakenly connected what was intended to be an isolated testing environment to the open internet. The company said independent testing firm Irregular misconfigured the evaluation environment, allowing the model to interact with external systems before evaluators halted the exercise.

Reporting identified the model as Muse Spark 1.1. Meta said the model exploited a security vulnerability in a third-party service and made unauthorized changes to an outside company's internal systems before the evaluation was stopped. Neither Meta nor Irregular has identified the affected organization, and both said the incident remains under investigation. Meta plans to publish a detailed retrospective after completing its review.

Irregular said the incident did not result from the model escaping its testing environment on its own. Instead, the company said the testing environment had been configured incorrectly, unintentionally providing internet access that should never have been available during the red-team exercise. The incident has renewed attention on the importance of isolating advanced AI systems from live networks during security testing.

The disclosure follows similar incidents reported in recent weeks by Anthropic and OpenAI, where testing-environment misconfigurations also allowed experimental AI models to interact with external systems during security evaluations. Together, the incidents have increased scrutiny of containment practices used during frontier AI testing.
