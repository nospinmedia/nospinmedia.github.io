Early voting surpasses 2022 midterm total

More than 50.5 million ballots cast before Election Day

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 1, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

More than 50.5 million Americans had already cast ballots in the 2026 midterm elections by Saturday, surpassing the entire early-voting total from the 2022 midterms with voting still underway ahead of Election Day on November 3.

The nationwide total was nearly 5 million ballots above the approximately 45.9 million early votes cast during the full 2022 midterm cycle. Separate reporting from Reuters on Saturday placed the latest total above 51 million, reflecting continued voting as states updated their counts. An Associated Press analysis earlier in the week found more than 43 million ballots had been cast with five days remaining, nearly 10 million more than at the same point in 2022.

Several competitive states have reported substantial increases in early participation. Texas surpassed 7 million early votes, about 1.5 million more than its total early vote in 2022. Earlier AP data also showed early voting running 83% higher in South Carolina, 44% higher in Maine, 34% higher in Kansas and 32% higher in Michigan compared with the same point four years ago.

Party-registration figures provide information about who has voted early but do not show which candidates those voters selected. In states reporting party affiliation, Democrats held an advantage among early voters, although many states do not report partisan registration data and millions of voters are registered as unaffiliated or with other parties.

The unusually high early turnout comes before an election that will determine control of Congress. Republicans currently hold a 53-47 majority in the Senate and a narrow majority in the House, while competitive Senate, House and gubernatorial races are being contested across the country.
