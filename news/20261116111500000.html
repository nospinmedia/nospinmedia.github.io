International student enrollment drops sharply at U.S. colleges

New foreign enrollment falls 15% as schools cite visas, travel restrictions and costs

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 16, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

International student enrollment at U.S. colleges and universities has fallen sharply this fall, with new foreign student enrollment dropping 15% as schools report difficulties involving visas, travel restrictions, rising costs and increased competition from universities in other countries.

A new survey from the Institute of International Education, covering 809 U.S. institutions, found total international student enrollment declined 9% in fall 2026 compared with the previous year. The figures include students enrolled in classes and those participating in Optional Practical Training, or OPT.

Graduate programs experienced some of the largest losses, with international graduate enrollment falling 15%. Undergraduate enrollment declined 3%, while participation in OPT, which allows eligible international graduates to temporarily work in the United States, fell 6%.

Among institutions reporting declining international enrollment, 88% cited the visa application process as a factor, 70% cited travel restrictions, 60% said students were choosing universities in other countries and 45% pointed to costs. The survey does not attribute the decline to any single cause.

The trend also carries financial consequences because international students frequently pay full tuition and spend money on housing, food and other services. NAFSA: Association of International Educators estimates the fall decline represents approximately $2.4 billion in lost economic activity and more than 31,000 fewer jobs nationwide.

Despite the downturn, 81% of surveyed colleges and universities said recruiting international students remains a priority, and three-quarters reported maintaining or increasing their international recruitment budgets.

The United States remains one of the world's largest destinations for international higher education, but the latest numbers show the pipeline of new students continuing to shrink.
