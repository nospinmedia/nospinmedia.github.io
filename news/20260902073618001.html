USS Abraham Lincoln Reaches Thailand After 286 Days at Sea

Carrier Begins First Port Visit of Extended Deployment

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 2, 2026 at 7:39 PM ET

The USS Abraham Lincoln arrived at Laem Chabang, Thailand, on Sept. 2 for its first scheduled port visit after spending 286 consecutive days at sea during an extended deployment that included operations in the Middle East. The visit gives nearly 5,000 sailors and Marines aboard the nuclear-powered aircraft carrier and its strike group an opportunity for rest, resupply and maintenance following one of the longest recent operational deployments for a U.S. carrier.

Originally deployed from San Diego in November 2025, the carrier's mission expanded beyond its initial Pacific assignment as regional tensions in the Middle East intensified. During the deployment, the strike group supported U.S. military operations, maintained a sustained naval presence and conducted flight operations over an extended period before beginning its transit across the Indo-Pacific. The port call marks the first significant opportunity for crew liberty since the deployment began.

The Abraham Lincoln arrived with several escort ships from Carrier Strike Group 3, while Thai authorities coordinated transportation, logistics and security for thousands of visiting service members during the planned five-day visit. The stop will allow the Navy to replenish supplies, perform routine maintenance and provide crew members with time ashore before the strike group continues its voyage.

Extended deployments have drawn increased attention in recent years because of their potential effects on personnel readiness, morale and ship maintenance. Navy officials said the Thailand visit is an important milestone in sustaining both the crew and the carrier following months of continuous operations. After completing the scheduled port call, the strike group is expected to continue its return transit across the Pacific.
