Landfill collapse kills 30 in Guinea's capital

Heavy rains bury homes as rescue crews search for survivors

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 24, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

At least 30 people were killed after a massive mound of waste collapsed at the Dar Es Salam landfill in Conakry, Guinea, burying nearby homes and makeshift shelters following heavy overnight rain. The landslide struck around 2 a.m. in the Gbessia commune after torrential rainfall destabilized the city's largest open-air landfill, sending garbage and mud crashing into surrounding neighborhoods. Rescue crews continued searching through the debris with excavators amid fears additional victims could still be trapped.

The Guinean government said six people were seriously injured and 16 others suffered minor injuries, while numerous homes were destroyed. Witnesses said the collapse was large enough to engulf an apartment building along with smaller structures occupied by families living beside the landfill. One resident told reporters she lost all five of her children in the disaster, highlighting the human toll of the collapse.

The tragedy occurred just days after authorities announced plans to permanently close the landfill because of longstanding safety concerns during the rainy season. Officials had issued evacuation notices to residents living nearby, but many families remained in the area when the landfill gave way. Prime Minister Amadou Oury Bah visited the scene to oversee emergency operations, while Territorial Administration Minister Djénabou Touré pledged assistance for survivors and said remaining families would be relocated away from the hazardous site.

The disaster has renewed attention on Guinea's infrastructure challenges despite the country possessing some of the world's largest bauxite reserves and major Simandou iron ore deposits. According to the World Bank, 43.7% of Guinea's population lives below the national poverty line. Authorities said rescue operations would continue until the site is fully searched, while the government reviews additional measures to prevent similar tragedies at vulnerable waste disposal sites.
