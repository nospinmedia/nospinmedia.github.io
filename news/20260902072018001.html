House Votes 413-2 to Censure Rep. Chuck Edwards

Ethics Probe Finds Improper Conduct Toward Staff

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 2, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The U.S. House of Representatives voted 413-2 on Sept. 1 to formally censure Rep. Chuck Edwards, a North Carolina Republican, following a bipartisan House Ethics Committee investigation into his treatment of female staff members. The rare disciplinary action required Edwards to stand in the well of the House while Speaker Mike Johnson publicly read the censure resolution. Edwards and Rep. Ralph Norman of South Carolina cast the only votes against the measure.

The Ethics Committee concluded that Edwards violated standards of conduct governing the workplace after determining he engaged in inappropriate behavior toward two female staff members. According to the committee's report, Edwards gave expensive personal gifts, including jewelry and other items, wrote personal poetry, and created an inappropriate workplace environment that violated House rules governing official conduct. The committee found the conduct was inconsistent with the standards expected of members of Congress and recommended formal censure.

Edwards apologized on the House floor for what he described as poor judgment but denied intentionally sexually harassing anyone. He also announced earlier this year that he would not seek reelection as the ethics investigation continued. The censure represents one of the strongest forms of discipline available to the House short of expulsion and becomes part of the permanent congressional record.

Only 29 members of the House have been formally censured in the chamber's history, making the action relatively uncommon. The overwhelming bipartisan vote reflected broad agreement with the Ethics Committee's findings and recommendations following its months-long investigation. While censure does not remove a lawmaker from office, it serves as an official public condemnation by the House for conduct found to violate its ethical standards.
