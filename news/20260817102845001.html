DR Congo Ebola Outbreak Becomes Nation's Deadliest

Death toll reaches 2,325 as Bundibugyo strain spreads across six provinces

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Democratic Republic of the Congo has recorded the deadliest Ebola outbreak in its history after health authorities confirmed 2,325 deaths as of August 16, surpassing the 2,299 deaths reported during the 2018–2020 outbreak in eastern Congo. Officials also reported 4,945 confirmed infections, including 101 new cases during the latest reporting period, highlighting the continued acceleration of the epidemic.

The outbreak, declared on May 15, 2026, has spread across 55 health zones in Ituri, North Kivu, Haut-Uélé, Tshopo, South Kivu, and Bas-Uélé. Health officials say the epidemic is being driven by the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, which currently has no approved vaccine or virus-specific antiviral treatment. The absence of proven medical countermeasures has complicated efforts to slow transmission and reduce fatalities.

Response operations continue to face significant obstacles. The World Health Organization, Congolese health officials, and humanitarian organizations have cited armed conflict, attacks on medical teams, unpaid healthcare workers, and community distrust as major barriers to containing the outbreak. U.N. humanitarian chief Tom Fletcher warned the epidemic is spreading at an unprecedented pace, while WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus has called for additional international funding, personnel, and medical supplies to prevent the crisis from worsening.

The outbreak has already exceeded the country's previous Ebola record in just a few months and continues to expand geographically. Public health officials say rapid case identification, isolation, contact tracing, and safe burial practices remain the most effective tools available while researchers continue evaluating potential treatments and vaccines for the Bundibugyo strain.
