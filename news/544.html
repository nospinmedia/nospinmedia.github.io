California curbs AI posing as doctors

Details inside

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 14, 2025 at 7:49 AM ET

California has enacted a law prohibiting artificial intelligence systems from presenting themselves in ways that imply they are licensed medical professionals. The new measure, A.B. 489, bans AI chatbots and tools from using titles or language that could mislead users into thinking they are receiving advice from a doctor or nurse. Supporters say the law aims to prevent confusion and protect consumers as AI tools become more common in health contexts.

Developers of AI health tools must now clearly disclose that users are interacting with software rather than a human clinician. Regulators can take action against systems or marketing materials that blur this distinction. The law draws a line between general wellness support and the practice of medicine, which remains governed by licensing boards and strict professional standards.

The legislation follows global concern over AI tools that offer symptom checks, mental health support, or personalized guidance. Studies show some users may overestimate the authority of these tools, treating them like clinical advisors. Lawmakers argue that guardrails are necessary before cases of harm or misdiagnosis become widespread.

Critics warn that overly rigid rules may slow innovation, especially for underserved communities relying on low-cost digital tools. Supporters counter that clarity will ultimately improve trust and product safety. Where should regulators draw the line between helpful guidance and misleading impersonation?
