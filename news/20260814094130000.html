President Trump Imposes Up To 100% Tariffs On Imported Drones

Heavy And Thermal-Capable Models Face Highest Duties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

President Trump signed a proclamation on August 13, 2026, imposing tariffs of up to 100% on imported unmanned aircraft systems and key drone components under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act, citing national security concerns and the need to strengthen domestic manufacturing. The action follows a Department of Commerce investigation led by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick into foreign reliance within the U.S. drone industry.

Under the order, drones with a maximum takeoff weight exceeding 25 kilograms, along with aircraft equipped with thermal imaging systems and certain related hardware, will face a 100% tariff. Smaller consumer and commercial drones that do not meet those criteria will generally be subject to a 25% duty. Reduced tariff rates apply to qualifying imports from several allied economies, including 15% for the European Union, Japan, Liechtenstein, South Korea, Switzerland, and Taiwan, while qualifying imports from the United Kingdom will face a 10% tariff. Most provisions take effect on September 3, 2026, while some component tariffs will phase in after 180 days.

Industry analysts expect the new tariffs to increase costs for sectors including public safety, utilities, construction, agriculture, and infrastructure inspection, where larger and thermal-equipped drones are widely used. The proclamation also directs the Commerce Department to develop incentives intended to expand U.S. drone manufacturing and reduce dependence on foreign suppliers. Industry analysts frequently estimate that DJI Technologies accounts for roughly 70% of the U.S. commercial drone market, meaning the new tariffs could significantly affect purchasing decisions across multiple industries.

The announcement builds on previous federal restrictions involving foreign-made drone technology and reflects the administration's broader use of Section 232 authorities to address supply chain and national security concerns. Additional guidance on product classifications, implementation, and eligibility for reduced allied tariff rates is expected before the first duties take effect in early September.
