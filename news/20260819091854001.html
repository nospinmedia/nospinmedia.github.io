Trump Pauses 50% Canada Tariffs for Three Days

U.S. and Canada race to finalize trade agreement

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 19, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

President Trump late Tuesday paused planned 50% tariffs on Canadian imports for three days, saying the delay will allow the United States and Canada to finalize a tentative trade agreement before the duties take effect. In a social media post, President Trump said the tariffs would be paused “subject to the finalization of documents.” The postponement runs through the end of August 21, 2026, temporarily easing pressure on businesses and supply chains that rely on cross-border trade.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed the delay, saying negotiators had made substantial progress but were still working through technical details. The proposed tariffs, announced under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, would apply to roughly $20 billion in Canadian exports, including products such as alcoholic beverages, dairy items and sporting goods. The dispute affects one of the world's largest trading relationships, with approximately $880 billion in annual bilateral trade.

The three-day pause temporarily avoids a major escalation that Canadian officials had warned would trigger retaliatory measures. Administration officials say negotiations remain focused on long-running disputes involving market access, digital trade and broader economic security issues. President Trump also suggested the developing agreement could include discussion of reviving the Keystone XL Pipeline, though no formal commitment has been announced by either government.

Negotiators are now working to complete the legal framework before the pause expires at 11:59 p.m. on August 21. If no agreement is reached, the planned tariffs could still take effect, renewing uncertainty for manufacturers, exporters and consumers on both sides of the border. The outcome of the talks will likely shape North American trade relations well beyond the immediate dispute and could influence future discussions surrounding the USMCA trade framework.
