Record U.S. Cyclospora outbreak declared over

Nearly 13,000 sickened, but contamination source remains unresolved

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

U.S. health officials have declared the nation's largest recorded Cyclospora outbreak over after nearly 13,000 people were sickened in a multistate outbreak linked to processed iceberg lettuce. The CDC announced the end of the outbreak on September 11, while the FDA said it is confident that all recalled lettuce associated with the outbreak is no longer available in stores or restaurants.

The outbreak was linked through epidemiologic and traceback evidence to iceberg lettuce processed by Taylor Farms de Mexico in central Mexico. Federal investigators ultimately counted 12,883 illnesses across 21 states, including 570 hospitalizations and two deaths. Taylor Farms voluntarily recalled implicated lettuce in July after investigators traced illnesses to products served or sold through multiple businesses. The outbreak represented the largest known U.S. cluster of cyclosporiasis, an intestinal illness caused by the parasite Cyclospora cayetanensis.

What remains unresolved is exactly how the lettuce became contaminated. Cyclospora spreads through food or water contaminated with human feces, but investigators have not determined the specific environmental or production failure that allowed contamination on such a large scale. The FDA has completed onsite inspections and sample collection at lettuce-growing operations in Mexico and says its investigation is continuing even though the outbreak itself is considered over.

The lettuce-linked outbreak also does not account for every U.S. Cyclospora case reported this year. Since May 1, federal officials have received reports of nearly 20,000 laboratory-confirmed cases, more than 16 times the number reported during the comparable period in 2025. Thousands of those illnesses have not been tied to the Taylor Farms outbreak and remain part of separate investigations.

For consumers, officials say the recalled lettuce has passed its best-by dates and should no longer be in commerce, meaning there is no current recommendation to avoid lettuce generally because of this outbreak. Standard produce-safety precautions remain advised, although washing produce cannot guarantee removal of Cyclospora.
