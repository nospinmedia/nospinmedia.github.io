South Korea says North Korean mines caused DMZ blasts

Three soldiers injured as Seoul demands apology

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 6, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff says a joint investigation with the United Nations Command concluded that North Korean anti-personnel mines deliberately planted south of the Military Demarcation Line caused two explosions that injured three South Korean soldiers inside the Demilitarized Zone. Seoul is demanding an apology from Pyongyang and the removal of remaining mines.

The explosions occurred on September 21 in the western section of the DMZ while South Korean troops were clearing a route through an area that had rarely been accessed for decades. Two soldiers suffered serious injuries and a third was less seriously wounded. Investigators said GPS measurements placed the two blast sites and another mine discovered nearby about 10 meters south of the Military Demarcation Line, on the South Korean side.

The military said forensic evidence also pointed to North Korea. The mines contained TNT and thermosetting resin consistent with North Korean anti-personnel mines, while fragments recovered from the injured soldiers matched North Korean plastic mines previously recovered from the Imjin River. Investigators concluded the mines had been deliberately buried rather than carried south by flooding, which has sometimes moved North Korean mines across the border.

South Korean forces began removing mines around the blast site on October 5 and said operations would continue if North Korea does not remove them. The United Nations Command, which oversees the 1953 Korean War Armistice Agreement, separately determined that an active North Korean mine discovered south of the demarcation line constituted an armistice violation.

North Korea has denied responsibility. Kim Yo Jong, sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, dismissed Seoul's allegations as fabricated and rejected demands for an apology. The dispute comes as North Korea continues reinforcing sections of the border with mines, barriers and fencing, while South Korea's government has sought to reopen dialogue with Pyongyang.
