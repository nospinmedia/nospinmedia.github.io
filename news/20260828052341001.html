Trump orders Lake Ontario renamed 'Lake America'

Federal agencies given 30 days to update U.S. maps

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 28, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

President Trump signed an executive order directing all federal agencies to begin using the name "Lake America" instead of Lake Ontario in official U.S. government maps, records and databases. The order instructs the Department of the Interior to update the Geographic Names Information System (GNIS) within 30 days, making the new designation the federal standard for domestic government use. The action applies only to U.S. federal agencies and does not change the internationally recognized name of the lake or require other countries to adopt it.

Speaking at the White House, Trump said the United States plays a major role in protecting, maintaining and economically benefiting from the Great Lakes system and argued that the new name better reflects America's contribution. The administration said federal agencies should begin implementing the change once the Interior Department completes the required updates, while maps and documents produced outside the federal government would not be required to adopt the new designation.

Canadian officials quickly rejected the move. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Lake Ontario has carried its historic name for centuries and noted that it originates from the Wendat language. Because the lake is shared by both countries, Canada's official maps and records will continue using the existing name. International organizations and most commercial map providers are also expected to continue recognizing Lake Ontario unless they independently choose otherwise.

The executive order comes during a period of heightened tensions between the United States and Canada over trade and economic policy. It follows Trump's earlier executive action renaming the Gulf of Mexico as the Gulf of America for U.S. federal purposes. While supporters describe the latest order as a symbolic assertion of national identity, critics argue it is unlikely to have any legal effect outside the United States and may further strain relations between the two neighboring countries.
