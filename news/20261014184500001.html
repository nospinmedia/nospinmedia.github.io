Six Flags Shuts Down 9 Roller Coasters Across 6 Parks

Safety Reviews Follow Fatal Fall From Georgia's Goliath Ride

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 2:45 PM ET

Six Flags has temporarily closed nine roller coasters across six North American amusement parks following the death of a man who was ejected from a ride at Six Flags Over Georgia on Monday, October 12, 2026.

The victim, identified as 42-year-old Hassan Jihad Ibn Kariem of Atlanta, died after falling from the park's Goliath roller coaster, which reaches approximately 200 feet high and travels at speeds of up to 70 miles per hour. Kariem worked as an information technology manager at a local school.

According to preliminary findings from the Cobb County Police Department, Kariem struck other passengers before falling beneath the attraction. Several people were injured, although authorities have not provided a complete accounting of their injuries.

Witnesses described seeing Kariem rise out of his seat as the roller coaster traveled over a hill near the end of the ride. Investigators have not determined why he was ejected or whether the restraint system malfunctioned.

In response, Six Flags suspended operations on nine similar attractions while safety reviews continue. The company said the closures were precautionary and emphasized its commitment to protecting guests and employees.

Among the affected attractions are Nitro at Six Flags Great Adventure in New Jersey, Raging Bull at Six Flags Great America in Illinois, and Goliath at Six Flags Magic Mountain in California. The company has not announced reopening dates for the affected rides.

The investigation involves Cobb County police, Georgia's state amusement ride safety authorities, and the roller coaster's manufacturer, Bolliger & Mabillard. Investigators are expected to examine maintenance records, restraint systems, operating procedures, witness accounts, and available video footage.

The incident has renewed questions about amusement park safety oversight. Unlike traveling carnival rides, permanent amusement park attractions generally fall under state rather than federal regulatory authority. Inspection requirements therefore vary across the country.

Roller coaster accidents resulting in fatalities remain uncommon, but the Georgia incident has raised concerns about restraint design, inspection procedures, and whether consistent safety standards should apply nationwide.

The closures represent a significant development beyond the original fatal accident, affecting attractions thousands of miles from Georgia while investigators work to establish what happened.
