Pakistan Imposes Emergency Rule in Imran Khan Party Stronghold

Provincial Government Suspended as Army Deploys Amid Political Unrest

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 12, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Pakistan has declared a state of emergency in its northwestern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, suspending the regional government controlled by the party of jailed former Prime Minister Imran Khan. The unprecedented intervention in the province's current political leadership comes amid escalating militant violence and growing tensions between Khan's supporters and the federal government.

President Asif Ali Zardari approved the emergency measures on Sunday, October 11, following recommendations from Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif's cabinet. The decision invokes Articles 232 and 234 of Pakistan's Constitution, allowing federal authorities to assume provincial government responsibilities under specified emergency conditions.

The federal government accused the provincial administration, led by Khan's Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf party, of failing to cooperate adequately with counterterrorism efforts. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa borders Afghanistan and has experienced increasing militant attacks, creating serious security challenges for authorities.

Under the new arrangement, Governor Faisal Karim Kundi will oversee provincial administration with assistance from an advisory council. Federal Law Minister Azam Nazeer Tarar said the emergency administration was initially planned for six months.

Pakistan's government also authorized the deployment of army personnel to support civilian authorities and protect residents, public property, and government installations. The military deployment adds another dimension to an already tense confrontation between federal officials and the opposition-controlled province.

Khan's party strongly rejected the decision. PTI spokesperson Zulfikar Bukhari characterized the intervention as a disguised political takeover and warned that it could deepen instability. Opposition leaders have also questioned the constitutional justification for removing the elected provincial administration.

The emergency declaration comes as PTI supporters prepare further demonstrations demanding Khan's release from prison. Authorities have erected shipping-container barricades and increased security around Islamabad to prevent large opposition marches from entering the capital.

Khan, who served as prime minister from 2018 until his removal in 2022, remains imprisoned while challenging multiple criminal convictions and legal proceedings. His supporters maintain that the cases against him are politically motivated, an allegation rejected by government authorities.

The confrontation raises broader questions about provincial autonomy, constitutional emergency powers, and how Pakistan can address militant violence while maintaining democratic governance.
