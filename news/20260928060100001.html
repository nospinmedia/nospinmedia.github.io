Serbia's president resigns to run for prime minister

Vučić enters October election after nearly two years of protests

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 28, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Serbian President Aleksandar Vučić resigned on September 27 to run for prime minister in a snap parliamentary election, ending his second presidential term early as a student-led movement mounts a major challenge to his governing Serbian Progressive Party. Serbia's parliamentary election is scheduled for October 25, while a new presidential election must be held by the end of December.

Vučić formally signed his resignation after addressing the public at the presidential palace in Belgrade. He said he wanted to participate in the parliamentary campaign as an ordinary citizen and would transfer his presidential duties to National Assembly Speaker Ana Brnabić, who will serve as acting president. Vučić was first elected president in 2017 and reelected in 2022; Serbia's constitution prevents him from seeking a third presidential term.

The election follows nearly two years of demonstrations that began after a concrete canopy collapsed at the railway station in Novi Sad on November 1, 2024, killing 16 people. Students demanding accountability for the disaster expanded their campaign into a broader movement focused on corruption, government transparency and political influence over public institutions. Vučić and his allies have rejected allegations that they bear responsibility for the problems cited by protesters.

Vučić will lead the SNS electoral list and seek the prime minister's office, a position he previously held before becoming president. His opponents include the student-backed Students Win movement, whose candidate list includes university professors and other professionals. The group is campaigning on anti-corruption measures, investigations of allegedly improperly acquired property and greater transparency in public spending.

Shortly before resigning, Vučić also announced pardons for 49 people, including 23 connected to events surrounding the protests. He has said that if his opponents win the October election, he will recognize the result.
