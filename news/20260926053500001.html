Trump moves to cancel $810 million approved by Congress

Lawmakers challenge use of disputed pocket rescission

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 26, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

President Trump moved on September 25 to cancel $810 million in federal spending previously approved by Congress, using a disputed maneuver known as a pocket rescission. The administration sent the rescission request to lawmakers just days before the September 30 end of the fiscal year, when the affected funding is scheduled to expire.

Under the Impoundment Control Act of 1974, a president may ask Congress to rescind previously appropriated money, and Congress generally has 45 legislative days to consider the request. In a pocket rescission, however, a request is submitted close enough to the expiration of the funds that the money can lapse before Congress completes that review. The Office of Management and Budget, led by Director Russell Vought, argues that the administration has authority to use the procedure.

The largest proposed cancellation is approximately $567 million in Department of Health and Human Services funding connected to refugee and other resettlement services. The package also targets funding administered by the Department of Education, Department of Homeland Security, Department of Housing and Urban Development and Treasury Department, including programs involving migrant education, legal assistance and housing counseling.

The move drew objections from lawmakers, including Republican Sen. Susan Collins of Maine, chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee. Collins has previously argued that allowing congressionally approved money to expire through a pocket rescission violates Congress's constitutional power of the purse. The nonpartisan Government Accountability Office has also concluded that the Impoundment Control Act does not permit funds to be withheld through their expiration date. The administration disputes that interpretation.

The new package follows the administration's 2025 attempt to cancel $4.9 billion in foreign aid through the same mechanism, a dispute that produced legal challenges over presidential and congressional spending authority.
