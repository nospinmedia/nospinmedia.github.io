U.S. Sanctions Ecuador Cocaine Smuggling Network

Treasury Blocks 15 Targets and 10 Fishing Vessels

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 21, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The U.S. Department of the Treasury has imposed sanctions on 15 individuals and entities and identified 10 Ecuadorian commercial fishing vessels as blocked property for their alleged roles in a large-scale cocaine trafficking network that moved narcotics from Ecuador through Mexico to the United States. The action, announced on August 20, targets a maritime operation based near Manta, Ecuador, that authorities say concealed drug trafficking activities behind legitimate commercial fishing businesses.

According to the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC), the network used fishing vessels to transfer cocaine to high-speed "go-fast" boats traveling through the Eastern Pacific Ocean while also supplying fuel, food, and other logistical support during the voyages. U.S. officials said the cocaine shipments were ultimately received in Mexican waters by the Sinaloa Cartel and the Cártel de Jalisco Nueva Generación (CJNG) before being smuggled across the U.S. border. Treasury officials also linked the network to Ecuadorian criminal organizations Los Choneros and Los Lobos, both previously designated by the United States as Foreign Terrorist Organizations.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the sanctions were intended to disrupt the financial infrastructure supporting transnational drug trafficking organizations. The designations, issued under Executive Order 14059, freeze any U.S.-based assets held by the sanctioned individuals and entities while prohibiting American citizens and businesses from conducting transactions with them. Officials identified members of the Mero family and the fishing company Arcasdenoe S.A. among the principal targets, alleging they used commercial fishing operations to support maritime cocaine smuggling.

The sanctions are part of expanding U.S.-Ecuador cooperation against organized crime as Ecuador continues battling escalating cartel violence and growing use of its Pacific coastline as a cocaine export route. U.S. officials said the financial restrictions complement ongoing maritime interdiction operations aimed at disrupting trafficking networks before shipments reach North America.
