Saudi Arabia Investigates Air Defense Failures After Deadly Airport Attacks

13 Killed, Hundreds Injured as Officials Examine How Houthi Missiles Broke Through

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 13, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Saudi Arabia has launched an investigation into why its air defense systems failed to stop a series of missile and drone attacks by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels, including strikes on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh that killed at least 13 people and injured more than 300. The investigation, reported on Monday, October 12, 2026, raises questions about the kingdom's ability to protect critical civilian infrastructure despite its extensive military defenses.

Two regional officials familiar with the investigation said preliminary findings indicate the Houthis launched multiple drones before sending a combination of cruise and ballistic missiles toward Riyadh. Investigators are examining whether the initial drone attacks complicated Saudi defenses and why warning systems failed to provide sufficient advance notice. The weapons traveled more than 1,000 kilometers, or approximately 620 miles, from Yemen before reaching the Saudi capital.

The attacks began escalating last week, with a particularly deadly strike on Saturday, October 10, that killed 12 people, including an American citizen, and wounded more than 300 others. Another projectile struck the airport's passenger terminal on Sunday, killing a Pakistani national and injuring 11 people. An Indian citizen subsequently died from injuries sustained in Saturday's attack, bringing the reported death toll to at least 13.

Saudi Arabia uses American-made Patriot missile defense systems and other interceptors to protect its territory. However, Houthi forces possess missiles equipped with maneuverable warheads that can complicate interception. Investigators have not publicly established whether equipment limitations, operational failures or the combination of different incoming weapons caused the defenses to be breached.

The attacks have severely disrupted commercial aviation. Riyadh's international airport has remained closed, airlines have suspended flights, and foreign governments have warned travelers about continuing security risks. Saudi Arabia has also sought additional air defense assistance from international partners as its interceptor supplies face increasing pressure.

President Trump has indicated that the United States is considering greater involvement in Saudi Arabia's military campaign against the Houthis, although no decision on direct American intervention has been announced.

The escalation also threatens international energy markets and commercial shipping near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a strategically important passage connecting the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden. Saudi Arabia's confrontation with the Houthis has intensified alongside the broader regional conflict involving Iran.
