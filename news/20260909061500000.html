TSA restores gate access for some people without plane tickets

PreCheck members can enter secure areas at 13 airports

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 9, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

The Transportation Security Administration has launched a new program allowing some people without plane tickets to pass through airport security and enter gate areas, restoring a tradition that largely disappeared after the September 11, 2001 attacks. The free Gateside by TSA PreCheck program is initially available at 13 U.S. airports and is open to eligible TSA PreCheck members and participants in other trusted-traveler programs who have an active Known Traveler Number.

Approved visitors can use the program to accompany departing travelers to their gates, meet arriving passengers, visit friends during layovers or access restaurants and shops beyond security. Applicants must request access online one to three days before their planned airport visit and receive approval before arriving. Once approved, visitors present an acceptable government-issued photo ID and their approval status, then go through a designated TSA PreCheck lane. Access is valid for one calendar day, with same-day re-entry permitted.

The first participating airports are Mesa Gateway, John Glenn Columbus, Dallas-Fort Worth, Detroit Metropolitan, Wichita Dwight D. Eisenhower, Indianapolis, Harry Reid in Las Vegas, Los Angeles International, Bill and Hillary Clinton National, Will Rogers International, Eppley Airfield, San Diego International and Salt Lake City International. TSA says additional airports are expected to join in the coming months.

Before 9/11, friends and relatives commonly accompanied passengers to airport gates. That changed after the attacks led to sweeping aviation-security reforms and the creation of the TSA, with secure gate areas generally restricted to ticketed passengers and authorized personnel. Some airports later created their own limited visitor-pass programs, but Gateside establishes a broader TSA-run system built around existing trusted-traveler screening.

TSA says the number of passes may be limited during busy travel periods to prevent congestion and maintain security operations.
