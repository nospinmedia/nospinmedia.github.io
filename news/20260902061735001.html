Pennsylvania Officer Charged in Fatal Student Shooting

Prosecutors Say Bodycam Contradicts Account

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 2, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

A Pennsylvania police officer has been charged with voluntary manslaughter in the fatal shooting of an unarmed college student after prosecutors said body-camera footage contradicted the officer's account of the encounter. Montgomery County District Attorney Kevin Steele announced the charges against Upper Pottsgrove Township Police Cpl. Sean Farrell on Sept. 1, concluding that the use of deadly force was not legally justified based on the evidence gathered during the investigation.

The shooting occurred on Aug. 23, when 22-year-old Glenwood Earl Pysher IV, a senior at Saint Elizabeth University, mistakenly went to the wrong home after becoming separated from friends during a night out. The home's occupants called 911 believing someone was attempting to enter the residence. According to prosecutors, Farrell encountered Pysher outside the home and fired two shots within seconds. Steele said body-camera video showed Pysher was unarmed, had his hands visible and did not charge or aggressively advance toward the officer, contradicting Farrell's statements to dispatchers and investigators.

Steele said investigators determined Farrell had access to less-lethal options, including a department-issued Taser, but instead used deadly force. Based on the evidence, prosecutors filed charges under Pennsylvania law that include voluntary manslaughter and related offenses. The criminal case will now proceed through the court system, where prosecutors must prove the allegations beyond a reasonable doubt.

Court records did not immediately indicate a public response from Farrell or his attorney following the filing of the charges. The case has drawn widespread attention as another closely watched examination of police use of force and the role of body-camera evidence in evaluating officers' actions. The investigation's findings are expected to remain under scrutiny as the prosecution moves forward.
