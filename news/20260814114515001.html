Pentagon Says Iran Maritime Enforcement Can Continue Indefinitely

Treasury Plans Tougher Sanctions To Deepen Economic Pressure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

The United States signaled it is preparing for a prolonged campaign to pressure Iran as Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said on August 13, 2026 that American naval forces can sustain maritime enforcement operations against Iranian shipping for "indefinitely" if necessary. Speaking during a visit to Panama, Hegseth said the U.S. Navy has sufficient personnel and vessels to rotate forces while maintaining operations aimed at protecting commercial shipping and increasing pressure on Tehran as diplomatic efforts remain stalled.

According to U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM), enforcement operations have redirected 55 commercial vessels, disabled three that failed to comply with instructions, and carried out two boardings since the current operation resumed on July 14, 2026. U.S. officials say the mission was reestablished after a temporary ceasefire collapsed amid renewed attacks and escalating tensions around the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world's most important energy shipping routes.

The military campaign is expected to be paired with additional financial pressure. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has indicated the administration is preparing further sanctions and related measures intended to tighten restrictions on Iran's economy and reduce revenue available to the government. Officials describe the economic actions as complementing ongoing maritime enforcement while continuing diplomatic efforts to deter further regional escalation.

The security risks facing commercial shipping remain significant. On July 14, two ADNOC Logistics & Services crude tankers — the Mombasa B and Al Bahyah — were struck while operating near the Strait of Hormuz, leaving at least one sailor dead and several others injured. U.S. officials cite attacks such as those as evidence that sustained naval operations remain necessary to help safeguard international shipping lanes, while Iranian officials continue to criticize the American-led operation.
