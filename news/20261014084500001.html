Houthi Missile Attack Hits Saudi Aramco Headquarters

Fire Erupts at Dhahran Complex as Saudi Forces Strike Yemen's Oil Facilities

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Saudi Aramco's headquarters complex in Dhahran, Saudi Arabia, was struck during a missile attack on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, as fighting intensified between the kingdom and Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels. An administrative building caught fire, but Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Energy reported no injuries or fatalities. The attack came amid Saudi-led airstrikes against Houthi-controlled energy infrastructure in Yemen, raising concerns about the security of oil facilities in one of the world's most important petroleum-producing regions.

The Saudi Energy Ministry confirmed that an Aramco administrative building in the kingdom's Eastern Province had been targeted and that emergency crews responded to a limited fire. Reuters, citing a company source, reported that an office tower inside Aramco's headquarters complex was struck and its upper floors damaged. Saudi authorities had not publicly identified the specific building involved. The extent of the damage remained under assessment, and officials had not confirmed whether the incident affected oil production or exports.

The attack occurred as the Saudi-led coalition carried out airstrikes against Houthi-controlled facilities in Yemen. Targets included the Al-Sabahah fuel station near Sanaa and fuel-loading infrastructure at the Red Sea port of Hodeidah. Coalition spokesman Major General Turki al-Maliki said the operations were intended to weaken facilities supporting Houthi military activities. The coalition said precautions were taken to limit civilian harm.

Saudi air defenses also intercepted missiles and drones targeting several areas of the kingdom, including Riyadh, Dhahran, and Al-Ahsa Governorate. Saudi Civil Defense reported that debris from two intercepted missiles fell into residential neighborhoods in Riyadh, injuring four civilians and damaging 15 homes. Separately, missile fragments were reported in a residential area of Dhahran.

The latest escalation follows deadly attacks on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh that killed 13 people and injured hundreds. The Houthis have threatened additional strikes against Saudi energy infrastructure, describing oil installations as military targets.

Oil prices rose Wednesday as traders assessed the possibility of further attacks on Saudi facilities and disruptions to regional energy supplies. Although the Aramco headquarters attack caused no reported casualties, the incident highlighted the vulnerability of critical infrastructure as the conflict expands.
