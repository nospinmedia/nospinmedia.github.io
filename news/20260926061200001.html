Banned news outlets return to White House after court order

Some journalists were initially denied entry despite restored access

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 26, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Journalists from CNN, MS NOW and Politico returned to the White House on September 24 after a federal judge ordered the Trump administration to immediately restore their access, ending a dayslong standoff that disrupted normal television coverage of President Trump. Some journalists from the three organizations were initially turned away Thursday morning before being allowed back onto the grounds later in the day.

U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly, who was appointed by Trump, issued a temporary restraining order shortly after midnight requiring the administration to reverse the ban while the outlets' lawsuit proceeds. The organizations sued after their White House credentials were deactivated following Trump's announcement that they would be barred because of reporting he characterized as false or unfair. They argued that excluding them based on their coverage violated constitutional protections.

The ban had effects beyond the three organizations. Major television networks declined to replace CNN in the rotating White House television pool, resulting in several days without the normal independent TV pool coverage that provides video and audio of presidential events to news organizations nationwide. The pool resumed operations after access was restored.

Implementation of Kelly's order was not immediate. PBS NewsHour reported that some staff from the affected organizations were again denied entry Thursday morning. As the outlets returned to federal court seeking enforcement, CNN and MS NOW correspondents were readmitted and Politico reporters were also allowed back onto White House grounds.

The temporary order restores access while the broader lawsuit continues; it does not resolve the underlying constitutional dispute over the White House's authority to revoke press credentials. The case centers on whether the administration can restrict journalists' access because of objections to their reporting and what procedural protections must be provided before credentials are withdrawn.
