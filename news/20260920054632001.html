President Trump announces new "AI Force"

AI czar planned as administration rejects development slowdown

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 20, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump says his administration will create an "AI Force" and appoint an "AI Czar" as the White House pushes for continued rapid development of artificial intelligence despite growing calls for additional safeguards. Trump compared the proposed organization to the creation of the U.S. Space Force, but the administration has not yet publicly detailed the AI Force's size, membership, budget, legal authority or where it would operate within the federal government.

Trump announced the initiative on September 19, saying the United States should foster AI development rather than slow it. He described fears that advanced AI could eventually threaten humanity as a "hoax" and said existing criminal and civil laws could be used against harmful applications of the technology. Trump also said AI could eventually account for as much as 25% of U.S. gross domestic product, presenting that figure as his own projection while emphasizing competition with China.

The planned AI czar would follow David Sacks, whom Trump named White House AI and cryptocurrency czar in December 2024. Sacks later ended his service as a special government employee but remains a technology adviser and co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology. Trump has not announced who will receive the new AI czar appointment.

The announcement comes amid a broader dispute over how quickly increasingly capable AI systems should be developed. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has called for slowing frontier development, while former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon has warned about potential dangers from future self-improving systems. Other technology leaders, including Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, have argued that development should continue rapidly while companies ensure products are safe before release.

The White House has separately developed a voluntary framework for evaluating advanced AI models, but details of that framework have not been publicly released.
