Pilot Killed as Small Plane Strikes Pennsylvania State Police Helicopter

NTSB and FAA open probe into Carlisle Airport collision

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 20, 2026 at 9:08 AM ET

A pilot died and two Pennsylvania State Police troopers were injured after a small plane collided with a state police helicopter at Carlisle Airport in Cumberland County, Pennsylvania, on Wednesday evening. Authorities said the crash involved a Cessna 150 and a Bell 407 assigned to the agency’s aviation unit. The pilot, the airplane’s sole occupant, was pronounced dead at the scene, while the troopers suffered non-life-threatening injuries.

The collision occurred just before 7:00 p.m. EDT while the helicopter crew was conducting low-altitude maneuvers near the runway. Preliminary information indicates the inbound Cessna 150 struck the helicopter during its landing approach, causing the Bell 407 to overturn. State police later identified the injured troopers as Cpl. Bryce Corman and Tpr. Jason Mills. Officials said Corman kicked out a helicopter window and helped pull Mills to safety after the crash, despite Mills suffering more extensive injuries.

The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) and the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) have launched a joint investigation into the collision. Investigators will examine flight paths, weather conditions, radio communications, airport operations, and the performance of both aircraft to determine what caused the accident. Officials emphasized that all information released so far is preliminary and that no conclusions have been reached.

Authorities have not yet released the identity of the pilot or details about the flight's origin and destination. Investigators are expected to remain at the scene while documenting evidence before issuing a preliminary report in the coming weeks. A final determination of probable cause typically takes much longer as the NTSB completes its analysis.
