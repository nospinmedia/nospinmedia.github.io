Six major ferry disasters in six weeks as another 130 go missing

Indonesia capsizing adds to deadly global string of accidents

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 13, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Another major passenger-ferry disaster is unfolding, adding to an extraordinary recent string of deadly accidents around the world. Indonesian rescuers were searching Sunday for about 130 people missing after the Virgo Transport 8 capsized in the Java Sea, with 6 people confirmed dead and at least 107 rescued. The vessel was carrying 243 passengers and crew from Surabaya in East Java to Banjarmasin on Borneo when it encountered rough weather and lost contact early September 13.

The Indonesia disaster is at least the sixth major passenger-ferry accident to receive international attention in roughly six weeks. The recent incidents include: - August 2 — Indonesia: The KMP Mutiara Sentosa II caught fire off East Java. Authorities ultimately accounted for 233 survivors and 5 deaths. - August 11 — Zimbabwe: An overloaded ferry capsized on Lake Kariba. Authorities had recovered at least 92 bodies by August 17 while recovery efforts continued. - August 30 — Northern Cyprus: A ferry carrying about 267 people capsized after leaving Kyrenia. At least 8 people died and roughly 18 remained missing in subsequent reporting. - September 9 — Philippines: A passenger ferry caught fire while traveling from Manila toward Coron. The confirmed toll has reached 76 dead, with 13 people still missing. - September 11 — Vanuatu: The MV Matui sank in rough seas between Santo and Ambae. At least 1 person died, 15 were found alive and more than 30 remained missing as search operations continued. - September 13 — Indonesia: The Virgo Transport 8 capsized in the Java Sea, leaving 6 confirmed dead and about 130 missing in the latest search figures.

The accidents occurred in different countries and under different circumstances, and authorities have not identified evidence of a common cause or a broader worldwide increase in ferry disasters. Rough weather was involved in several incidents, while investigators have also examined issues including overloading, fire, vessel condition and maritime safety enforcement. Indonesia, with more than 17,000 islands, relies heavily on passenger ships, and serious maritime accidents have repeatedly raised concerns about enforcement of safety standards.
