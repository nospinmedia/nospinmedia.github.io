U.S. bans nearly $1 billion in Canadian imports

Alcohol, dairy products and motorcycles face new restrictions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 30, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The United States has imposed outright bans on nearly $1 billion worth of Canadian imports, including alcoholic beverages, some dairy products and motorcycles, escalating an already tense trade dispute between the neighboring countries. The restrictions ordered by President Trump took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on September 29 and go beyond tariffs by preventing the targeted Canadian products from entering the U.S. market.

The restrictions cover an estimated $967 million in Canadian imports based on 2025 trade figures, according to an analysis by the American Action Forum cited by AP. Alcohol accounts for about 87% of that amount. The affected products include certain Canadian beer, wine and spirits, dairy products including whey, and motorcycles. Quebec-based BRP Inc. said its three-wheeled Can-Am Spyder and Canyon motorcycles are among the vehicles excluded from the U.S. market.

The bans are the latest step in a trade fight that intensified over the summer. President Trump previously imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion in Canadian products, arguing that Canadian policies discriminate against U.S. dairy, automotive and alcohol producers. Canada responded with tariffs ranging from 15% to 50% on a comparable amount of American goods. The new U.S. import prohibitions were imposed after that Canadian retaliation.

The immediate economic effect may be relatively small compared with approximately $880 billion in annual two-way trade between the United States and Canada. Some targeted products were already subject to tariffs high enough to make importing them uneconomical. Larger companies may also have ways to limit the impact: Crown Royal can ship whisky in bulk for processing in the United States, while Labatt has U.S. bottling operations. Smaller Canadian breweries and distilleries that rely on shipping finished products across the border have fewer alternatives.

The restrictions add another complication to U.S.-Canada trade relations as the countries approach discussions over the future of the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA).
