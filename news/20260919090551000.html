President Trump says three news outlets are banned from White House

CNN, MS NOW and Politico challenge threatened access restrictions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 19, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump said on September 18 that he was banning CNN, MS NOW and Politico from the White House, accusing the three news organizations of reporting what he called "fake news." Trump said the action was effective immediately and suggested additional outlets could face similar restrictions, but he provided no details about how the ban would be implemented.

There was no immediate indication Friday that the restrictions were actually being enforced. Journalists from the three organizations remained on the White House grounds later that afternoon, according to The Associated Press. CNN and Politico said they would defend their rights to report on the administration, while press organizations criticized restrictions based on dissatisfaction with an outlet's coverage.

The dispute raises questions about how the government may regulate access after establishing press facilities for credentialed journalists. In Sherrill v. Knight, a federal appeals court ruled in 1977 that although the White House is not required to provide journalists access, once press facilities are established, access cannot be denied arbitrarily or based on the content of a journalist's reporting. The ruling also established procedural protections when credentials are denied.

The announcement follows other disputes between the administration and news organizations. The Associated Press sued after its reporters were restricted from some presidential events following the outlet's decision to continue using the name Gulf of Mexico while also noting Trump's order renaming it the Gulf of America. That legal dispute remains ongoing.

The latest announcement could also affect the traditional television press pool if enforced because CNN participates in the rotating group that provides independent coverage of presidential activities when space is limited.
