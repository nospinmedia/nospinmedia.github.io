U.S. charges three men over alleged Iran-backed terror plots

Nearly 20 attacks targeted Jewish communities and a U.K. base housing U.S. forces

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 11, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

U.S. prosecutors have charged three men accused of participating in an Iran-supported network that plotted nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks against Jewish communities in the United States and Europe, along with a planned assault on a British airbase housing American military personnel.

A superseding indictment unsealed Tuesday in Manhattan federal court names Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi, a dual Iranian-Iraqi national; Mohsen Mohajeri, an Iranian national; and Mohammad Ali al-Hassani, an Iraqi national. Prosecutors allege they operated on behalf of organizations aligned with Iran, including Kataib Hezbollah and Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Iran has denied involvement in the British incident.

The indictment provides new details about a suspected September 27 plot targeting RAF Fairford in England, where U.S. bombers and military personnel had been stationed. British authorities detained several men after three suspicious vans were seen traveling toward the base. Prosecutors allege some vans carried barrels of fuel and that participants had obtained metal objects that could be used as shrapnel while disguising the vehicles as fuel-delivery vans.

Al-Saadi had already been arrested in the United States in May on separate terrorism charges and was being held in Brooklyn. Prosecutors allege he nevertheless continued coordinating with associates through coded phone and video messages passed through family members in Iraq. He has pleaded not guilty to the earlier charges. Mohajeri and al-Hassani are not in U.S. custody.

Authorities also allege the broader network targeted Jewish schools, synagogues, Israeli businesses and individuals in the United States and Europe. The alleged campaign included arson attacks and other violent acts, according to prosecutors.

The Fairford incident led the United States to withdraw bombers stationed at the base. British authorities have separately charged two people under the country's National Security Act in connection with suspected assistance to Iranian intelligence.

The charges remain allegations, and the defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty.
