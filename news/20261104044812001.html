Democrats win control of House and Senate

Midterm results end unified Republican government

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 4, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Democrats won control of both the U.S. House of Representatives and U.S. Senate in Tuesday's midterm elections, ending two years of unified Republican control in Washington and setting up divided government for the remainder of President Trump's term.

The Democratic gains extended well beyond traditional battlegrounds. Democrats won Senate races in Kansas, South Carolina and Texas, while also defeating Republican Texas Gov. Greg Abbott. In the House, Democrats won all 20 closely watched competitive races that had been decided by early Wednesday, according to Reuters. Competitive Senate contests in Mississippi and Alaska remained uncalled early Wednesday.

Voter surveys pointed to widespread dissatisfaction with the country's direction and concern about the cost of living. Exit polling cited by Reuters found 50% of voters viewed the election as an opportunity to rebuke Trump, while 70% said they wanted Congress to provide stronger checks and balances on the president. A separate AP voter survey of roughly 80,000 voters found about 4 in 10 identified the economy as the country's most important issue, while approximately half said the cost of living was crucial to their congressional vote.

The change in congressional control will have immediate consequences when the new Congress takes office in January. Democrats will gain control of House committees and their investigative authority, while a Democratic Senate will be able to block presidential nominees. Reuters reported that Democratic leaders are expected to investigate the administration and seek concessions on major legislation. Trump retains the power to veto legislation passed by Congress and said after the election that he was willing to work with Democrats on some issues while warning he would veto bills he considered unacceptable.
