Meta and States Explore Mid-Trial Settlement

Youth social media lawsuit could end before jury reaches verdict

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 5:42 PM ET

Meta Platforms and a coalition of 29 state attorneys general have discussed a possible mid-trial settlement in a landmark federal lawsuit accusing the company of designing Facebook and Instagram to encourage compulsive use by children and teenagers. The discussions come during the second week of a trial in Oakland, California, where four lead states—California, Colorado, Kentucky, and New Jersey—are presenting claims on behalf of the broader coalition. Settlement negotiations were reported by people familiar with the matter, and neither Meta nor the lead state attorneys general publicly commented on the discussions.

The states allege that Meta knowingly developed platform features that increased youth engagement while misleading users and parents about the effectiveness of safety measures. The broader lawsuit also claims Meta violated the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) by collecting personal information from children under 13 without parental consent. Meta denies the allegations, arguing it has invested heavily in youth safety tools and contests claims that its platforms are responsible for a nationwide youth mental health crisis.

The settlement discussions follow testimony from Instagram head Adam Mosseri, who defended the company's safety initiatives, including its "Take a Break" feature, while acknowledging that relatively few teenagers initially enabled the tool before it later became a default setting for younger users. The trial is expected to continue for several more weeks if no agreement is reached, with additional executives and former employees expected to testify.

The case is one of the most significant legal challenges Meta has faced over child safety and social media design. The states are seeking substantial financial penalties along with court-ordered changes to how Facebook and Instagram operate for younger users. A negotiated settlement could resolve the litigation without a jury verdict while requiring operational reforms, financial payments, or both. If negotiations fail, the case is expected to continue toward a verdict that could influence future regulation and litigation involving social media platforms.
