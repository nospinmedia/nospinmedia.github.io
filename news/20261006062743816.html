President Trump's approval holds at record-low 32%

Reuters/Ipsos poll finds Hispanic voters shifting away from Republicans

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 6, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump's job approval remains at 32%, the lowest level of his political career, while Hispanic voters have moved sharply away from him and Republican congressional candidates, according to a new Reuters/Ipsos poll completed October 5.

Only 24% of Hispanic adults approve of Trump's performance, the lowest level recorded among that group during either of his presidential terms. Hispanic voters were an important part of Trump's 2024 victory, when he won nearly half of their vote after receiving roughly one-third in 2020.

The shift also appears in the upcoming congressional elections. Among Hispanic registered voters, 52% said they planned to vote for Democratic candidates in the November 3 midterms, compared with 26% supporting Republicans. Reuters reported that the 26-point Democratic advantage was the largest among Hispanic voters in its polling since Trump returned to office.

Across all registered voters, Democrats held a 44% to 37% advantage in the congressional ballot question. Republicans continued to lead among voters age 50 and older, but their advantage had narrowed to 44% to 42%, compared with an eight-point advantage in December. Voters under 50 favored Democrats by 18 percentage points.

Economic concerns remain central to voter dissatisfaction. The poll found 33% of Americans identifying inflation and the cost of living as the country's most important problem. Among Hispanic respondents, 20% approved of Trump's handling of living costs while 78% disapproved.

The Reuters/Ipsos survey was conducted online from September 30 through October 5 using Ipsos' probability-based KnowledgePanel. It included a nationally representative sample of 4,506 U.S. adults and had a margin of error of approximately 2 percentage points for the full sample.
