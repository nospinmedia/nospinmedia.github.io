LA review raises questions about DOJ's 20,000-voter list

Nearly 39% with reported birthplaces in sample were U.S.-born

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 21, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

A Los Angeles County review has raised new questions about a Department of Justice list of more than 20,000 people federal officials described as likely noncitizens on California's voter rolls. County officials said a sample showed that a substantial share of people with usable birthplace information reported being born in the United States.

The findings were disclosed in a court declaration from Aman Bhullar, an official with the Los Angeles County registrar-recorder's office. Of the more than 20,000 names provided by the federal government, about 7,000 had Los Angeles County addresses. County officials examined a sample of 200 people from the list. Among records where birthplace information was meaningfully reported, 38.9% indicated a U.S. birthplace.

The review found other discrepancies. About 43% of the sampled registrations had already been canceled, while 71% of the people sampled had never voted. County officials said 86 people in the 200-person sample had been mailed ballots for the November 3 midterm election.

The findings do not by themselves establish the citizenship of every person reporting a U.S. birthplace. The Justice Department has argued that self-reported birthplace information in voter records is not definitive proof of citizenship and says California should verify the eligibility of people identified by federal agencies.

The dispute is part of an ongoing federal lawsuit against California. The DOJ previously gave state and Los Angeles County election officials 48 hours to review the list and asked that ballots connected to people whose citizenship could not be confirmed be segregated and withheld from counting. California officials refused, saying the federal government had not provided sufficient evidence or explained how the list was compiled.

The Department of Homeland Security, which compiled the list, has not publicly disclosed its matching methodology. The new county analysis adds factual information to the dispute after the federal government sought court intervention over the same 20,000 names.
