Trump Orders Pentagon To Scale Back South Korea Drills

Annual Ulchi Freedom Shield Exercises Begin Despite Directive

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

President Trump directed Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to substantially reduce joint U.S. military exercises with South Korea, issuing the order just before the allies launched the annual Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise on August 17. Trump said the drills are expensive, send what he considers an unnecessarily hostile message to North Korea, and cited South Korea's refusal to participate in U.S. efforts related to Iran as part of his reasoning.

Despite the directive, South Korea's military confirmed that Ulchi Freedom Shield began as scheduled. The 11-day exercise involves about 18,000 South Korean troops alongside U.S. forces and is designed to improve combined readiness against North Korean military threats. Officials said the training includes command-post operations and field exercises addressing modern battlefield challenges such as drones, cyberattacks, and GPS interference.

The announcement marks a significant shift in U.S. policy toward one of its closest Asian allies. Approximately 28,500 U.S. troops remain stationed in South Korea under the 1953 Mutual Defense Treaty, and combined exercises have long been viewed by Washington and Seoul as a key component of deterrence on the Korean Peninsula. North Korea routinely condemns the drills as preparations for war, while the allies maintain they are defensive in nature.

Trump previously suspended or reduced large-scale military exercises during his first administration following diplomatic engagement with Kim Jong Un. The latest directive revives that approach, though Pentagon officials have not yet detailed how current or future exercises will be modified. With the drills already underway, the immediate operational impact remains unclear, while lawmakers and defense analysts are expected to closely examine how the decision could affect allied readiness and regional security.
