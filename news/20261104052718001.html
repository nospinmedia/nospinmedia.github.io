Abdul El-Sayed wins Michigan Senate race

Democrat becomes first Muslim elected to U.S. Senate

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 4, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed defeated Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan's closely watched U.S. Senate race, securing a major battleground-state victory for Democrats and becoming the first Muslim elected to the U.S. Senate.

El-Sayed, a physician and former director of the Detroit Health Department, won a race that drew national attention because of both Michigan's status as a closely divided state and the battle for control of the Senate. Rogers, a former congressman and former chair of the House Intelligence Committee, had sought to return to Washington as a senator after previously representing Michigan in the House.

The victory gives El-Sayed a place in U.S. political history. Muslim Americans have previously served in the House, including Michigan Democrats Rashida Tlaib and Hansen Clarke and Minnesota Democrat Ilhan Omar, but no Muslim candidate had previously been elected to the Senate.

Michigan was again a major national battleground in the 2026 midterms, with both parties investing heavily in the contest. The state has shifted between the parties in recent national elections, making its Senate race an important test of each party's ability to win voters across the industrial Midwest.

El-Sayed's victory also contributed to the broader Democratic gains that gave the party control of the Senate. His win came during an election in which Democrats captured several closely watched Senate contests around the country, ending Republican control of the chamber.

Beyond its effect on the balance of power, the result adds another prominent Democrat to Michigan's statewide delegation and gives El-Sayed a national platform when the new Congress convenes in January 2027.
