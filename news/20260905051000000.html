Supreme Court restores cheaper broadcast rates for parties

FCC policy returns as midterm ad spending accelerates

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court on September 4 temporarily restored a federal policy allowing political parties and certain joint fundraising committees to obtain discounted television and radio advertising rates when coordinating with candidates. The unsigned order granted an emergency request from the National Republican Congressional Committee (NRCC) and National Republican Senatorial Committee (NRSC) and stayed an August ruling by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 4th Circuit while further litigation proceeds.

The dispute centers on the Federal Communications Commission's "lowest unit charge" rules, which require broadcasters to offer legally qualified candidates favorable rates during specified periods before elections. On March 30, the FCC's Media Bureau issued guidance saying political parties and joint fundraising committees could also qualify under certain conditions when buying advertising coordinated with candidates. Four Democratic candidates — Jon Ossoff, Kristen McDonald Rivet, Sherrod Brown and Roy Cooper — challenged that interpretation. A divided 4th Circuit panel set aside the guidance on August 25, concluding the discounted rates were reserved for candidates.

The Supreme Court focused primarily on a procedural issue rather than finally resolving whether the FCC's interpretation of the advertising law is correct. The justices said the 4th Circuit likely lacked statutory jurisdiction because the candidates sought judicial review while their application challenging the Media Bureau guidance remained pending before the full FCC. The Court also found that the Republican committees faced irreparable harm because broadcasters had begun withdrawing favorable rates, increasing advertising costs during the final weeks before the November 3 midterms. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson publicly dissented, saying she did not believe the committees were likely to succeed on their jurisdictional argument.

The ruling arrived as the 60-day general-election window for lowest-unit-charge rates began. The NRCC said it had already reserved $10.8 million in television advertising across 23 House races during September, while the NRCC and NRSC told the Court they had budgeted tens of millions of dollars based on access to the lower rates. The restored FCC guidance applies to qualifying coordinated advertising regardless of political party.
