Judge halts border construction across Texas’ Big Bend region

Order covers roughly 500 miles while lawsuit proceeds

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 3, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

A federal judge has temporarily blocked the Trump administration from continuing border barrier construction and other ground-disturbing work across the Big Bend region of Texas, including Big Bend National Park, while a lawsuit challenging the projects proceeds. U.S. District Judge Kathleen Cardone issued the preliminary injunction October 2, finding that the challengers were likely to succeed on at least one of their claims and could suffer serious, irreparable harm without court intervention.

The order reaches beyond the national park. The Big Bend sector stretches roughly 500 miles along the U.S.-Mexico border, accounting for about one-quarter of the approximately 2,000-mile southern border. Cardone's order prevents the government from building border barriers or related infrastructure or conducting construction and ground-disturbing activities in the sector unless it complies with applicable laws.

The lawsuit was brought by the Center for Biological Diversity, Friends of the Ruidosa Church and Big Bend-area river guide and landowner Danny William Miller. The plaintiffs are challenging the Department of Homeland Security's use of waivers to bypass environmental and other legal requirements for accelerated border construction. Their claims include constitutional challenges involving separation of powers and private property.

The Big Bend projects are part of a broader $46 billion Trump administration effort to expand infrastructure along the southern border using a combination of 30-foot steel barriers, vehicle barriers, roads and surveillance technology. Inside Big Bend National Park, U.S. Customs and Border Protection has planned roads, surveillance equipment and vehicle barriers rather than the 30-foot walls being constructed in some other locations.

Construction in the park had already generated opposition from local residents, environmental groups and politicians from both parties. CBP Commissioner Rodney Scott previously paused work there after bulldozers were seen clearing land in August, but the temporary pause had expired before Cardone issued the injunction.

The ruling does not permanently cancel the projects or resolve the underlying lawsuit. It prevents additional construction while the court considers whether the government's legal waivers and construction plans can stand.
