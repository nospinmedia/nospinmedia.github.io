FAA, NTSB Probe Marine One Loss of Separation Near D.C.

Jet departed Reagan National as helicopter lifted off; White House says no danger

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 7, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) are investigating a "momentary loss of separation" involving Marine One carrying President Trump and a departing commercial airliner near Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport. The incident occurred on August 4, 2026, as the presidential helicopter lifted from the White House Ellipse toward Joint Base Andrews. Federal officials said the aircraft continued moving away from one another after the brief reduction in required separation and were never on a collision course.

Current procedures require arrivals and departures at Reagan National to pause when presidential helicopter flights use conflicting routes. Those measures were strengthened after the January 29, 2025, midair collision near the airport that killed 67 people. Air traffic recordings indicate Marine One made the required "three minutes to lift" call before departure, but the control tower initially responded that the transmission was "broken and unreadable" before later acknowledging the flight and warning of a departing jet.

The White House said the president was never in danger. Spokesperson Kush Desai said Marine One is flown by highly experienced pilots and that the flight remained safe throughout the operation. Marine Corps spokesperson Capt. Jacob Sugg also said there were no "close calls," while the FAA confirmed the incident remains under investigation.

Investigators are reviewing air traffic communications, radar data, staffing levels, and whether post-2025 safety procedures were followed before the commercial aircraft was cleared for departure. The review will examine why airport traffic was not paused before Marine One lifted off and whether communication problems contributed to the sequence of clearances.
