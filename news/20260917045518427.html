Saudi defenses destroy drone south of Mecca

Kingdom calls security of holy sites a ‘red line’

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 17, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Saudi Arabia's air defenses intercepted and destroyed a drone south of Mecca on September 15, with the Saudi-led military coalition accusing Yemen's Houthi movement of launching it toward Islam's holiest city. The Houthis denied targeting Mecca, creating a direct dispute over the intended target as fighting between the two sides intensifies.

Coalition spokesperson Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki said the drone was destroyed before it entered prohibited airspace over Mecca. He declared the security of Islam's two holiest sites and pilgrims a "red line" and said the coalition would take necessary deterrent measures against the Houthis. The coalition described the incident as the second Houthi attempt to target Mecca, citing a July 2017 ballistic missile launch that Saudi authorities also said was aimed toward the city.

The Houthis, however, rejected the Saudi accusation. Houthi spokesperson Hazem al-Assad called the claim that the group had targeted Mecca a lie. The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, which represents 57 Muslim-majority countries, condemned what it described as an attack on the holy city and blamed the Houthis.

The incident comes during a renewed escalation between Saudi Arabia and the Iran-aligned Houthi movement. The Houthis have recently seized territory along Yemen's Red Sea coast near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a strategically important shipping route, and have announced attacks elsewhere in Saudi Arabia. Saudi Arabia has also carried out airstrikes in Yemen as the conflict intensifies after years of relative calm.

Mecca is the focal point of the annual Hajj pilgrimage and home to the Grand Mosque, making any perceived threat to the city particularly sensitive across the Muslim world. Saudi authorities had issued security alerts in Mecca and other cities amid the recent attacks.
