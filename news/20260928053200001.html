Trump meets Anthropic CEO as AI safety fight intensifies

Dinner follows Pentagon dispute and Amodei's call to slow development

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 28, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump planned a late Sunday dinner with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, bringing together two figures who have publicly disagreed over how quickly artificial intelligence should advance and what safeguards should govern increasingly powerful systems. Trump told reporters after returning to Joint Base Andrews that he was having dinner with Amodei at 10 p.m. on September 27, while Axios reported the private White House dinner would be their first one-on-one meeting.

The meeting comes after months of friction between the administration and Anthropic, the company behind Claude. Earlier this year, Anthropic resisted a Pentagon demand that its technology be available for any lawful military use, maintaining safeguards against applications including mass surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. The Pentagon subsequently designated Anthropic a supply chain risk, and a federal appeals court on September 25 rejected the company's challenge to that designation, allowing the military to continue removing Claude models from its systems.

The two men also differ sharply over the pace of AI development. Amodei has recently argued that the industry should slow development enough for safety measures to keep pace with rapidly improving capabilities. He has warned that within months advanced AI systems could potentially coordinate large numbers of autonomous agents capable of operating across the internet. Trump has instead emphasized rapid American development and competition with China, while publicly dismissing some warnings that AI could destroy humanity.

The dinner comes as the White House increases its engagement with major AI companies. Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson are scheduled to meet with leading AI executives on September 29. Axios reported that Amodei missed a recent state dinner attended by executives including Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg and Jensen Huang because of a scheduling conflict, after which Trump personally invited him to Sunday's dinner.

Neither the White House nor Anthropic publicly detailed a specific agenda for the private meeting ahead of time.
