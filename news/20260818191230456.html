CBP Halts Border Work Inside Big Bend National Park

On-site review ordered after bipartisan pushback

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) has paused all construction activity inside Big Bend National Park while an on-the-ground review is conducted, a rare step back for a signature border initiative under President Trump. CBP Commissioner Rodney Scott announced the stand-down on Monday, August 17, 2026, saying he ordered the pause and would meet local leaders and stakeholders before deciding next steps. The agency did not say how long the pause will last.

The pause follows days of backlash after bulldozers were seen clearing land in the park last week; the Department of Homeland Security said that work was tied to “survey and design.” Earlier this year, CBP stepped away from a 30-foot bollard wall through the park but continued pursuing a new road, lighting, vehicle barriers, and buried fiber-optic sensors. Gov. Greg Abbott said he spoke with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and that the secretary committed there would be no wall or barriers in the park—though Abbott did not address the roadwork that has alarmed local residents. Brewster County Sheriff Ronny Dodson said he plans to meet Scott during the visit.

The decision underscores how opposition has grown around Big Bend, where critics warn construction could scar a landscape that anchors local tourism and dark-sky stargazing, and where rugged canyons and desert already deter crossings. Activists welcomed the pause but called it only a first step, urging CBP to remove heavy equipment, repair any damage, and publicly outline its plan. The broader push remains funded: Congress approved about $46 billion last summer to accelerate border projects, with the administration invoking waivers to move quickly.
