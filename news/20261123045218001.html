Amtrak train derails after striking truck in Louisiana

Four hospitalized as passenger cars remain upright

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 23, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

An Amtrak Sunset Limited passenger train partially derailed in Louisiana on November 22 after striking an 18-wheeler that had become stuck on a railroad crossing east of Lafayette, sending at least four people to hospitals with injuries described as non-life-threatening.

The eastbound Sunset Limited Train 2 had departed Los Angeles on November 20 and was traveling toward New Orleans on Union Pacific tracks when the collision occurred at about 6:15 p.m. The truck was reportedly hauling a bulldozer and had become immobilized at a crossing in the 100 block of Thru-Way Park Road, near U.S. Highway 90 and Broussard.

The impact knocked the lead locomotive and two baggage cars off the tracks, while all passenger cars remained upright. Amtrak reported 95 passengers aboard along with crew members. Acadian Ambulance dispatched 12 ambulances to the scene as emergency crews evaluated passengers.

Amtrak, Lafayette Police and local reporting said four people were transported to hospitals with non-life-threatening injuries. The Advocate, citing Acadian Ambulance, reported five patients were transported. No fatalities were reported, and additional people with minor injuries were evaluated at the scene.

Lafayette Mayor-President Monique Blanco Boulet confirmed the derailment and emergency response. Amtrak arranged buses to help passengers continue toward their destinations following the crash.

The derailment also affected road and rail operations. Police closed the eastbound right lane of U.S. Highway 90 near the crossing during recovery and track-clearing work, and Amtrak canceled the corresponding November 24 Sunset Limited departure following the wreck.

The Lafayette Police Department, Amtrak and railroad personnel are investigating how the tractor-trailer became stuck on the crossing before the train arrived.
