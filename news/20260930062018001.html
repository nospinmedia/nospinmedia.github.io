Navy awards Boeing $20 billion-plus deal for next-generation fighter

F/A-XX will replace Super Hornets beginning in the 2030s

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 30, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

The U.S. Navy has selected Boeing to develop its next-generation carrier-based fighter, awarding the company a contract valued at more than $20 billion for the F/A-XX program. The September 29 decision gives Boeing responsibility for developing both of the U.S. military's new sixth-generation crewed fighters after the company was selected in 2025 to build the Air Force's F-47.

The F/A-XX contract covers the full-scale development phase and includes multiple test aircraft for ground evaluations, airworthiness testing, systems development and weapons integration. Boeing defeated Northrop Grumman in the final competition after Lockheed Martin was eliminated earlier. Detailed specifications and much of the aircraft's design remain classified.

The carrier-based F/A-XX is intended to initially supplement and eventually replace the Navy's F/A-18E/F Super Hornets, with the transition expected to begin in the 2030s. The aircraft will operate alongside the F-35C and is expected to provide greater range, advanced stealth and the ability to work with crewed and uncrewed aircraft. Navy planning has also connected the fighter with Collaborative Combat Aircraft, autonomous aircraft designed to operate alongside crewed fighters.

The program is particularly important for the Navy because the effective combat range of carrier air wings has declined as potential adversaries have developed weapons capable of threatening aircraft carriers from greater distances. Michael Duffey, the Pentagon's acquisition chief, said the F/A-XX is intended to extend operational reach and improve the Navy's ability to operate in contested airspace.

The award is also a major win for Boeing's defense business and its operations in St. Louis, Missouri, where the company is constructing a large secure manufacturing facility for advanced military aircraft. Boeing now faces the challenge of developing the Navy's F/A-XX while simultaneously working on the Air Force's F-47 program.
