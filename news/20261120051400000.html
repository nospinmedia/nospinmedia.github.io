JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard vendors targeted in cyberattack

Customer contact information exposed as companies say payment networks were unaffected

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 20, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard say outside service providers they use were targeted in a cyberattack that exposed some customer information, highlighting the security risks created when financial companies share data with third-party vendors. The companies said their core payment networks were not compromised.

JPMorgan, the largest U.S. bank, said attackers accessed certain marketing data stored by vendors that may include customer names and email addresses. The bank said it will directly contact customers believed to have been affected and, where appropriate, provide support including complimentary credit monitoring. JPMorgan emphasized that the cyberattack was unrelated to the transaction-processing disruption earlier this week, which the bank attributed to a software bug and which NSM previously reported.

A cyber extortion group called n0n claimed on November 16 that it obtained cardmember engagement records involving JPMorgan and subsequently posted similar claims involving Visa and Mastercard. Data reviewed by Reuters appeared to contain more than 16,000 customer email addresses, some accompanied by phone numbers and postal addresses, along with 124 bank employee email addresses. Reuters said it could not independently verify the authenticity of the material.

Visa said claims involving its customers were connected to an incident at a third-party service provider in the Asia-Pacific region. The company said Visa's own systems were not involved and that no payment information, including card numbers, was affected. The posted data included records associated with Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Australia, Hong Kong, Taiwan, the Philippines, Malaysia and Macau.

Mastercard said a separate third-party provider incident exposed a limited number of email addresses belonging to cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program. Reuters found Mastercard-related marketing data associated with customers in North America, the Middle East and Africa, along with a small number of Mastercard employee email addresses. Mastercard said its payment network and customers' ability to use their cards were unaffected.

None of the three companies has publicly identified the affected third-party vendors or disclosed an exact total number of customers involved. All three are listed on n0n's leak site as not having paid a ransom.
