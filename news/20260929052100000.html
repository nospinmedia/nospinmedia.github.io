Iranian missile strike injured 8 U.S. Marines in Strait of Hormuz

Sept. 14 attack was not publicly disclosed at the time

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 29, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Eight U.S. Marines were injured when an Iranian anti-ship cruise missile struck a vessel in the Strait of Hormuz on September 14, according to three U.S. officials. The attack and the Marines' injuries were not publicly disclosed by the Defense Department at the time and emerged nearly two weeks later through news reports.

The Marines — seven enlisted personnel and one officer — were operating as part of a battalion landing team when the missile hit. Officials said they were aboard a maritime vessel that was not a U.S. Navy ship, but declined to identify the vessel or describe its mission. The Marines suffered smoke inhalation and possible traumatic brain injuries, including concussion symptoms such as headaches. Officials said none was seriously injured and all eight returned to duty shortly after the attack.

The Marines' injuries were added roughly 10 days later to the Pentagon's Defense Casualty Analysis System, which recently listed 861 U.S. service members wounded during the conflict with Iran. A U.S. official said casualty reporting can be delayed because some symptoms are not immediately apparent and the database does not update in real time.

The Defense Department did not announce an attack on U.S. forces in the region on September 14. When reporters sought information about the newly disclosed incident, the Pentagon referred questions to U.S. Central Command, which declined to comment. The Washington Post separately confirmed that eight Marines suffered minor injuries, including smoke inhalation and head trauma, during an Iranian attack on a nonmilitary vessel in September.

The disclosure comes as the Strait of Hormuz remains a flashpoint in the continuing conflict with Iran. The narrow waterway is one of the world's most important maritime routes, and military activity and attacks on vessels have continued to create risks for personnel and shipping operating in the region.
