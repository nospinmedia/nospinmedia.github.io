U.S. military mission in Iraq officially ends

Iran-backed groups celebrate as security concerns grow

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 30, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

The U.S. military mission in Iraq officially ended September 30, completing the withdrawal of American forces from their remaining bases and closing the Iraq phase of Operation Inherent Resolve, the campaign launched in 2014 against the Islamic State. The departure follows a 2024 agreement between Washington and Baghdad establishing a timetable for ending the U.S.-led coalition's military mission in Iraq.

The withdrawal closes another chapter in more than two decades of American military involvement in the country. U.S.-led forces invaded Iraq in 2003, withdrew in 2011 and returned in 2014 after Islamic State seized large areas of Iraq and neighboring Syria. More than 4,500 U.S. service members died during the Iraq war and subsequent military operations.

Iran-backed armed groups that had demanded an American withdrawal celebrated the departure, while Iraqi and Kurdish security officials have raised concerns about what comes next. Islamic State no longer controls the territory it once held, but sleeper cells remain active. Iraqi and Kurdish officials have warned that losing U.S. intelligence, surveillance and logistical support could make it more difficult to track militants and respond to emerging threats. Iraqi security forces, including the Peshmerga in the Kurdistan Region, will now take the lead in counterterrorism operations inside Iraq.

The departure could also reshape the balance between Baghdad, Washington and Iran, whose allied political factions and armed groups already hold substantial influence in Iraq. Baghdad is simultaneously trying to bring armed groups under state control, although several powerful Iran-aligned militias have resisted disarmament efforts. The United States and Iraq are expected to maintain a bilateral relationship that includes security cooperation even after the coalition mission ends.

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi previously described October 1 as a 'new day' for Iraq, saying the country would enter the period free of the coalition's foreign military presence and with responsibility for its own security firmly in Iraqi hands.
