DHS Proposes $103,265 H-1B Filing Fee

Rulemaking revives policy after courts blocked earlier order

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 25, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has proposed a new regulation that would require employers to pay an additional $103,265 fee when filing cap-subject H-1B visa petitions. Published as a proposed rule by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), the measure would apply to the annual 65,000 regular H-1B visas and the 20,000 visas reserved for applicants holding advanced degrees from U.S. universities. The fee would be charged in addition to existing filing costs and is not yet in effect.

According to DHS, the proposal is intended to recover a portion of the federal government's costs for administering the lawful immigration system. The agency estimates the fee would generate approximately $8.8 billion annually based on an expected 85,000 cap-subject petitions. The proposal states that the revenue would help fund immigration benefit adjudications, fraud detection, national security vetting, immigration court operations, consular visa processing, labor standards enforcement and interagency coordination involving DHS, the Department of Justice, the Department of State and the Department of Labor.

The proposal follows a legal setback for the Trump administration after federal courts blocked an earlier attempt to impose a $100,000 H-1B fee through a presidential proclamation. In June 2026, U.S. District Judge Leo T. Sorokin ruled the previous fee exceeded presidential authority, and the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals later declined to suspend that decision while the administration appealed. By pursuing the fee through the federal rulemaking process, DHS is seeking to establish the charge under existing statutory authority after allowing for public notice and comment.

The proposed regulation is subject to a 30-day public comment period before DHS decides whether to issue a final rule. Business organizations, immigration attorneys and technology industry groups are expected to challenge both the legal basis for the fee and its potential impact on employers that rely on highly skilled foreign workers.
