Turkey raids LGBTQ+ groups and blocks online accounts

Dozens detained as nationwide investigation targets organizations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 15, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Turkish authorities have launched a sweeping crackdown targeting LGBTQ+ organizations, activists and HIV-support groups, with police raiding association offices, detaining dozens of people and blocking websites and social media accounts. Justice Minister Akin Gurlek said coordinated investigations involve 162 suspects, nine associations and 13 businesses across 15 provinces, including Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir, Aydin and Mersin.

Authorities say the campaign, called "My Family is Safe," is intended to protect children, families and public order. Prosecutors are investigating allegations including prostitution and obscenity and have claimed minors were being improperly influenced on issues involving sexual orientation and gender identity. Officials have also cited alleged criminal activity and foreign funding received by some organizations. The allegations have not been proven in court.

At least 47 people were detained during raids in Istanbul and Ankara over the weekend, according to prosecutors. In Istanbul, authorities targeted 38 suspects and detained 26, while another 21 people were detained in Ankara during an investigation involving the LGBTQ+ rights organization Kaos GL. On Monday, prosecutors in Izmir said another 18 people had been detained, with 16 ordered jailed pending trial and two placed under judicial supervision.

Police also raided offices belonging to LGBTQ+ associations and blocked access to dozens of websites and social media accounts connected to organizations, activists and rights advocates. Authorities additionally blocked the X account of Amnesty International's Turkey branch. The Justice Ministry instructed prosecutors to rapidly investigate alleged obscene or criminal online material involving children and seek access restrictions when considered necessary.

Rights organizations sharply criticized the operation. The Turkish Human Rights Association described it as state-led discrimination and an attempt to dismantle civil society, while Nacho Sanchez Amor, the European Parliament's Turkey rapporteur, called the arrests a major escalation of repression.

The crackdown comes as President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan promotes a "Decade of Family and Population" initiative focused on declining birth rates and an aging population. Turkey does not criminalize same-sex relationships, but authorities have increasingly restricted LGBTQ+ demonstrations and organizations in recent years.
