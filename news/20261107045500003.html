Russia launches massive overnight attack on Kyiv

Missiles and drones damage homes and strain energy grid

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 7, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Russia launched a large overnight missile and drone attack against Kyiv and surrounding areas into Saturday, November 7, damaging residential buildings and infrastructure as Ukrainian authorities reported injuries and disruptions to essential services.

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 172 drones along with multiple ballistic and cruise missiles during the attack. Ukrainian forces reported intercepting 149 drones and four missiles, while strikes or falling debris were recorded at dozens of locations. Russia's Defense Ministry said its forces struck Ukrainian military and energy-related targets in and around the capital.

In Kyiv's Solomianskyi district, authorities said a missile destroyed a two-story kindergarten building and damaged nearby apartment buildings. At least six people were injured, including a 14-year-old child, according to Ukrainian officials. Emergency crews responded to damaged buildings while authorities assessed additional impacts across the city.

The attack also affected Ukraine's energy system. Strikes damaged electrical infrastructure in the Kyiv and Zhytomyr regions, contributing to emergency power outages as utilities worked to stabilize the grid. Kyiv officials also reported disruptions affecting water and heating services in parts of the capital. The latest damage comes as Ukraine prepares its energy network for another winter under continued Russian attacks.

The overnight barrage follows repeated Russian attacks on Ukraine's electricity and heating infrastructure during the war. Ukraine has accused Moscow of deliberately targeting civilian energy systems, while Russia says its strikes are directed at infrastructure supporting Ukraine's military operations.

Saturday's attack represents a significant new development from earlier Russian strikes that had already disrupted electricity and water supplies in Kyiv. Ukrainian authorities were continuing to assess damage from the latest barrage as repair crews worked to restore affected services.
