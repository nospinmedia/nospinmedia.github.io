North Korea Fires Multiple Ballistic Missiles Toward Japan

One Travels Over 900 Kilometers as Rival Weapons Tests Raise Tensions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 12, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

North Korea launched multiple ballistic missiles toward waters east of the Korean Peninsula on Monday, October 12, 2026, continuing a series of weapons demonstrations that have heightened tensions with South Korea and Japan. At least one missile traveled more than 900 kilometers (559 miles) before falling into the sea, according to South Korea's military.

Jang Do-young, spokesperson for South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff, said the missiles were launched from an area near Pyongyang, the North Korean capital. The military detected different types of missiles but did not immediately disclose the total number launched or provide detailed flight information for every weapon.

Japan's Deputy Defense Minister Takashi Koyari reported detecting at least two missiles launched approximately 17 minutes apart. Japanese officials said the weapons landed outside Japan's exclusive economic zone. One traveled approximately 220 kilometers (137 miles), while another covered more than 910 kilometers (565 miles) along an irregular flight path.

Japanese defense officials said the longer-range missile displayed characteristics suggesting it could be a hypersonic weapon, although its precise capabilities had not been independently established. Hypersonic weapons are designed to travel at extremely high speeds, with some systems capable of maneuvering during flight to complicate interception.

The launches followed South Korea's recent test of its first domestically developed hypersonic weapon. South Korean President Lee Jae Myung personally observed that demonstration, which officials described as an important step toward strengthening the country's conventional military capabilities.

South Korean military officials suggested North Korea's latest launches could be intended as a show of force following Seoul's weapons demonstration. However, Pyongyang had not publicly confirmed that motivation.

The launches also occurred amid renewed tensions along the heavily fortified border separating the two Koreas. South Korea recently accused North Korean forces of planting landmines that injured three South Korean soldiers near the Demilitarized Zone. North Korea rejected responsibility for that incident.

South Korea's presidential office condemned Monday's missile launches as violations of United Nations Security Council resolutions and called for an immediate halt to further testing. Japan also protested the launches.

Diplomatic efforts to reduce tensions remain stalled. North Korean leader Kim Jong Un has continued expanding the country's missile and nuclear weapons programs while rejecting recent offers from Washington and Seoul to resume negotiations.
