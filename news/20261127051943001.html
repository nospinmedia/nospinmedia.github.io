Taiwan enters final day of campaigning as China sends warplanes

30 military aircraft detected near island ahead of Saturday's elections

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 27, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Taiwan entered the final day of campaigning Friday ahead of island-wide local elections as its defense ministry reported another surge in Chinese military activity, including 30 Chinese military aircraft operating around the self-governed island.

Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense said 26 of the 30 aircraft crossed the median line of the Taiwan Strait while taking part in a "joint combat readiness patrol" alongside Chinese warships. Beijing claims Taiwan as its territory, while Taiwan's government rejects China's sovereignty claims.

China's Defense Ministry said Thursday that "reunification" was an "unstoppable trend" and argued that Taiwan's residents would be safer under the protection of China's military.

Taiwan's Central Election Commission says approximately 19.65 million people are eligible to vote Saturday, including roughly 600,000 first-time voters. Balloting will take place at 18,244 polling stations across 8,906 electoral districts, with voters choosing mayors, county magistrates, councilors and thousands of other local officials across Taiwan's 22 cities and counties.

Campaigning must end at 10 p.m. Friday. Taiwan also prohibits publishing, reporting or citing election opinion polls during the final 10 days before voting under its Public Officials Election and Recall Act.

The elections do not directly determine Taiwan's policy toward China. Campaigning has focused heavily on domestic concerns including transportation, housing, wages and how the benefits of Taiwan's semiconductor and artificial-intelligence boom are distributed. The results will nevertheless provide a measure of support for President Lai Ching-te's Democratic Progressive Party and the opposition Kuomintang ahead of the 2028 presidential and legislative elections.

The KMT won 14 cities and counties in the previous local elections, compared with five for the DPP.

Major contests include Taipei, where KMT Mayor Chiang Wan-an faces DPP lawmaker Puma Shen; New Taipei City, where KMT candidate Lee Shu-chuan faces DPP legislator Su Chiao-hui; and Kaohsiung, where DPP legislator Lai Jui-lung faces KMT legislator Ko Chih-en.
