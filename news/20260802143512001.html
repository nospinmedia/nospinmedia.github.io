Heat Dome Drives 115°–118° Temps Across the West

Washington Declares Emergency; Rare ‘PDS’ Fire Warning Issued

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 3, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

A powerful ridge of high pressure is trapping heat across the Western U.S., pushing temperatures to seasonal peaks and sharply elevating fire danger as authorities roll out new restrictions. On August 1, 2026, Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson declared a statewide emergency and imposed broad limits on outdoor and agricultural burning through the end of September amid escalating wildfire risk.

Forecasts and preliminary readings from the National Weather Service show triple-digit extremes stretching from the deserts to the northern Rockies: Las Vegas hit 115°F, Phoenix was expected to reach 116°F, El Centro, California, was forecast to set a record at 118°F, and Great Falls, Montana, was projected near 104°F. In eastern Washington, forecasters issued an unprecedented “Particularly Dangerous Situation” Red Flag Warning, citing high winds, very low humidity and extremely dry fuels that can cause rapid fire spread.

Officials warned that persistently hot nights—many remaining in the upper 80s to low 90s—limit recovery for people and power systems. Cooling centers have opened in several cities, and residents are urged to avoid midday outdoor activity, check on vulnerable neighbors, and hydrate. Fire managers cautioned that the combination of heat, wind and tinder-dry vegetation could turn new ignitions into fast-moving fires, prompting evacuation orders with little notice.

Meteorologists expect the heat to linger into August 2, 2026, before easing in parts of the northern tier, but critical fire weather may persist where gusty winds and dry lightning overlap parched landscapes. The emergency order in Washington—paired with the “PDS” fire alert—reflects the elevated risk across the inland Northwest even as some locales begin to cool.

- Key risks: heat illness, rapid wildfire spread, strain on electrical grids - Government actions: statewide emergency and burn restrictions in Washington; expanded heat and fire weather alerts by the NWS
