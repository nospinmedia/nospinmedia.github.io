Ukrainian Drones Strike Two Key Russian Energy Hubs

Refinery Fire And Baltic Export Damage Deepen Pressure On Fuel Network

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Ukraine launched long-range drone strikes overnight against two strategically important Russian energy sites, igniting a fire at the Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat oil and petrochemical complex in Bashkortostan and damaging infrastructure at the Ust-Luga fuel export port in Leningrad Oblast. The attacks are part of Kyiv's expanding campaign to target Russia's refining and export network, aiming to disrupt energy infrastructure that supports Moscow's wartime economy.

Ukraine's General Staff said the strike on Salavat hit a facility located roughly 1,300 kilometers from Ukrainian-controlled territory, making it one of the deepest confirmed attacks of the war. The refinery and petrochemical complex is among Russia's largest, processing approximately 74 million barrels of crude oil annually while producing gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, and petrochemicals. Bashkortostan Governor Radiy Khabirov confirmed an industrial fire and said two civilians were injured. Separately, officials in Leningrad Oblast reported drone attacks around the Ust-Luga Baltic export terminal, where fires and infrastructure damage were reported as air defenses engaged multiple drones. Authorities said the full extent of the damage is still being assessed.

The latest strikes follow a series of Ukrainian attacks on Russia's energy sector this month. On August 11, a drone strike forced the Orsknefteorgsintez refinery to halt operations, with regional officials warning repairs could take up to six months because replacement equipment is affected by international sanctions. Analysts say repeated attacks on refineries, storage facilities, and export terminals have increased operational costs for Russia and periodically disrupted fuel production and logistics, although the long-term impact on overall export capacity remains uncertain.

Russian authorities acknowledged fires at Salavat and damage near Ust-Luga, while Ukrainian officials described the operation as part of a sustained strategy targeting military-related energy infrastructure. Independent verification of the full extent of the damage at both sites remains limited, and officials on both sides are expected to release updated assessments as inspections continue.
