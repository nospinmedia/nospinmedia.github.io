Fed expected to raise interest rates for first time since 2023

Quarter-point increase could make borrowing more expensive

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 16, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

The Federal Reserve is widely expected to raise its benchmark interest rate on Wednesday, September 16, marking its first rate increase in more than three years as policymakers confront persistent inflation and sharply higher energy prices. Economists and financial markets expect a 0.25 percentage-point increase, which would lift the federal funds target range to 3.75% to 4.00%.

The anticipated move follows an August Consumer Price Index report showing prices rising 3.4% from a year earlier, still well above the Fed's 2% inflation target. Gasoline prices have risen sharply, and oil prices above $100 per barrel have added concerns that higher energy and transportation costs could spread through the broader economy. Financial markets were pricing in roughly a 90% or greater chance of a quarter-point increase ahead of Wednesday's decision.

For consumers, a Fed hike can eventually contribute to higher rates on credit cards, auto loans and other variable-rate borrowing, although individual rates do not necessarily move immediately or by the same amount as the Fed's benchmark. Mortgage rates are influenced heavily by longer-term bond yields rather than directly by the federal funds rate. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was already around 7.43% in mid-September, up roughly a percentage point since early July. Savers, meanwhile, could benefit if banks increase yields on savings accounts and certificates of deposit.

The meeting is also the first expected rate increase under Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh. President Trump has publicly called for lower interest rates, while Warsh has emphasized the need to bring inflation under control. The Fed operates independently when setting monetary policy, and Wednesday's decision will be made by the Federal Open Market Committee.

The rate announcement is scheduled for 2 p.m. ET, followed by Warsh's press conference at 2:30 p.m. Officials will also release updated projections for inflation, economic growth and future interest rates, providing clues about whether another increase could follow later this year.
