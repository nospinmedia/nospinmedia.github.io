Alfalfa Sprouts Recalled After 55 People Sickened

E. Coli and Salmonella Outbreak Spans 15 States

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 22, 2026 at 10:17 AM ET

Federal health officials are warning consumers not to eat certain alfalfa sprouts after 55 people across 15 states became sick in an outbreak involving both Shiga toxin-producing E. coli and Salmonella. The illnesses have been linked through epidemiological and traceback evidence to alfalfa sprouts grown by Everything Sprouts, LLC, a Minneapolis, Minnesota company, and sold under the Calco and Everything Sprouts brands. Four people have been hospitalized and no deaths have been reported.

The investigation began after people became ill between May 31 and August 8, 2026. Of 34 patients interviewed about foods they ate before becoming sick, 26, or 76%, reported eating alfalfa sprouts. Investigators then traced sprouts served or sold at restaurants and grocery stores back to Everything Sprouts. The Food and Drug Administration began an inspection and sample collection at the company's facility on August 19 and recommended a recall. Everything Sprouts subsequently announced a voluntary recall of certain lots distributed beginning May 27, including some sprouts packaged in 5-ounce plastic containers.

The CDC says 46 people were infected with E. coli, seven with Salmonella, and two people were infected with both pathogens. Outbreak strains include E. coli O26:H11, O103:H25, and O168:H8, along with Salmonella Agona. Everything Sprouts said its own daily testing since the outbreak began had not produced a positive laboratory result, while federal officials said epidemiological and supply-chain evidence identified its sprouts as a source of illnesses. The FDA investigation remains ongoing to determine the contamination source and whether additional products or companies are involved.

The FDA advises consumers, restaurants, retailers, and foodservice operators not to eat, sell, or serve Calco or Everything Sprouts brand alfalfa sprouts. Consumers should discard or return affected sprouts and clean and sanitize refrigerators, containers, and other surfaces that may have contacted them.
