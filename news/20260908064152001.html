UK moves to ban trade with Israeli West Bank settlements

U.S. ambassador warns British businesses could face fallout

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 8, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

The United Kingdom is moving to ban trade in goods and some services connected to Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, a significant change in British policy that has already drawn criticism from Israel and warnings of possible economic consequences from the United States.

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband is expected to detail the measures in Parliament Tuesday as part of what he has described as a broader reset of Britain's approach toward Israel and the Palestinians. The government says the restrictions are intended to prevent British economic activity from supporting settlements that the UK considers illegal under international law and to protect the prospects for a two-state solution.

The move follows renewed Israeli settlement expansion, including the E1 project east of Jerusalem. Britain and more than 20 international partners condemned the E1 plan in August, saying it would divide the West Bank and undermine the territorial continuity of a future Palestinian state. The British government summoned Israel's chargé d'affaires after Israel launched a tender for the project.

The policy has also created friction with Washington. U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee warned that British businesses could face consequences under American state laws restricting participation in boycotts of Israel. He specifically raised the possibility that companies doing business with the British government could encounter restrictions in states such as Florida. The precise effect would depend on how Britain's rules are written and how individual U.S. laws are applied.

Israeli officials have strongly criticized the British move and threatened diplomatic retaliation. The UK, however, is distinguishing between trade with Israel itself and commercial activity tied specifically to settlements in occupied territory. Overall UK-Israel trade is worth billions of pounds annually, while settlement-related commerce represents only a small portion of that relationship.

The change follows a 2024 International Court of Justice advisory opinion that said states should distinguish in their economic dealings between Israel and the Palestinian territories occupied since 1967.
