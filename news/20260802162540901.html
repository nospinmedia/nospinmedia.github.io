Two Firefighting Helicopters Collide Near Athens, Killing 2

Winds cripple water drops as British, Greek crew survive

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 4, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Two Bell firefighting helicopters collided midair on August 2, 2026 over Psatha, west of Athens, while battling a wind-driven wildfire, killing 2 crew members and injuring 2 others. Officials said the dead were a Greek flight coordinator and a Danish pilot, while a British pilot and another Greek crew member survived. Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis expressed sorrow for the loss during operations near Porto Germeno, a seaside community on the Gulf of Corinth.

Authorities said each helicopter carried a two-person crew and was operating in difficult conditions over western Attica. Nearly 500 firefighters and 23 aircraft were deployed as gale-force winds pushed flames through dry forest, at times preventing aircraft from safely reaching the sea to collect water. The fire began Friday near Agios Vasileios and swept toward Porto Germeno; evacuations included 254 people moved by sea on Friday and 12 more on Saturday. Officials also reported evacuations on Kefalonia, while smoke and shifting winds threatened the industrial district near Megara.

The collision highlights the risks of aerial firefighting during Europe’s intensifying summer wildfire season, when strong winds, high temperatures and rugged terrain complicate safe flight operations and reduce the effectiveness of water drops. Crews continued ground operations across western Attica as authorities issued additional evacuation orders and warned residents to follow emergency alerts.

Sunday’s crash occurred amid a broader regional emergency. This year’s fires follow record damage across Europe in 2025, when more than 10,000 square kilometers burned. In Greece, memories remain sharp of the 2018 Mati disaster, which killed 104 people and stands as Europe’s deadliest wildfire of this century. Officials cautioned that conditions remain volatile and that casualty figures and damage assessments could change as response and investigations proceed.
