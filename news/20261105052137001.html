CVS Health plans split into two companies

Retail pharmacies to separate from Aetna and Caremark businesses

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 5, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

CVS Health plans to break apart its healthcare conglomerate, separating its nationwide retail pharmacy business from operations that include health insurer Aetna and pharmacy benefit manager CVS Caremark.

Under the plan announced November 4, CVS's roughly 9,000 retail pharmacies would become a separate publicly traded company. The remaining CVS Health business would retain Aetna, Caremark and primary-care provider Oak Street Health, separating the company's consumer-facing drugstores from much of the insurance and healthcare-services operation it assembled through years of acquisitions.

CVS expects the separation to be completed in the first half of 2028, subject to final board approval and other customary requirements. The company intends for the transaction to qualify as tax-free for federal income-tax purposes, with existing CVS Health shareholders receiving ownership in both companies.

The restructuring represents a major reversal of CVS's strategy of combining pharmacies, insurance, prescription-benefit management and medical care within a single corporation. CVS acquired Aetna for about $69 billion in 2018 and later expanded further into healthcare services, including its acquisition of Oak Street Health in 2023.

The announcement came alongside CVS's third-quarter results. The company reported quarterly revenue of approximately $108.4 billion, up 5.4% from a year earlier, while adjusted earnings reached $2.09 per share.

CVS said the two businesses can continue working together through commercial agreements after the separation. The company has not indicated that the restructuring will immediately change how customers fill prescriptions or how Aetna members receive coverage. Details about leadership, branding and the final structure of the standalone pharmacy company are expected as the separation moves forward.
