EU enlargement chief cancels Serbia visit over Mladić tributes

Government-assisted return of war criminal's remains draws criticism

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

European Union Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos canceled a planned visit to Serbia on September 4, saying the glorification surrounding the death of convicted war criminal Ratko Mladić was incompatible with the values required of countries seeking EU membership. The decision came one day after a Serbian government aircraft transported Mladić's remains from the Netherlands to Belgrade, where soldiers carried his Serbian flag-draped coffin from the plane and government officials joined family members in receiving it.

Mladić, the former commander of Bosnian Serb forces during the 1992-1995 Bosnian War, died on August 27 at age 84 while serving a life sentence in United Nations custody in The Hague. He was convicted of genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes, including genocide for his role in the 1995 Srebrenica massacre, in which more than 8,000 Bosniak Muslim men and boys were killed. He was also convicted for crimes connected to the siege of Sarajevo and other wartime atrocities.

Kos said Serbia's path toward EU membership requires confronting the past, reconciliation and respect for victims of war crimes. Serbia has been an EU candidate country since 2012 and began accession negotiations in 2014, but its membership process has progressed slowly amid disputes over democratic reforms, foreign policy and relations with neighboring countries.

Serbian President Aleksandar Vučić criticized Kos's explanation for canceling the trip. Serbian officials had initially discussed a state funeral for Mladić, but the government had not confirmed one following objections from European officials. An association of retired generals instead planned a commemoration at the Army Hall in Belgrade, followed by a funeral on September 7 at a church in a Belgrade suburb. Vučić said the government would provide logistical and transportation assistance.

The controversy has renewed debate over how Serbia's treatment of convicted wartime figures fits with the reconciliation standards expected during its EU accession process.
