North Korea Tests Two Types of Submarine-Launched Cruise Missiles

Underwater Weapons Demonstration Raises Questions About Nuclear Capabilities

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 15, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

North Korea announced on October 15, 2026, that it had successfully tested two types of submarine-launched strategic cruise missiles in waters off its eastern coast. The launches took place Wednesday, October 14, as Pyongyang continues expanding its naval weapons capabilities amid heightened tensions with South Korea and the United States.

According to North Korea's state-run Korean Central News Agency (KCNA), the tests were conducted by the country's missile development authorities to evaluate the readiness of underwater combat systems and improve the navy's ability to carry out strategic missions. North Korean officials described the launches as a demonstration of the reliability of the country's military deterrent.

The missiles were fired into the East Sea, also known as the Sea of Japan. KCNA identified one of the weapons as the Pulhwasal-3-31, a strategic cruise missile North Korea had previously tested. The government said the launches did not endanger neighboring countries.

Senior military officials, including Kim Jong Sik, a member of the ruling Workers' Party's Central Military Commission, and Jang Chang Ha, director of the country's missile development organization, observed the tests. North Korean leader Kim Jong Un was not identified among those supervising Wednesday's launches.

South Korea's military confirmed tracking the missiles. Officials indicated that the weapons appeared to have been launched using two different systems, including a vertical launch tube and a torpedo tube. However, the precise submarine involved and the weapons' operational capabilities remain subjects of analysis.

The launches could represent an important development in North Korea's efforts to establish a credible underwater missile force. Submarine-launched weapons can be more difficult to detect before launch than land-based systems, potentially complicating defensive planning for neighboring countries.

North Korea has described the weapons as strategic, terminology commonly associated with its nuclear deterrence program. However, the latest tests do not independently establish that the missiles carried nuclear warheads or that an operational submarine-based nuclear strike capability has been demonstrated.

The tests follow additional North Korean ballistic missile launches earlier in October, including weapons fired toward the sea on October 12. They also come as Pyongyang continues developing submarines intended to carry advanced missile systems.

North Korea unveiled its Hero Kim Kun Ok submarine in 2023, describing it as capable of launching nuclear weapons. Outside analysts have questioned aspects of the vessel's capabilities, and the latest launches may provide additional evidence about the progress of that program.
