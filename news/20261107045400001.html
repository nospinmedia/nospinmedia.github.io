Democrats take full control of Arizona government

First state trifecta for the party since 1966

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 7, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Arizona Democrats have won control of both chambers of the state Legislature while retaining the governor's office, giving the party complete control of state government for the first time since 1966.

The Associated Press called enough legislative races on Friday, November 6, to determine that Democrats had flipped both the Arizona House of Representatives and Arizona Senate. Democratic Gov. Katie Hobbs also retained the governor's office. Republicans had controlled the Arizona House for 60 years and the Senate since the early 1990s.

The change significantly alters the balance of power in Phoenix. During her first term, Hobbs vetoed more than 540 measures approved by the Republican-led Legislature, including legislation involving taxes, immigration and voting laws. Hobbs has said her priorities include addressing water supplies and affordability and increasing oversight of Arizona's school voucher program. Democratic control of the Legislature means the governor will now work with chambers led by her own party rather than divided government.

Arizona was part of a broader shift in state governments during the 2026 midterm elections. Democrats also gained full control of state government in Michigan, Minnesota, Pennsylvania, Nevada and Wisconsin, according to AP reporting. Democrats additionally flipped both legislative chambers in New Hampshire, while Republican Gov. Kelly Ayotte won reelection there. The National Conference of State Legislatures said the election could ultimately produce the largest number of legislative seats and chambers changing party control since 2010, with some races still being counted.

The Arizona result is particularly notable because it ends decades of Republican legislative control and gives Democrats the ability to advance legislation without needing support from a Republican-controlled chamber.
