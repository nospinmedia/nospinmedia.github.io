Anak Krakatau eruption shuts airports and disrupts hundreds of flights

Volcanic ash strands thousands of passengers across Indonesia

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Indonesia closed airspace around six airports Sunday after an eruption of Anak Krakatau sent volcanic ash across parts of western Indonesia, disrupting hundreds of flights and leaving thousands of passengers facing cancellations and delays. Soekarno-Hatta International Airport, the country's main international gateway near Jakarta, was among the airports affected after tests confirmed volcanic ash in the area. By mid-afternoon, authorities said 516 flight movements had been disrupted nationwide, including 202 at Soekarno-Hatta affecting about 22,800 passengers.

Indonesia's Geological Agency said Anak Krakatau, located in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra, had shown signs of increased activity since Friday before erupting for about 30 seconds at 3:53 a.m. Sunday. Closed-circuit video showed lava flaring from the volcano. Officials reported no casualties and issued no evacuation order, but warned residents and visitors to remain at least 3 kilometers from the active crater.

Satellite observations identified two major ash clouds. One reached approximately 20,000 feet and drifted northeast over Jakarta, Banten, West Java and nearby waters. A second climbed to about 50,000 feet and spread over portions of Banten, Lampung and Bengkulu provinces, the southern Sunda Strait and the Indian Ocean west of Sumatra. Volcanic ash can severely damage aircraft engines and other systems, making continued operations unsafe even when an airport itself is undamaged.

AirNav Indonesia closed airspace around Soekarno-Hatta and Halim Perdanakusuma airports in Jakarta along with four other airports, including Radin Inten II Airport in Lampung. The eruption also disrupted flights at Bali's I Gusti Ngurah Rai International Airport and at Medan. Civil aviation director general Lukman F. Laisa warned that cancellations could produce cascading problems involving aircraft rotations, fleet availability and terminal capacity as airlines attempt to restore their schedules.
