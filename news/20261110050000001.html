Trump questions midterm results after previously accepting them

President points to early voting and machines without providing evidence

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 10, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

President Trump is questioning the integrity of the 2026 midterm elections, less than a week after saying he was "absolutely willing" to accept the results and would work with Democrats following major Republican losses.

In a November 9 Truth Social post, Trump asked whether the election was "honest" and questioned how pre-election polls could have been wrong. He pointed to what he called unusually large early-voting "dumps" and wrote that "it's all about the machines," but did not identify specific jurisdictions, voting systems or evidence showing that votes had been improperly counted or changed.

The comments represent a shift from Trump's response on election night. As results came in on November 3, Trump told The New York Times that he was "absolutely willing to accept" them and said his administration would work with Democrats. He initially attributed Republican losses partly to his own absence from the ballot.

Democrats won control of both the House and Senate and made gains in other races across the country. Pre-election polling had generally indicated substantial Democratic gains, although some surveys underestimated their eventual size. Early and mail voting also produced strong Democratic results in several major races.

No evidence has emerged showing widespread voting-machine manipulation or fraudulent ballot counting that would account for the election results. The Associated Press examined several Election Day allegations, including claims involving ballot scanners and supposedly fraudulent ballots, and found equipment problems or false and unverified claims rather than evidence of coordinated election fraud. Nearly all U.S. voters also cast ballots using systems that produce or involve a paper record that can be reviewed during audits and recounts.

Trump's latest statements do not by themselves constitute a formal legal challenge to any election result. Election contests and recounts are governed by individual state laws and generally require challenges to specific results or procedures rather than broad allegations about nationwide voting.
