North Korea fires two ballistic missiles hours apart

Latest launches follow another weapons test just eight days earlier

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 21, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

North Korea fired two short-range ballistic missiles from its eastern coast on September 20, launching the weapons roughly three hours apart as regional governments monitored another round of weapons testing by Pyongyang.

South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said both missiles were launched from the Wonsan area toward the East Sea. The first was detected at about 3 p.m. local time and traveled approximately 450 kilometers (280 miles). A second launch was detected shortly before 6 p.m. and traveled more than 600 kilometers (373 miles). South Korean and U.S. intelligence authorities tracked the launches and shared information with Japan.

Japan's Defense Ministry also confirmed the launches and said both missiles landed outside the country's exclusive economic zone. No damage to aircraft or ships was reported. South Korea's National Security Council condemned the tests, while officials in Seoul said the launches violated United Nations Security Council resolutions restricting North Korea's ballistic missile program.

The launches marked North Korea's second round of missile testing in just over a week. Pyongyang fired multiple short-range ballistic missiles on September 12, also from its eastern coast. North Korea has recently criticized military exercises involving the United States, South Korea and Japan, describing them as threats to its security and warning that it could respond with additional military measures.

North Korea has continued expanding its missile and nuclear programs despite international sanctions and repeated U.N. resolutions restricting those activities. The latest launches add to a series of weapons tests this year as the United States, South Korea and Japan continue coordinating their monitoring and responses to Pyongyang's missile program.
