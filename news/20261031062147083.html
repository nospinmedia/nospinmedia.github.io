Texas grand jury indicts ICE officer after vehicle strike

Agent faces felony assault charge as ICE disputes prosecution

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 31, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

A Harris County, Texas, grand jury has indicted U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer Mario Alberto Morales on a felony charge of aggravated assault with a deadly weapon after he struck a man with an unmarked SUV during an immigration operation in Houston.

The charge stems from an October 10 pursuit in which Miguel Parra-Hernandez, 48, ran through a parking lot after getting out of a pickup truck. Video released by prosecutors shows Morales' SUV pursuing Parra-Hernandez before striking him. Parra-Hernandez suffered a broken leg and other injuries and remains in federal custody while receiving rehabilitation treatment.

Harris County District Attorney Sean Teare alleges that Morales intentionally drove into Parra-Hernandez. Prosecutors released surveillance and body-camera footage along with a recording of Morales' 911 call as part of their announcement. Body-camera footage recorded after the collision captures Morales acknowledging that he hit Parra-Hernandez with the vehicle. Morales has said he did not intentionally strike him and was attempting to avoid another vehicle.

ICE disputes the prosecution, calling allegations that Morales intentionally struck Parra-Hernandez false and describing the indictment as politically motivated. Morales' attorney has also said the collision was an accident and that his client committed no crime. The state charge is an allegation, and Morales has not been convicted.

The case could also produce a legal dispute over state authority to prosecute federal officers for actions taken while performing their duties. ICE has argued that local authorities lack jurisdiction to investigate federal officers acting in an official capacity. Teare rejects that position. Federal officers can raise immunity defenses under the Constitution's Supremacy Clause, leaving courts to determine whether those protections apply to the circumstances of this case.

The second-degree felony charge carries a possible sentence of two to 20 years in prison if Morales is convicted.
