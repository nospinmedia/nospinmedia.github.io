Florida dengue outbreak surpasses 250 local cases

Largest continental U.S. outbreak in decades

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 4, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Florida is experiencing what appears to be the largest dengue outbreak in the continental United States in decades, with state health officials reporting more than 250 locally acquired cases so far in 2026. The vast majority have been reported in Hillsborough County, which includes Tampa, marking an unusually large outbreak of a disease that is typically associated with travel outside the continental U.S.

Available state records and medical literature indicate that no state has reported this many locally acquired dengue cases in a calendar year since at least the 1940s. Florida's previous recent high was nearly 200 locally acquired cases in 2023. Health officials have also reported one death this year involving an elderly Tampa-area woman.

Dengue is spread primarily through infected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes and can cause fever, severe headaches, muscle and joint pain, nausea and rash. Some infections progress to severe dengue, which can cause bleeding, shock and potentially death. Most dengue cases reported in the continental United States are associated with travel, but local transmission occurs when a mosquito bites an infected person and subsequently transmits the virus to another person nearby.

Florida health officials and local mosquito-control agencies have intensified surveillance and mosquito-control efforts in affected areas. Hillsborough County has been under a mosquito-borne illness alert, while other counties, including Pinellas, Orange and Pasco, have also reported locally acquired infections and increased mosquito-control measures. Residents are being urged to drain standing water, cover exposed skin and use EPA-approved insect repellent to reduce mosquito bites.

Federal surveillance data remain preliminary and can lag behind state reporting, making Florida's figures the more current measure of the outbreak. The CDC says locally acquired dengue remains uncommon in the continental United States but has warned that mosquitoes capable of transmitting the virus are established across large parts of the country.
