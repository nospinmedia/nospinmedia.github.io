Israel votes with Netanyahu’s political future at stake

First national election since Oct. 7 attack could reshape government

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 27, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Israelis are voting Tuesday in the country’s first national election since the October 7, 2023, Hamas-led attack, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu facing a close contest that could determine whether his long tenure in power continues.

Polling stations opened at 7 a.m. local time for roughly 7.3 million eligible voters, including about 330,000 first-time voters. Netanyahu’s Likud and its right-wing allies are competing against an opposition led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, whose Yashar party has emerged as the largest opposition party. Opinion polling ahead of the vote indicated a close race, with neither side assured of controlling the 120-seat Knesset.

The election comes after three years dominated by the October 7 attack and subsequent wars involving Gaza, Lebanon and Iran. Netanyahu has emphasized his wartime leadership and relationship with President Trump, while Eisenkot and other opponents have focused heavily on accountability for the security failures surrounding October 7. Eisenkot joined Netanyahu’s war cabinet after the attack but left eight months later after criticizing the prime minister’s leadership.

Israelis vote for political parties rather than directly for prime minister, making the final balance of seats and subsequent coalition negotiations critical. No Israeli party has ever won an outright parliamentary majority. Parties must receive at least 3.25% of the national vote to enter the Knesset, and roughly a dozen parties could clear that threshold. If neither major bloc secures a workable majority, negotiations over a new government could continue for weeks.

Polls close at 10 p.m. local time, when Israeli television networks are expected to release exit polls. The final vote tally is expected by October 30, with official results scheduled for November 4. President Isaac Herzog will then consult party leaders before selecting the candidate he believes has the best chance of forming a government.
