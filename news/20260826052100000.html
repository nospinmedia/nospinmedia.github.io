White House Considers New Trade Measures Against Canada

Officials weigh additional tariffs after Ottawa's $20B retaliation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

The Trump administration is considering additional trade measures against Canada after Ottawa announced retaliatory tariffs on C$27.6 billion (about US$20 billion) worth of American imports. White House officials said discussions are underway on possible new duties and other trade restrictions targeting sectors that have so far avoided the steepest penalties, signaling that the dispute between the two countries could escalate further if negotiations remain stalled.

The latest deliberations follow Canada's announcement that it will impose retaliatory tariffs of 15%, 25%, and 50% on more than 700 categories of U.S. goods beginning September 8, 2026. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne said the measures were designed to match recently imposed U.S. tariffs while protecting Canadian industries. Ottawa also unveiled a C$7.5 billion support package for businesses and workers expected to be affected by the expanding trade conflict.

According to administration officials, the United States is evaluating additional actions that could affect Canadian automobiles, auto parts, steel, aluminum, and other major export sectors. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer has continued discussions with Canadian officials, but efforts to reach a broader agreement have so far failed after disputes over market access, supply management policies, and other longstanding trade issues. No final decision on additional U.S. tariffs has been announced.

Business groups and manufacturers on both sides of the border have warned that another round of tariffs could further disrupt integrated North American supply chains, increase production costs, and raise prices for consumers. The latest exchange of retaliatory measures has intensified concerns about the future of the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) as both governments continue to defend their respective trade policies while leaving open the possibility of renewed negotiations.
