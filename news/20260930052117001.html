Congress votes to formally end penny production

Existing pennies stay legal as cash rounding expands

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 30, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Congress has passed the bipartisan Common Cents Act, sending legislation to President Trump that would formally end production of the penny after 234 years. The Senate approved the measure by voice vote late September 28, following earlier House approval. The legislation gives congressional authorization to a change already underway: the U.S. Mint stopped producing pennies for circulation in 2025 under the Trump administration.

The financial argument for eliminating the coin has grown as production costs increased. According to the U.S. Mint, producing a single penny now costs almost 4 cents, and ending production is expected to generate about $56 million in annual savings. The penny was among the first coins produced after the Mint was established in 1792. The Common Cents Act was sponsored in the House by Rep. Lisa McClain, a Michigan Republican, and Rep. Robert Garcia, a California Democrat.

Pennies already in circulation will remain legal tender, meaning consumers can continue spending them and businesses can continue accepting them. The Mint produced 3.2 billion pennies in 2024, and billions more remain in circulation. As supplies gradually decline, the legislation allows businesses to round cash transaction totals up or down to the nearest 5 cents when exact change is unavailable. Electronic, credit-card and other non-cash transactions can continue to be charged to the exact cent.

The legislation also addresses another increasingly expensive coin: the nickel. It would give the government flexibility to use a less expensive composition, including an inner layer of zinc, if testing shows the change can reduce costs without significantly disrupting coin-operated machines. Retail organizations supported congressional action partly because businesses have faced uncertainty about how to handle cash purchases as penny supplies decline.

President Trump is expected to sign the legislation. Once enacted, the change would make permanent the end of circulating penny production while leaving the coins Americans already have usable as money.
