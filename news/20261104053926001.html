Roy Cooper wins North Carolina Senate race

Democrat flips key seat after defeating Michael Whatley

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 4, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Democrat Roy Cooper won North Carolina's U.S. Senate election Tuesday, defeating Republican Michael Whatley and flipping a key battleground seat as Democrats gained control of the Senate nationally.

Cooper, a former two-term governor of North Carolina, entered the race with an extensive record of winning statewide elections. Before serving as governor from 2017 to 2025, he spent four terms as the state's attorney general. His victory sends him back into elected office representing a state that has remained closely divided in recent national elections.

Whatley, a former chairman of the Republican National Committee and former leader of the North Carolina Republican Party, sought to keep the seat in Republican hands. The race became one of the most closely watched Senate contests of the 2026 midterms because of North Carolina's competitive political landscape and the national battle for control of Congress.

The result gives Democrats a significant pickup in a state where Republicans have generally had the advantage in federal statewide races. North Carolina has voted Republican in most recent presidential elections while remaining competitive in races for governor and other statewide offices. Cooper's previous electoral success made the former governor one of the Democratic Party's strongest potential candidates for the seat.

His victory also contributed to the broader Democratic gains that shifted control of the Senate following Tuesday's elections. Democrats captured several competitive and Republican-held seats around the country, including races in Texas and Maine, while also winning the closely watched Michigan contest.

Cooper will join the new Democratic Senate majority when the next Congress convenes in January 2027, giving North Carolina a new senator and Democrats another seat from a major Southern battleground.
