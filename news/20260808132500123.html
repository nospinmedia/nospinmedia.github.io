U.S. Sheds 23,000 Jobs in July 2026

Unemployment dips to 4.1% as 264,000 exit labor force

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 8, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that nonfarm payrolls fell by 23,000 in July 2026, the first monthly decline since February, while the unemployment rate edged down to 4.1%. The agency said the lower jobless rate coincided with 264,000 people leaving the labor force rather than stronger hiring, signaling continued softness in the employment market.

The report also revised payroll gains from the previous two months down by a combined 103,000 jobs. Economists had expected employers to add roughly 100,000 jobs in July, making the decline a significant miss. Job losses were concentrated in public education and several consumer-facing industries, while construction and manufacturing posted modest gains. Reported changes included: - Local government education: −50,000 - Restaurants and bars: −26,000 - Retailers: −19,000 - Construction: +22,000 - Manufacturing: +5,000

The labor force participation rate fell to 61.4%, the lowest level since February 2021, highlighting weaker workforce participation even as the headline unemployment rate declined. Daniel Zhao, chief economist at Glassdoor, described the report as disappointing, saying, “We can’t really put lipstick on a pig here.” The White House emphasized areas of continued strength, with spokesman Kush Desai pointing to gains of 22,000 construction jobs and 5,000 manufacturing jobs. So far in 2026, employers have added an average of about 61,000 jobs per month, well below the 2023–2024 average of 166,000.

The report arrives as the Federal Reserve weighs whether inflation remaining above its 2% target and higher energy costs justify tighter monetary policy despite signs of a slowing job market. Investors viewed the report as reducing the likelihood of a near-term interest rate increase, shifting attention to upcoming inflation data ahead of the next FOMC meeting.
