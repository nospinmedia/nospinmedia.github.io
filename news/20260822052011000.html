TikTok Agrees to $400 Million Child Privacy Settlement

DOJ Deal Ends Federal COPPA Lawsuit Over Children's Data

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 22, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The U.S. Department of Justice announced that TikTok and its parent company, ByteDance, have agreed to pay $400 million to resolve federal allegations that the platform violated the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) by collecting personal information from children under 13 without obtaining verifiable parental consent. The settlement resolves a lawsuit filed in August 2024 after a referral from the Federal Trade Commission, which alleged the company knowingly allowed young children to create standard accounts, retained their personal data, and failed to honor some parental requests to delete accounts and information.

Under the agreement, TikTok will pay $300 million immediately, with an additional $100 million becoming payable after a federal court vacates a 2019 consent decree involving predecessor app Musical.ly. Justice Department officials described the agreement as one of the largest financial recoveries ever obtained in a federal children's online privacy case. The settlement also requires TikTok to continue maintaining enhanced compliance procedures designed to ensure the platform meets federal requirements governing the collection and retention of children's data.

Federal officials said TikTok has implemented significant operational changes since the lawsuit was filed, including stronger age-verification systems, expanded parental controls, and improved processes for identifying and deleting accounts created by children who are not permitted to use the standard service. The settlement follows broader corporate restructuring that reduced ByteDance's ownership stake in TikTok's U.S. operations while increasing oversight by American investors, though the agreement focuses specifically on privacy compliance rather than ownership or national security concerns.

The case represents another major federal enforcement action targeting how large technology platforms handle children's personal information. Regulators have increasingly emphasized that online services must provide meaningful parental consent mechanisms and promptly remove children's data when required by law. The settlement resolves the government's civil claims without requiring further litigation while imposing substantial financial penalties and reinforcing ongoing compliance obligations for one of the world's largest social media platforms.
