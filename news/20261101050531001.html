Iran orders military response without geographic limits

Khamenei tells commanders to expand potential U.S. and Israeli targets

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 1, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Iranian Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei has ordered the country's military to prepare an immediate response without geographic limits if the United States or Israel launches another attack, as diplomatic efforts continue to prevent renewed fighting.

Khamenei delivered the instructions during a reported meeting with senior military commanders, including armed forces chief of staff Gen. Ali Abdollahi, army commander Maj. Gen. Amir Hatami and Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps commander Maj. Gen. Ahmad Vahidi. According to a statement from Khamenei's office, he said any renewed military or terrorist attack should receive an immediate and effective response without geographic restrictions.

Khamenei also instructed commanders to update Iran's potential target list so that U.S. and Israeli assets would be within reach of Iranian forces. The commanders briefed him on military readiness around the Strait of Hormuz, Gulf coastline, Gulf of Oman and Iran's borders, according to reporting on the meeting.

The warning comes as fears grow that fighting could resume following the U.S. midterm elections. President Donald Trump has been considering additional military action against Iran, while Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has also raised the possibility of renewed strikes. The United States and Israel launched attacks against Iran on February 28, beginning the current conflict and prompting Iranian retaliation across the region.

Diplomatic contacts are continuing at the same time. Qatari mediators held lengthy talks in Tehran before discussions continued in Doha, while Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said senior officials had discussed a proposal from U.S. Vice President JD Vance. Araghchi described the U.S. effort to open talks as a positive step but said major disagreements remain over sanctions, the U.S. military presence in the region and navigation through the Strait of Hormuz.
