Native American women make history across U.S. elections

Flanagan wins Senate seat as Haaland becomes first Native woman governor

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 9, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Native American women achieved several historic victories in the 2026 elections, winning offices in the U.S. Senate, a governor's race and tribal governments across the country. Among the biggest milestones, Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota became the first Native American woman elected to the U.S. Senate, while Deb Haaland became the first Native American woman elected governor of a U.S. state.

Flanagan, a Democrat and citizen of the White Earth Nation, defeated Republican Michele Tafoya in Minnesota's Senate race. Flanagan previously served as Minnesota's lieutenant governor. Her victory breaks a barrier more than 230 years after the Senate was established. Only four Native Americans, all men, had previously served in the chamber, according to the Associated Press.

In New Mexico, Democrat Deb Haaland, a citizen of the Laguna Pueblo, defeated Republican Gregg Hull to become governor. Haaland had already made history twice at the federal level: in 2018 she was among the first two Native American women elected to Congress, and in 2021 she became the first Native American to serve as a U.S. Cabinet secretary when she took over the Department of the Interior.

Historic results also occurred within tribal governments. Crystalyne Curley was elected the first woman president of the Navajo Nation, one of the country's largest federally recognized tribes. Curley previously served as speaker of the Navajo Nation Council. In western New York, longtime council member Tina Abrams became the first woman elected president of the Seneca Nation of Indians.

Native representation also continued in the U.S. House, where Kansas Democrat Sharice Davids, a member of the Ho-Chunk Nation, and Oklahoma Republican Josh Brecheen, a Choctaw citizen, won reelection. Additional Native candidates won congressional seats in New York and Michigan.

The victories place Native women simultaneously in influential positions across federal, state and sovereign tribal governments, marking a significant expansion of Indigenous representation in elected leadership.
