Judge blocks Trump’s second birthright citizenship order

Supreme Court ruling protects children in class

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A federal judge has temporarily blocked President Trump’s latest attempt to narrow birthright citizenship, ruling that the new policy is likely unconstitutional for children already protected by a nationwide class action. U.S. District Judge Deborah L. Boardman in Maryland issued the preliminary injunction on September 2, preventing federal agencies from denying or refusing to recognize the citizenship of children covered by the case while litigation continues.

President Trump signed the revised executive order on August 6, 2026, after the U.S. Supreme Court struck down his broader birthright citizenship policy in Trump v. Barbara on June 30. In that 6-3 decision, the Court held that children born in the United States to parents who are unlawfully or temporarily present are citizens at birth under the 14th Amendment’s Citizenship Clause. Boardman wrote that the Supreme Court had already resolved the status of children within the certified class and said the latest order was "almost certainly unconstitutional" as applied to them.

The August order sought narrower exceptions, including children whose parents are classified as "alien enemies," work for foreign governments in the United States, engage in fraud or certain commercial transactions to obtain citizenship, or enter the country for what the administration describes as "birth tourism." The injunction bars agencies including the Department of State, Department of Homeland Security, and Social Security Administration from taking actions that interfere with citizenship rights of children covered by the class action.

Justice Department attorneys argued that the challenge was premature because federal agencies had not yet released final implementation guidance. Boardman rejected that argument, although she allowed agencies to continue preparing guidance while enforcement remains blocked. The lawsuit includes immigrant families along with CASA and the Asylum Seeker Advocacy Project. The administration can appeal the injunction as the broader dispute over the scope of the August order continues.
