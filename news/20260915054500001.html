Watchdog finds detainees held in tiny metal enclosures

Florida's closed 'Alligator Alcatraz' failed detention standards

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 15, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

A federal watchdog found that 79 immigration detainees at Florida's now-closed "Alligator Alcatraz" detention center were confined in small metal enclosures offering about 18 square feet of floor space, sometimes for nearly two hours. The Department of Homeland Security Office of Inspector General said use of the enclosures was unprecedented among detention facilities its inspectors had examined and presented significant risks to detainees' health and safety.

The state-run facility, officially known as the Florida Soft-Sided Facility, operated in the Everglades near Ochopee, Florida. During an unannounced inspection in January 2026, investigators found staff used the metal enclosures as so-called "calming areas." Records showed detainees were placed in them between July 17, 2025, and January 18, 2026, for periods ranging from several minutes to nearly two hours. Investigators also found at least one instance in which an enclosure appeared to have been used after a detainee failed to comply with a command.

The watchdog documented problems extending beyond the small enclosures. Standards required at least 75 square feet of floor space per detainee in dormitory housing, while the facility provided about 28 square feet per person at maximum capacity. Detainees generally received recreation for only one hour three days a week and showers three times a week. Inspectors also identified deficiencies involving clean drinking water, personal hygiene, medical care, food service, environmental health and safety, and access to legal materials.

The facility opened in July 2025 as Florida and the federal government expanded immigration detention capacity. It ceased operations in June 2026, months before the inspector general's findings became public.

DHS disputed allegations that detainees were subjected to inhumane conditions and said Florida maintained authority over the facility's day-to-day operations. The inspector general said the facility nevertheless housed people detained under federal immigration authority and was expected to comply with applicable detention standards.
