Death Toll Rises to 38 After Russian Strike Ignites Ukraine Ammunition Depot

Investigation launched into storage near civilian homes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 30, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A Russian drone strike on a military warehouse in the village of Myla, west of Kyiv, triggered a massive secondary explosion that has killed at least 38 people, making it the deadliest single attack in Ukraine this year. Ukrainian officials said four people remain missing, while 20 others, including two children, were injured. The explosion and resulting fires damaged at least 50 homes and forced more than 370 residents to evacuate as ammunition continued detonating for hours after the initial strike.

Ukrainian authorities said the warehouse contained military ammunition, including artillery shells and mines, which exploded after the strike. Among the victims were 34 residents of a nearby care facility for elderly and disabled people that suffered catastrophic damage during the blast. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the tragedy unacceptable and said ammunition should never be stored near residential neighborhoods, pledging that those responsible would be held accountable if safety rules were violated.

The Security Service of Ukraine (SBU), the National Police, and prosecutors have opened a criminal investigation into whether the warehouse was lawfully storing explosive materials and whether all required permits and safety regulations had been followed. Investigators are examining possible negligence related to the placement and management of the military stockpile. Ukrainian officials emphasized that the inquiry is focused on domestic compliance with safety laws and does not diminish Russia's responsibility for launching the attack.

Russia's Ministry of Defense said its forces targeted facilities involved in military logistics and long-range drone production, while Ukrainian officials maintained that the enormous loss of civilian life resulted from the detonation of ammunition stored near populated areas. The disaster has intensified debate within Ukraine over wartime logistics, balancing military necessity against civilian safety, and whether ammunition storage practices should be revised as Russian strikes increasingly target supply infrastructure.
