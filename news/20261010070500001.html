Ukraine Strikes Russian Fuel Sites Hours After Trump-Putin Diesel Deal

Kyiv Signals Attacks on Russia's Energy Infrastructure Will Continue

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 10, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Ukraine struck Russian fuel infrastructure early Saturday, October 10, 2026, just hours after President Trump announced an agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin intended to bring millions of tons of Russian diesel onto U.S. and global markets.

Ukrainian drones targeted an oil facility in Rostov-on-Don, where Russian officials reported a fire following the overnight attack. The strike was part of Ukraine's continuing campaign against Russian refineries, fuel depots and other energy infrastructure that Kyiv says supports Moscow's military operations and provides revenue for the war.

Another reported Ukrainian attack targeted fuel infrastructure in Russia's Belgorod region, continuing a pattern of increasingly frequent long-range strikes inside Russian territory. The attacks came as Ukraine made clear that the new U.S.-Russia diesel arrangement would not stop its campaign against Russia's petroleum industry.

The timing was notable because Trump had announced the diesel agreement with Putin only hours earlier. Under the arrangement, Russia is expected to release substantial quantities of diesel into international markets as the United States temporarily eases some sanctions restrictions on Russian petroleum transactions. Trump has said increasing supplies could help reduce elevated diesel prices affecting transportation, agriculture and other sectors of the economy.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy strongly criticized the agreement, arguing that allowing additional Russian energy sales would provide Moscow with revenue while Russian attacks against Ukraine continue. Kyiv has repeatedly argued that reducing Russia's income from oil and petroleum products is an important part of weakening its ability to finance the war.

Ukraine's attacks on Russian refineries and fuel facilities have increasingly become part of that strategy. Repeated drone strikes have damaged refining and storage infrastructure, forcing repairs and periodically reducing Russian fuel-processing capacity. Moscow has accused Ukraine of deliberately targeting civilian energy infrastructure, while Kyiv maintains that facilities supporting Russia's military and war economy are legitimate targets.

The latest strikes also highlight a potential complication for Trump's diesel agreement. Additional Russian exports depend partly on the country's ability to refine, store and transport fuel, while Ukraine is simultaneously attempting to disrupt that infrastructure.

There is no indication that the Rostov strike was launched specifically in response to the Trump-Putin agreement; Ukraine's campaign against Russian energy facilities began long before Friday's announcement. However, Kyiv has signaled that the diplomatic development will not cause it to halt those operations.
