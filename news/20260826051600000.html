State Department Pauses Immigrant Visa Interviews Worldwide

Global consular training delays appointments as public-charge review expands

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The U.S. Department of State has directed American embassies and consulates worldwide to temporarily pause and reschedule immigrant visa interview appointments while consular officers complete a global training initiative on evaluating whether applicants are likely to become a public charge under Section 212(a)(4) of the Immigration and Nationality Act. The department said appointment schedules are being adjusted to accommodate the training, but it has not announced when normal interview operations will resume.

Applicants at diplomatic posts in countries including the Philippines, Mexico, Kenya, and South Korea reported receiving notices that their scheduled interviews had been postponed and would be rescheduled at a later date. Immigration attorneys said many affected applicants had already completed medical examinations, gathered required documentation, and spent significant money on travel and other expenses after waiting months or years for interview appointments.

The worldwide pause follows an August 21 ruling by U.S. District Judge Jeannette Vargas, who struck down a State Department policy that suspended immigrant visa processing for applicants from 75 countries, concluding the measure exceeded the secretary of state's authority under federal immigration law. State Department officials said the new global training initiative is separate from that ruling and is intended to ensure consular officers apply public-charge standards consistently when reviewing immigrant visa applications.

The temporary suspension is the latest immigration action under President Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, whose administration has expanded visa screening, pursued additional visa revocations, and tightened immigration enforcement during Trump's second term. While officials describe the training as necessary to ensure uniform application of existing law, immigration advocates argue the delays will add to already lengthy visa backlogs and further postpone family reunification and employment-based immigration.
