Kenya helicopter crash kills five Americans and Ecuador’s intel chief

Probe focuses on tourist flight that went down near Mount Ololokwe

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 20, 2026 at 8:16 PM ET

A chartered helicopter crashed on the slopes of Mount Ololokwe in Samburu County, northern Kenya, on August 19, 2026, killing all seven people aboard, Kenyan authorities said. Among the dead were five Americans and Michele Sensi-Contugi, director general of Ecuador’s Strategic Intelligence Centre, along with his wife Stephany Hollihan. The U.S. Department of State confirmed the American deaths and said the U.S. Embassy in Nairobi is providing consular assistance.

Authorities and local responders reported no survivors at the steep crash site. The aircraft, a Eurocopter EC130 B4 registered 5Y-GYM and operated by Lady Lori Kenya Ltd., ignited after impact. The helicopter departed the Loisaba Conservancy and went down en route to Ewasonyiro at about 9:13 a.m. local time, according to preliminary information released by Kenyan officials. Family and employer statements identified two of the American victims as José Alberto Suárez, 55, president and general manager of several Telemundo stations in Florida, and Miami restaurateur Roger Duarte.

Kenya’s Air Accident Investigation Department and the Kenya Civil Aviation Authority have opened a formal investigation into the crash. Investigators are reviewing weather conditions, terrain, maintenance records, radio communications, and any available flight telemetry to determine what caused the accident. Officials have not identified a likely cause, and no mechanical or human-factor conclusions have been released.

The helicopter was operating along a popular safari route through Kenya's mountainous conservation region when it crashed. The disaster has renewed attention on aviation safety for tourist charter flights operating in rugged terrain, where changing weather, elevation, and navigation challenges can complicate flight operations. Investigators are expected to release additional findings as the inquiry progresses.
