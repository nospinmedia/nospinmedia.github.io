Saudi Arabia halts some European oil deliveries after pipeline attack

Satellite images show damage as key Hormuz bypass remains shut

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 16, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Saudi Arabia has canceled some oil deliveries to Europe after drone attacks damaged and shut its East-West oil pipeline, escalating a disruption to one of the world's most important alternative crude-export routes. The 1,200-kilometer (746-mile) pipeline connects Saudi oil fields in the east with the Red Sea port of Yanbu, allowing crude to bypass the Strait of Hormuz, where shipping has already been severely disrupted by the regional conflict.

Satellite imagery taken after the attack shows significant fire damage at infrastructure along the pipeline route southeast of Medina. Saudi officials initially described the shutdown as a precaution while specialized teams assessed the pipeline, but estimates of the repair timeline have since varied considerably. Sources familiar with the damage have said repairs could require several weeks, while U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright has said he expects operations could resume within days.

The pipeline had recently been carrying roughly 4 million to 5 million barrels of crude per day, equivalent to about 4% to 5% of global oil supply, as Saudi Arabia increasingly relied on the route to avoid disruptions through Hormuz. With the pipeline unavailable, Saudi Arabia has also halted oil loadings at Yanbu, while some scheduled European deliveries have been canceled as buyers seek alternative supplies.

The disruption comes at an especially sensitive moment for global energy markets. Before the current conflict, roughly 20 million barrels per day of oil moved through the Strait of Hormuz, but industry estimates cited in reporting indicate flows have fallen sharply. That made the East-West pipeline one of Saudi Arabia's most important remaining export alternatives. Oil prices have climbed above $100 per barrel amid the tightening supply picture.

Saudi Arabia says the drones that struck the pipeline originated in Iraq, and Baghdad has launched an investigation. No group has claimed responsibility for the pipeline attack. The pipeline's uncertain repair timeline and the cancellation of deliveries now raise questions about how long Saudi Arabia can maintain exports while multiple regional energy routes remain under pressure.
