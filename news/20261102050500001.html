Supreme Court takes up Colorado religious preschool dispute

Case tests conditions on public funding for faith-based schools

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 2, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court is set to hear arguments Tuesday in a dispute over whether Colorado can require Catholic preschools to follow state nondiscrimination rules as a condition of participating in its publicly funded universal preschool program.

The case, St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy, was brought by two Catholic parishes and the Archdiocese of Denver. They argue that Colorado's requirements violate the First Amendment's Free Exercise Clause by forcing religious schools to choose between participating in a generally available public benefit and operating according to their religious beliefs.

Colorado launched its universal preschool program in 2023, providing state-funded preschool through public schools and participating private providers, including religious schools. Providers generally must agree not to discriminate based on characteristics including religion, sexual orientation and gender identity.

The Catholic schools point to the Supreme Court's 2022 decision in Carson v. Makin, which held that Maine could not exclude religious schools from a tuition-assistance program because they provided religious instruction. The Colorado plaintiffs argue that the same principle prevents the state from conditioning preschool funding on their willingness to abandon religiously based enrollment practices.

Colorado argues that a different precedent, Employment Division v. Smith from 1990, applies because its nondiscrimination requirements are neutral and generally applicable rather than specifically targeting religion.

The schools dispute that characterization. They argue Colorado permits some secular enrollment preferences, including preferences involving children from low-income families or children with disabilities, while refusing comparable accommodations for religious practices. That distinction, they contend, means the rules are not generally applicable.

Lower federal courts rejected the Catholic schools' constitutional challenge before the Supreme Court agreed to hear the case. Oral arguments are scheduled for November 3, with a decision expected later in the court's current term.
