Greene Says U.S. Officials Discussed Nuclear Use Against Iran

Former congresswoman provides no evidence for claim

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 20, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Former U.S. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene alleged that officials in the Trump administration have discussed using nuclear weapons against Iran during strategy meetings, an extraordinary claim that remains unverified. Greene made the allegation publicly on August 16, writing that officials were discussing nuclear weapons and insisting she was not speculating. She did not identify the officials involved, explain how she obtained the information or provide documents or other evidence supporting her account.

USA Today reported Greene's allegation and sought comment from the White House, which had not responded as of its August 17 update. Anadolu Agency separately reported the claim and likewise said Greene offered no evidence to substantiate it; the White House also did not immediately respond to that outlet's request for comment. No independent confirmation establishing that such nuclear-use discussions occurred has emerged in the reporting reviewed for this story.

Greene, a former Republican congresswoman from Georgia who has become a vocal opponent of the U.S. conflict with Iran, characterized the alleged discussions as dangerous and invoked the 1945 atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki while arguing against any U.S. nuclear escalation. Her public warning concerns what she says is occurring inside government strategy discussions; it is not evidence that President Trump has ordered, approved or publicly threatened a nuclear strike on Iran.

President Trump has continued to say that preventing Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon is a central U.S. objective. On August 17, Trump wrote that Iran cannot be allowed to possess a nuclear weapon, but that statement did not threaten American nuclear-weapons use. The distinction is important: the verified development is that Greene, a former member of Congress and former Trump ally, publicly made the allegation. Whether administration officials have actually discussed using nuclear weapons against Iran remains unconfirmed.
