Shein Cuts Hong Kong IPO Valuation To About $25 Billion

Fast-fashion giant lowers expectations ahead of planned share sale

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 2:46 PM ET

Fast-fashion retailer Shein is preparing to market its planned Hong Kong initial public offering at a company valuation of about $25 billion to $28 billion, according to people familiar with the offering. The revised target is substantially lower than the company's $98.2 billion private valuation in 2022 and below estimates discussed earlier this month, reflecting weaker investor sentiment and slowing business growth.

The lower valuation follows slowing revenue growth, a reported $99 million operating loss during the first quarter of 2026, and increasing pressure from trade policy changes affecting low-cost imports into the United States. Shein has also faced growing competition from rival online retailer Temu, while continuing to navigate political and regulatory scrutiny over its supply chain and labor practices.

The company previously explored stock market listings in both New York and London, but those efforts stalled amid regulatory concerns. Hong Kong has now emerged as Shein's preferred listing venue, with bookbuilding expected to begin this week ahead of a planned market debut later this month, although the final valuation and offering terms remain subject to investor demand.

The reduced valuation reflects broader changes in how investors are pricing high-growth e-commerce companies after several years of higher interest rates and slower consumer spending. Analysts say the offering will serve as an important test of investor appetite for major international retail listings and could influence future public offerings by other global consumer brands.
