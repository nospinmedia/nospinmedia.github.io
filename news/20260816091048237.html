Australia Sets Nov. 2 Start For Major Gun Buyback

New South Wales expects up to 274,000 firearms to be surrendered

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 16, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

New South Wales will begin Australia's largest gun buyback in decades on November 2, 2026, with officials estimating that as many as 274,000 registered firearms could be surrendered under tighter ownership rules adopted after the deadly Bondi Beach attack. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and NSW Premier Chris Minns announced the rollout on August 16, describing it as larger in scope within the state than the landmark buyback that followed the 1996 Port Arthur massacre.

The program follows gun-law changes enacted after 15 people were killed during a Hanukkah celebration at Bondi Beach on December 14, 2025. NSW now generally limits licensed owners to four firearms, with exemptions allowing up to 10 for certain primary producers and professional shooters. The buyback will target firearms that exceed those limits as well as weapons affected by new restrictions.

The first phase will offer fixed compensation based on firearm type, ranging from A$450 for air rifles to as much as A$1,000 for revolvers and some rifles. A second phase planned for early 2027 will address higher-value firearms, with compensation of up to A$10,000 per weapon. Authorities estimate roughly 50,000 licence holders could be affected by the new limits and classifications.

NSW contains more than 1.1 million registered firearms, and officials estimate the buyback could remove roughly a quarter of them. The Commonwealth government is supporting the program and has offered similar arrangements to other states and territories, but participation across Australia remains uneven, with several jurisdictions declining or still considering whether to join.

Supporters argue the program extends Australia's long-standing approach of tightening firearm laws after mass-casualty attacks, while opponents say it places substantial costs on compliant gun owners and may provide inadequate compensation for some firearms.
