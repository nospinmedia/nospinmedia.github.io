Hurricane Lala Skirts Hawaii's Big Island

One dead as storm leaves outages, flooding and flight disruptions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 16, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Hurricane Lala passed just south of Hawaii's Big Island on August 15, 2026, avoiding a direct landfall but still bringing damaging winds, heavy rain, dangerous surf and widespread disruptions across the state. The National Weather Service said the storm's track spared Hawaii what would have been the Big Island's first direct hurricane landfall since 1871, but officials warned that hazardous conditions would continue through the weekend.

Gov. Josh Green said at least one person died in a vehicle crash near South Point during the storm. Lala strengthened into a Category 1 hurricane with sustained winds near 75 mph as it passed the southern tip of the island, knocking down trees and power lines and leaving tens of thousands of customers without electricity. State officials also reported approximately 190 flight postponements and damage to at least 19 buildings that lost portions of their roofs.

Tropical storm warnings remained in effect for Maui, Molokai, Lanai, Kahoolawe, Oahu, Kauai and Niihau as Lala continued moving west-northwest. Forecasters warned of additional flash flooding, mudslides in steep terrain and dangerous surf as rain bands spread across the island chain. The Central Pacific Hurricane Center forecast 8 to 12 inches of rain in parts of the Big Island and Maui, with lower but still significant totals elsewhere across the state.

Although the storm is expected to continue moving away from Hawaii and gradually weaken over cooler waters, emergency officials warned that flooding, saturated ground, high surf and scattered power outages could continue through at least August 17. Damage assessments are underway across multiple islands, and residents have been urged to avoid flooded roads, stay away from hazardous coastlines and follow local emergency guidance as recovery efforts begin.
