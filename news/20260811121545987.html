NOAA: Lower 48 Records Hottest Month Ever

July 2026 Tops Dust Bowl Heat Mark

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 10:22 AM ET

The Lower 48 states recorded their hottest month since national records began in 1895, with July 2026 averaging 76.9°F, according to new data released by NOAA on August 10, 2026. The milestone surpassed previous national July records set during the 1936 Dust Bowl and the widespread heat of 2012, highlighting how persistent high temperatures affected much of the country throughout the month.

NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information reported that July's average temperature exceeded the previous records by roughly 0.1–0.2°F. Overnight heat played a major role, with the national average low reaching 64.2°F, breaking the previous July record from 2022 by 0.7°F. Every one of the 48 contiguous states finished at least 1°F above its 20th-century average. During early July, more than 185 million people were placed under heat alerts. While destructive flooding struck areas including the Texas Hill Country and eastern New York, NOAA also reported that about 48.5% of the Lower 48 remained in drought as of August 4.

Persistently warm nights can increase health risks because people, infrastructure and agriculture have less opportunity to recover from extreme daytime heat. NOAA said the month also produced numerous local records, including Salt Lake City, which reached an all-time high of 109°F on July 12. The widespread heat across the Mountain West, Southwest, Northern Plains and Southeast increased concerns about wildfire conditions, electrical demand and heat-related illness, particularly among older adults and outdoor workers.

Breaking records established during the 1936 Dust Bowl and the hot summer of 2012 underscores how unusual July 2026 was across the contiguous United States. Forecasters said the record reflected not only extreme daytime temperatures but also historically warm nights spanning much of the nation.
