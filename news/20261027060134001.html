FTC and 20 states sue Dollar General over pricing

Lawsuit alleges shoppers paid more at checkout than shelf prices

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 27, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The Federal Trade Commission and a bipartisan coalition of 20 states have sued Dollar General, alleging the discount retailer repeatedly charged customers higher prices at checkout than the prices displayed on store shelves.

The federal lawsuit alleges that pricing discrepancies have occurred for years at Dollar General stores across the country. Regulators say shoppers sometimes discovered the higher price only after reaching the register, while others may not have noticed the difference at all. The complaint alleges the practices violate federal and state consumer-protection laws.

According to the FTC, government inspectors have documented pricing problems at Dollar General locations in multiple states. The agency said some stores failed state pricing inspections at rates substantially higher than competing retailers. State regulators have previously taken enforcement actions against Dollar General over similar discrepancies between shelf labels and checkout prices.

The case has particular significance because of Dollar General's enormous reach. The Tennessee-based company operates more than 20,000 stores across the United States, including many in rural and lower-income communities where customers may have fewer nearby shopping alternatives. Regulators argue that even relatively small pricing discrepancies can add up for households closely managing grocery and household budgets.

The lawsuit seeks monetary relief for consumers and court orders requiring Dollar General to improve pricing accuracy. The government is also seeking civil penalties for alleged violations of state laws.

Dollar General said it disagrees with the allegations and intends to defend itself. The company said it takes pricing accuracy seriously and has invested in technology, staffing and operational changes intended to improve the consistency of shelf and register prices.

The allegations have not been proven in court, and the lawsuit begins a legal process that could result in a settlement, dismissal or trial.
