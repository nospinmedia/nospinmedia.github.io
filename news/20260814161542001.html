Hurricane Warning Issued For Hawaii's Big Island As Tropical Storm Lala Nears

Heavy Rain, Flooding, And Dangerous Surf Expected This Weekend

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 6:51 PM ET

A hurricane warning is in effect for Hawaii County as Tropical Storm Lala continues strengthening while approaching the Hawaiian Islands. Forecasters with the National Hurricane Center said the system is expected to reach hurricane strength before passing near the Big Island on Saturday, bringing the threat of damaging winds, life-threatening flooding, dangerous surf, and mudslides. A tropical storm watch remains in effect for Maui County, including Maui, Lanai, Molokai, and Kahoolawe.

As of the latest advisory, Lala was located about 455 miles east-southeast of Hilo with maximum sustained winds of 60 mph while moving west-northwest at 12 mph. Forecasters expect additional strengthening over warm Pacific waters during the next one to two days. Rainfall totals of 8 to 12 inches are forecast across portions of the Big Island and Maui, with isolated locations potentially receiving up to 25 inches, creating a significant risk of flash flooding, landslides, and rapidly rising streams.

Emergency officials urged residents to complete storm preparations before tropical-storm-force winds arrive. Coastal communities could also experience dangerous surf, rough seas, localized storm surge, and scattered power outages. Authorities advised residents to monitor official forecasts closely, avoid unnecessary travel during the storm, and be prepared for rapidly changing conditions through the weekend.

The storm represents Hawaii's most significant tropical cyclone threat in several years and could become the first hurricane to directly affect the Big Island since 2018 if current forecasts hold. Officials cautioned that even small changes in the storm's track or intensity could significantly alter where the greatest impacts occur, and additional forecast updates are expected as Lala approaches the islands.
