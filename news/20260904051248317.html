El Niño expected to intensify into a very strong event

Near-100% chance it persists through February 2027

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 4, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The World Meteorological Organization says the current El Niño is firmly established and is expected to strengthen into a very strong event before peaking near the end of 2026, increasing the risk of major shifts in rainfall, temperature and extreme weather around the world. WMO forecasts released September 3 put the probability that El Niño will persist through February 2027 at nearly 100%, an unusually high level of certainty for the agency's seasonal outlooks.

El Niño develops when waters in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific become unusually warm, altering atmospheric circulation and influencing weather patterns across large parts of the globe. The current event is already among the four strongest recorded since 1950, according to reporting on the WMO update, and exceptionally warm Pacific Ocean conditions are supporting further intensification. Reuters reported that scientists believe the developing event could potentially become the strongest El Niño on record, although that outcome is not yet certain.

A very strong El Niño can raise the likelihood of drought in some regions while increasing risks of heavy rainfall, flooding and extreme heat elsewhere. The WMO said its national meteorological partners are increasing preparedness and early-warning efforts because the climate effects are expected to continue well into 2027. The phenomenon itself is naturally occurring, but unusually warm global oceans and human-driven climate warming can amplify the temperatures and impacts associated with an El Niño event.

The WMO also said this is the first time one of its El Niño/La Niña updates has expressed such strong confidence that an event will persist through the forecast period. UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned that the strengthening event is adding another layer of risk to an already warming climate, while WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo urged governments to prepare for potentially severe disruptions to agriculture, water supplies, health and disaster response.
