Trump Orders Federal Contractors To Alert White House About Congress

Companies Could Lose Federal Work For Failing To Follow New Rules

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 22, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

President Trump has ordered federal contractors and subcontractors to notify the White House before responding to congressional requests involving their work or communications with the executive branch, creating new requirements for tens of thousands of businesses that perform work for the federal government.

The directive, Executive Order 14438, titled "Protecting the Executive's Privileges and Enhancing Efficiencies in Federal Contracting," was issued Friday, November 20. Contractors must immediately notify both the White House and their contracting agencies about pending or future congressional requests and follow government instructions concerning information claimed to be protected by executive privilege.

Prime contractors must also ensure their subcontractors comply. Agencies can consider enforcement measures against companies that fail to follow the requirements, including contract termination or cancellation and suspension or debarment from future federal contracts. The Office of Management and Budget is directed to develop guidance for implementing and monitoring the rules.

The White House cited the Federal Property and Administrative Services Act and common law as legal authority for the order. It argues that congressional requests must have a valid legislative purpose and cannot improperly intrude on core executive functions. The administration says coordination is necessary to protect privileged deliberations and sensitive information.

The directive could leave contractors facing competing demands from the executive and legislative branches. A contractor that provides information despite White House instructions could risk its federal contracts, while refusing to comply with a congressional subpoena could potentially trigger congressional enforcement proceedings, including contempt disputes.

The order comes weeks before Democrats take control of both chambers of Congress in January 2027 and prepare expanded oversight investigations. Democratic Rep. Jamie Raskin called the directive "totally unprecedented and blatantly unlawful." The administration maintains that it is protecting legitimate executive-privilege interests.

Disputes over executive privilege and congressional subpoenas can ultimately lead to negotiations between the branches or litigation over whether particular information must be disclosed.
