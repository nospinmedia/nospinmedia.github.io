4 Americans among 6 killed in Athens building explosion

Suspected gas leak caused collapse near the Acropolis

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 27, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Four Americans are among six people killed after an explosion caused a building to collapse in the historic Plaka district of Athens, Greece, near the Acropolis.

The explosion happened Friday morning and brought down a two-story building, sending debris across the surrounding street and damaging neighboring structures. Rescue crews searched the rubble into Saturday before authorities confirmed that six bodies had been recovered.

The U.S. State Department confirmed Saturday that four of those killed were American citizens. U.S. Ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle said the U.S. Embassy was working with the victims' families and thanked Greek emergency crews for their response.

Athens Mayor Haris Doukas said the explosion is believed to have been caused by a major gas leak, although investigators are still working to determine exactly what happened. Authorities evacuated nearby buildings because of damage from the blast.

Two women were injured in an adjacent building on Lysikratous Street, according to the fire department. The search-and-rescue operation involved about 30 firefighters and three search dogs.

Plaka is one of Athens' best-known tourist districts and sits at the foot of the Acropolis, Greece's most visited ancient landmark. Video and photographs from the scene showed extensive rubble in the street and vehicles crushed by falling debris.

Greek authorities are continuing their investigation into the cause of the explosion.
