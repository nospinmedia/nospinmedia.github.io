Kratom found during two Ole Miss student death investigations

Governor orders state help as toxicology tests remain pending

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 24, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Authorities in Oxford, Mississippi, are investigating the deaths of two University of Mississippi students after packaged kratom purchased from a retail store was found during both investigations. Officials have emphasized that they have not determined that the deaths are connected or that kratom or any other substance contributed to either death.

Friends identified the students as Aidan Hamilton, an 18-year-old freshman from Englewood, Colorado, and Robert Strang, a 20-year-old junior from Atlanta, Georgia. Both died on September 21 in separate locations. Hamilton died at Stewart Hall on the Ole Miss campus, while Strang died at an off-campus home. Toxicology testing is underway, and results could take several weeks.

Lafayette County Metro Narcotics said retail-packaged kratom was found during both investigations. Authorities urged people to avoid kratom as well as medications or substances that were not prescribed to them or obtained from a trusted, regulated source. Officials stressed that the warning should not be interpreted as evidence that kratom caused either death. Kratom products are already prohibited locally: the City of Oxford banned them in 2019, and Lafayette County followed with a ban in 2021.

Mississippi Governor Tate Reeves said he instructed state agencies to assist local authorities and expedite toxicology testing. The Mississippi Department of Public Safety and Mississippi Bureau of Narcotics are among the agencies assisting the investigation.

Separate concerns have also emerged about cychlorphine, a highly potent synthetic opioid that local medical professionals and student groups have warned may not be detected by standard fentanyl test strips. Law enforcement has not confirmed that cychlorphine was found in either case or played any role in the students' deaths, and investigators are awaiting toxicology results before determining what substances, if any, contributed.

The university has urged the campus community not to spread unverified claims while the investigations continue. Officials have confirmed two student deaths and say their causes remain under investigation.
