U.S. approves $2.1 billion Patriot sale to Ukraine

Allies will fund 500 interceptors as Kyiv seeks faster deliveries

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 31, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The U.S. State Department has approved a proposed $2.1 billion sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptor missiles to Ukraine as Kyiv seeks additional air-defense ammunition against Russian ballistic missile attacks.

The package includes the 500 Patriot Advanced Capability-3 Missile Segment Enhancement interceptors, spare parts and logistical and program support. Lockheed Martin is the principal contractor. The PAC-3 MSE is designed to intercept ballistic missiles and other aerial threats and is used with Ukraine's existing Patriot air-defense systems.

The purchase will not use U.S. government funds. According to the State Department, Ukraine will pay using contributions from Canada, Germany and Norway routed through the U.S. JUMPSTART program, along with Ukrainian national funds financed through the European Union's Ukraine Support Loan. The proposed sale remains subject to the U.S. foreign military sales process and congressional review.

The authorization comes as Ukraine says it urgently needs more Patriot interceptors. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said this week that Kyiv had been waiting nearly a month for U.S. approval of critical missile supplies while Russia intensified attacks on Ukrainian cities and energy infrastructure. Zelenskyy welcomed the new authorization but said newly manufactured missiles could take years to reach Ukraine, urging allies to provide interceptors from existing inventories while those stocks are replenished later.

The State Department did not announce a delivery schedule for the new package. The approval is also separate from Patriot missiles already purchased for Ukraine by NATO allies through other programs, meaning authorization of the 500-missile package does not indicate that those interceptors are immediately available for shipment.
