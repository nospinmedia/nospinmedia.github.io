Severe Flooding Forces Rooftop Rescues Across Four Corners Region

About 600 Farmington Households Told to Evacuate as Rivers Rise

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 24, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Severe flooding across the Four Corners region forced rooftop rescues, evacuations and widespread road closures Friday as rivers rose across parts of New Mexico, Colorado, Utah and Arizona.

In Farmington, New Mexico, about 600 households were told to evacuate as the San Juan and Animas rivers rose. New Mexico National Guard crews used boats, helicopters and amphibious vehicles to reach residents trapped by several feet of water. San Juan County officials said some people were rescued from rooftops and flooded homes.

The flooding followed weeks of unusually wet weather that left the ground saturated. About 4 inches of rain had fallen in parts of the region since Wednesday, and additional rain remained possible through the weekend. Officials warned that even after skies cleared in some areas, water moving downstream could continue raising river levels.

Floodwaters were also expected to move into the Navajo Nation, where officials urged residents to prepare themselves, their families and their animals and not wait until conditions worsened before evacuating.

In southwest Colorado, National Guard personnel were deployed and mandatory evacuations were ordered along several rivers. Colorado Gov. Jared Polis declared a disaster emergency covering 11 counties. Flooding damaged roads and surrounded vehicles and recreational trailers in some communities.

Utah was also recovering from earlier flooding that affected dozens of homes, while flood watches remained in effect in parts of the state. The broader severe-weather outbreak included tornado damage earlier in the week in Arizona.

Officials reported extensive flood damage but, as of Friday's reporting, no known deaths or serious injuries from the Four Corners flooding.
