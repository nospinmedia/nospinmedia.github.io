Nirmal Purja and nine climbers confirmed dead after Pakistan avalanche

Broad Peak expedition ends in tragedy as recovery efforts continue

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 2, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Renowned British-Nepali mountaineer Nirmal "Nimsdai" Purja and nine other climbers have been confirmed dead after an avalanche struck their expedition on Broad Peak in northern Pakistan, bringing a tragic end to a multinational climbing team attempting the world's 12th-highest mountain. Rescue officials and expedition organizers confirmed the deaths after aerial searches and drone reconnaissance located the victims following several days of difficult recovery operations in the Karakoram range. Harsh weather, steep terrain and avalanche risk complicated efforts to reach the site.

Purja, 43, was internationally celebrated for climbing all 14 mountains higher than 8,000 meters in a record 189 days during 2019, an achievement documented in the Netflix film "14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible." A former member of the United Kingdom Special Boat Service (SBS), he later founded Elite Exped, becoming one of the world's most recognizable high-altitude climbers and expedition leaders.

Officials said the expedition included climbers from Nepal, Pakistan, the United States, Oman and China. Among those identified were American climber Mallory Geis, Omani climber Nadhira Ahmed Al Harthy, and several experienced Nepalese guides. Pakistan's military and civilian rescue teams supported the search, but authorities said recovering all of the victims remains extremely challenging because of unstable snow conditions and the remote, high-altitude location.

Broad Peak rises to 8,051 meters (26,414 feet) near K2 and is considered one of the world's most technically demanding 8,000-meter peaks. Although avalanche hazards are a constant risk in the Karakoram, mountaineering experts described the scale of this disaster as one of the deadliest to strike an international expedition in Pakistan in recent years.

Tributes poured in from climbers, governments and outdoor organizations around the world, remembering Purja for redefining the limits of high-altitude climbing and inspiring a new generation of mountaineers through his record-breaking achievements and humanitarian work.
