Warren Buffett steps down as Berkshire chairman

Son Howard takes over in next stage of succession

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 19, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Warren Buffett has stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, ending another chapter in his more than six decades at the company he transformed from a struggling textile manufacturer into a conglomerate valued at roughly $1 trillion. The 96-year-old will become chairman emeritus, remain a Berkshire director and continue as a shareholder.

Buffett's oldest son, Howard Buffett, has become chairman effective immediately under Berkshire's long-planned succession strategy. Howard Buffett has served on the company's board since 1993. The chairman transition follows an earlier leadership change at the start of 2026, when longtime Berkshire executive Greg Abel succeeded Warren Buffett as chief executive. Abel had already overseen Berkshire's non-insurance businesses since 2018.

Warren Buffett took control of Berkshire in 1965 and had served as chairman since 1970. Under his leadership, the company expanded far beyond its textile roots, acquiring and investing in businesses spanning insurance, railroads, utilities, manufacturing and consumer products. Berkshire's holdings include GEICO, BNSF Railway, Dairy Queen and See's Candies, along with major stock investments.

The leadership structure now separates the chairman and CEO positions, with Howard Buffett leading the board and Abel running Berkshire's operations. Warren Buffett said in a letter to shareholders that Abel had exceeded the high expectations he had for him and had fully taken control of the CEO role. Buffett has continued going to Berkshire's Omaha office and looking for investments even after relinquishing the chief executive position.

The transition also leaves Abel responsible for deploying Berkshire's enormous financial resources, including a cash reserve of roughly $365 billion, while maintaining the decentralized business model Buffett established.
