New Ebola case detected in rebel-held eastern Congo

Infection emerges two months after local outbreak was declared over

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

A new Ebola infection has been confirmed in rebel-controlled eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, more than two months after the AFC/M23 rebel group declared areas under its control free of the disease. The case was detected in the Kayna health zone of North Kivu, near Lubero, amid a much larger Ebola epidemic that continues elsewhere in Congo and has already become the country's largest recorded outbreak.

The patient is a 21-year-old man who arrived from Butembo, a city in government-controlled territory. According to AFC/M23 officials, he was identified as a suspected Ebola case at a health checkpoint on September 2 and transferred to a quarantine facility. Laboratory testing confirmed infection with Bundibugyo ebolavirus the following day. Officials said it remains unclear where he contracted the virus. Contact tracing has begun and surveillance has been increased in an effort to identify any additional infections.

AFC/M23, which controls large areas of North Kivu and South Kivu, declared territories under its administration Ebola-free in late June after 21 days without a newly reported case. However, the World Health Organization had not officially declared transmission over in those areas. Only four Ebola cases had previously been reported in rebel-controlled territory since Congo's current outbreak began in May.

The distinction is significant because Ebola remains widespread elsewhere in the country. WHO data through September 2 showed 6,342 confirmed cases and 3,072 confirmed deaths in Congo, with transmission reported across 60 health zones in six provinces. The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo species of Ebola, for which there is currently no approved vaccine or specific treatment, although vaccine and therapeutic candidates are being tested.

Conflict and divided territorial control have complicated surveillance and disease response in eastern Congo. WHO has warned that insecurity, population displacement and difficulties reaching affected communities can allow transmission chains to go undetected.
