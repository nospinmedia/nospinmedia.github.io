Trump Asks Supreme Court to Revive Lawsuit Against Hillary Clinton

Lower Courts Dismissed 2016 Election Case and Imposed Nearly $1 Million in Sanctions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 10, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

President Trump has asked the U.S. Supreme Court to revive his dismissed lawsuit against Hillary Clinton and several other political figures, seeking another opportunity to pursue allegations that his opponents conspired to damage his reputation during the 2016 presidential election.

The appeal, filed on Friday, October 9, 2026, challenges lower court rulings that rejected Trump's lawsuit and imposed nearly $1 million in financial sanctions against him and one of his attorneys. The Supreme Court has not agreed to hear the case.

Trump originally filed the lawsuit in March 2022, naming Clinton, the Democratic National Committee, former FBI Director James Comey, former British intelligence officer Christopher Steele, and numerous others.

The complaint alleged that Clinton and her associates coordinated efforts to create and spread a false narrative connecting Trump's presidential campaign to Russia. It included claims under federal racketeering laws and other legal theories.

In 2022, U.S. District Judge Donald Middlebrooks dismissed the lawsuit, finding substantial legal and factual deficiencies. The judge concluded that the complaint improperly attempted to use the federal courts to pursue political grievances rather than legally sustainable claims.

Middlebrooks subsequently ordered Trump and attorney Alina Habba to pay nearly $1 million in legal fees and costs, finding that the lawsuit contained frivolous legal arguments and knowingly false factual allegations.

The 11th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals upheld the dismissal and sanctions in 2025, agreeing that many of the arguments presented by Trump and his legal team lacked a legitimate legal foundation.

In their new Supreme Court filing, Trump's attorneys argue that the lower courts improperly punished legal advocacy and failed to account for his presidential responsibilities. They contend that Trump delayed bringing the lawsuit during his first administration to fulfill his constitutional duties and that this delay should affect the applicable filing deadlines.

The underlying dispute remains connected to investigations into Russian interference in the 2016 election. Special Counsel Robert Mueller's 2019 investigation concluded that Russia interfered in the election through extensive operations intended to benefit Trump. However, investigators did not establish that Trump's campaign criminally conspired or coordinated with the Russian government in those activities.

A separate investigation led by Special Counsel John Durham later criticized the FBI's handling of the original Russia investigation, including its reliance on information originating from Trump's political opponents.

The Supreme Court must now decide whether to consider Trump's appeal. If the justices decline review, the lower court rulings and financial sanctions will remain in place.
