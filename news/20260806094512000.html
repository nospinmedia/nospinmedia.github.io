Senate Panel Moves To Hold Anthony Fauci In Contempt

Fifth Amendment fight centers on Biden-era pardon as DOJ referral looms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 6, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

The Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee is scheduled to vote on August 6, 2026, on whether to hold Dr. Anthony Fauci in contempt of Congress and refer the matter to the U.S. Department of Justice. The proposed contempt citation follows Fauci's refusal to answer questions during a July 29, 2026 committee hearing examining federal decisions made during the COVID-19 response.

During that hearing, Fauci invoked the Fifth Amendment more than 100 times. Committee Republicans, led by Sen. Rand Paul, argue that Fauci cannot claim a risk of self-incrimination because President Joe Biden issued him a presidential pardon in January 2025 covering official acts from 2014 through that month. Fauci's legal team maintains that the constitutional protection may still apply because a presidential pardon does not necessarily eliminate potential state-level criminal exposure or other legal risks.

If the committee approves the resolution, it plans to transmit its certification to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Columbia, which will determine whether to pursue criminal contempt charges. Under 2 U.S. Code § 192, criminal contempt of Congress is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 12 months in jail and a fine of up to $1,000. While Congress can recommend prosecution, the final decision rests with the Justice Department.

Fauci retired from federal service in 2022 after more than five decades at the National Institutes of Health, including nearly four decades leading the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases. The committee vote is expected later Thursday. If the resolution passes, federal prosecutors will review the referral before deciding whether any criminal charges are warranted.
