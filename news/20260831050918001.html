Trump Urges FCC Action Against NBC's Kristen Welker

NBC Defends Anchor as Free Speech Debate Grows

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 31, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

President Donald Trump called on the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) to take action against NBC News anchor Kristen Welker after she said during a Washington, D.C., WRC-TV preview of Meet the Press that his 2026 Republican primary endorsements had produced "some mixed results." Posting on Truth Social on August 30, Trump accused Welker of making deliberately inaccurate statements and argued that his endorsement record was far stronger than she described.

Trump said his endorsed candidates had won 98% of contested Republican House primaries and 100% of Republican Senate primaries, asserting that Welker's comments misled viewers. He urged FCC Chairman Brendan Carr to issue a "rebuke or punishment" against the veteran journalist and later posted "FCC TO THE RESCUE!" while criticizing media coverage and polling. NBC News responded by defending Welker, describing her as "one of the best in the business" and saying the network fully supports her reporting and journalism.

The episode has renewed attention on the legal limits of the FCC's authority over broadcast journalism. Under Section 326 of the Communications Act of 1934, the commission is prohibited from censoring broadcast content or directing editorial decisions, while the First Amendment protects political speech and commentary. Although the FCC can investigate allegations of intentional news distortion in limited circumstances, it generally does not regulate opinions, political analysis, or editorial judgment.

The dispute comes amid broader tensions between the Trump administration and major news organizations over media coverage, polling, and broadcast regulation. It also follows other recent disputes involving broadcast licensing and federal oversight of television networks. Legal experts have noted that any effort to penalize a journalist solely for political commentary would face significant constitutional and statutory hurdles under longstanding protections for press freedom.
