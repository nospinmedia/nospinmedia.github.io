Powerful Earthquake Rocks Panama, Damaging Buildings

Major Aftershock Follows as Thousands Evacuate and Power Outages Spread

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 10, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A powerful earthquake struck Panama on Friday, October 9, 2026, damaging buildings, disrupting electricity, and sending thousands of frightened residents into the streets. Initially measured at magnitude 7.7 by the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), the earthquake was centered near Pitaloza Arriba on Panama's Azuero Peninsula, approximately 200 kilometers southwest of Panama City. The USGS subsequently published a reviewed magnitude of 7.6, illustrating how preliminary earthquake measurements can change as scientists analyze additional data.

The earthquake struck at approximately 12:56 p.m. local time, producing intense shaking across central Panama and reaching the capital. Buildings sustained damage in Herrera, Los Santos, Veraguas, and Coclé provinces, while residents evacuated offices, hospitals, and other structures. In Penonomé, buildings suffered structural damage, and historic religious structures were among the properties affected.

President José Raúl Mulino described the situation as a major disaster as emergency personnel began evaluating damaged roads, buildings, and essential services. Electricity disruptions affected six provinces, complicating recovery efforts and leaving some communities without reliable power.

The initial earthquake was followed by dozens of aftershocks, including a powerful magnitude 6.6 earthquake near Río Grande. Authorities urged residents to remain cautious around damaged structures as additional tremors continued.

A tsunami threat was initially announced for parts of the Pacific coast, prompting warnings and precautionary measures. The warning was subsequently lifted as monitoring agencies evaluated the risk.

Despite the earthquake's considerable strength, authorities had not confirmed any fatalities by Friday evening. Several injuries were reported, although a definitive nationwide injury count had not been established. Emergency assessments remained underway, and the full extent of structural damage was still being determined.

Critical infrastructure largely avoided catastrophic damage. The Panama Canal remained operational, while Tocumen International Airport resumed operations following temporary precautionary disruptions. These developments helped limit broader transportation and economic consequences.

The earthquake highlights the seismic hazards facing Central America, where interactions between tectonic plates can generate powerful earthquakes. Officials continued inspecting vulnerable buildings, roads, and public facilities while monitoring aftershocks.
