Mississippi Officer Who Fatally Shot 1-Year-Old Kohen Wiley Resigns

Interim Police Chief Also Steps Down as Family Seeks Federal Investigation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 24, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The Mississippi police officer who fatally shot 1-year-old Kohen Wiley outside a Walmart has resigned days after state investigators cleared him of criminal wrongdoing, while the city's interim police chief has also stepped down.

Sgt. Hunter Foster, 33, resigned from the Senatobia Police Department, Mayor Greg Graves confirmed. The city's board of aldermen accepted Foster's resignation on October 21. Officials have not said whether he was asked to resign.

Foster fired into a moving vehicle on June 14 while officers were responding to a reported shoplifting incident at a Walmart in Senatobia, about 40 miles south of Memphis. Kohen was sitting on his mother's lap in the front passenger seat and was killed. The driver was also wounded.

The Mississippi Attorney General's Office announced earlier this week that its investigation found Foster's use of force justified and cleared him of criminal wrongdoing. Foster's attorney has said the officer fired because the vehicle was moving toward him and he had no choice.

Kohen's family disputes that conclusion. Attorney Ben Crump, who represents the family, has called for the U.S. Department of Justice to investigate the shooting and for Foster to lose his law-enforcement certification. Foster remained certified as an officer following his resignation, according to state officials.

The state released body-camera and other video of the encounter Thursday. The footage shows officers pursuing the vehicle and Foster firing as it moved through the parking lot. Kohen's mother and another woman in the vehicle have denied stealing diapers and have not been charged.

In another development, interim Senatobia Police Chief Angelica Maze resigned from the chief's position Friday. Local officials have not publicly linked her decision to Foster's resignation, and Maze is expected to remain with the department in another role.

The resignations add a new chapter to a case that has drawn scrutiny over police use of force and competing interpretations of the released video.
