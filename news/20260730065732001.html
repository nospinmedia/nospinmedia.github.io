Dr. Anthony Fauci Invokes Fifth Amendment During Senate Hearing On COVID-19 Origins

Rand Paul Plans Contempt Vote As Democrats Call Proceedings A Partisan Attack

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 30, 2026 at 10:17 AM ET

Former National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases Director Dr. Anthony Fauci repeatedly invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination during a Senate hearing examining the origins of COVID-19 and his management of the federal pandemic response. Fauci, who appeared under subpoena, declined to answer more than 100 questions from members of the Republican-led Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee.

Committee Chairman Rand Paul questioned Fauci about federal funding connected to coronavirus research in Wuhan, China, previous testimony before Congress and more than 1,000 pages of pandemic-era diary entries recently obtained from government computers. Paul argued that selected diary passages revealed differences between Fauci's private discussions and his public statements about whether the virus could have emerged from a laboratory. Fauci has repeatedly denied misleading Congress or concealing evidence about the pandemic's origins.

Before questioning began, Fauci said he was acting on the advice of his attorneys because Paul and other political opponents had repeatedly called for his prosecution and imprisonment. Fauci accused Paul of conducting an "unhinged" campaign against him and said the hearing appeared designed to expose him to new criminal allegations rather than obtain information for a legitimate legislative purpose.

Paul argued that a preemptive pardon issued by former President Joe Biden in January 2025 eliminated Fauci's risk of prosecution for previously covered conduct and therefore weakened his basis for invoking the Fifth Amendment. Fauci's attorneys maintained that the pardon would not protect him from accusations involving statements made during the new hearing. Republican Senator James Lankford also acknowledged during the proceedings that Fauci retained the right to invoke the constitutional protection.

Democratic senators defended Fauci and described the hearing as a partisan attempt to punish a retired public health official. Republicans argued that unanswered questions about research funding, public messaging and pandemic policy still require accountability. Paul said the committee would vote the following week on whether to hold Fauci in contempt for refusing to answer. He acknowledged that the dispute over Fauci's constitutional rights may ultimately have to be resolved by the courts.
