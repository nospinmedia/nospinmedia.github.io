DNA links four grizzlies to deaths of mother and daughter

Officials say rare attack near Bella Coola was likely predatory

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 11, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

DNA testing has conclusively linked a female grizzly bear and her three yearling cubs to the deaths of a mother and her adult daughter near Bella Coola, British Columbia, wildlife officials announced Tuesday. All four bears were killed because authorities determined they posed an extreme risk to public safety.

The victims were Jenny Balcarras, 48, and her daughter Selah Reed, 20. The women had gone hiking near the remote community, about 1,000 kilometers (620 miles) north of Vancouver, when they disappeared. Family members raised the alarm after Reed missed a routine Saturday night telephone call with her sister.

Searchers located Balcarras first and later found Reed's remains approximately 150 meters (490 feet) away in a bear cache, an area where bears store food. The British Columbia Conservation Officer Service subsequently captured the four grizzlies, and forensic testing connected all of them to the deaths.

Inspector Jeff Tyre said investigators believe the attack likely involved predatory behavior, which he described as very rare. Officials said they do not know how the encounter began. Tyre said bears involved in fatal attacks cannot be rehabilitated or relocated because of the danger they pose to the public.

DNA testing also established that these bears were not involved in a separate November 2025 attack in the same region. In that incident, a grizzly attacked a group of roughly 20 elementary school students and teachers, injuring 11 people, including two critically.

The latest deaths have deeply affected the Nuxalk Nation, which was still recovering from last year's attack. Local authorities imposed a voluntary curfew and arranged police escorts for children traveling to school. Community support services are also being provided to residents.

Nuxalk leaders have asked residents to continue taking precautions outdoors, keep young children close, remove potential bear attractants and avoid the Snooka West Trail area while the community recovers.
