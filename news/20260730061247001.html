Samsung Warns AI Memory Chip Shortage Could Persist Through 2028

Company Locks More Production Into Long-Term Data Center Contracts As Demand Surges

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 30, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Samsung Electronics warned that shortages of memory chips used in artificial intelligence systems and data centers could continue through 2028, as rapidly expanding AI infrastructure keeps demand ahead of available manufacturing capacity. The company said market conditions may become even tighter during 2027 before new factories and production lines begin providing meaningful relief.

Executive Vice President Jaejune Kim said the supply squeeze is affecting advanced memory products including high-bandwidth memory, commonly known as HBM, and server-grade DRAM used in AI accelerators and cloud computing systems. Unlike a shortage affecting every type of semiconductor, the pressure is concentrated heavily in memory products required by major AI developers and data center operators.

Samsung said the industry's traditional system of short-term purchasing is also changing. Large technology companies are increasingly approaching chip manufacturers directly and seeking multi-year contracts that guarantee access to future production. Samsung has completed long-term supply agreements with five major data center customers and is negotiating with five additional companies. The contracts extend for at least five years and may include minimum prices and advance payments.

The South Korean technology company plans to place approximately 60% to 70% of its memory production capacity under long-term agreements. That strategy could provide Samsung with more predictable revenue while leaving less uncommitted capacity available to smaller customers purchasing chips through the open market.

Samsung said increasing production cannot happen quickly because constructing and equipping advanced semiconductor factories can take several years. Manufacturers must also balance spending on specialized AI memory against continued demand for conventional chips used in smartphones, personal computers, vehicles, and consumer electronics.

The warning accompanied record quarterly results fueled by sharply higher memory-chip prices and strong demand from companies building AI data centers. Samsung and rival SK Hynix are expanding production, but both companies face high construction costs, growing Chinese competition, and uncertainty over how long the current AI investment boom will continue.
