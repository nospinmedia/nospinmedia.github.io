American Red Cross Declares National Blood Supply Crisis

Type O Inventory Falls Below One-Day Supply As Hospitals Urge Immediate Donations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 1, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

The American Red Cross has declared a national blood supply crisis, only the second such declaration in the organization's history, after blood donations fell to a four-year summer low while hospital demand continued to rise. The organization said inventories of Type O positive blood have dropped to less than a one-day supply, prompting emergency conservation measures and distribution limits to help ensure lifesaving transfusions remain available for trauma patients, emergency surgeries and other critical medical needs.

The Red Cross, which supplies roughly 40% of the nation's blood, said a combination of extreme summer heat, poor air quality, seasonal illnesses and reduced donor participation has significantly disrupted collections during one of the busiest periods of the year for hospitals. Officials warned that trauma centers and emergency departments rely heavily on Type O blood because it can often be used before a patient's blood type is confirmed, making adequate supplies especially important during life-threatening emergencies.

To preserve available inventory, the organization has implemented temporary limits on Type O blood distributions while urging eligible donors to schedule appointments as soon as possible. The Association for the Advancement of Blood and Biotherapies (AABB) and the American Hospital Association echoed the appeal, encouraging healthy individuals to donate to help stabilize the national blood supply. Health officials emphasized that every donation can support multiple patients through the separation of whole blood into red cells, plasma and platelets.

The Red Cross said maintaining a stable blood supply depends on consistent donations throughout the year rather than during emergencies alone. Officials warned that if donations do not increase quickly, hospitals could face growing pressure to conserve blood products and prioritize the most urgent transfusions until inventories recover.
