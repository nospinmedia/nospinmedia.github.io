Oil climbs above $108 as Saudi pipeline remains shut

Yanbu export stocks may last only days

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 14, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Oil prices climbed above $108 a barrel on September 14, 2026, as the shutdown of Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline intensified concerns about global supplies already constrained by disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude rose more than 3% during trading, while West Texas Intermediate also gained nearly 3%, as markets assessed how long Saudi Arabia can maintain exports through its Red Sea facilities without the pipeline operating.

The approximately 1,200-kilometer (745-mile) East-West pipeline was shut after drone attacks originating from Iraq damaged facilities along the route. The pipeline has allowed Saudi Arabia to move roughly 4 million barrels per day, about 4% of global oil supply, from its eastern production areas to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, bypassing the heavily disrupted Strait of Hormuz. Satellite imagery from Planet Labs PBC has shown extensive fire damage at infrastructure along the pipeline route southeast of Medina. Saudi authorities have not announced when full operations will resume.

The immediate concern is the amount of crude already stored at Yanbu. Saudi oil buyers and traders told Reuters that inventories there could sustain current export levels for only five to seven days if pipeline flows do not restart. Saudi Arabia also holds smaller supplies at Egyptian terminals, but industry sources said those stocks would eventually be exhausted without renewed pipeline deliveries. Estimates for repairs vary from days to several weeks, and partial pumping could potentially resume before all repairs are completed.

Pressure on oil markets increased further after a regional meeting scheduled in Oman between Iran and Gulf countries to discuss shipping through the Strait of Hormuz was postponed. Omani Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi said the delay was made in the interests of reaching regional consensus. The combination of restricted Hormuz traffic and the Saudi pipeline shutdown has left major Middle Eastern oil-export routes under simultaneous strain.
