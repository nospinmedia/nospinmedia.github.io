Christa Pike returns to prison after surviving Tennessee execution attempt

Death sentence remains as legal questions turn to whether state can try again

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 11, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Christa Pike, who survived Tennessee's attempt to execute her by lethal injection, has been discharged from the hospital and returned to prison after spending 10 days receiving medical treatment.

Pike was taken back to the Debra K. Johnson Rehabilitation Center in Nashville on October 10, 2026, according to her attorneys. She remains under a death sentence despite the state's unsuccessful attempt to execute her on September 30.

During the execution attempt, Tennessee officials administered two doses of pentobarbital, the drug used in the state's lethal injection protocol. Pike remained conscious, complained of severe pain and eventually experienced serious medical distress. Officials halted the procedure and emergency medical personnel transported her to a hospital.

Her attorneys said Pike will continue receiving medical care following her return to prison. They have also challenged the circumstances surrounding the failed execution and sought preservation of evidence connected to the procedure as questions remain about why the drugs failed to cause her death.

Pike was sentenced to death for the 1995 murder of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer in Knoxville. She has spent nearly three decades under a death sentence.

Surviving an execution attempt does not automatically invalidate that sentence. The U.S. Supreme Court ruled in 1947 that a second execution attempt did not necessarily violate the Constitution after an electric chair malfunctioned during the attempted execution of Louisiana prisoner Willie Francis.

Pike's case could nevertheless raise new Eighth Amendment questions because Tennessee began carrying out the execution, administered lethal drugs and stopped only after she suffered serious complications and failed to die.

Tennessee officials are reviewing what happened during the execution attempt. No new execution date for Pike has been announced.

The case now extends beyond questions about Tennessee's lethal injection procedures to whether the state should be permitted to make another attempt to carry out Pike's death sentence after its first attempt caused significant medical harm but did not kill her.
