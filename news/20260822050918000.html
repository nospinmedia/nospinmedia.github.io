Federal Judge Blocks 75-Country Immigrant Visa Policy

Court Orders State Department to Resume Individual Reviews

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 22, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

A federal judge has struck down a Trump administration policy that suspended immigrant visa processing for applicants from 75 countries, ruling that the blanket directive exceeded the authority of the U.S. Department of State and conflicted with federal immigration law. U.S. District Judge Jeannette A. Vargas of the Southern District of New York vacated the policy and ordered consular officials to resume evaluating immigrant visa applications on an individual basis rather than applying a nationality-wide prohibition.

The policy, implemented on January 21, 2026, directed consular officers to refuse immigrant visas and green cards for applicants from dozens of countries—including Colombia, Egypt, Nigeria, Russia, and Thailand—based on concerns that they could become public charges. Immigration advocacy organizations, including the Catholic Legal Immigration Network (CLINIC), Democracy Forward, and African Communities Together, challenged the directive in CLINIC v. Rubio, arguing that it unlawfully replaced individualized eligibility reviews with a categorical nationality-based restriction.

In her 61-page ruling, Judge Vargas concluded that the directive violated the Administrative Procedure Act and conflicted with Section 1152(a)(1)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act, which prohibits discrimination in the issuance of immigrant visas based on nationality. The court found that the policy improperly prevented consular officers from exercising the case-by-case discretion required under federal law. The decision also vacates visa refusals issued under the blanket policy since January 2026, requiring the State Department to reopen affected family-sponsored, employment-based, and diversity visa applications for lawful review.

The Department of Justice is expected to seek an emergency stay while appealing the decision, meaning additional court proceedings are likely before the ruling becomes final. Even so, the decision represents a significant legal setback for one of the administration's major immigration initiatives and restores individualized processing for thousands of pending immigrant visa applicants while the case continues through the federal courts.
