UN-backed mission cites possible U.S. war crimes in Iran

Investigators focus on strikes that killed at least 178 civilians

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 18, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A United Nations-backed fact-finding mission says there are reasonable grounds to believe the United States committed war crimes in two strikes in Iran that killed at least 178 civilians, including children. The findings concern attacks during the opening days of the U.S.-Israeli military campaign against Iran and represent allegations by independent investigators rather than a judgment by an international court.

The mission examined a February 28 strike on the Shajareh Tayyebeh Primary School in Minab and another strike on a sports complex and nearby residential area in Lamerd. Investigators concluded there were reasonable grounds to believe the attacks violated international humanitarian law. In the Minab case, the mission said evidence indicated the school was deliberately targeted rather than struck accidentally. The investigators said they found no evidence that the school was being used for military purposes at the time.

The White House and U.S. State Department rejected the mission's conclusions and criticized the U.N. Human Rights Council. U.S. officials have disputed responsibility for aspects of the findings, including the Lamerd strike. The mission said its investigation was also limited because U.S. officials declined to engage with investigators and Iranian authorities did not respond to requests for information.

The same report also accused Iranian authorities of serious abuses during their crackdown on domestic protests, finding reasonable grounds to believe authorities committed crimes against humanity including murder, torture and persecution. Investigators called on both governments to end violations and provide reparations to victims.

The mission's findings do not themselves establish criminal liability. They document evidence and legal conclusions that could contribute to future accountability efforts, while any determination of individual criminal responsibility would require a competent judicial process.
