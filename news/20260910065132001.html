Philippine ferry fire kills 5 as more than 80 remain missing

Rescuers search waters off Palawan after blaze aboard Manila-Coron vessel

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 10, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

At least five people have died and more than 80 remain unaccounted for after a passenger ferry caught fire off Palawan in the western Philippines, prompting a large search-and-rescue operation on Thursday. The MV June Aster was traveling from Manila to Coron when the fire broke out Wednesday evening as the vessel neared its destination.

The ferry's official manifest listed 134 people aboard, including 117 passengers and 17 crew members. Philippine Coast Guard officials said 43 people had been rescued in the latest count reported by Reuters, while earlier Coast Guard figures listed 42 rescued and 87 unaccounted for. Authorities cautioned that the totals were changing as rescuers continued to locate survivors and verify the passenger list.

The fire began at about 6:45 p.m. on September 9, roughly two kilometers offshore. Video released by the Philippine Coast Guard showed much of the vessel engulfed in flames and heavy smoke. The fire was later extinguished, but the badly damaged ship remained afloat and listing while rescue teams searched nearby waters.

The Philippine Coast Guard, military personnel, local fishermen and private volunteers joined the operation. Rough seas and strong currents complicated the search, and officials deployed additional vessels and aircraft as daylight operations expanded Thursday.

The cause of the fire remains under investigation. Survivors told Reuters they heard a loud explosion that appeared to come from the cargo hold before the blaze spread rapidly, but authorities have not determined what started it. The ferry had passed required safety inspections and was carrying vehicles and general cargo in addition to passengers.

The operator, Atienza Inter-Island Ferries, said it was cooperating with authorities and assisting rescue efforts and affected families.
