DHS says ICE will not patrol polling places

Targeted enforcement remains possible as voting groups prepare legal response

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 2, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The Department of Homeland Security says ICE has no plans to station agents at polling places or broadly patrol voting locations during the 2026 midterm elections, but officials have not ruled out targeted enforcement in limited circumstances. The distinction has prompted election officials and voting-rights organizations to prepare for potential disputes as early voting gets underway.

DHS told state election administrators that immigration agents would not be stationed at polling places. In a statement to the Associated Press, the department said ICE is not planning operations targeting voting locations and instead conducts intelligence-driven enforcement. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin has said agents could still respond to a specific public-safety threat or execute an individual warrant. President Trump, asked Thursday whether he planned to send ICE agents to polling places, said he had not thought about it.

Federal law places significant restrictions on armed federal personnel at voting locations. A law dating to the Civil War prohibits deploying troops or armed personnel where an election is being held except when necessary to repel armed enemies of the United States. Election-law specialists interviewed by the AP said the restriction applies broadly to armed federal officers, while additional federal and state laws prohibit voter intimidation. Whether agents conducting targeted law-enforcement activity near a polling location would violate those restrictions could become the subject of litigation.

Organizations representing Latino, Black and Asian American voters say they are preparing because even reports of immigration agents near voting locations could discourage some eligible citizens from voting. OCA-Asian Pacific American Advocates is recruiting election monitors, while a coalition is organizing a VoteSafe program in Georgia, Michigan, North Carolina and Ohio to reassure voters and provide community support.

Election officials are preparing as well. Los Angeles County election chief Dean Logan said officials intend to have personnel available to ensure voters are not disrupted while casting ballots.
