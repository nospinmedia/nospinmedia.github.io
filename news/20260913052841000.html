Rail obstruction investigated after French train derailment

44 injured as police examine possible sabotage

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 13, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

French authorities are investigating possible sabotage after a regional passenger train derailed in Cléon, Normandy, injuring 44 people and prompting investigators to examine a piece of rail found on the track. The train was carrying 186 passengers from Rouen to Caen when it derailed Friday evening in the Seine-Maritime department of northwestern France.

The derailment occurred at about 7:45 p.m. on September 11, according to French Transport Minister Philippe Tabarot. An 18-year-old woman was critically injured and evacuated by helicopter to Rouen University Hospital. Local authorities activated a mass-casualty response plan, deploying 130 firefighters, five medical teams and 80 emergency vehicles. Prosecutor Sébastien Gallois said alcohol and drug tests performed on the train driver were negative.

Investigators are focusing on a piece of rail discovered on the track. A spokeswoman for the French national police communications service, SICOP, told the Associated Press that investigators believed the rail was "probably" linked to the derailment. Authorities were still working to determine how it came to be on the track and whether the circumstances involved a deliberate act of sabotage or another cause. The prosecutor's office had not formally concluded that sabotage occurred, making earlier descriptions of the derailment as confirmed criminal sabotage premature.

SNCF Réseau, which manages France's rail infrastructure, suspended traffic on the affected line and cut electrical power so emergency crews could operate safely. Passenger accounts described increasingly violent jolts during the derailment, with windows breaking and stones from the track entering at least one carriage.

The investigation now centers on establishing how the rail obstruction reached the track and whether someone intentionally placed it there. Authorities had not publicly identified a suspect or motive as of the latest reporting.
