Israeli strike kills senior Hamas commander in southern Gaza

Hamas says Rafah brigade chief helped plan October 7 attack

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 15, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A senior Hamas military commander who the group says helped plan and oversee the October 7, 2023 attack on Israel has been killed in an Israeli airstrike in southern Gaza, with both Hamas and the Israeli military confirming his death.

Hamas' armed wing, the Al-Qassam Brigades, identified the commander as Samir Abu Younis, 53, head of its Rafah brigade. Hamas said Abu Younis was killed Saturday in the Al-Mawasi area of Khan Younis. Medics said other people were wounded in the strike. Al Jazeera reported that the attack hit a tent sheltering displaced people and wounded two others.

The Israeli military said it killed Abu Younis in an overnight strike in the southern Gaza Strip. Israel said he had commanded Hamas' Rafah brigade since his predecessor, Nael Abu Obeid, was killed in an Israeli strike on September 15.

According to Hamas, Abu Younis joined the Al-Qassam Brigades in 1991 and held several senior operational positions in Rafah before becoming brigade commander. The group described him as one of the key commanders involved in planning and overseeing the October 7 attack. Hamas said his funeral would be held Sunday afternoon in Khan Younis.

The strike comes despite a ceasefire that began in October 2025 and largely halted the major fighting between Israel and Hamas. The agreement has not completely stopped Israeli strikes in Gaza, nor has it resolved demands for Hamas to disarm.

Gaza health authorities say at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire took effect, while five Israeli soldiers have been killed during the same period. Those figures come from the respective authorities and cannot be independently verified in full during the continuing conflict.

The broader war began after Hamas-led fighters attacked southern Israel on October 7, 2023, killing about 1,200 people, according to Israeli figures, and taking hostages. Palestinian authorities say Israel's subsequent military campaign in Gaza has killed more than 74,000 people.

Abu Younis' death removes another senior commander from Hamas' military structure in southern Gaza only about two months after his predecessor was killed. It also comes as the ceasefire remains in effect but continues to face repeated violence, leaving unresolved questions over Hamas' disarmament, Israeli military operations and whether the truce can produce a longer-term settlement.
