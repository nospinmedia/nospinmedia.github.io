Iowa Senate Race Moves Into Toss-Up Territory

Trump Questions GOP Strategy as Republicans Face Tougher Midterm Map

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 18, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The race for Iowa's open U.S. Senate seat has become one of the country's most competitive contests as Republicans confront a more difficult political environment less than three weeks before the November 3 midterm elections.

Republican Rep. Ashley Hinson and Democratic state Rep. Josh Turek are competing to replace retiring Republican Sen. Joni Ernst. The Cook Political Report now rates the contest a Toss Up, a significant change from earlier in the campaign when the seat was considered more favorable to Republicans. Decision Desk HQ also classifies Iowa as a Toss Up.

Recent polling reflects the close race. A Marist Poll conducted October 8-11 found Turek at 48% and Hinson at 45% among likely voters, within the survey's 6.6-percentage-point margin of error. Other recent surveys have also shown a closely divided electorate, with neither candidate establishing a consistent advantage.

The increasingly competitive Iowa contest comes as President Trump has expressed frustration with Republican preparations for the midterms. Reuters, citing people familiar with the discussions, reported that Trump has faulted senior political aides and questioned whether the party's campaign strategy and spending are producing results. His concerns have included longtime political aide James Blair, who leads the pro-Trump MAGA Inc. super PAC.

Reuters reported that Trump has questioned the effectiveness of approximately $200 million available to MAGA Inc. and expressed frustration over Republican vulnerabilities in congressional races. The president has also urged supporters to approach the midterms as though his own name were on the ballot in an effort to increase Republican turnout.

Iowa's competitiveness extends beyond the Senate race, with several congressional contests and the governor's race also drawing increased national attention. The state backed Trump in 2024, making this year's close contests a notable test of whether its recent Republican advantage will continue.
