Son of former CIA Director John Brennan arrested on federal charge

FBI says phone contained about 88 child sexual abuse images

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 14, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

Kyle Owen Brennan, the 40-year-old son of former CIA Director John Brennan, was arrested Friday and charged in federal court with transporting child sexual abuse material following an investigation that began with reports of suspected material uploaded to an Adobe account in 2024.

Federal prosecutors charged Brennan under 18 U.S.C. § 2252A(a)(1) and (b)(1). The case, United States v. Brennan, No. 1:26-mj-00444, was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia. Brennan was arrested by FBI agents at his workplace in Herndon, Virginia, before appearing in federal court in Alexandria before U.S. Magistrate Judge William E. Fitzpatrick.

According to the FBI affidavit, the investigation began after the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children received CyberTipline reports concerning suspected material uploaded through Adobe. The Warrenton Police Department traced an IP address used for an April 7, 2024 upload through Comcast records, eventually linking the activity to Brennan.

The investigation was forwarded to the Fauquier County Sheriff's Office, which searched Brennan's Warrenton residence and seized his iPhone during a traffic stop on July 24, 2024. The case was later referred to the FBI. Federal investigators reviewing a forensic extraction from the phone reported finding approximately 88 images that appeared to depict minors engaged in sexually explicit conduct, according to the affidavit.

Brennan faces a federal charge of transportation of child sexual abuse material. If convicted, the offense carries a mandatory minimum of five years and maximum of 20 years in prison. A criminal complaint contains allegations, and Brennan is presumed innocent unless proven guilty.

Court records do not accuse John Brennan, who served as CIA director from 2013 to 2017, of involvement in his son's alleged conduct.
