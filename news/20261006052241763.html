President Trump approves firing-squad execution of Nidal Hasan

Fort Hood gunman would be first US service member executed since 1961

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 6, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

President Trump has approved a plan for the U.S. Army to execute former Army Maj. Nidal Hasan by firing squad for killing 13 people and wounding more than 30 others in the 2009 Fort Hood mass shooting, the Pentagon announced. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth recommended the firing-squad method, and the secretary of the Army will determine the time and location of the execution.

Hasan, a former Army psychiatrist, was convicted at a 2013 court-martial of 13 counts of premeditated murder and 32 counts of attempted premeditated murder. He acknowledged during his trial that he carried out the November 5, 2009 attack at Fort Hood, Texas, saying he acted in an effort to protect Islamic insurgents from U.S. military forces. A military jury sentenced him to death.

The case has moved through the military appeals system for years. Hasan asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review his case in 2024, arguing that his right to a public trial had been violated when the trial judge cleared the courtroom during discussions involving his court-appointed standby attorneys. The Supreme Court declined to hear the case in 2025.

If carried out, Hasan's sentence would be the first U.S. military execution of a service member since 1961, when Army Pvt. John A. Bennett was hanged after being convicted of rape and attempted murder. The firing-squad method would make Hasan's execution even more unusual; firing squads have rarely been used in modern American capital punishment.

The Pentagon's announcement follows the completion of Hasan's lengthy appellate process and moves the military closer to carrying out a death sentence imposed more than a decade ago. Hasan remains on military death row while the Army determines the execution's timing and location.
