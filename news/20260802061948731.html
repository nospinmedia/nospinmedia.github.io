Wildfires force sea evacuations as Greece battles dozens of new blazes

High winds threaten areas near Athens while France gains ground against fires

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 2, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Greece has become the latest focal point of Europe's severe wildfire season after powerful winds fueled dozens of new blazes, forcing hundreds of residents and tourists to evacuate coastal communities near Athens by land and sea. Greek authorities said firefighters responded to more than 70 wildfires within 24 hours as gale-force winds and prolonged drought created extreme fire conditions across much of the country.

The most dangerous fires burned near Porto Germeno and Agios Vasileios along the Gulf of Corinth, where flames cut off roads leading from coastal beaches. The Greek Coast Guard, assisted by local fishing vessels and private boats, evacuated more than 300 people from shoreline areas after escape routes became impassable. Another wildfire spread across Mount Poikilo, reaching the western outskirts of Athens and prompting the precautionary evacuation of patients from Daphni Psychiatric Hospital.

Firefighting operations involved hundreds of personnel supported by water-dropping aircraft, helicopters and international crews from other European countries. Authorities issued the highest wildfire danger alerts across parts of Attica, Crete and the Peloponnese, warning that continued high winds could rapidly spread existing fires or ignite new ones. Emergency alerts urged residents to follow evacuation orders immediately and avoid traveling through threatened areas.

Elsewhere in Europe, firefighters reported improving conditions in parts of France after days of battling destructive wildfires that forced widespread evacuations and threatened vineyards and tourist destinations. However, officials cautioned that extreme heat, dry vegetation and shifting winds continue to leave large areas of southern Europe vulnerable to additional fires as the summer progresses.

Greek emergency officials said damage assessments and containment efforts remain ongoing, with additional evacuation orders possible if weather conditions deteriorate. Authorities urged residents and visitors to remain alert as forecasts continue to indicate elevated wildfire risk across several regions.
