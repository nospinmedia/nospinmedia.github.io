Trump says U.S. no longer needs a deal with Iran

President directs Vance away from Iran diplomacy and toward domestic priorities

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 13, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

President Trump says the United States no longer needs a deal with Iran and has directed Vice President JD Vance to shift his attention away from negotiations with Tehran, signaling a halt to the administration's latest diplomatic effort.

In a November 12 Truth Social post, Trump wrote that the United States did not need an agreement with Iran and said the period for pursuing one had passed. He cited U.S. leverage over Iranian assets and the Strait of Hormuz while directing Vance to devote his attention to domestic issues, including allegations of fraud and crime in Democratic-led states and cities.

Vance had been serving as a leading administration contact on Iran diplomacy. Trump's announcement came after reporting that the vice president was considering direct talks with a senior Iranian security official as Washington explored whether negotiations could resume following the recent conflict.

The diplomatic effort also faced a major disagreement over Iran's nuclear program. A senior Iranian security official said Tehran would not agree to the removal of its enriched nuclear material, an issue that has remained central to negotiations between the two countries.

Trump's statement follows his comments earlier this week that the U.S. military campaign involving Iran was effectively over and that additional strikes were highly unlikely. The administration is maintaining economic and military pressure on Tehran while stepping back, at least for now, from efforts to reach a formal agreement.

Oil markets reacted to the reduced prospects for renewed negotiations, with benchmark crude prices rising following Trump's announcement. Trump has also said Saudi Arabia and other Gulf countries urged him to avoid further military escalation with Iran.
