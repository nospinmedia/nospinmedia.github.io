President Trump's Approval Falls to 33%

Iran war concerns deepen ahead of midterms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

President Trump's job approval rating has fallen to 33%, the lowest level of his current term and equal to the lowest recorded during his first term, according to a new Reuters/Ipsos poll. The survey found that 64% of Americans disapprove of his performance, widening his net approval deficit to 31 percentage points as Republicans prepare to defend narrow congressional majorities in the November 3 midterm elections.

The four-day online poll concluded on August 17 and collected responses from 1,166 U.S. adults nationwide. Its margin of error was approximately 3 percentage points in either direction. Trump's approval declined from 35% in a Reuters/Ipsos survey completed earlier in August and has now matched the low point his approval reached in December 2017.

Public concern about the continuing war with Iran was widespread. Approximately 80% of respondents—including 87% of Democrats and 71% of Republicans—said they expect U.S. involvement to continue for an extended period. Only 16% believed the conflict would probably end within a few weeks. About one in five Americans said the war had been worth its costs, while only about half of Republican respondents agreed.

The conflict has restricted oil movement through the Strait of Hormuz, contributing to higher gasoline prices and renewed pressure on household expenses. Trump has defended those costs as necessary to prevent Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon, while alternating between warnings of additional military action and statements that an agreement was approaching.

The poll also found Democrats gaining an advantage on economic issues. Among registered voters, 38% said Democrats had the better economic approach, compared with 35% who favored Republicans. Republicans retained a narrow immigration-policy advantage of 40% to 38%, but that gap has contracted from 26 percentage points in January 2025. Because the survey questioned adults nationally rather than voters in individual states or congressional districts, it measures public sentiment but does not directly predict specific midterm races.
