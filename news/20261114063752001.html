SpaceX launches 15th Starship flight as 22-hour orbital test continues

Booster catch aborted while Ship 42 prepares for first upper-stage recovery attempt

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 14, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

SpaceX's 15th integrated Starship test flight remains underway after launching from Starbase, Texas, on Friday, beginning a roughly 22.5-hour orbital mission that has already deployed satellites but encountered a problem during the attempted recovery of its Super Heavy booster.

The rocket lifted off at 4:47 p.m. CST Friday with Ship 42 mounted atop Super Heavy. About 6.5 minutes after liftoff, SpaceX abandoned plans to return the booster to Starbase for capture by the launch tower's large mechanical arms, commonly called "chopsticks." A malfunction during the return sequence forced controllers to divert the booster to its backup recovery area, where it performed a controlled splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico.

Ship 42 continued into low Earth orbit and remains there Saturday morning. During the mission, the spacecraft successfully deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, giving the flight an operational payload mission in addition to its Starship development objectives.

Flight 15 differs significantly from earlier Starship test flights, which generally lasted about an hour. Ship 42 is scheduled to remain in orbit until Saturday afternoon before returning to Texas. SpaceX is targeting approximately 3:17 to 3:20 p.m. CST, or 4:17 to 4:20 p.m. EST, for the spacecraft's return and attempted capture at Starbase.

If successful, the maneuver would mark the first tower catch of a returning Starship upper stage. SpaceX has previously captured Super Heavy boosters using the tower, but recovering the spacecraft itself is another major step toward the company's goal of rapidly reusing both stages of the system.

NASA is also relying on a modified Starship as a lunar lander for its Artemis program, making progress in orbital operations, reentry and vehicle recovery important beyond SpaceX's commercial launch plans.
