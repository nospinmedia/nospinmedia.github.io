August ties record for hottest month ever measured globally

Temperatures rise 1.65°C above preindustrial levels

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 10, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

August 2026 was the warmest August ever recorded globally and effectively tied July 2023 as the hottest calendar month in the modern record, according to data released Thursday by the Copernicus Climate Change Service. The global average surface air temperature reached 16.96°C (62.53°F), about 1.65°C above the 1850–1900 preindustrial average.

Copernicus said August was the first month since November 2025 to rise more than 1.5°C above preindustrial levels. That does not mean the long-term Paris Agreement threshold has officially been crossed, because the agreement refers to sustained warming over many years rather than a single month. The average temperature over the latest 12-month period, from September 2025 through August 2026, was 1.48°C above preindustrial levels.

The period from June through August 2026 tied 2024 as the warmest global boreal summer on record. Western Europe experienced its warmest summer in the Copernicus dataset, while Europe as a whole recorded its third-warmest summer. Large areas of France, Italy, Hungary, Romania and the Balkans were generally 3°C to 5°C above average during August.

Ocean temperatures also reached record territory. The average sea-surface temperature across the extra-polar oceans was 21.07°C, the highest August value recorded and tied with March 2024 for the highest monthly average across all calendar months. Daily sea-surface temperatures reached an all-time high of 21.11°C on August 24 and 25.

Copernicus attributed the exceptional warmth to continued human-driven global heating from burning fossil fuels combined with rapidly strengthening El Niño conditions in the tropical Pacific. Reuters and AP likewise reported that climate scientists identify greenhouse gas emissions as the primary driver, with El Niño adding to the warming.
