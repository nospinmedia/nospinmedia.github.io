Iran vows to use 'all available means' against U.S. naval blockade

More ships hit as Strait of Hormuz confrontation escalates

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 24, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Iran says it will use "all available means" to confront the U.S. naval blockade of its ports, calling the operation military aggression as new attacks on commercial ships intensify the confrontation around the Strait of Hormuz.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei made the warning in Tehran on Monday, November 23, saying Iran would not tolerate the blockade. He argued that the restrictions constitute military aggression and give Iran the right to defend itself under Article 51 of the U.N. Charter.

"We will certainly not tolerate these actions and will use all available means to counter U.S. aggression," Baghaei said.

The maritime crisis continued into Tuesday morning. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said a bulk carrier anchored about 23 nautical miles northeast of Khor Fakkan, United Arab Emirates, was struck by an unknown projectile, causing a fire aboard the vessel. No casualties were reported.

Iran's navy separately reported that two additional oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines in the eastern Strait of Hormuz early Tuesday. Those incidents followed other tanker mine strikes reported during the preceding days, extending a series of attacks affecting one of the world's most important energy shipping routes.

The United States imposed its blockade on Iranian ports after Iran restricted traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. The American operation began in April, was temporarily lifted during diplomatic efforts in June and was reinstated on July 14 after those efforts collapsed.

Iran says Washington must halt hostilities, lift the naval blockade and waive economic sanctions before normal commercial passage through the strait can resume. Iranian officials say the waterway could reopen quickly if those conditions are met.

President Trump has acknowledged the intensifying attacks. In a November 22 Truth Social post, Trump said Iran had launched more drones and missiles during the previous week "than at any time for over a year," while saying the attacks had caused no damage.

Diplomatic efforts continue despite the escalation. Iranian officials said a Qatari delegation recently held two hours of talks in Tehran, while Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has said regional negotiations should still be given a chance.

The latest ship incidents show the crisis remains active, with commercial vessels facing continuing danger while Tehran and Washington maintain their competing demands over the blockade and passage through the Strait of Hormuz.
