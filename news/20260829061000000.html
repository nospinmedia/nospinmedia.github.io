ICE Detains Milo Yiannopoulos Pending Deportation

British commentator held after final removal order

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 29, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has taken British political commentator Milo Yiannopoulos into custody after determining he had overstayed his U.S. visa and was subject to a final order of removal. The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) said Yiannopoulos, 41, was arrested at Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport on August 27 and will remain in ICE custody while arrangements are made to remove him to the United Kingdom. DHS said he legally entered the United States in May 2019 but remained after his authorized period of admission expired.

According to DHS, an immigration judge issued a final order of removal on July 22, 2026, after Yiannopoulos failed to appear for a scheduled immigration hearing. Federal officials said he is currently being held at an ICE detention facility in Louisiana pending deportation. Immigration authorities did not announce a timetable for his removal, and it was not immediately clear whether Yiannopoulos has retained legal counsel to challenge the order or seek additional relief.

Yiannopoulos rose to prominence as an editor at Breitbart News before resigning in 2017 amid controversy over remarks he made during an interview. In recent years he worked with the musician Ye, formerly known as Kanye West, and briefly served as an intern for former Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene. He has also publicly supported aggressive immigration enforcement, including advocating expanded deportations and stronger legal protections for ICE officers, positions that drew renewed attention following his detention.

The case comes as the Trump administration continues emphasizing enforcement against visa overstays alongside illegal border crossings. While most public attention has focused on unlawful entries, DHS has repeatedly stated that individuals who remain in the country after their visas expire are also subject to removal under federal immigration law when they do not maintain lawful status or fail to comply with immigration court proceedings.
