U.S. Army officer suspected of spying for Russia transferred to American custody

NATO-assigned lieutenant colonel was arrested by British counterterrorism police

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 18, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

A U.S. Army lieutenant colonel arrested in Britain on suspicion of spying for Russia has been transferred from British police custody to the U.S. military, moving a rare international counterintelligence case into American hands.

Lt. Col. Brandon Carroll Tyner, 41, had been held since his November 10 arrest near Gloucester in southwestern England. British authorities transferred him to U.S. military custody on Tuesday, November 17, after consultations involving the Crown Prosecution Service, the Attorney General's Office and American authorities under existing international protocols.

Tyner was assigned to NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps (ARRC) at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire. The multinational headquarters helps command rapidly deployable NATO forces and is located only a few miles from GCHQ, Britain's signals intelligence agency. British counterterrorism police also searched two locations in Gloucestershire as part of the investigation.

British authorities arrested Tyner on suspicion of offenses under Sections 1 and 3 of the National Security Act 2023, covering obtaining or disclosing protected information and conduct intended to assist a foreign intelligence service. Authorities identified Russia as the country involved in the suspected activity. A U.S. official has said investigators believe no classified information ultimately reached Russian hands.

U.S. Army Counterintelligence Command leader Brig. Gen. Sean Stinchon said the case "should serve as a stark warning" to service members suspected of violating their oath. FBI Director Kash Patel said the bureau has "zero tolerance" for anyone who betrays their oath or puts U.S. national security at risk.

British police said the case is unrelated to a separate investigation into an alleged Iranian-backed plot involving RAF Fairford, a Gloucestershire airbase where U.S. bombers have been stationed.

Tyner has not been convicted, and U.S. authorities have not publicly detailed the charges he may face.
