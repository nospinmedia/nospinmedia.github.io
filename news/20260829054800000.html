ICE Plans Purchase of Robot Dogs for Field Operations

DHS seeks up to $2 million for hazardous inspection missions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 29, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is preparing to purchase quadruped robotic systems to assist agents during hazardous field operations, according to a Department of Homeland Security (DHS) procurement forecast published on August 28. The notice authorizes spending of up to $2 million for Boston Dynamics Spot robotic platforms and related equipment, with the agency saying the machines are intended to improve officer safety by entering potentially dangerous environments before personnel do.

According to the procurement documents, the robot dogs would be equipped with cameras, sensors and other payloads to conduct inspections in confined spaces, unstable structures and locations that may pose risks to human officers. DHS officials said the robots are intended for reconnaissance, hazard assessment and situational awareness rather than enforcement actions. Officials stated the machines will not be used to make arrests or carry weapons. Spot robots can navigate uneven terrain, climb stairs and, when equipped with optional accessories, manipulate doors or other obstacles remotely.

The planned acquisition reflects the Trump administration's continued expansion of technology within federal immigration enforcement. It follows recent procurement activity aimed at providing additional equipment intended to improve officer safety during enforcement operations. Supporters argue robotic systems can reduce risks to agents by allowing remote inspection of unknown environments before officers enter. Civil liberties organizations, however, have questioned the broader use of advanced surveillance technologies in immigration enforcement and warned that expanding automation could increase privacy concerns and reduce public oversight.

Boston Dynamics has repeatedly stated that its commercial policies prohibit customers from weaponizing its robots or using them as autonomous lethal systems. The company says it requires customers to follow acceptable-use guidelines intended to keep the platforms focused on industrial, public safety and inspection applications. ICE has not announced when the procurement will be completed or when the robots could enter operational service.
