Supreme Court Denies Trump Rehearing In Carroll Verdict

$5 million judgment is final; separate $83.3M appeal on immunity still pending

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court on August 17, 2026 rejected President Trump’s rare petition to rehear his appeal of the $5 million civil verdict won by writer E. Jean Carroll, closing the door on further challenges in that case. The unsigned order, issued without noted dissents, leaves intact the federal jury’s 2023 finding that Trump sexually abused Carroll and defamed her.

The lawsuit—filed under the New York Adult Survivors Act—stemmed from Carroll’s account of a 1996 assault in a Bergdorf Goodman dressing room and a subsequent October 2022 social media post in which Trump called her claim a hoax. After the Court first declined to hear the case on June 29, a Manhattan judge authorized disbursement, and court filings show $5,625,005.48 (the award plus post‑judgment interest) was released to Carroll in July. Following Monday’s order, Carroll’s attorney Roberta Kaplan said the jury’s verdict “is now final and cannot be challenged in any court.”

The denial effectively exhausts Trump’s appellate options in the case known as “Carroll II,” while a separate $83.3 million defamation judgment from January 2024—often called “Carroll I”—remains on a parallel track. In that matter, Trump and the U.S. Department of Justice are asking the Supreme Court to recognize presidential immunity for statements he made about Carroll in 2019 while in office.

The justices did not explain their reasoning for denying rehearing, which is typical. The ruling underscores that the civil liability established by the 2023 jury verdict stands, independent of the broader immunity arguments now before the Court in the larger-dollar defamation case. Readers should note the different timelines and remedies: the $5 million case addressed sexual abuse and a 2022 statement, while the $83.3 million case concerns defamation tied to 2019 comments.
