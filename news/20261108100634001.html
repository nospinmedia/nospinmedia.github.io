Astronomers discover dead star surrounded by two debris rings

Unusual system may offer glimpse of our solar system’s distant future

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Astronomers have discovered a white dwarf surrounded by two distinct rings of planetary debris, the first such configuration observed around this type of dead star and one that could offer clues about the distant future of planetary systems like our own.

The white dwarf, J1328−1450, is about 1,600 light-years from Earth in the constellation Virgo. Researchers observed it using the X-shooter spectrograph on the European Southern Observatory’s Very Large Telescope in Chile. The star contains about 47% of the Sun’s mass compressed into a body roughly the diameter of Earth.

Rather than directly photographing two rings, astronomers identified them through their light. A typical single rotating debris disc produces a two-peaked emission profile, but observations of J1328−1450 revealed four distinct emission peaks, indicating two separate concentric rings. Astronomers previously identified 21 white dwarfs with gaseous debris discs, all of which had single-disc structures.

The rings also have different chemical signatures. Both contain emissions associated with calcium and oxygen, while magnesium emission comes from the inner ring and is strongly depleted in the outer ring. Researchers said that could mean the rings came from separate rocky parent bodies or from different layers of a disrupted planetary object.

The study, published in Nature Astronomy, was led by astronomer Nicola Pietro Gentile Fusillo of the University of Trieste and INAF, with researchers including Felipe Lagos-Vilches and Christopher J. Manser. Scientists propose that two surviving rocky bodies may help shape the rings. One could be approximately the size of Pluto, with the other smaller.

In about 5 billion years, the Sun is expected to become a red giant and eventually a white dwarf. Mercury and Venus are expected to be engulfed, while the fate of Earth remains uncertain. Surviving planets, moons and asteroids could later be disturbed and torn apart near the Sun’s remnant, potentially producing similar debris structures.
