Man charged with murder in death of Tasia Fortune

Medical examiner rules Mississippi hanging a homicide

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Jackson, Mississippi, police have arrested 51-year-old Jarques Ratliff on a murder charge in the death of Tasia Fortune, a 29-year-old Black woman who was found hanging from a tree behind a vacant home last month. The arrest came after the Mississippi State Medical Examiner's Office officially classified Fortune's death as a homicide Thursday evening, allowing investigators to move forward with warrants they had prepared during the investigation.

Fortune's body was discovered on August 3 behind an abandoned home in west Jackson. Police Chief RaShall Brackney said investigators secured two search warrants and an arrest warrant for Ratliff. Officers did not find him while executing the search warrants but later encountered him walking on Capitol Street and took him into custody. Police said Ratliff had an association with Fortune but have not disclosed the nature of their relationship or identified a motive.

Brackney said investigators have interviewed nearly 15 people, collected forensic evidence and pursued numerous leads since Fortune was found. The chief emphasized that Ratliff's arrest does not conclude the investigation and said additional arrests are expected. Authorities have not publicly detailed the evidence supporting the murder charge or released the medical examiner's findings explaining how Fortune was killed.

The circumstances of Fortune's death drew national attention and calls for answers, particularly because of Mississippi's history of racial violence and lynching. Her mother, Christy Spivey, had repeatedly said she did not believe her daughter died by suicide. After learning of Ratliff's arrest, Spivey expressed gratitude to investigators but said the family still has significant unanswered questions about what happened and why.

Fortune was a mother of four children and originally from Kentucky. Her death also attracted attention because two other people had been found hanging in separate Mississippi cases during the previous year. Authorities determined those deaths were suicides, while Fortune's death has now officially been classified as a homicide.
