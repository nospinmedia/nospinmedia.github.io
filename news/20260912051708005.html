Hong Kong jails Tiananmen vigil leaders for up to 7 years

Court rules call to end one-party rule was incitement to subversion

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

A Hong Kong court sentenced three veteran pro-democracy activists to prison terms ranging from five years and two months to seven years and three months for inciting subversion through their leadership of the group that organized the city's annual vigils commemorating the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown. Chow Hang-tung, 41, received the longest sentence at seven years and three months, while Lee Cheuk-yan, 69, was sentenced to seven years. Both had pleaded not guilty.

Former lawmaker Albert Ho, 74, received five years and two months after pleading guilty before the trial. All three were leaders of the now-disbanded Hong Kong Alliance in Support of Patriotic Democratic Movements of China, which organized the Tiananmen commemorations for three decades. The court also fined the Alliance HK$1.5 million, about US$191,000.

The case centered on the Alliance's longstanding call to "end one-party dictatorship." A panel of three judges handling the national security case concluded that the slogan and the group's activities sought to undermine the constitutional order and rule of the Chinese Communist Party. Lee and Chow were convicted in August of inciting others to participate in acts aimed at subverting state power. The court maintained that the defendants were prosecuted for their conduct rather than simply for holding political views.

Hong Kong's large annual candlelight vigils were held at Victoria Park and for years represented one of the most prominent public commemorations of the Tiananmen crackdown on Chinese-controlled territory. Authorities prohibited the gathering beginning in 2020, initially citing COVID-19 restrictions. The Alliance disbanded in September 2021 amid mounting pressure following the introduction of the national security law.

The National Security Law, imposed by Beijing in 2020 following the massive anti-government protests of 2019, criminalizes acts including secession, subversion, terrorism and collusion with foreign forces. Hong Kong and Chinese authorities say the law restored stability following the unrest, while critics and foreign governments argue its enforcement has sharply restricted political opposition and civil liberties. Lee and Chow plan to appeal their convictions.
