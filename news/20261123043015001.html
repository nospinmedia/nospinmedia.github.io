Army invokes emergency powers for Iowa explosive detonation

Plan moves forward despite appeals court pause

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 23, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

The U.S. Army plans to detonate approximately 9,500 pounds of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown, Iowa, after invoking federal emergency munitions-response provisions that it says allow the operation to proceed without the environmental permit paused by a federal appeals court.

The 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals paused the Environmental Protection Agency permit on November 19 after the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club's Iowa chapter challenged the planned detonation. In a November 22 court filing, the Justice Department said the Army would instead proceed under an emergency explosives and munitions-response exemption to the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act, citing 40 C.F.R. § 264.1(g)(8). The detonation is now scheduled for Wednesday, November 25.

The material consists of 68 barrels of degraded and crystallized lead azide discovered at the plant. Army officials have warned that falling temperatures could further destabilize the explosive compound and increase the danger of an uncontrolled explosion. The Army says moving or chemically treating the material presents greater immediate risks and that controlled detonation is the safest available response.

Environmental groups represented by Earthjustice dispute whether safer alternatives were adequately considered and are challenging the Army's decision to proceed. The groups have also raised concerns about airborne lead contamination following the blasts.

Iowa Governor Kim Reynolds activated the State Emergency Operations Center to coordinate monitoring and public safety measures. Updated Iowa Department of Health and Human Services guidance advises people within modeled plume areas to remain indoors with windows and doors closed for 24 to 48 hours, use indoor-air recirculation when possible and wet-clean exposed outdoor surfaces afterward. The agency also recommends consulting healthcare providers about blood lead testing, particularly for pregnant women and children under age 6. The Army has said updated plume maps will be released six hours before the operation and updated hourly afterward.
