Evacuation lifted after explosive threat at Illinois ethanol plant

Bomb technicians neutralize device as FBI investigates

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 15, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Authorities have lifted a one-mile evacuation zone around the BioUrja Renewables ethanol plant in Peoria, Illinois, after bomb technicians rendered a suspicious device safe and an extensive search of the industrial facility found no additional threats.

The emergency began Friday afternoon when BioUrja contacted police about an electronic bomb threat at 1:08 p.m. A suspicious package was discovered about an hour later, and by 6:45 p.m. authorities had determined it represented a credible threat. Bomb technicians rendered the device safe at approximately 9:12 p.m., but officials kept the evacuation zone in place overnight while teams prepared to search the plant for additional devices.

Peoria Police Chief Brad Dixon emphasized that investigators were not formally calling the recovered item a bomb while its contents undergo comprehensive laboratory analysis. Peoria Mayor Rita Ali had publicly described the object as a bomb. Dixon said authorities knew enough about the device to treat it as a credible explosive threat but declined to disclose details that could interfere with the criminal investigation.

The response was unusually extensive because BioUrja produces ethanol and stores highly combustible materials. Authorities established a one-mile safety radius based on the potential danger posed by materials at the facility rather than solely on the suspicious device. The evacuation covered portions of Peoria and extended across the Illinois River into East Peoria. Residents needing shelter were directed to Manual High School in Peoria and the EastSide Centre in East Peoria.

The U.S. Coast Guard also closed a four-mile section of the Illinois River between mile markers 160 and 164, stopping commercial vessel traffic through the affected area. The Coast Guard fully reopened the waterway at approximately 1 p.m. Saturday, when authorities also lifted the evacuation order, removed roadway barricades and allowed residents to return.

Bomb technicians and BioUrja employees began searching the facility at about 6 a.m. Saturday. The plant covers a large industrial area, making the search a lengthy process. No additional suspicious devices were found.

The continuing investigation involves Peoria police, the FBI, ATF, Illinois State Police, the Peoria Fire Department and other agencies. Authorities have not announced an arrest or identified a suspect, and they have not publicly disclosed who sent the electronic threat or why the ethanol facility may have been targeted. No injuries were reported.
