Northeast flights resume after major FAA equipment outage

Cut fiber line triggers hundreds of delays and cancellations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 22, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Flights resumed Monday night at major Northeast airports after an equipment outage disrupted air traffic across the region, producing hundreds of delays and cancellations and affecting travelers well beyond the airports where ground stops were imposed.

The Federal Aviation Administration said operations resumed at Newark Liberty International Airport, John F. Kennedy International Airport and LaGuardia Airport after the disruption. Ground stops or other restrictions also affected Philadelphia International Airport, Teterboro Airport and other airports as problems involving frequencies at the Philadelphia Terminal Radar Approach Control facility disrupted air traffic operations.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said an Amtrak construction crew accidentally damaged a Verizon fiber line in New Jersey, causing the telecommunications outage. Verizon said construction contractors working near an Amtrak rail line dug up and cut its fiber cable. Duffy later said the damaged line had been repaired and airport operations could resume, although airlines and airports continued working through delays and cancellations caused by the disruption.

The effects spread across the Northeast. At Boston Logan International Airport, more than 450 flights were delayed and more than 70 were canceled by Monday evening, while some aircraft originally headed for New York-area airports were diverted to Boston. Flights into the affected New York airports were also restricted from numerous regions across the United States and Canada.

The outage demonstrated how failure of telecommunications infrastructure serving air traffic control can quickly ripple through the national aviation network. Even after the underlying equipment problem was repaired, displaced aircraft, delayed crews and backed-up schedules meant disruptions continued for travelers.

The FAA and transportation officials said normal operations were returning Monday night, but passengers were advised to continue checking with their airlines as carriers worked through the remaining backlog.
