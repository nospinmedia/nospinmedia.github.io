Morocco Detains 111 Near Ceuta In Border Sweep

Security tightened after online calls for another mass crossing

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 16, 2026 at 10:38 AM ET

Moroccan authorities detained 111 people on August 15, 2026, near Fnideq after online messages encouraged another mass attempt to enter Ceuta, Spain's autonomous enclave on the North African coast. Security forces used tear gas to disperse groups gathering several kilometers from the border fence, preventing a large-scale crossing before it reached the frontier.

Officials said those detained included 109 migrants from sub-Saharan Africa and two Moroccan nationals. During the operation, journalists from Spanish news outlets EFE and TVE were instructed to leave the area as police secured the border zone.

Spain has also strengthened security around Ceuta following the major migrant surge in late July 2026. Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska said more than 500 additional National Police officers have been deployed, bringing the total security presence in the enclave to over 1,600 personnel. Authorities have also installed a floating maritime barrier to discourage attempts to swim around border controls.

Ceuta and nearby Melilla are the European Union's only land borders with Africa and have long been focal points for irregular migration. Moroccan and Spanish officials said they are continuing to monitor social media for calls encouraging additional mass crossings and will maintain close coordination to prevent further incidents.
