White House Gives Lisa Cook 21 Days To Contest Removal

Move follows 5–4 Supreme Court ruling; deadline set for August 26

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 8, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The White House has restarted its effort to remove Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, issuing a formal notice giving her 21 days to respond to mortgage-fraud allegations. The letter, signed by Dan Scavino, states that President Trump is considering her removal and sets an August 26, 2026, deadline for Cook to submit evidence or legal arguments in her defense.

The notice, dated August 5, 2026, says it is being issued pursuant to the U.S. Supreme Court's recent ruling on the case. The allegations stem from claims made in August 2025 by Federal Housing Finance Agency Director Bill Pulte, who accused Cook of listing two properties—one in Ann Arbor, Michigan, and another in Atlanta—as her primary residence on separate 2021 mortgage documents. Cook has denied wrongdoing, and her attorneys, Abbe Lowell and Norm Eisen, have called the effort a "baseless" attempt to undermine the independence of the central bank. No criminal charges have been filed.

The renewed effort follows the Supreme Court's 5–4 decision on June 29, 2026, written by Chief Justice John Roberts, which blocked Cook's immediate removal and ruled she must first receive formal notice and an opportunity to respond. The Court did not decide whether a Federal Reserve governor may ultimately be removed "for cause" under the Federal Reserve Act, but said the administration could pursue the process if it followed the required legal procedures.

The dispute has become a closely watched test of the Federal Reserve's institutional independence as the central bank continues to navigate inflation, interest-rate policy, and broader economic uncertainty. Cook remains in office while the response period runs through August 26, after which additional court proceedings are widely expected if the administration moves forward.
