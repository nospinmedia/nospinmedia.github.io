Debris found from medical jet missing off Nantucket

Search continues for six people aboard Boston-bound flight

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 4, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The U.S. Coast Guard has confirmed that debris found south of Nantucket, Massachusetts, came from a medical evacuation jet that disappeared while carrying six people from Bermuda to Boston. Search crews continued looking for the occupants after locating the wreckage following an extensive air-and-sea operation.

The Canadian-registered Gulfstream G100, registration C-GRJP, was operated by Ontario-based Latitude Air Ambulance. The aircraft carried two pilots, two medical personnel, a patient and a family member. The four crew members were Canadian citizens, while the patient and family member were from Bermuda. The flight was headed to Boston Logan International Airport when communications were lost early Saturday, October 3.

Before disappearing, the crew reported electrical problems and declared an emergency. Air traffic recordings captured controllers struggling to communicate with the aircraft, including a controller asking whether the crew was "coming in blind." Flight-tracking data showed the jet descending rapidly from roughly 24,000 feet to about 10,000 feet before tracking information ended. The available reporting does not establish what caused the aircraft to go down.

Coast Guard crews searched more than 1,600 square miles before confirming that a debris field belonged to the missing aircraft. The response included aircraft, helicopters and surface vessels, with the Coast Guard cutter William Sparling continuing the search overnight. Authorities said the six people aboard remained unaccounted for after the debris was discovered, and their next of kin had been notified.

Canadian transportation authorities are working with U.S. officials, while the National Transportation Safety Board has been gathering information about the disappearance. The discovery of debris shifts the operation from simply locating the missing aircraft to an increasingly urgent search for the people who were aboard, while investigators work to determine what happened during the flight's final minutes.
