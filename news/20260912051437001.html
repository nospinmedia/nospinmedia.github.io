Saudi pipeline shut after drones launched from Iraq

Baghdad dismisses commander as Riyadh delays retaliation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Saudi Arabia temporarily shut its critical East-West oil pipeline after drone attacks launched from Iraq struck the pipeline in the Riyadh and Medina regions, adding pressure to oil routes already strained by the continuing Middle East conflict. Saudi officials said the attacks caused injuries and material damage, while the Ministry of Energy described the shutdown as a precautionary measure as the extent of the damage was assessed.

The 1,200-kilometer (745-mile) pipeline is Saudi Arabia's main overland route for moving crude from the country's eastern oil-producing region to the Red Sea, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz. The route has become especially important since conflict disrupted traffic through Hormuz. Ship-tracking companies and analysts cited by Reuters estimated that the pipeline had recently been carrying about 4 million to 5 million barrels per day, equal to roughly 4% to 5% of global oil supply. Saudi officials had not detailed the attack's effect on exports as of September 11.

Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry said several drones involved in the attack originated in Iraqi territory but did not identify who launched them. Iraq subsequently confirmed that investigators determined the attacks came from a site in southern Maysan province. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered an investigation into the provincial operations command and dismissed its commander. Iraq also condemned attacks threatening Saudi security and pledged to pursue those responsible.

Riyadh said it would hold off on military retaliation after a request from the Iraqi prime minister, giving Baghdad an opportunity to take action. Saudi Arabia said it nevertheless reserved the right to take measures needed to protect its territory and critical infrastructure. Iraq also closed the Shalamcheh and Chazabeh border crossings with Iran until further notice as regional security concerns intensified.

The pipeline shutdown comes as Saudi oil exports face pressure from disruptions at both the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea, increasing the importance of alternative routes for getting Gulf crude to international markets.
