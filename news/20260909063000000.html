Lawmakers tell Supreme Court Trump lacks power to restrict mail voting

Case could affect November election rules nationwide

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 9, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

261 current and former members of Congress are urging the U.S. Supreme Court to keep new federal mail-voting requirements blocked, arguing that President Trump does not have constitutional authority to unilaterally regulate federal elections through the executive branch.

The lawmakers filed a friend-of-the-court brief Tuesday in State of California, et al. v. United States Postal Service, et al., a case stemming from litigation in Massachusetts federal court. The filing was signed by all 47 members of the Senate Democratic Caucus, 199 current House Democrats and 15 former Republican House members. They argue that the Constitution gives states primary responsibility for administering elections while granting Congress, rather than the president, authority to alter federal election rules.

The dispute involves a U.S. Postal Service rule developed following a March executive order from Trump. Among other requirements, the rule would require states using the mail for federal ballots to submit information about mail voters through a USPS portal, use federally approved ballot-envelope designs and incorporate specific barcodes. Certain noncompliant ballots could be refused by the Postal Service.

On September 4, U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani in Boston issued a preliminary injunction preventing mandatory portions of the rule from taking effect for elections through November 3, including the midterm elections. Talwani concluded that the challengers were likely to succeed on key claims and found that implementing the requirements so close to Election Day risked disrupting state election systems.

The Trump administration argues that the Postal Service has legal authority to establish standards for mail moving through its system and says delaying the rule creates uncertainty as states begin distributing ballots. It has asked the Supreme Court to stay Talwani's injunction while litigation continues.

The case is moving quickly. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson gave opponents until 4 p.m. Wednesday, September 9, to respond to the administration's latest emergency application. The Supreme Court has not yet decided whether the challenged restrictions are constitutional, making the lawmakers' assertion an argument before the Court rather than a final legal ruling.
