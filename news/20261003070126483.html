FDA finds Cyclospora at sites tied to massive lettuce outbreak

12,883 people sickened and 570 hospitalized across 21 states

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 3, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has closed its investigation into the nation's largest documented multistate Cyclospora outbreak after finding the parasite at two sites in Mexico connected through its traceback investigation to recalled iceberg lettuce. The outbreak sickened 12,883 people across 21 states, hospitalized 570 and resulted in two deaths.

The FDA said one positive environmental sample came from a tank holding outgoing wastewater at the Taylor Farms de Mexico processing facility. A second was collected from a drainage ditch at an iceberg lettuce grower identified during the agency's traceback investigation. The findings add to epidemiological evidence and traceback data supporting the connection between the outbreak and recalled shredded iceberg lettuce from Taylor Farms de Mexico.

The agency cautioned that it still does not have enough evidence to conclusively determine exactly how the lettuce became contaminated. Genetic analysis of the two environmental samples has been completed, but the FDA is continuing to determine how those results relate to genetic testing of samples from people who became ill. The implicated grower has been removed from Taylor Farms' supplier program.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention declared the outbreak over on September 11, and the FDA says all recalled lettuce associated with this outbreak is now off the market. Taylor Farms voluntarily recalled iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico on July 17. The recall included certain Marketside iceberg salad and shredded lettuce products sold at Walmart as well as lettuce supplied to food-service customers. Recalled products were distributed widely across the United States, including New Hampshire, Massachusetts and Maine.

Cyclospora is a microscopic parasite that can cause prolonged diarrhea, loss of appetite, stomach cramps, bloating, nausea and fatigue. Illness can last weeks or longer without treatment.

The FDA is now planning 10 prevention activities before the 2027 growing season, including increased surveillance of produce historically associated with Cyclospora and additional work with growers and international partners.
