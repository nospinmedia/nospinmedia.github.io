Eight dead after Brooklyn apartment building collapse

Criminal inquiry opens as city inspects 100 other construction sites

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 13, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Eight people, including two children, were killed after a three-story mixed-use building collapsed in Brooklyn's Flatbush neighborhood, prompting a criminal investigation and emergency inspections at construction sites across New York City.

The building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue collapsed just after 4 p.m. Wednesday, November 11, falling toward an adjacent excavation pit at 2625 Bedford Avenue. Six victims died at the scene and two at hospitals. The dead included children ages 9 and 10 and six adults, while 18 people were injured, including 14 civilians and four firefighters.

The FDNY ended search-and-rescue operations Thursday afternoon after determining no additional victims remained in the rubble. The site was then turned over to the New York City Department of Buildings and NYPD as investigators began examining how the structure failed.

Brooklyn District Attorney Eric Gonzalez has opened a criminal inquiry into whether criminal conduct contributed to the collapse. Mayor Zohran Mamdani also announced that the Department of Buildings is conducting safety inspections at 100 other construction sites linked to five entities associated with the neighboring Bedford Avenue project.

City records show the adjacent construction site received a stop-work order and $12,500 in fines earlier this year for unsafe demolition and missing protections for neighboring properties before excavation resumed in September. The collapsed building, constructed in 1930, had 98 open housing code violations, while a pre-construction survey documented cracks and described part of the wall facing the excavation as being in poor condition.

The collapse also damaged a neighboring building and prompted vacate orders for three nearby Flatbush Avenue properties. Investigators have not yet determined the cause or assigned responsibility.
