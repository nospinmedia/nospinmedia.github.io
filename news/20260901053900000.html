North Carolina teen indicted over San Diego mosque attack

Prosecutors seek murder convictions for alleged livestream accomplice

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 1, 2026 at 2:46 PM ET

A North Carolina grand jury has indicted Sarah Lindsey Santiago, 17, on three counts of first-degree murder and one count of conspiracy for her alleged role in helping facilitate the May 18, 2026 mass shooting at the Islamic Center of San Diego. Forsyth County District Attorney Jim O'Neill announced the indictment on August 31, alleging Santiago knowingly assisted the attackers before and during the assault despite being hundreds of miles away from the crime scene.

Prosecutors allege Santiago coordinated online with the gunmen, Cain Clark, 17, and Caleb Vazquez, 18, before the attack. According to investigators, she agreed to capture and distribute a GoPro livestream of the shooting, helped disseminate the attackers' white supremacist manifesto, and purchased a white supremacist patch that one of the shooters wore during the attack. Authorities said the two gunmen killed three people, including security guard Amin Abdullah, whose actions during the shooting helped protect approximately 140 children inside the mosque before the attackers later died by suicide.

Under North Carolina law, prosecutors argue that individuals who knowingly aid and abet a planned homicide can face the same criminal liability as those who carry out the killings. Santiago has been ordered held without bond and is being prosecuted as an adult. Her attorney, Alan Doorasamy Sr., has denied the allegations and said his client is innocent of all charges.

The indictment is expected to become a closely watched test of how criminal courts apply traditional conspiracy and accomplice laws to alleged online participants in violent attacks. Prosecutors contend the case demonstrates that individuals who intentionally assist acts of mass violence through digital coordination or distribution efforts may face the same homicide charges as those who commit the killings, while the defense is expected to challenge the extent of Santiago's alleged criminal responsibility.
