FBI disrupts alleged Russian assassination plot in Washington

Prosecutors say operatives offered $40,000 to kill dissident

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 16, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Federal authorities say they disrupted an alleged Russian intelligence assassination network that attempted to recruit people inside the United States to surveil and kill a prominent Russian dissident believed to be living in the Washington, D.C. area. The Justice Department unsealed charges on September 15 against five defendants accused of participating in a broader operation targeting Russian dissidents and countries supporting Ukraine.

The defendants are former Russian intelligence colonel Yuri Khrameev, 63; his son and FSB officer Kirill Khrameev, 27; Cuban national Oemis Romagoza Durruthy, 35; Cuban national Yaidel Delgado Suarez, 35; and Venezuelan national Angel Eduardo Castro, 22. Prosecutors say all five are living abroad and remain at large. All are charged with conspiracy to finance terrorism, while Yuri Khrameev, Suarez and Castro also face a murder-for-hire conspiracy charge.

According to the indictment, Suarez recruited a U.S.-based person during the summer of 2026 to conduct surveillance at two locations associated with the dissident, offering $1,000 to $1,500 for photographs and videos. Prosecutors allege Suarez and Castro then offered that person $40,000 to "eliminate" or "disappear" the target. When the recruit declined to personally carry out the killing, the defendants allegedly sought someone else who would do it. The dissident was not publicly identified.

Federal prosecutors say the alleged network operated internationally since at least 2024. In another case, Kirill Khrameev allegedly recruited a U.S. citizen to surveil a person in Lithuania before Yuri Khrameev offered approximately $25,000 to kill that target. Prosecutors also allege network members helped coordinate attacks and sabotage operations in Czechia and Lithuania targeting countries perceived as supporting Ukraine.

The FBI said multiple field offices worked together to disrupt the U.S. operation. The indictment contains allegations that have not been proven in court, and all five defendants are presumed innocent unless convicted.
