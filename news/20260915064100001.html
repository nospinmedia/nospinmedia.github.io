Houthis seize two more strategic Red Sea islands

Advance expands rebel reach along crucial shipping corridor

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 15, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels have seized Greater Hanish and Lesser Hanish, two strategically located islands in the Red Sea, expanding a rapid offensive that has already given the group control of Yemen's Red Sea coastline and new positions near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The latest captures follow the Houthis' seizure of the port city of Mokha and Mayun Island, also known as Perim, which sits directly in the Bab el-Mandeb shipping chokepoint.

The new island seizures extend the Houthis' reach along a maritime corridor connecting the Gulf of Aden, Red Sea and Suez Canal. The route has become particularly important during the wider regional conflict because shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains severely disrupted. Saudi Arabia has increasingly depended on its East-West Pipeline to move crude from its eastern oil fields to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, but that pipeline is also expected to remain largely out of service for several weeks following a recent drone attack attributed to Iran-backed militias in Iraq.

Fighting inside Yemen has intensified alongside the Houthi territorial gains. Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces and their allies have launched airstrikes against Houthi positions and movements as they attempt to slow the advance. The Houthis have also continued missile and drone attacks against targets in Saudi Arabia, including military and oil infrastructure.

The renewed conflict is producing a growing humanitarian toll. Recent fighting has displaced nearly 94,000 people, according to reporting citing humanitarian officials, while more than 500 people have been killed during a week of intensified combat. The escalation has raised fears that Yemen could return to the level of sustained warfare seen before the 2022 truce, which sharply reduced fighting after years of civil war.

The Houthis' expanding presence does not mean they control all commercial traffic through the Red Sea, but their positions give them greater ability to threaten vessels near one of the world's most important shipping routes. The group has previously targeted commercial ships and has declared Saudi vessels subject to a blockade.
