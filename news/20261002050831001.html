U.S. sends third carrier and thousands more troops toward Middle East

Military buildup expands options as Trump weighs renewed Iran strikes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 2, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

The United States is sending thousands of additional sailors and Marines and another aircraft carrier toward the Middle East, potentially putting three U.S. carrier strike groups in the region as President Trump considers renewed strikes against Iran.

The USS Theodore Roosevelt recently departed San Diego, while the USS Makin Island Amphibious Ready Group left with the 13th Marine Expeditionary Unit. The Makin Island group includes the amphibious ships USS John P. Murtha and USS Anchorage. The deployments involve roughly 9,000 to 10,000 additional U.S. personnel, according to reporting on the buildup. The USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington are already operating in the region. If the Washington remains after the Roosevelt arrives, the Navy could temporarily have three carrier strike groups there at the same time.

The deployments do not necessarily mean three carriers will remain in the region for an extended period. The Roosevelt had been expected to relieve the George Washington, and military officials could still rotate existing forces home. The new ships nevertheless give U.S. commanders substantially more naval and air capability while the administration considers its next steps in the conflict with Iran. The Makin Island group could similarly overlap with or eventually replace the USS Boxer Amphibious Ready Group, which is already deployed in the region.

Trump told TIME in an interview published Thursday that it was "possible" the United States would increase bombing of Iran after the November 3 midterm elections. The deployments come after months of conflict and intermittent attacks between the United States and Iran, along with unsuccessful efforts to reach a lasting settlement. The unusually large naval concentration also places additional demands on Navy ships and personnel after months of extended deployments.
