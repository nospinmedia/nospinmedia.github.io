Nine Fired FBI Employees Sue Trump Administration for Their Jobs Back

Former Agents and Analysts Say Mar-a-Lago Investigation Led to Unlawful Firings

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 16, 2026 at 2:49 PM ET

Nine former FBI employees fired after working on the investigation into President Trump's retention of classified documents at Mar-a-Lago sued the Trump administration on October 15, 2026, seeking reinstatement, back pay and a court declaration that their terminations were unlawful.

The lawsuit was filed in federal court in Washington, D.C., on behalf of former special agents, intelligence analysts and staff operations specialists who were fired over two days in February 2026. The plaintiffs say they were assigned to the classified-documents investigation as part of their work on specialized counterintelligence and counterespionage squads and did not volunteer for the case.

The complaint alleges the employees were terminated because administration officials viewed their work on the investigation, internally known as Plasmic Echo, as politically hostile to President Trump. The plaintiffs characterize the firings as political retribution and say they occurred without an internal investigation, advance notice, hearing or opportunity to appeal. Those claims have not been adjudicated by the court.

FBI Director Kash Patel has defended personnel removals at the bureau and told Congress that employees who were fired had participated in what he described as the weaponization of the FBI. The former employees reject that characterization, arguing that they were performing assignments given to them as career law enforcement personnel.

The nine plaintiffs collectively had more than 100 years of FBI service, according to the lawsuit. Their previous work included responses to the Boston Marathon bombing, hostage recoveries and the investigation into the 2024 assassination attempt against President Trump in Butler, Pennsylvania.

The Mar-a-Lago investigation began after classified government records were found at President Trump's Florida estate following his first term. It ultimately produced federal criminal charges against Trump. He denied wrongdoing, and the case was dismissed in 2024 after U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon ruled that special counsel Jack Smith had been unlawfully appointed.
