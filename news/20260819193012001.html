Liberia Agrees to Accept 1,200 U.S. Third-Country Deportees

First transfer flight scheduled for Aug. 20

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 19, 2026 at 2:45 PM ET

Liberia announced it will accept up to 1,200 non-citizen migrants deported from the United States over the next year under a new third-country removal agreement, marking one of the largest arrangements of its kind under the Trump administration's immigration policy. The first group of 20 people, none of whom are Liberian citizens, is scheduled to arrive in Monrovia on August 20, 2026.

Liberian Information Minister Jerolinmek Piah said those transferred will not be treated as detainees and will instead be received as guests of the country. Officials said they will be allowed to apply for asylum in Liberia or leave voluntarily if they choose. The government added that the initial group was screened before departure and includes migrants from several regions whose home countries are not receiving them.

The agreement is part of the United States' broader effort to expand third-country deportation arrangements when direct returns to migrants' countries of origin are not possible. Human rights organizations, including Refugees International and Human Rights First, continue to challenge the policy, arguing that sending migrants to countries where they have no prior ties could conflict with international protections against refoulement and other legal safeguards.

The U.S.-Liberia framework was negotiated in 2025 and publicly outlined earlier this year. While litigation over third-country removals continues in U.S. courts, Liberian officials say preparations are moving forward and the first transfer flight remains scheduled for August 20. The agreement is expected to remain closely watched as a test of the administration's broader immigration strategy and the legal challenges surrounding third-country deportations.
