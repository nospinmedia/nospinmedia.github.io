Supreme Court allows Big Bend border work to resume

Lower-court construction freeze put on hold

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 10, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court has cleared the way for the Trump administration to resume border barrier construction in the Big Bend region of West Texas while a legal challenge to the project continues.

The court on November 9 put on hold an injunction issued by U.S. District Judge Kathleen Cardone, who had stopped construction and other ground-disturbing activity across the Big Bend sector. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only justice to publicly dissent from the Supreme Court's decision. The order is temporary and does not resolve the underlying lawsuit over the government's authority to waive environmental and other legal requirements.

The project includes border barriers, roads and surveillance infrastructure across the remote region. Inside Big Bend National Park, federal plans include roughly 17 miles of vehicle barriers along with extensive patrol-road and security infrastructure. Other portions of the project outside the park include taller pedestrian barriers. Customs and Border Protection has not announced when work will restart.

The lawsuit was brought by the Center for Biological Diversity and local plaintiffs challenging waivers issued by the Department of Homeland Security that allow the government to bypass environmental and historic-preservation requirements. Opponents argue the construction threatens wildlife, desert landscapes, cultural resources and the region's tourism economy. They have also pointed to the Big Bend sector's historically low share of migrant apprehensions compared with other parts of the southern border.

The Trump administration argues the barriers are needed because illegal crossings and drug seizures still occur in the region and because leaving gaps could shift trafficking activity there. Congress provided about $46 billion for broader border projects, and federal officials have been moving forward with barriers and related infrastructure along the U.S.-Mexico border.
