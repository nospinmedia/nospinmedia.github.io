U.S. disables cargo ship attempting to breach Iran blockade

Military expands interdictions toward Iranian land routes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

The U.S. military disabled a commercial cargo vessel in the Gulf of Oman after it attempted to breach the American naval blockade of Iran, as the military establishes a new task force to expand interdiction efforts beyond maritime routes.

U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) said American forces struck the St. Kitts and Nevis-flagged M/V Huda on Saturday, November 7, after the vessel failed to comply with instructions while attempting to leave the blockade area following a port call in Iran. U.S. forces warned the civilian crew and directed them away from the section of the ship that would be targeted before an American military aircraft used precision-guided munitions against the vessel’s stern, disabling it.

The Huda is the latest merchant vessel disabled by U.S. forces since maritime enforcement resumed in July, with reports placing the number of ships disabled for noncompliance at five or six. CENTCOM says the broader maritime interdiction campaign has redirected 147 commercial vessels and prevented Iran from exporting more than $25 billion in crude oil.

CENTCOM has also established Task Force Iron Gate, headquartered in Tampa, Florida, to coordinate efforts targeting Iranian commerce beyond the existing naval blockade. The task force will focus on the movement of advanced weapons, dual-use technology and sanctioned materials across Iran’s land borders and through the Caspian Sea.

CENTCOM Commander Adm. Brad Cooper said the task force will work with interagency partners to constrain Iran’s ability to rebuild weapons factories and finance military operations. The initiative broadens an enforcement campaign that until now has centered heavily on maritime traffic around Iran.

The new task force and the strike on the Huda represent separate but connected elements of the expanding U.S. campaign to restrict Iranian trade and military supply networks.
