Pentagon Moves to Remove Stars and Stripes Leaders

Editor, Publisher and Reporter Challenge Dismissals

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 22, 2026 at 4:20 PM ET

The Department of Defense has issued separation notices to Stars and Stripes Editor-in-Chief Erik Slavin, Publisher Max Lederer, and reporter Lara Korte, citing alleged insubordination after the journalists publicly defended the military newspaper's editorial independence. The notices were delivered on August 21, escalating a months-long dispute between Pentagon leadership and the independent news organization that serves members of the U.S. armed forces around the world.

According to the separation notices, Pentagon officials alleged the employees violated directives by making unauthorized public statements during media interviews, including comments to CBS News regarding the publication's editorial independence. Slavin and Korte had stated that any effort by Defense Department officials to censor accurate reporting would cross a fundamental line for the newsroom. Lederer, who has led Stars and Stripes for 19 years, had previously announced plans to retire at the end of September, but now faces an accelerated departure under the proposed action.

The dismissals follow broader changes implemented by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, whose office has sought greater oversight of the military-funded publication. Earlier this year, the Pentagon removed Stars and Stripes ombudsman Jacqueline Smith, whose role included protecting editorial independence, and appointed Navy Capt. William Urban as deputy publisher. The publication has also recently produced investigative reporting on living conditions aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln, adding to tensions between newsroom leadership and Pentagon officials.

The affected employees have been given five days to respond to the proposed terminations through the federal civil service process. Press freedom organizations and military journalism advocates have raised concerns that the actions could undermine longstanding protections intended to preserve the editorial independence of Stars and Stripes, while Defense Department officials maintain the personnel decisions were based on workplace conduct rather than the content of the publication's reporting.
