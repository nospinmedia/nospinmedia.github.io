Trump's $5,000 pledge catches Republicans by surprise

$1.2 trillion proposal divides GOP lawmakers after convention

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 11, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

President Donald Trump's promise to give every adult American a $5,000 payment if Republicans retain control of Congress is facing skepticism from some members of his own party, while others are moving toward legislation to make the proposal possible. The reaction provides the first indication of how Trump's surprise convention pledge could fare if Republicans attempt to turn it into policy.

Trump announced the payments during the Republican midterm convention in Dallas, tying them directly to a Republican victory in the November 3 elections. He doubled down on the proposal during the convention's second night, although other prominent Republican speakers largely avoided discussing it.

The potential price is substantial. With roughly 245 million adult U.S. citizens, providing $5,000 to each would cost more than $1.2 trillion, according to a CBS News calculation. Trump has said revenue generated by tariffs could help fund the payments, but current tariff collections are far below what would be required to cover universal checks of that size.

Republican lawmakers are already divided. Sen. Bernie Moreno of Ohio supports the idea and said he intends to prepare legislation for the payments following the election. Rep. David Schweikert of Arizona, however, has raised concerns about the potential effects on inflation, interest rates and federal finances. Senate Majority Leader John Thune has not committed to supporting the proposal, while other Republicans have also questioned its cost.

Another unresolved issue is whether Trump could distribute the money without Congress. The president has suggested congressional approval might not be necessary, but spending federal money generally requires congressional authorization. No detailed administration plan explaining eligibility, funding, timing or the legal mechanism for distributing the proposed payments has been released.

That makes the Republican response an important development beyond Trump's original announcement. The proposal has moved from a campaign promise made at the convention to a debate within the party over whether and how it could actually be enacted.
