Tupac Shakur's Killer Sentenced Nearly 30 Years After Murder

Former Gang Leader Duane Davis Gets 40 Years to Life in Prison

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

Nearly 30 years after rapper Tupac Shakur was fatally shot in Las Vegas, a Nevada judge has sentenced the man convicted of orchestrating his murder to 40 years to life in prison. Duane "Keffe D" Davis, 63, a former leader of the South Side Compton Crips gang, received the sentence on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, bringing a significant legal conclusion to one of the most widely followed unsolved killings in American music history.

Clark County District Court Judge Carli Kierny imposed the sentence after a jury convicted Davis of first-degree murder in August 2026. Davis received credit for time already served following his September 2023 arrest. He remains the only person ever charged and convicted in connection with Shakur's killing. His attorneys have challenged the prosecution's evidence, and Davis has indicated that he intends to appeal.

Shakur was shot on September 7, 1996, while riding in a BMW driven by Death Row Records executive Marion "Suge" Knight near the Las Vegas Strip. The shooting occurred hours after a Mike Tyson boxing match at the MGM Grand, where Shakur and members of his entourage had been involved in a confrontation with Davis's nephew, Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson. Shakur, who was 25 years old, died from his injuries six days later.

Prosecutors argued that Davis organized the retaliatory shooting and supplied the firearm used in the attack. Their case relied heavily on statements Davis made during media interviews and in his 2019 memoir, in which he described being inside the vehicle involved in the shooting. Davis identified Anderson as the gunman, although Anderson denied involvement before his death in 1998.

Defense attorneys argued that Davis exaggerated his role for publicity and financial gain, emphasizing the absence of physical evidence directly connecting him to the murder. The jury rejected those arguments and found him guilty.

During Tuesday's sentencing hearing, Shakur's sister, Sekyiwa Shakur, and stepbrother, Maurice "Mopreme" Shakur, described the lasting impact of his death on their family. They urged the court to impose a severe punishment, emphasizing the loss of a brother whose influence extended far beyond music.

Shakur remains one of hip-hop's most influential artists, with his recordings, poetry, and commentary on race, poverty, and inequality continuing to reach new generations. The sentencing marks a major milestone in the criminal case, although Davis's expected appeal means further legal proceedings remain possible.
