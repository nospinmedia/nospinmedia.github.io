Deadly Airstrike Hits Gold Mine Near Sudan-Egypt Border

At Least 13 Killed as Rights Group Reports Toll Could Be 72

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 12, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

An airstrike on a gold-mining area near the disputed border between Sudan and Egypt has killed at least 13 miners, with a Sudanese human rights organization reporting a substantially higher death toll of 72. The attack occurred Saturday, October 10, 2026, in a remote mountainous region where thousands of people depend on small-scale gold mining for their livelihoods.

The Sudan Doctors Network, which monitors casualties during Sudan's ongoing conflict, initially reported 13 deaths and at least 28 injuries. However, Emergency Lawyers, a Sudanese organization documenting violence against civilians, subsequently reported that preliminary information indicated 72 people had been killed and more than 50 injured.

The higher casualty figure has not been independently verified, and the differing reports leave the full scale of the disaster uncertain.

A survivor identified as Abdel-Aziz Yousef told the Associated Press that an unidentified aircraft attacked an artisanal gold mine Saturday morning. He described miners fleeing the mountainous area amid fears of additional strikes.

Emergency Lawyers accused the Egyptian military of carrying out the attack, alleging that fighter jets targeted civilian miners inside Sudanese territory. However, Egypt had not publicly commented on the latest strike, and responsibility has not been independently established.

The attack occurred in a region where territorial disputes and concerns about unauthorized mining have complicated relations between Sudan and Egypt. Both countries claim the resource-rich Halaib Triangle, and Egyptian authorities have previously conducted operations targeting illegal mining and smuggling near the border.

In June, Egypt announced a security operation against unauthorized mining within territory it considers Egyptian. Authorities reported detaining hundreds of people, including foreign nationals. Rights organizations have also alleged that earlier airstrikes killed miners in the same mountainous region.

Gold mining has become increasingly important to Sudan's economy during the civil war between the country's military and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, which began in April 2023. The conflict has displaced millions, disrupted traditional employment, and increased dependence on informal mining operations.

The latest deaths raise questions about civilian protection in disputed border regions, where miners may face dangers from military operations as well as the hazardous conditions associated with informal extraction.
