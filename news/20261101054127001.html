U.S. warns Russia of credible mass-casualty threat in Moscow

FSB confirms receiving intelligence as museum complex is closed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 1, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The U.S. Embassy in Moscow warned of a credible threat of a mass-casualty event at the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve, while Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) confirmed that it had received information from the United States about preparations for a possible terrorist attack in Moscow.

The embassy's security alert identified the threat as occurring on October 31 at Tsaritsyno, a palace museum and park complex in southern Moscow. It advised people to avoid the site, remain alert and stay away from crowds. The embassy also said the U.S. government was coordinating with appropriate Russian authorities on a response.

The FSB separately said it had received information from U.S. partners through established channels concerning preparations for a terrorist attack in Moscow and that Russian authorities were taking appropriate measures. Neither the embassy nor the FSB publicly identified a suspected group, individual or motive behind the threat.

The Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve was temporarily closed and access to its park restricted following the warning, according to Russian state media citing law enforcement agencies. Additional local reporting said an event at the complex was evacuated. No attack had been reported as of the latest available reporting.

The warning recalls an earlier exchange of intelligence between the two countries. In March 2024, the U.S. Embassy warned that extremists had imminent plans for an attack in Moscow. Days later, gunmen attacked the Crocus City Hall concert venue outside Moscow, killing more than 140 people.

The latest warning shows that U.S. and Russian authorities continue to exchange at least some terrorism-related threat information despite broader tensions between the two governments.
