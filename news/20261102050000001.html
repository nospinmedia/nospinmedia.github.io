FBI blames North Korea for record $6.3 billion Binance hack

Withdrawals resume as billions flow out of exchange

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 2, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The FBI has formally blamed North Korea for the theft of more than $6 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance, identifying the state-linked cyber activity as TraderTraitor and warning that hackers are already working to launder the stolen assets.

About $6.3 billion in cryptocurrency was stolen from Binance on October 29, making the breach the largest cryptocurrency heist on record. Blockchain researchers said attackers compromised Binance's hot-wallet infrastructure across multiple networks and moved the assets within roughly 20 minutes. Security researchers have also identified abuse involving Ethereum's EIP-7702 delegation mechanism as part of the attack.

The FBI, working with the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, said TraderTraitor actors were converting stolen assets primarily into Ethereum and were expected to use decentralized finance and instant-swap services for additional laundering. Federal investigators published cryptocurrency addresses connected to the theft and urged exchanges, blockchain firms and other virtual-asset businesses to block transactions involving them.

Industry partners moved quickly to freeze assets that could be controlled by their issuers, with roughly $2.7 billion to $3 billion reportedly immobilized. Other cryptocurrency remains outside that type of control, leaving billions of dollars in stolen assets still accessible to the attackers.

Binance resumed customer withdrawals in phases on November 1 after temporarily suspending them following the breach. By Sunday afternoon, the exchange said it had processed approximately $4.6 billion in withdrawals and $1 billion in deposits, producing a net outflow of about $3.6 billion. Binance reported approximately $171 billion in assets.

The exchange said it had replenished about 90% of the stolen amount and pledged to cover the remaining losses through its user-protection reserve, futures insurance fund and corporate treasury, leaving customers financially unaffected.
