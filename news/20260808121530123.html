Senate Confirms Todd Blanche as Attorney General in 50–49 Vote

President Trump’s former defense lawyer wins pre-dawn approval

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 8, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The U.S. Senate early Saturday, August 8, 2026, confirmed Todd Blanche as U.S. Attorney General in a 50–49 vote, elevating President Trump’s onetime personal defense lawyer to permanent control of the Department of Justice. The narrow, largely party-line outcome ends weeks of uncertainty and places a close Trump ally atop federal law enforcement.

The tally hinged on Sen. Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, who announced his support on Friday, while Republicans Susan Collins of Maine and Lisa Murkowski of Alaska joined all Democrats in opposition. Blanche had been serving as acting attorney general since April, following the removal of former Attorney General Pam Bondi. With this vote, he becomes the second confirmed attorney general of Trump’s second term.

Blanche’s confirmation followed commitments that addressed key Republican concerns. Under pressure from Sen. John Cornyn of Texas and Sen. Thom Tillis of North Carolina, he formally rescinded a proposed $1.8 billion “Anti-Weaponization Fund” tied to a settlement that had sparked bipartisan criticism. Lawmakers also scrutinized provisions related to tax-audit immunity for the president and family members; Blanche and the department signaled limits and changes amid that pushback. Supporters argued his leadership would stabilize the department; critics warned that installing the president’s former defense attorney could test perceptions of DOJ independence.

Before joining the administration, Blanche represented Trump in multiple high-profile cases, experience that supporters say equips him to manage politically fraught decisions and that opponents say raises conflict-of-interest concerns. As attorney general, Blanche will set nationwide charging priorities, oversee federal prosecutions and civil enforcement, and steer department policy at a time of intense partisan scrutiny of the Justice Department’s work.
