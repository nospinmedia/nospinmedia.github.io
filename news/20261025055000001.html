New York Archdiocese agrees to $800 million abuse settlement

About 1,300 survivors would share second-largest U.S. Catholic payout

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 25, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The Archdiocese of New York and attorneys representing approximately 1,300 survivors of clergy sexual abuse have agreed to an $800 million settlement, potentially ending more than seven years of litigation and creating what would be the second-largest Catholic archdiocese sexual-abuse settlement in U.S. history.

The agreement was announced Saturday and still requires formal signatures, which are expected during the week of November 6. It follows a framework reached in May 2026 that required approval from all participating claimants before a final agreement could move forward. The claims involve people who alleged they were sexually abused by clergy over a period spanning decades.

If finalized, the settlement would trail only the $880 million agreement reached by the Archdiocese of Los Angeles with about 1,000 survivors in 2024. Other major recent Catholic settlements include a $395 million agreement involving the Archdiocese of San Francisco and a settlement of at least $230 million involving the Archdiocese of New Orleans.

The New York cases were made possible in part by the state's 2019 Child Victims Act, which temporarily opened a window allowing survivors to bring lawsuits over childhood sexual abuse even when the normal statute of limitations had expired. The law led to thousands of claims against individuals and institutions across New York.

New York Archbishop Ronald Hicks said the agreement could provide a measure of healing and reconciliation for survivors while allowing the archdiocese to turn its attention back to its religious and community work. Attorneys representing survivors described the agreement as an important step toward accountability.

The agreement would resolve the participating survivors' claims against the archdiocese, but it does not erase the underlying allegations or the decades-long impact described by survivors. Finalization of the settlement remains dependent on completion of the formal agreement.
