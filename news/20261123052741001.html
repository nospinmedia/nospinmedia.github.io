Trump hosts third meme coin gala as $TRUMP falls below $1

Investor turnout and token holdings shrink sharply from 2025 event

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 23, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

President Trump hosted a private dinner for major holders of his $TRUMP meme coin at Trump National Golf Club in Sterling, Virginia, on November 22, as the cryptocurrency traded at a record low below $1 and the event renewed scrutiny over presidential financial conflicts and foreign participation.

The token briefly approached $75 after its January 2025 launch but had fallen about 98% from that peak to around $0.90. Organizers said 185 wallets qualified for Sunday's dinner, while the final public leaderboard listed 130 wallets holding about $8.6 million in $TRUMP. The inaugural May 2025 contest had 220 wallets representing approximately $148 million.

Trump told attendees his cryptocurrency policies were aimed at keeping the United States competitive with China. He also discussed the stalled CLARITY Act, which would establish a federal regulatory structure for digital assets, and spoke about moving more Wall Street transactions onto blockchain technology.

The event has drawn scrutiny over who can purchase access. Public Citizen found that 66 of 130 leaderboard wallets (51%) received tokens directly from exchanges that prohibit U.S. customers, including Binance, Bybit and BloFin. Among the 29 VIP wallets, 15 were funded through such exchanges. The findings indicate potential foreign participation but do not establish individual wallet owners' nationalities.

Only 29 wallets (22%), holding about $270,000, were linked exclusively to U.S.-serving exchanges such as Coinbase or Kraken. Organizers also extended registration by four days after more than 5,000 eligible wallets initially did not register.

Trump's June 2026 financial disclosure reported at least $635 million from the meme coin and $2.3 billion in Trump family earnings across four major cryptocurrency ventures through April. White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly has said neither Trump nor his family has engaged in conflicts of interest, and the White House has characterized the dinner as a private event rather than an official government function.
