Colombia Earthquake Death Toll Climbs Above 20

Rescue operations continue as government coordinates emergency response

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 10, 2026 at 11:20 AM ET

The death toll from the powerful 7.4-magnitude earthquake that struck western Colombia on August 10, 2026, has climbed above 20 people as rescue crews continue searching damaged and collapsed buildings. The latest reports differ slightly on the exact toll: El País reported at least 22 deaths, while Reuters reported at least 20 in its latest available update. Authorities are continuing to revise figures as emergency teams reach affected communities.

Pereira has suffered the largest confirmed loss of life, with Mayor Mauricio Salazar reporting 18 deaths and people trapped inside collapsed buildings. Deaths have also been reported in Manizales, where part of the city's neo-Gothic cathedral was damaged. In Quibdó, Chocó Governor Nubia Carolina Córdoba-Curi reported injuries and significant structural damage near the earthquake's epicentral region.

The quake was centered near San José del Palmar in Chocó and was felt across much of Colombia and in neighboring countries. The U.S. Geological Survey measured the earthquake at magnitude 7.4 and placed the epicenter just south of San José del Palmar. Buildings were damaged or collapsed in cities including Cali, Pereira, Quibdó and Manizales, while authorities temporarily suspended flights at airports in several western Colombian cities so facilities could be inspected for structural damage. No tsunami threat was issued.

President Abelardo de la Espriella ordered the creation of a Unified Command Post to coordinate damage assessments, rescue operations and assistance for affected communities. He also announced plans to travel to the disaster zone. Colombia's disaster agency received reports from multiple departments as local authorities continued assessing buildings, roads and other infrastructure.
