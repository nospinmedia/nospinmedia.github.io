France Begins Nationwide Ban on Unsolicited Sales Calls

New opt-in law requires consent before telemarketing begins

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 10:36 AM ET

France began enforcing a nationwide ban on unsolicited commercial telemarketing on August 11, 2026, replacing its previous opt-out system with a strict opt-in requirement. Under the new rules, businesses may contact consumers with sales calls only after obtaining explicit consent, a move the French government says is intended to reduce nuisance calls and better protect consumers from fraud.

The law applies to both landlines and mobile phones. Limited exceptions allow businesses to contact existing customers about products or services related to an active contract, or when a consumer has specifically agreed to receive marketing calls. Companies must now maintain verifiable records showing that consent was granted before making telemarketing calls.

Violations carry significant financial penalties. Individuals can face fines of up to €75,000 per illegal call, while companies can be fined as much as €375,000 per violation. Consumer protection authorities are responsible for enforcing the new requirements, which replace France's previous Bloctel do-not-call registry.

Supporters say the law will sharply reduce unwanted calls and make it more difficult for fraudulent telemarketing operations to target consumers. Industry groups, however, expect the rules to reshape the telemarketing business, including call centers outside France that primarily serve French customers. Officials in Morocco, where many French-language call centers operate, have warned that tens of thousands of jobs could be affected as companies adjust to the new requirements.

France joins a small group of countries that require consumers to opt in before receiving most marketing calls, marking one of Europe's strongest restrictions on unsolicited telemarketing.
