Jim Jordan elected to lead House Republicans after midterm losses

Ohio lawmaker defeats Steve Scalise 106-73 as GOP prepares for minority

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 11, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

House Republicans have elected Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio as their leader for the next Congress, choosing a new direction after the party lost control of the House in the 2026 midterm elections. Jordan defeated current House Majority Leader Steve Scalise of Louisiana by a 106-73 vote in a closed-door secret ballot Tuesday.

Jordan will become House minority leader when the 120th Congress begins in January. He succeeds Speaker Mike Johnson, who announced after the election that he would not seek a Republican leadership position once Democrats take control of the chamber. Jordan, 62, currently chairs the House Judiciary Committee and was a co-founder and the first chairman of the conservative House Freedom Caucus.

The election puts Jordan in charge of a substantially smaller Republican conference and makes him the party's principal House counterpart to the incoming Democratic majority. Jordan has said Republicans will focus on presenting their policy alternatives while also responding to expected Democratic oversight and investigations of President Trump and his administration. He will also be responsible for helping Republicans attempt to regain the House majority in the 2028 elections.

Republicans selected several other leaders during Tuesday's elections. Rep. Tom Emmer of Minnesota won the race to become minority whip after defeating Rep. August Pfluger of Texas, while Rep. Lisa McClain of Michigan retained her position as Republican Conference chair. Speaker Johnson congratulated the incoming leadership team after the votes.

The leadership changes extend beyond the House. Senate Republicans reelected Sen. John Thune of South Dakota as their leader. He is set to become Senate minority leader in January as Democrats take control of that chamber. House Democrats are expected to elevate current Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York to speaker when the new Congress convenes.
