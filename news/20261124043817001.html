U.S. citizen deported to Mexico after authorities rejected citizenship claim

Justice Department now acknowledges 25-year-old was American

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 24, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The Justice Department has formally acknowledged that Brian José Morales García, a 25-year-old U.S. citizen born in Denver, was deported to Mexico after authorities rejected his repeated claims that he was an American, according to federal court filings and multiple reports.

Morales García's case began on April 3, 2026, when he was riding with coworkers to an air-conditioning job near Fredericksburg, Texas. A Texas Department of Public Safety trooper stopped their pickup truck. Morales García did not have identification with him but said he was born in Denver and had a U.S. birth certificate and Social Security card at his home in Austin.

Immigration authorities were contacted, and Morales García was taken into custody. He has said agents accused him of lying about his citizenship and pressured him after he continued insisting he was American. His lawsuit alleges that family and friends attempted to provide documentation establishing his citizenship but authorities did not accept it before he was sent to Mexico on April 7.

At the time, U.S. Customs and Border Protection publicly disputed his account. The agency said record checks showed he was illegally in the country and said Morales García had acknowledged entering the United States illegally. Morales García later said he made that statement after agents accused him of lying and warned that he could go to prison.

His attorneys subsequently provided additional evidence, including a certified Colorado birth record, hospital records and witness affidavits. After approximately four months in Mexico, the federal government reached an agreement allowing Morales García to return to the United States on August 30 while his lawsuit continued.

The government's position on his citizenship has now changed unequivocally. In a motion filed in federal court, U.S. Attorney Justin R. Simmons acknowledged that Morales García is a dual citizen of the United States and Mexico. The filing marks the first clear acknowledgement by the administration that the man it removed from the country was an American citizen.

Morales García is suing Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the Texas Department of Public Safety and the Gillespie County Sheriff's Office, seeking damages over his detention and removal. The federal government is asking U.S. District Judge Robert Pitman to dismiss the case, arguing in part that the government has immunity involving immigration enforcement. The judge has not ruled on that request.

The administration also disputes Morales García's characterization of his removal, arguing that he voluntarily went to Mexico. His attorneys reject that characterization and are seeking to hold both agencies and individual officials accountable.

The case is not the first time federal immigration enforcement has ensnared people whose records indicated possible U.S. citizenship. A Government Accountability Office investigation examining federal data from fiscal year 2015 through March 2020 found that ICE arrested 674, detained 121 and removed 70 people whose agency records indicated potential U.S. citizenship. GAO stressed that limitations in federal recordkeeping prevented those figures from serving as a definitive count of citizens wrongly detained or removed.

The GAO also found that ICE issued immigration detainers for at least 895 potential U.S. citizens during the period and later canceled roughly 74% of those detainers. Administrative immigration charges used to remove people who lack legal status do not apply to U.S. citizens.

Morales García's case therefore raises a broader question about the safeguards used when someone targeted by immigration enforcement claims U.S. citizenship. In this case, authorities removed him four days after the traffic stop, while the federal government would later acknowledge in court that the citizenship claim he had made from the beginning was correct.
