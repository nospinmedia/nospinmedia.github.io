Lettuce-linked Cyclospora outbreak expands to 20 states

More than 11,400 illnesses now tied to recalled produce

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 28, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Federal health officials have expanded the nation's largest recorded Cyclospora outbreak to 20 states, with Georgia, Tennessee, and Texas newly added to the investigation. The Food and Drug Administration said 11,458 illnesses are now linked to recalled iceberg lettuce associated with Taylor Farms de Mexico, up from about 10,900 cases reported the previous week. Two deaths connected to the outbreak have been reported in Michigan.

The investigation centers on iceberg lettuce grown at a farm in central Mexico operated by Taylor Farms de Mexico, part of major U.S. produce supplier Taylor Farms. Federal and state health officials traced illnesses to lettuce distributed through restaurants, food-service operations and retail products. Taylor Farms began recalling potentially affected iceberg lettuce in July 2026, including products sold under several brands and distributed across multiple states.

The FDA, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, state health departments and Mexican authorities are continuing inspections and testing at the farm. The FDA says it remains confident that all recalled iceberg lettuce connected to this specific outbreak is now off the market. New cases can continue to appear in official totals even after contaminated food has disappeared from stores because diagnosing and reporting cyclosporiasis can take weeks.

Cyclospora cayetanensis is a microscopic parasite that infects the intestines and commonly causes prolonged watery diarrhea, stomach cramps, nausea, fatigue and loss of appetite. The parasite is generally spread when food or water becomes contaminated with fecal material and cannot be reliably removed from produce simply by washing. Federal surveillance has recorded an unusually large number of Cyclospora infections nationwide during 2026, although not all of those illnesses have been tied to the Taylor Farms lettuce outbreak.

The outbreak has also renewed scrutiny of federal oversight of imported produce. Reporting by CBS News found that the FDA had not inspected the Taylor Farms de Mexico facility for about seven years before the current investigation, although gaps in foreign food inspections have persisted across multiple administrations.
