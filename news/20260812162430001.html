10-State AG Coalition Sues To Block OCC Escrow-Interest Preemption

States say May 2026 rule violates Dodd-Frank and costs homeowners interest

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 13, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

A coalition of 10 state attorneys general filed suit on August 11, 2026, seeking to block a federal banking rule that they say strips states of authority to require banks to pay interest on homeowners’ mortgage escrow accounts. Rhode Island Attorney General Peter F. Neronha joined counterparts led by Oregon in challenging the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), a regulator within President Trump’s Treasury Department. The case, titled Oregon et al. v. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, argues the OCC exceeded its authority and violated the Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act.

The lawsuit targets OCC rules finalized in May 2026 that (1) codify national banks’ discretion over mortgage escrow account terms and (2) issue a formal preemption determination declaring that federal law supersedes state interest‑on‑escrow requirements, including New York General Obligations Law § 5‑601 and Rhode Island General Laws § 19‑9‑2(a). States joining the complaint are: California, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, and Vermont. Officials say the rule lets large national banks keep interest that, under many state laws, must be credited back to borrowers.

State attorneys general contend the OCC’s action conflicts with recent court rulings and statutory limits on preemption under the National Bank Act and Dodd‑Frank. They point to a First Circuit decision in 2025—Conti v. Citizens Bank, N.A.—which allowed claims under Rhode Island’s interest‑on‑escrow law to proceed, and contrast it with a Second Circuit ruling in May 2026 holding the NBA preempts New York’s escrow‑interest mandate following the Supreme Court’s remand in Cantero v. Bank of America, N.A.*** The coalition says the court split underscores why a regulator should not short‑circuit case‑by‑case analysis Congress required.

What it means for homeowners: in states with escrow‑interest laws, national‑bank mortgages could stop earning mandated interest unless the rule is vacated, while state‑chartered lenders would still have to pay—an asymmetry state officials argue disadvantages smaller local institutions during a housing affordability crunch.
