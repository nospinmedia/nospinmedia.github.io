Medicare for All support reaches record 66%

KFF poll finds backing up 13 points since 2020

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 7, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Support for Medicare for All has reached its highest level since KFF began polling on the proposal in 2016, with 66% of U.S. adults saying they favor a national health plan in which all Americans receive insurance through a single government program. 34% oppose the idea.

The KFF Health Tracking Poll, conducted September 16–21, 2026, surveyed 1,328 U.S. adults and has a margin of sampling error of plus or minus 3 percentage points. Support has risen substantially since October 2020, when 53% favored Medicare for All. The increase extends across political groups: 84% of Democrats and 72% of independents now favor the proposal, while 34% of Republicans support it. Republican opposition remains substantial at 66%, including 48% who strongly oppose it.

The results also show how much public opinion depends on how the proposal is described. When respondents were told Medicare for All could result in delays for medical tests and treatments, only 24% favored it. Support fell to 35% when respondents heard it could increase taxes and to 38% when told it could threaten the existing Medicare program. Conversely, support rose to 76% when respondents were told the system could guarantee health insurance as a right and to 75% when told it could eliminate premiums and cost-sharing.

Replacing private insurance remains a significant dividing point. When respondents were told Medicare for All would eliminate private health insurance companies, 52% still favored the proposal and 47% opposed it. KFF also found that many privately insured Americans misunderstand how most Medicare for All proposals would work: 57% believe they would probably be able to keep their existing private coverage, although major single-payer proposals would replace most private health insurance.

The poll comes as health care costs have become a prominent issue ahead of the 2026 midterm elections, with progressive candidates in several races campaigning on Medicare for All.
