Passengers stop pilot from crashing Israel-bound jet

Flydubai plane plunged nearly 14,000 feet before safe landing

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 1, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Passengers and crew aboard a Flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv helped stop a co-pilot who stabbed the captain and attempted to crash the aircraft on September 30, according to Israeli officials. The jet plunged nearly 14,000 feet in less than 30 seconds before those aboard regained control.

The wounded captain, identified in subsequent reporting as Smit Machchhar, an Indian national, managed to release the cockpit door, allowing passengers and crew to enter and overpower the co-pilot. Two additional Flydubai pilots traveling aboard the aircraft then took control and landed the Boeing 737 safely at Tabuk, Saudi Arabia. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said 174 people were aboard flight FZ1073, most of them Israeli nationals.

The captain and co-pilot were hospitalized in Saudi Arabia after the landing. An Israeli official identified the co-pilot as an Omani national, but his motive remained under investigation. Flydubai said the underlying reasons for the incident were unknown and cautioned against premature speculation. Saudi authorities detained the co-pilot, according to Israeli officials.

The UAE General Civil Aviation Authority confirmed it is investigating the incident, including its operational and security aspects, in coordination with relevant authorities. Subsequent reporting and images showed damage to the aircraft's rudder after landing, although authorities have not established publicly when or how that damage occurred. Flydubai also suspended flights to and from Israel while the investigation continues.

The sudden descent initially raised fears of a hijacking and prompted Israel to scramble fighter jets. Passengers later continued to Israel aboard a replacement Flydubai aircraft, arriving at Ben Gurion Airport more than 10 hours after the incident began. Israel's emergency services reported treating passengers for minor injuries after their return.
