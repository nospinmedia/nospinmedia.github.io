Supreme Court allows White House ballroom project to proceed

5-4 order lifts construction injunction while lawsuit continues

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 1, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court issued a 5-4 emergency order allowing the Trump administration to resume construction of a planned 90,000-square-foot ballroom at the White House, temporarily lifting lower court injunctions that had halted the project. The unsigned order concluded that the administration was likely to succeed in arguing that the National Trust for Historic Preservation lacked legal standing to challenge the project, allowing work to continue while the underlying lawsuit proceeds through the courts.

The ballroom project, estimated to cost approximately $400 million, is planned as an expansion near the East Wing of the White House. Administration officials argued that delaying construction would interfere with broader modernization plans and government operations. The legal dispute stems from claims that permanent structural changes to federal property require additional congressional authorization, an argument that had persuaded lower courts to block construction earlier this year.

Chief Justice John Roberts dissented, joined by Justices Sonia Sotomayor, Elena Kagan, and Ketanji Brown Jackson. Roberts wrote that the preservation group had demonstrated sufficient grounds to challenge the project and argued that allowing construction to move forward before resolving the legal questions could undermine congressional authority over federal construction projects. The majority did not decide the ultimate legality of the ballroom itself, addressing only whether the injunction should remain in place while litigation continues.

The Supreme Court's decision permits above-ground construction to resume immediately but does not resolve the underlying constitutional and statutory issues raised in the lawsuit. Those questions will continue to be litigated in lower courts, where judges will consider whether the executive branch had the legal authority to undertake the project without additional approval from Congress.
