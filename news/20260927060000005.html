House Democrats map out Trump investigations if they win majority

Impeachment remains an option as committees prepare oversight plans

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 27, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

House Democrats are preparing a broad slate of investigations into President Donald Trump, his family and his administration if they win control of the House in November's midterm elections.

Winning the majority would give Democrats control of House committees, larger investigative staffs and subpoena authority. Lawmakers already serving as the top Democrats on several committees are identifying areas they intend to investigate if they become committee chairs.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Democrats have not ruled impeachment in or out. While another impeachment of Trump remains possible, lawmakers could also pursue impeachment proceedings against Cabinet officials depending on what investigations uncover.

Representative Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the House Oversight and Government Reform Committee, has identified five investigative priorities. They include Trump family business dealings, the administration's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein investigation, ICE operations, the Department of Health and Human Services under Robert F. Kennedy Jr., and broader allegations involving corporate conduct.

Representative Jamie Raskin, who could chair the Judiciary Committee, said Democrats would focus on what he described as systemic corruption and areas where Congress could respond through legislation.

Foreign Affairs Committee ranking Democrat Gregory Meeks said his committee would examine Trump's international dealings, including decisions surrounding the Iran war and U.S. operations involving Venezuela.

Natural Resources Committee ranking Democrat Jared Huffman has identified another set of potential investigations, including the White House ballroom project, Trump's proposed Washington arch and the Trump family's involvement in mineral deals.

The plans remain contingent on the outcome of the November 2026 midterm elections. Republicans currently control the House, meaning Democrats do not have the committee authority they would need to carry out the full oversight agenda described by lawmakers.
