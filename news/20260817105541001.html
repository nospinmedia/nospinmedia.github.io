Binance Gave Client Data To Russian Investigators, Files Show

Records reportedly used in prosecution over Ukraine donations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance provided Russian investigators with customer identification records and transaction data that were later used in a terrorism-financing case against Yuri Belenkiy, a Russian IT specialist accused of donating cryptocurrency to Ukraine, according to documents reviewed by Reuters. The disclosures have drawn attention because Binance announced it had exited the Russian market in 2023 following international sanctions.

According to the records, Russia's Investigative Committee requested information on users who transferred cryptocurrency to wallets promoted by exiled Kremlin critic Arkady Babchenko. The documents indicate Binance supplied Belenkiy's passport information, Bulgarian residency records, home address, and transaction history showing transfers totaling more than $700 between January 2023 and March 2024. Binance said it complies with lawful requests from law enforcement agencies in jurisdictions where it operates.

Privacy advocates and legal experts said the disclosures raise questions about cross-border data protection because Belenkiy was living in Bulgaria when the transactions occurred, potentially bringing the case under the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). They also questioned whether appropriate international legal procedures were followed before the information was shared with Russian authorities.

The report is likely to increase scrutiny of how cryptocurrency exchanges handle government requests for customer information, particularly in politically sensitive investigations involving international sanctions and the war in Ukraine. No regulatory action related to the newly disclosed records has been announced.
