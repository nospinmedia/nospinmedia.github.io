U.S. floats NATO membership in exchange for Ukraine leaving Donbas

Zelenskyy says proposal emerged during Abu Dhabi peace talks

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 15, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says U.S. negotiators raised a potentially significant new idea during peace discussions: whether Ukraine would withdraw its forces from the Donbas if it were admitted to NATO.

Zelenskyy gave unusually direct details about the discussion, saying: "The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?'" He said the exchange led him to believe that "NATO is back on the agenda," although he stressed that no agreement had been reached and described reconciling the positions of Ukraine and Russia as "an extremely difficult conversation."

The question arose during recent U.S.-brokered negotiations in Abu Dhabi involving American, Ukrainian and Russian officials. U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner have been leading Washington's efforts to negotiate an end to the war. Zelenskyy characterized the Donbas-for-NATO concept as something Washington raised for discussion rather than an agreed peace proposal, and Ukraine has not accepted it.

The idea would connect two of the most difficult issues in negotiations. Russia wants control of the entire Donbas and has long opposed Ukrainian NATO membership. Russian forces control much of the region, but Ukraine continues to hold territory there and has repeatedly rejected demands that it simply surrender land it still controls. Zelenskyy also emphasized that any potential arrangement would not include Ukraine formally recognizing Russian sovereignty over occupied Ukrainian territory.

Zelenskyy has said a comprehensive peace agreement could ultimately be presented to Ukrainians in a national referendum if it included sufficient security guarantees, financing for a strong Ukrainian military and reconstruction assistance. That leaves open the possibility of voters deciding on a broader settlement without indicating that Kyiv has accepted the specific Donbas-for-NATO concept.

The Abu Dhabi discussions ended without resolving the territorial dispute, although the parties reached an agreement in principle on a prisoner exchange involving more than 1,000 people.

Jared Kushner said Saturday that "Both sides understand that practical compromises will be necessary to end this war" and said Washington is working to facilitate direct engagement between Russia and European countries. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov separately said Moscow is open to European representatives participating in future negotiations.

The NATO discussion therefore represents a potentially important change in the diplomatic conversation, but remains preliminary: Washington raised the question, Ukraine has not agreed to withdraw from Donbas, and Russia has not agreed to accept Ukraine as a NATO member.
