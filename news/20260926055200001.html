Taxpayer-funded ads promote Trump on national television

Lawmakers question whether campaign violates federal spending rules

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 26, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

A new television advertising campaign promoting President Trump and his administration is being produced and aired with U.S. taxpayer money, prompting lawmakers to question whether federal funds are being used for prohibited government propaganda. The ads began appearing this week on national cable and broadcast television, including Fox News Channel and CBS.

The first ad opens with Trump declaring that the United States will defeat "communism, socialism and Marxism" and uses a montage of images from his second term alongside messages promoting administration policies. It features the song Love Me by artist JMSN and ends with UFC CEO Dana White praising Trump. An on-screen disclosure states that the advertisement was "Paid for by the U.S. Government." CBS News reported that the government produced the content and purchased airtime through an advertising agency. Multiple versions of the campaign, including 30-second and 60-second spots, have been produced.

The White House says the commercials are public service announcements rather than political advertisements. A spokesperson described them as educational and patriotic, while another White House official noted that Trump is not on the 2026 ballot and that the advertisements contain no electoral call to action. Campaign finance experts interviewed by CBS News said the commercial does not appear to violate election law because it does not explicitly support a candidate for public office, although separate federal restrictions on government propaganda could raise legal questions.

Democratic Sen. Maggie Hassan of New Hampshire asked White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles to disclose the campaign's cost, funding source and contractors. Democratic congressional appropriators, including Sen. Patty Murray and Rep. Rosa DeLauro, separately demanded that the advertisements be withdrawn, alleging that taxpayer funding was being improperly used for political promotion. Those claims have not been adjudicated by a court.

JMSN also said publicly that he did not authorize his music to be used for a political agenda or campaign.
