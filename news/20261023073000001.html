Polling group says its 2026 election surveys were completely fabricated

Fake numbers spread online and were cited in coverage of several primary races

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 23, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

A polling organization that published surveys of 2026 elections has acknowledged that its results were fabricated, raising questions about how easily unverified polling can enter political coverage and influence perceptions of competitive races.

The organization, Median Strategies, presented itself as a polling firm and released results from multiple state and local contests. It later announced that the numbers were invented as part of what it described as a "social experiment" examining how polling information spreads through political media and social networks.

The fake polls appeared in polling aggregators, social media discussions and some political coverage before the deception was disclosed. Politico reported that the numbers may also have affected perceptions of several races by making candidates appear stronger or weaker than they actually were. There is no evidence, however, establishing that the fabricated polls changed the outcome of an election or directly caused voters to choose one candidate over another.

PBS NewsHour reported that Median Strategies published false polling in Wisconsin, where polling generally struggled to anticipate the Democratic gubernatorial primary. Democratic Socialist Francesca Hong had appeared ahead in some polling but ultimately lost to David Crowley. The race was unusually volatile, with candidates dropping out and one later reentering, while some legitimate surveys showed as many as 30% to 40% of voters undecided shortly before the election.

Polling experts caution that the broader problems seen during the 2026 primary season cannot be blamed on the fake organization. Courtney Kennedy, senior vice president of U.S. affairs at Ipsos, told PBS that primary polling is particularly difficult and can have roughly three times as much error as rigorous national polling. Low-cost methods such as surveys conducted through text-message links can also produce samples that do not accurately represent the electorate.

PBS said neither it nor other national news organizations used Median Strategies' data. The episode nevertheless demonstrates how professional-looking polling numbers can circulate without the transparency expected from established polling organizations.

Experts recommend checking whether a pollster identifies its researchers, explains its sampling and weighting methods, publishes its questions and has an established track record before treating its results as reliable.
