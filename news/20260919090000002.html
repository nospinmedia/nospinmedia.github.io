Cuba's power grid collapses again

Millions lose electricity in sixth major outage since January

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 19, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Cuba's national power grid collapsed again on Friday, September 18, leaving millions of people without electricity as the island struggles with severe fuel shortages and aging electrical infrastructure. It was at least the sixth major blackout since January, continuing an energy crisis that has disrupted daily life across the country.

Cuba's Ministry of Energy and Mines said a failure in transmission lines in the western part of the country caused that section of the grid to collapse as far as Matanzas province, after which the eastern section became disconnected. Cuba's state newspaper Granma reported that unstable weather further complicated conditions on the electrical system. Officials began restoration procedures and worked to establish small power-distribution systems in several provinces.

By Friday evening, electricity had been restored to only about 5% of customers in Havana, where roughly 862,000 customers are served by the grid. Authorities were creating smaller power 'micro-islands' in Camagüey, Pinar del Río and Granma, while systems supplying hospitals and other emergency facilities were among those being reactivated.

Cuba's electrical problems have intensified during a severe economic and energy crisis. U.S. restrictions imposed in January have sharply limited fuel supplies reaching the island, while shortages of replacement parts have made maintaining Cuba's aging thermoelectric plants increasingly difficult. According to reporting from The Associated Press, only one tanker carrying fuel has reached Cuba since the restrictions began, arriving from Russia in March.

Even when the national grid remains operating, residents in some areas regularly experience outages lasting more than 30 consecutive hours. The disruptions affect transportation, workplaces and flights while also interfering with cooking, water supplies, internet access and telephone service. Cuba's two previous nationwide blackouts occurred in early August, following major outages in July and March.
