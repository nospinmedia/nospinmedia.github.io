Pentagon releases 71 new UFO files

Secret program studied warp drives, wormholes and unexplained encounters

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 19, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

The Pentagon released 71 files related to unidentified anomalous phenomena, commonly called UAPs or UFOs, on September 18, marking the government's sixth release of records this year. The collection includes 55 PDF documents, 15 videos and one audio file, with records dating from 1952 through 2025. Most of the newly released files contain at least some redactions.

Among the most significant records are documents from the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program, or AAWSAP, a Defense Intelligence Agency initiative established in 2008 to examine potential aerospace threats and technologies decades into the future. The program ended in 2012. The newly released material includes studies examining theoretical concepts such as warp drives, wormholes, antigravity and invisibility cloaking. Their inclusion does not establish that such technologies exist or that the government successfully developed them.

One 2010 study examined reported medical effects involving people who researchers said had encountered unusual aerospace phenomena. Other files include military videos showing objects that investigators had not identified at the time the recordings were made. The release also contains a 1952 audio recording involving Air Force officer Edward Ruppelt, who led the early government effort known as Project Blue Book.

The files are part of a broader federal effort to release government records concerning UAPs. Earlier batches were published in May, June, July and August, bringing together material from the military, intelligence agencies, law enforcement and other government sources. The government's UAP database allows the public to review released documents, images, video and audio records.

The Pentagon has emphasized that unidentified does not mean extraterrestrial. In many cases, an object remains unidentified because investigators lack enough information to determine what it was, and the release of a document does not validate every theory or conclusion contained within it.
