Ukraine launches deepest drone strike of war into Russia

More than 3,000-km raid targets gas plants in Arctic energy heartland

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 10, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Ukraine's Special Operations Forces say long-range drones struck two major Russian gas-condensate facilities on September 9, reaching the Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug more than 3,000 kilometers from Ukraine. Ukrainian officials described the operation as their deepest strike inside Russia since the full-scale invasion began in 2022.

The targets were identified as the Novy Urengoy Gas Condensate Treatment Plant and the Purovsky Gas Condensate Processing Plant, both located in northwestern Siberia. Ukraine's military said its drones traveled more than 3,000 kilometers to reach the facilities, while Ukrainian drone manufacturer Fire Point said FP-1 drones involved in the operation flew more than 3,200 kilometers, or about 1,988 miles.

Russian authorities independently confirmed a drone incident at an industrial facility in Novy Urengoy. Regional governor Dmitry Artyukhov said falling drone debris caused a fire after the attack was repelled and reported no deaths or injuries. Russian presidential envoy Artem Zhoga also confirmed an attempted strike on a fuel and energy facility in the region. Russia had not independently confirmed damage to both facilities reported by Ukraine.

The location makes the attack significant. Yamalo-Nenets is the center of Russia's natural-gas industry and accounts for roughly 80% of the country's gas production. Ukrainian Special Operations Forces said the two plants process large volumes of gas condensate from fields across the Yamal region and contribute revenue to the Russian economy.

Ukraine said the Novy Urengoy facility has a design processing capacity of about 19.5 million metric tons annually, while the Purovsky plant processed 13.4 million metric tons of gas condensate in 2025. Kyiv has increasingly targeted Russian oil and gas infrastructure as part of its long-range campaign against facilities it says help finance Moscow's war effort.
