Daniel Kinahan Extradited From UAE, Charged In Dublin

First case under Ireland–UAE extradition treaty

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 11, 2026 at 9:31 AM ET

Daniel Kinahan, the alleged leader of the Kinahan Organized Crime Group, was extradited from the United Arab Emirates to Ireland on August 9, 2026, following a months-long legal fight over his return. The 49-year-old was flown to Casement Aerodrome near Dublin under heavy security before appearing at Dublin's Special Criminal Court, where he was formally charged with directing the activities of a criminal organization. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Prosecutors allege Kinahan directed the activities of a criminal organization between October 18, 2015, and April 6, 2017. He was remanded in custody following Sunday's court appearance and transferred to Portlaoise Prison, Ireland's maximum-security prison. His next court appearance is scheduled for October 5, 2026. Kinahan has not been convicted of the charge and will have the opportunity to contest the allegations in court. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The extradition is significant because Kinahan's case represents the first extradition carried out under the permanent Ireland–UAE extradition treaty, which became operational in May 2025. Another alleged Kinahan organization figure, Sean McGovern, was returned from the UAE to Ireland in May 2025, but his extradition was conducted under a separate one-off arrangement because his arrest preceded the treaty taking effect. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Kinahan had lived openly in Dubai for nearly a decade before UAE authorities arrested him in April 2026 on an Irish warrant. He challenged his extradition through the UAE courts before losing his final appeal in late July, clearing the way for his return to Ireland. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Irish authorities allege the Kinahan organization developed into a major transnational criminal network involved in drug trafficking, money laundering and firearms trafficking. Kinahan and other alleged members of the organization were sanctioned by the U.S. Treasury Department in 2022, and U.S. authorities offered rewards of up to $5 million for information leading to the financial disruption of the organization or the arrest or conviction of several of its alleged leaders.

Kinahan's return is also an important test of expanded law-enforcement cooperation between Ireland and the UAE. Irish authorities had pursued him for years while he remained based in Dubai, and the new extradition treaty created a formal mechanism for Ireland to seek the surrender of suspects wanted for serious crimes.
