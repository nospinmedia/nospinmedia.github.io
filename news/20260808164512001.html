Puerto Rico Starts 48-Hour Water Rationing in San Juan Area

Shutoffs hit 180,000 customer accounts as record drought deepens

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 8, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Puerto Rico began mandatory rotating water shutoffs on August 7, 2026, imposing 48-hour service interruptions affecting about 180,000 customer accounts across the San Juan metropolitan area as a severe drought pushed water supplies to critical levels. The Puerto Rico Aqueduct and Sewer Authority (PRASA) said the alternating schedule is designed to stretch dwindling reserves while tanker trucks deliver potable water to the most vulnerable locations.

The rationing applies to parts of San Juan, Carolina, Trujillo Alto, Canóvanas, Loíza, Gurabo, and Juncos. PRASA official Luis González Delgado said water deliveries are being prioritized for hospitals and residences serving older adults. The restrictions follow San Juan's driest July in more than 120 years. The U.S. Drought Monitor reports about 25% of Puerto Rico is experiencing severe drought, with another 36% in moderate drought, and officials have not announced when the rationing might end.

Meteorologists at the National Weather Service in San Juan expect rainfall over the next two to three weeks to remain at least 50% below normal as El Niño conditions persist. Officials warned that dry weather could continue into September, meaning affected residents and businesses should prepare for continued alternating shutoffs, with water pressure and restoration times varying as the distribution system refills.

Local leaders have faced growing complaints about unreliable water service that predated the current drought, and Governor Jenniffer González acknowledged long-standing infrastructure challenges as Puerto Rico manages overlapping heat, power, and water issues. Previous rationing periods, including in 2015 and 2020, showed that outage schedules can change as reservoir levels and demand fluctuate. For now, officials continue urging conservation while maintaining scheduled shutoffs and tanker deliveries to critical facilities.
