Judge allows core of Trump's $15 billion New York Times lawsuit to proceed

Seven statements survive as court narrows defamation case

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 20, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

A federal judge has allowed part of President Trump's $15 billion defamation lawsuit against The New York Times and publisher Penguin Random House to proceed, while substantially narrowing the case. U.S. District Judge Steven Merryday ruled on Thursday, November 19, that four of Trump's six counts could continue, but dismissed claims involving 27 of 34 challenged statements.

The ruling leaves seven statements at issue: six from the 2024 book _Lucky Loser: How Donald Trump Squandered His Father's Fortune and Created the Illusion of Success_ and one from an October 2024 Times article by reporter Peter Baker. The surviving statements concern allegations involving Trump's taxes or other financial crimes. The ruling allows those claims to move into further litigation but does not establish that the statements were false or that Trump was defamed.

Merryday, a George H.W. Bush appointee, found that Trump's amended complaint plausibly alleged the defendants may have deliberately avoided information relevant to the truth of accusations involving complex financial crimes. The judge specifically addressed whether failing to seek opinions from qualified tax experts could support a claim of "purposeful avoidance of the truth."

That question is important because Trump, as a public figure, must ultimately satisfy the "actual malice" standard established by _New York Times Co. v. Sullivan_, requiring proof that a defamatory statement was made with knowledge that it was false or with reckless disregard for whether it was true.

The case had previously encountered procedural problems when Merryday dismissed Trump's original 80-page complaint and required a shorter, more focused filing. Court-ordered mediation has also failed, and the defendants unsuccessfully sought to transfer the litigation to New York.

The ruling comes amid several Trump lawsuits involving news organizations. A separate judge recently allowed his $10 billion lawsuit against the BBC to proceed, while another federal judge earlier dismissed Trump's defamation lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal involving its reporting about a letter to Jeffrey Epstein.
