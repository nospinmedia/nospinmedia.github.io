Trump again says Iran campaign will be limited

Warns U.S. remains ready to strike again

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump said on September 2 that the renewed U.S. military campaign against Iran is not expected to continue "too long," while emphasizing that American forces remain prepared to launch additional strikes if necessary. His remarks came after the largest exchange of fire between Washington and Tehran since fighting resumed following the collapse of a ceasefire earlier this summer.

Speaking at the White House, Trump said recent U.S. strikes destroyed Iranian military equipment near the Strait of Hormuz, including what he described as a rocket system designed to deploy naval mines. He also said U.S. forces targeted newly rebuilt Iranian radar and missile systems during what he called a "very heavy attack," adding that the United States could strike again "any time we want" if Iran continues threatening U.S. forces or regional shipping.

The comments reflect the administration's current position that military operations are intended to remain limited rather than become an open-ended war, even as exchanges between the two countries continue. Iranian officials have disputed the U.S. description of the strikes and accused Washington of hitting civilian areas, while also reporting additional casualties from the latest attacks. At the same time, Iran has continued launching missiles and drones toward locations associated with U.S. forces in the region, contributing to heightened tensions across the Persian Gulf.

The renewed fighting follows months of intermittent military action, retaliatory strikes and diplomatic efforts that have repeatedly raised hopes for de-escalation before hostilities resumed. Although Trump again expressed confidence that the current phase of the conflict would not last much longer, he did not announce a ceasefire or an agreement with Iran. Instead, he coupled his prediction with a warning that additional military action remains an option if the administration determines it is necessary.
