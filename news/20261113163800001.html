FAA halts departures nationwide amid communications problems

Flights already airborne allowed to land as safety concerns disrupt travel

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 13, 2026 at 4:43 PM ET

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) ordered a nationwide ground stop on Friday, November 13, 2026, suspending departures from airports across the United States after serious air traffic control communications problems raised safety concerns. The emergency restriction affects airlines nationwide, with no confirmed time for normal operations to resume.

The order took effect at approximately 3:54 p.m. Eastern Time. An FAA air traffic control advisory cited extraordinary circumstances and safety considerations, directing all departing aircraft to remain on the ground until further notice. Aircraft already airborne were permitted to continue toward landing, while international flights arriving from outside the United States were to be handled individually.

The FAA had previously halted traffic in Chicago before extending restrictions nationwide. Reports indicated problems involving air traffic control communications, but officials had not established a publicly confirmed technical root cause. The interruption occurred amid a separate disruption affecting Microsoft services, although no connection between the two incidents had been confirmed.

The nationwide restriction means flights preparing to depart cannot take off while the order remains active. Passengers may experience delays, missed connections and subsequent schedule disruptions as airlines adjust their operations. Travelers should check directly with their airlines for updated departure information and rebooking options.

Nationwide aviation shutdowns are exceptionally rare. Following the suspension of civilian air traffic after the September 11, 2001 attacks, the FAA ordered another nationwide departure halt in January 2023 after a failure involving its Notice to Air Missions system. That interruption lasted less than two hours.

The latest emergency also comes as federal officials seek improvements to aging air traffic control infrastructure. Congress approved $12.5 billion for FAA communications modernization in 2025, while the FAA and White House requested an additional $30.1 billion on Friday for broader system improvements.

As of this report, the nationwide departure restriction remained in effect, and officials had not announced a confirmed restoration time.
