North Korea Launches Ballistic Missile More Than 700 Km

Test comes days before U.S.–South Korea military drills

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 10:17 AM ET

North Korea launched a ballistic missile from its eastern coast early August 12, 2026, its second ballistic missile test in less than a week as the United States and South Korea prepare for major joint military exercises. South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said the missile was fired from the Wonsan area around 6 a.m. and traveled more than 700 kilometers (435 miles) toward waters between the Korean Peninsula and Japan.

Japan's Ministry of Defense also detected the launch and said the missile landed outside Japan's exclusive economic zone. South Korea's national security officials condemned the launch as a provocation, while Japanese officials lodged a protest with North Korea through diplomatic channels. The launch followed another ballistic missile fired from the Wonsan area on August 6, continuing a series of weapons tests by Pyongyang as regional governments monitor its expanding missile and nuclear programs.

The latest test came five days before the annual Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises are scheduled to begin on August 17. The 11-day U.S.–South Korean drills will run through August 27 and are designed to strengthen readiness against evolving North Korean threats. About 18,000 South Korean troops are expected to participate alongside U.S. forces, with training addressing conventional military operations as well as threats involving drones, cyberattacks and GPS interference.

North Korea has long condemned joint U.S.–South Korean military exercises as rehearsals for an invasion, while Washington and Seoul describe them as defensive training. Pyongyang has frequently conducted missile tests or issued warnings around major allied exercises. The specific type of missile launched Wednesday had not been publicly identified as regional militaries continued analyzing the flight.
