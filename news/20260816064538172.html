Taliban Marks Five Years In Power

U.N. says 2.4 million girls remain barred from school

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 16, 2026 at 2:53 PM ET

Taliban leaders marked the fifth anniversary of their return to power in Afghanistan on August 15, 2026, holding ceremonies in Kabul that celebrated what they described as restored security and national sovereignty. At the same time, United Nations officials warned that the country's restrictions on women and girls have become increasingly entrenched, leaving Afghanistan the only nation where girls are barred from secondary and higher education.

According to the United Nations, an estimated 2.4 million Afghan girls remain excluded from secondary school, while women continue to face broad restrictions on university attendance, employment, public activities and freedom of movement. Since returning to power in 2021, Taliban authorities have issued more than 100 decrees limiting women's participation in public life. U.N. officials warn that excluding girls from education is reducing the future supply of teachers, doctors, nurses and other professionals needed to support Afghanistan's long-term development.

Taliban officials defended their policies during the anniversary events, arguing that the country has achieved greater stability after decades of conflict and that nationwide security has improved. International recognition of the Taliban government remains limited, with most countries continuing to withhold formal diplomatic recognition while urging the restoration of women's rights and broader civil liberties.

Humanitarian agencies also warn that Afghanistan continues to face severe economic hardship, widespread poverty and funding shortages that have complicated relief efforts. While large-scale armed conflict has largely subsided since the Taliban takeover, aid organizations say restrictions on women and girls continue to have far-reaching social and economic consequences for families across the country.

Despite continued international criticism, Taliban leaders have shown no indication they intend to reverse their education policies. U.N. officials say millions of Afghan women and girls remain unable to fully participate in education, employment and public life, while humanitarian organizations continue urging the international community to support the Afghan people even as diplomatic recognition of the Taliban government remains limited.
