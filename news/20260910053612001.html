Trump tells GOP voters to pretend he is on November ballot

Republicans center unusual midterm convention around president

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 10, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

President Trump is urging Republican voters to approach the November 3 midterm elections as though he were personally on the ballot, putting himself at the center of the party's effort to retain control of Congress. Speaking at the Republican Party's unusual two-day midterm convention in Dallas, Trump told supporters to "pretend I'm on the ballot" and said he plans to campaign around the country for Republican candidates.

The convention, held September 9-10 at the American Airlines Center, is unusual because national party conventions are normally associated with presidential election years rather than midterms. Republicans structured the event as a large campaign rally and fundraising gathering, with Trump scheduled to address attendees on both nights. Vice President JD Vance, Cabinet officials, members of Congress and Republican candidates are also appearing during the event.

Trump's first-night speech focused heavily on motivating Republican voters ahead of competitive House and Senate races. He discussed immigration, tariffs, the war with Iran, energy prices and his administration's record while repeatedly framing the congressional elections as a direct test of his presidency. Republican National Committee leaders have similarly described the convention as an effort to put Trump at the center of the party's midterm campaign and use his support among Republican voters to increase turnout.

The event comes as Republicans defend narrow majorities in both chambers of Congress. Trump has said he intends to travel extensively for candidates during the final weeks of the campaign, while his political organization has begun spending from a large campaign fund in competitive races, including Senate contests.

Trump also used the convention to announce a separate proposal for $5,000 payments to adult U.S. citizens if Republicans retain both chambers of Congress. That proposal is being covered separately because it raises distinct questions about cost, congressional approval and implementation.
