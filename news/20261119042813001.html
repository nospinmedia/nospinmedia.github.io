Trump, Xi and Putin begin historic three-way talks

Nuclear weapons, Ukraine and Iran top unprecedented meeting

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 19, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin began an unprecedented three-way meeting in Shenzhen, China, on Thursday, November 19, bringing the leaders of the world's three largest nuclear powers together for talks centered on nuclear arms and the wars in Ukraine and Iran.

Trump opened the meeting by saying the United States, Russia and China are uniquely positioned to discuss reducing nuclear weapons and lowering the risk they will be used. Xi went further, saying nuclear weapons "should be comprehensively banned and thoroughly destroyed" and that "nuclear war cannot be won and must not be fought." The remarks highlighted differences between China's stated goal of nuclear abolition and Trump's push for a new arms-control arrangement involving all three countries.

Nuclear arms control has taken on greater urgency since the New START treaty expired February 5, 2026, leaving the United States and Russia without legally binding limits on their deployed strategic nuclear arsenals. Putin, meanwhile, emphasized the countries' status as victorious powers in World War II and proposed measures intended to protect national sovereignty from organizations he described as "rogue globalist organisations," specifically targeting the International Criminal Court.

Before the trilateral session, Trump held separate meetings with both leaders. His meeting with Putin lasted roughly 40 minutes. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov later said Putin left the meeting "in an excellent mood" and that their Ukraine discussions would continue during the trilateral talks. Trump's separate meeting with Xi lasted about one hour and was followed by a working lunch.

Ahead of meeting Xi, Trump said the United States and China shared an interest in restoring energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz and preventing Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon, saying the two countries felt strongly about both issues. Trump also called for China's cooperation in efforts to end the war in Ukraine.

The wider APEC gathering also produced direct criticism of Russia. Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese confronted Putin over the war in Ukraine, telling him to "end your war, end it now." Ukraine and European governments are not participants in the trilateral talks despite their potential consequences for European security.
