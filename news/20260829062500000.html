Ecuador Sentences Ex-President Lenín Moreno for Bribery

Former leader gets 5 years in Sinohydro corruption case

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 29, 2026 at 5:43 PM ET

An Ecuadorian court sentenced former President Lenín Moreno to five years in prison after finding him guilty of accepting bribes connected to construction of the Coca Codo Sinclair hydroelectric project, the country's largest power plant. The court also permanently barred Moreno from holding public office and ordered those convicted to repay three times the value of the illicit benefits they received. Because Moreno uses a wheelchair due to a disability resulting from a 1998 shooting, the court ruled that he will serve his sentence under house arrest.

Prosecutors said Chinese state-owned construction company Sinohydro paid approximately $76.1 million in bribes between 2008 and 2018 to secure contracts related to the roughly $2 billion hydroelectric project. According to the prosecution, the money was funneled through shell companies and fraudulent consulting agreements, with more than $1 million ultimately benefiting Moreno and members of his family while he served as Ecuador's vice president. Moreno denied the allegations throughout the proceedings and has announced plans to appeal the conviction.

The court also convicted several other defendants. Moreno's wife, Rocío González, and daughter, Irina Moreno, each received 30-month prison sentences as accomplices, while former Chinese Ambassador to Ecuador Cai Runguo was sentenced to five years in prison. The ruling represents one of Ecuador's most significant anti-corruption decisions involving a former head of state and follows years of investigations into alleged irregularities surrounding the Coca Codo Sinclair project, which has experienced repeated structural and operational problems since entering service.

Moreno becomes the latest former Ecuadorian president convicted on corruption-related charges, following previous cases involving Rafael Correa and Jamil Mahuad. The verdict is expected to remain under appellate review, but prosecutors described the decision as an important step in holding senior public officials accountable for alleged corruption tied to major infrastructure projects.
