Trump Threatens Penalties For U.S. Tech Firms Building AI Centers Abroad

President Says Keeping Data Centers At Home Is Key To Competing With China

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 21, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

President Trump says U.S. technology companies that build artificial intelligence data centers in other countries could face a "tremendous tax", arguing that the infrastructure powering advanced AI should remain in the United States as competition with China intensifies.

Trump made the comments during a White House news conference, saying companies should build their data centers domestically rather than moving projects overseas. He did not specify the size of any penalty, which companies would be covered or what legal authority would be used to impose it. Trump also acknowledged that he had not yet discussed the proposal with his staff, leaving its potential implementation unclear.

The statement comes as technology companies are spending heavily on data centers needed to train and operate increasingly powerful AI systems. The projects require large amounts of electricity, land and water, and proposed facilities have faced opposition in some U.S. communities over utility costs and environmental effects.

Trump suggested companies could make projects more attractive to communities through measures including lower utility bills, local tax benefits, community funding or direct payments. Technology companies including Google and Meta have also emphasized economic benefits and increased transparency as they seek approval for new facilities.

The domestic data-center push intersects with broader U.S. efforts to maintain an advantage over China in artificial intelligence. Washington has already restricted China's access to some advanced AI chips and recently tightened controls intended to prevent Chinese companies from accessing high-end computing through data centers in third countries.

Trump's proposal is separate from legislation recently passed by the Senate aimed at speeding approval of energy infrastructure while requiring data centers to cover their electricity use and associated grid upgrades. That legislation still requires action in the House.

For now, Trump's threatened penalty remains a policy proposal rather than an established tax or ban, with key details about its scope and enforcement unresolved.
