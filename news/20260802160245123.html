Truth Social Unveils $100,000 Fast Feed For Trump Posts

Lawmakers question market fairness and conflicts

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 3, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Trump Media & Technology Group launched a premium data service on August 1, 2026, offering faster delivery of Truth Social posts— including updates from President Donald Trump—to paying clients. Branded as a high-speed enterprise feed, the product is priced at up to $100,000 per month and is marketed to institutional users who rely on rapid information flow. The development matters because presidential statements can move markets, and preferential speed could let subscribers react before most investors or the general public.

According to company materials, the subscription provides a low-latency stream of posts from select accounts, giving customers priority access compared with standard app notifications. TMTG portrays the offering as a legitimate commercial licensing model common in the tech and financial-data industries. The company did not announce any restrictions specific to official presidential communications, and it did not detail how much sooner subscribers receive content relative to typical users.

The rollout immediately drew scrutiny from ethics experts and lawmakers, who warned the arrangement could create conflicts of interest and raise market-integrity concerns given Trump’s financial stake in TMTG. Rep. Jamie Raskin and Rep. Ritchie Torres said they are seeking reviews of whether selling priority access to potentially market-moving presidential posts is compatible with principles of fair disclosure and existing securities rules. Regulators have not announced any action, and there is no finding that the service violates law; critics emphasize, however, that even small speed advantages can confer a trading edge when the information comes from the nation’s chief executive.

Context from recent coverage notes that Trump has used Truth Social to issue policy-related statements, and that market participants closely monitor his posts for signals on tariffs, corporate conduct, and geopolitical events. Supporters of the new feed compare it to premium market data products already sold to professional users, while critics counter that the combination of a presidential account and a paywalled speed advantage is unprecedented. For now, the key questions are how quickly paying customers receive presidential content and whether guardrails will ensure equal access to information that could move markets.
