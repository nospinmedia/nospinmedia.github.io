Treasury yields jump to highest levels since 2007

Oil tops $103 as inflation concerns return

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 24, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

U.S. government borrowing costs surged Wednesday as oil prices climbed sharply, with the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield reaching its highest level since 2007 and renewed inflation concerns weighing on stocks.

The 10-year yield climbed to about 5.1% during trading on September 23, while longer-term Treasury yields also moved higher. The selloff in bonds came as Brent crude oil jumped nearly 4% and settled above $103 a barrel. Oil prices rose following renewed tensions involving the United States and Iran, including comments from Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian at the United Nations after President Trump warned of further military action against Iran.

The combination matters because higher energy prices can feed inflation by increasing transportation, manufacturing and household costs. Expectations for persistent inflation can in turn push bond yields higher as investors demand greater returns for holding longer-term government debt. Higher Treasury yields can also influence borrowing costs throughout the economy, including rates on mortgages, business loans and other forms of credit.

Stocks fell as yields and oil moved higher. The S&P 500 declined about 0.8%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost roughly 0.7%, and the Nasdaq Composite fell about 1.1%. Energy stocks benefited from higher oil prices while most other major sectors declined.

The move reversed some of the relief seen earlier in the week, when falling oil prices and signs of possible diplomatic progress between Washington and Tehran had helped Treasury yields retreat. Markets have been particularly sensitive to energy prices because disruptions connected to the Middle East conflict have raised concerns that expensive oil could keep inflation elevated and complicate decisions by the Federal Reserve.

Bond and energy markets will now remain sensitive to developments involving Iran, oil supplies and the outlook for U.S. inflation and interest rates.
