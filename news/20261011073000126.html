India detains thousands, locks down Delhi to stop election protests

Youth movement challenges removal of 130 million names from voter rolls

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 11, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Indian authorities detained more than 2,000 protesters and imposed sweeping security restrictions across New Delhi on October 10, 2026, preventing a planned demonstration demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. Thousands of police and paramilitary personnel were deployed, dozens of metro stations and markets were closed, and mobile internet services were suspended in parts of the capital.

The demonstration was organized by the youth-led Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) and allied student groups. Protesters accuse India's Election Commission of improperly removing approximately 130 million names from voter rolls through a process known as the Special Intensive Revision (SIR). They argue the changes disproportionately affect opposition supporters and could benefit Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).

The government and election authorities reject allegations of political manipulation, maintaining that electoral roll revisions are intended to remove duplicate, deceased and otherwise ineligible voters.

Police detained CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke after he arrived at Delhi's airport. Other student activists, opposition figures and journalists were also detained as authorities attempted to prevent demonstrators from reaching the planned gathering at Jantar Mantar.

Despite extensive restrictions, smaller demonstrations occurred at several locations across the capital. Protesters gathered in scattered groups after security barriers prevented access to the main protest site.

Human rights organizations criticized the government's response. Amnesty International called for the release of people detained solely for exercising their rights to peaceful assembly and expression. The Committee to Protect Journalists also urged authorities to allow reporters to cover the demonstrations without interference.

By October 11, subsequent reporting indicated that Dipke and two associates had been released from custody. The movement nevertheless announced plans for further demonstrations, signaling that the confrontation over electoral procedures remains unresolved.

The dispute comes ahead of important state elections scheduled for 2027, raising questions about public confidence in India's electoral institutions and the government's handling of political dissent.
