U.S. Plans New Bank Sanctions Over Iran

Treasury Signals Weekly Financial Pressure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 31, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the United States plans to impose sanctions on another foreign bank this week as part of a broader effort to further isolate Iran's financial system. Speaking ahead of Group of 20 finance meetings in Asheville, North Carolina, Bessent said the administration intends to increase pressure on financial institutions that continue facilitating transactions linked to Tehran, warning that additional sanctions should be expected on a regular basis.

Bessent said the administration is prepared to use what he described as "financial violence" to restrict Iran's access to international banking networks and capital. The announcement follows a proposed action by the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) targeting the United Arab Emirates branches of Banque Misr, Egypt's second-largest bank. U.S. officials allege those branches processed transactions benefiting Iranian entities in violation of U.S. sanctions authorities, though the proposal remains subject to the federal rulemaking process.

The administration's latest actions are part of its broader campaign to reduce Iran's ability to finance military activities and evade existing sanctions through foreign financial institutions. Treasury officials said they will continue working with allies and major trading partners to strengthen enforcement while warning banks that continued dealings with sanctioned Iranian entities could jeopardize access to the U.S. financial system. The announcement comes as Washington continues responding to heightened tensions with Iran following recent military exchanges in the Strait of Hormuz.

Financial sanctions have become one of the administration's primary tools for increasing economic pressure without direct military action. Secondary sanctions allow the United States to penalize foreign banks and businesses that conduct certain transactions with sanctioned Iranian entities, potentially restricting their access to U.S. dollar clearing and American financial markets. Markets and international banks will be watching closely to see which institution is designated next and whether additional countries tighten compliance with U.S. sanctions.
