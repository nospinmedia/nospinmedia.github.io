Flávio Bolsonaro leads Lula into Brazil runoff

Right-wing challenger takes 47% as final vote looms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 5, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Brazil's presidential election will be decided in an October 25 runoff after Senator Flávio Bolsonaro finished ahead of incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva in a surprisingly strong first-round showing.

With 99.99% of ballot boxes counted, Brazil's Superior Electoral Court (TSE) reported Bolsonaro with 56,104,268 votes, or 47.03% of valid ballots. Lula received 53,876,617 votes, or 45.16%. Neither cleared the majority required to win outright. Bolsonaro, 45, is the son of former President Jair Bolsonaro and is making his first run for the presidency. Lula, 80, is seeking a fourth nonconsecutive term.

The result gives Bolsonaro momentum entering the three-week runoff campaign after he performed better than pre-election polling had suggested. Cost-of-living concerns, crime and corruption were prominent issues during the campaign. Bolsonaro has called for a tougher approach to crime and closer economic ties with the United States, while Lula has emphasized his government's social programs and defended Brazil's sovereignty amid tensions with Washington.

The candidates will now compete for voters who backed eliminated contenders. Writer Augusto Cury finished third with about 2.89% of valid votes, followed by Renan Santos at about 2.24% and Ronaldo Caiado at about 2.18%. Bolsonaro said after the results that the vote showed that Brazil wants change, while Lula said the campaign would continue into the second round.

The runoff will determine the political direction of Latin America's largest economy and could affect Brazil's relationships with the United States and China, as well as domestic policy on public security, economic management and protection of the Amazon.
