CIA chief holds rare intelligence talks in Moscow

Kremlin confirms meeting as U.S. pursues Ukraine diplomacy

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 27, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

CIA Director John Ratcliffe held a rare meeting in Moscow this week with senior Russian intelligence officials, a visit that was initially unannounced but has now been confirmed by both the Kremlin and Russia's foreign intelligence service. Sergei Naryshkin, director of Russia's SVR, said on August 27 that he met Ratcliffe for what he described as a working-level discussion involving sensitive matters related to the two intelligence agencies. Ratcliffe did not meet Russian President Vladimir Putin, though Kremlin officials said Putin was briefed on the talks afterward.

Ratcliffe traveled to Moscow on August 25 aboard a U.S. military transport aircraft after departing from the Washington area and stopping in Riga, Latvia. Public flight-tracking data showed the aircraft remained in Moscow for roughly eight and a half hours before leaving. The trip marked the first known visit to Moscow by a sitting CIA director since William Burns traveled there in November 2021, months before Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

The substance of Ratcliffe's discussions has not been publicly disclosed. President Trump described the trip as "semi-routine" and said intelligence contacts between Washington and Moscow could contribute to broader efforts to end the war in Ukraine. Trump rejected speculation that the meeting centered on Iran or NATO. Separate U.S. media reports have said Ratcliffe may have delivered a warning against Russian aggression toward NATO territory, but neither Washington nor Moscow has publicly confirmed that account.

Ukrainian officials were informed in advance of the visit, and Kyiv was asked to limit long-range strikes around Moscow and other northern Russian areas while the U.S. delegation was present. Ukraine agreed to the temporary restriction, according to reporting citing people familiar with the request. The Kremlin said intelligence channels between the two countries remain active despite severely strained relations and described direct security-service contacts as useful even when broader diplomatic ties are poor.
