U.S. pulls all bombers from British air base

Suspected terror plot puts RAF Fairford security in spotlight

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 5, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The U.S. Air Force has withdrawn all of its bombers from RAF Fairford in England following a suspected terrorist plot targeting the base, which has been used by American aircraft for operations against Iran.

The Pentagon confirmed that B-1B Lancer and B-52 Stratofortress bombers deployed to Fairford had returned to their home bases in the United States. At least 10 of 12 B-1 bombers seen at Fairford were tracked leaving in recent days. The Pentagon declined to discuss aircraft movements while they were underway, citing operational security, and did not publicly say the suspected plot was the reason for each individual redeployment.

The departures follow a major counterterrorism investigation that began after three suspicious vans were spotted near the Gloucestershire base in late September. Police arrested five British men in their 20s on suspicion of explosives and terrorism offenses. Investigators said petrol was found in the vehicles but no explosive devices were discovered. All five were later released on bail while remaining under investigation.

A sixth suspect, a 25-year-old dual British-Iranian national, was subsequently arrested in London on suspicion of preparing terrorist acts and was also released on bail. British counterterrorism officials say they are examining multiple possibilities, including potential foreign-state involvement. British officials have pointed to indications of possible Iranian involvement, while Iran has denied any connection to the incident. No suspect has been charged.

RAF Fairford has particular strategic importance because its long runway supports American heavy bombers and the base has been used during U.S. operations against Iran. The withdrawal comes as Washington continues strengthening other military forces in the Middle East, meaning the movement does not represent a broader U.S. military pullback from the region.
