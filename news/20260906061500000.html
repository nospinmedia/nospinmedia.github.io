TikTok document details experiment that withheld safety feature from millions

Company declines senators' request for internal report tied to teen's death

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

A confidential TikTok document is receiving renewed scrutiny after the company declined to provide it to two U.S. senators investigating an experiment that withheld an algorithmic safety feature from millions of users. Bloomberg Businessweek obtained the 2023 internal report and made it public on September 4, weeks after first reporting on the experiment and its connection to 16-year-old Chase Nasca, who died by suicide in February 2022.

TikTok introduced an algorithmic safeguard in 2021 designed to interrupt so-called filter bubbles in which users could repeatedly receive potentially harmful material. According to the internal document, TikTok withheld the safeguard from a control group representing about 10% of U.S. users, or roughly 15 million people at the time, while measuring how the change affected engagement and daily active-user metrics. Nasca was among users in that control group.

The document's review of Nasca's account found that TikTok recommended thousands of videos involving sadness, hopelessness, loneliness, depression and suicide. It said the filter-bubble safeguards did not apply to his account "by design" because of the experiment. The document does not establish that TikTok caused Nasca's death, and TikTok has previously disputed claims that its platform caused users to die by suicide.

Following the initial reporting, Senators Marsha Blackburn and Richard Blumenthal sent TikTok executives 13 questions and requested an unredacted copy of the document. They asked why minors were included in the experiment, which employees and executives knew about it and whether other safety features had been withheld during algorithmic tests.

TikTok responded by a September 1 deadline but declined to provide detailed answers or the document, citing active litigation. The company said it remains committed to youth safety and maintains safety features, age-appropriate default settings and parental controls. Blackburn and Blumenthal criticized the refusal, while scrutiny of social-media companies' treatment of young users continues in Congress and U.S. courts.
