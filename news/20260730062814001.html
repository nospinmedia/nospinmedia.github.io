Coal Falls Below Half Of China's Electricity Generation For First Time

Renewables Cross 40% As Wind And Solar Expansion Reshapes The World's Largest Power System

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 30, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Coal supplied less than half of China's electricity during the first six months of 2026 for the first time, marking a major shift in the power system of the world's largest coal-consuming country. Xing Yiteng, an official with China's National Energy Administration, said coal accounted for 49.7% of electricity generation from January through June.

Renewable energy supplied 41.2% of the country's electricity during the same period, crossing 40% for the first time. Wind and solar power alone accounted for 24.6%, while natural gas and nuclear generation supplied much of the remaining electricity. The figures reflect China's rapid construction of solar farms, wind projects, transmission lines, and energy storage systems.

The milestone does not necessarily mean China is burning less coal overall. Electricity demand continues to rise as the country expands electric vehicle use, builds energy-intensive artificial intelligence data centers, and increases industrial production and exports. Analysts said coal-fired generation could still increase in absolute terms even as coal's percentage of the overall power mix declines.

China remains heavily dependent on coal for grid reliability and continues constructing coal-fired power plants, particularly as backup for periods when solar, wind, or hydropower production is limited. However, the growing share of renewable generation indicates that clean energy is supplying an increasing portion of new electricity demand.

Beijing has pledged to peak coal consumption and carbon emissions no later than 2030. Its current energy targets call for wind and solar to provide 30% of electricity by that year, although Greenpeace East Asia said the country could reach that level by 2028 if it continues expanding rooftop solar, battery storage, demand-response programs, and more flexible electricity markets.

China's coal share remains considerably higher than in the United States, where coal supplied about 17% of utility-scale electricity in 2025. However, it is now below India's reliance on coal and lignite, which accounted for approximately 69% of that country's electricity last year.
