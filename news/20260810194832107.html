Trump Signs Order Calling For Spaced-Out Childhood Vaccines

Order also calls for separate measles, mumps and rubella shots

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 10, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on August 10, 2026, calling for changes to federal childhood vaccine recommendations, including giving routine vaccines during separate medical visits and replacing the combined MMR vaccine with individual measles, mumps and rubella shots once those products become available in the United States.

Separate measles, mumps and rubella vaccines are not currently available in the U.S., so that portion of the order cannot be implemented immediately. The order directs federal health officials to develop recommendations on the timing and sequencing of childhood vaccines and encourages states to consider changes to their vaccination requirements. States retain authority over school-entry vaccine mandates.

Trump has repeatedly argued that children receive too many vaccines at once and has questioned the safety of the combined MMR vaccine. Extensive research has found no evidence that the MMR vaccine causes autism, and public health experts say the existing childhood vaccination schedule has been studied for safety and effectiveness. Experts also warn that requiring additional medical visits can increase the chance that children miss later doses and remain susceptible to vaccine-preventable diseases.

The American Academy of Pediatrics and other medical organizations have opposed efforts to space out routine vaccinations without medical justification. Combination vaccines such as MMR reduce the number of injections and appointments needed while providing protection against multiple diseases.

The August 10 order comes after the administration made broader changes to federal childhood vaccine policy earlier this year. A previous executive order signed on May 29 directed the CDC to review the childhood vaccination schedule using an assessment from the Department of Health and Human Services. Some administration vaccine-policy changes are also being challenged in federal court.
