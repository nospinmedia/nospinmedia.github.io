North Korea displays artillery near border as dispute with South reaches UN

Rarely visible howitzer appears as both sides clash over DMZ landmine blast

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 26, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Tensions between North Korea and South Korea have increased after both governments took a dispute over a deadly border mine to the United Nations Security Council, while North Korean artillery was visibly positioned near the heavily fortified frontier.

A Reuters witness at an observation point in South Korea on October 26 confirmed that a North Korean artillery piece was positioned in clear view near the border. South Korea's Yonhap news agency identified the weapon as a self-propelled 122-mm howitzer and described its appearance near the frontier as a rare public display of such artillery. Reuters also observed three North Korean armored vehicles with their guns pointed south and approximately 20 to 30 soldiers conducting fencing work inside the Demilitarized Zone.

The military activity comes amid a dispute over a September 21 landmine explosion in the DMZ that injured three South Korean soldiers. South Korea told the Security Council that North Korean troops had planted the mine and said the incident violated the armistice that ended fighting in the Korean War. The U.S.-led United Nations Command, which oversees the DMZ, has also said a North Korean mine placed across the military demarcation line violated the armistice.

North Korea denies responsibility. In a letter sent to the Security Council, Pyongyang accused Seoul of fabricating the incident to portray North Korea's ongoing border construction as a military incursion. North Korea also warned that violations of its sovereignty could prompt self-defense measures.

Pyongyang separately accused South Korea of firing on North Korean personnel. Seoul says its forces use warning broadcasts and warning shots when North Korean soldiers cross the military demarcation line. North Korean troops have been constructing roads and fences and laying mines near the boundary in recent years.

South Korea has proposed military talks aimed at clarifying the precise location of the border and reducing the risk of an accidental confrontation.
