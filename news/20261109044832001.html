Houthi missile attack damages Aramco building in Riyadh

Saudi defenses intercept missiles as debris hits financial district

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 9, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

A Saudi Aramco building in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District was damaged Sunday after Saudi air defenses intercepted missiles targeting the capital and other parts of the kingdom. Saudi officials said falling debris struck the building, damaging several floors but causing no reported injuries or deaths.

The Saudi-led coalition said its defenses intercepted two ballistic missiles and two cruise missiles targeting Riyadh and other regions. The coalition accused Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement of launching the weapons. Witnesses reported hearing a loud explosion in Riyadh, while images and video showed blown-out windows and other visible damage to the Aramco building. Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry described the damage as limited and said the building had been subjected to an attempted attack.

The Houthis later claimed responsibility for attacks on Riyadh's financial district and other targets. Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said the operation was retaliation for recent strikes in Yemen that the group blamed on Saudi Arabia. Saudi authorities have accused the Houthis of carrying out an intensifying campaign against Saudi infrastructure, including airports, ports and facilities connected to the country's energy industry.

The incident adds to renewed military tension between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis after years of efforts to reduce direct fighting connected to Yemen's civil war. Saudi Arabia has recently carried out strikes against Houthi-controlled areas of Yemen, while the Houthis have increased missile and drone attacks against Saudi targets. The Saudi-led coalition also said Sunday that it destroyed Houthi missile launchers in Saada and Sanaa.

Aramco is Saudi Arabia's state-controlled oil company and one of the world's largest energy producers. Officials reported no casualties at the Riyadh building, and available reporting did not indicate that the incident disrupted Saudi oil production or exports.
