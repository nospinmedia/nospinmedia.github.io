Abdul El-Sayed Wins Michigan Democratic Senate Primary

Narrow victory sets up November race against Mike Rogers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 6, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Abdul El-Sayed won Michigan's Democratic U.S. Senate primary after The Associated Press called the race on August 5, 2026, defeating Rep. Haley Stevens by fewer than 15,000 votes and less than 1 percentage point. Nearly 1.5 million ballots were counted in a contest that surpassed turnout in previous Michigan Senate and gubernatorial primaries and nearly every presidential primary in the state since at least 1978.

El-Sayed, 41, is a physician and former director of the Detroit Health Department who campaigned on Medicare for All, ending U.S. military aid to Israel, and limiting corporate influence in politics. He received support from progressive leaders including Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez. Stevens conceded after the race was called and pledged to support El-Sayed in the general election.

The primary became one of the country's most expensive Senate nomination contests. Stevens and allied groups spent more than $60 million on advertising, while the American Israel Public Affairs Committee's affiliated political operation spent approximately $32 million supporting Stevens or opposing El-Sayed. El-Sayed and his allies spent substantially less but built a campaign that reported visiting 110 cities, holding more than 500 events, and organizing approximately 12,000 volunteers.

El-Sayed will face Republican nominee Mike Rogers, a former U.S. representative endorsed by President Trump, in the November 2026 general election. The candidates are competing for the open seat being vacated by Democratic Sen. Gary Peters, who is not seeking reelection. The closely divided state is expected to attract substantial national spending because the result could affect control of the U.S. Senate.
