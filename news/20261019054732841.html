Trump Endorses Netanyahu Nine Days Before Israeli Election

President Says Israeli Leader Is the Partner U.S. Needs

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 19, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

President Trump endorsed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for reelection just nine days before Israel's national election, publicly backing the longtime Israeli leader after months of declining to do so. Israelis are scheduled to vote on October 27 in the country's first national election since the October 7, 2023, Hamas attack.

Trump announced his endorsement Sunday on Truth Social, calling Netanyahu a strong partner in confronting Iran and giving him his "Complete and Total Endorsement." Trump later told Axios that he had always intended to endorse Netanyahu but did not want to do it too early. He said the two leaders have a good relationship and that it was important for Israel to have a leader the United States could count on.

The endorsement comes at a consequential point in Netanyahu's campaign. Most recent polls have shown his coalition falling short of the 61 seats required to form a majority government. Trump remains popular among many Israelis, making his intervention potentially significant, although voters will ultimately determine whether Netanyahu remains prime minister.

Trump had repeatedly stopped short of endorsing Netanyahu during the campaign as disagreements emerged between the two leaders over Iran and Gaza. The two governments nevertheless continue to work closely on regional security. Trump specifically emphasized their cooperation against Iran when announcing his support.

Netanyahu thanked Trump publicly for the endorsement. His principal rival, former Israeli military chief Gadi Eisenkot, responded that he respected Trump and appreciated his support for Israel but said the election was a domestic matter that should be decided by Israeli citizens.

The timing also places U.S.-Israeli relations directly into the closing days of a closely watched election that could determine Israel's government and its approach to Iran, Gaza and broader Middle East security.
