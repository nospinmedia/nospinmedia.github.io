Saudi-Houthi fighting intensifies as oil stays above $100

Strikes threaten Saudi energy routes and Red Sea shipping

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 17, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Saudi Arabia's conflict with Yemen's Houthi movement has sharply escalated, with Saudi-backed forces carrying out airstrikes in Yemen while the Houthis claim new drone and missile attacks against Saudi military and energy targets. The widening fighting is threatening critical oil infrastructure and shipping routes at a time when global energy supplies are already under pressure.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group launched drone and missile attacks against the Saudi Red Sea oil port of Yanbu and an air base at Khamis Mushait. He also claimed the Houthis shot down a Saudi F-15 fighter jet, although independent evidence supporting that claim was not immediately available. Saudi-backed Yemeni officials acknowledged that their forces and Saudi forces were conducting airstrikes against Houthi positions, while the Houthis claimed hundreds of strikes had occurred during the week.

The escalation follows rapid Houthi advances along Yemen's Red Sea coast, including the seizure of towns and islands around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, one of the world's most important maritime passages. The fighting has also increased pressure on Saudi energy infrastructure. The kingdom's East-West Pipeline, its main route for bypassing the disrupted Strait of Hormuz, remained shut following an earlier attack blamed on Iran-aligned fighters in Iraq. Traders told Reuters that an extended shutdown could remove the equivalent of as much as 4% of global oil supply, while Brent crude was trading near $108 a barrel on Wednesday.

Saudi Arabia is also seeking additional air-defense assistance as its supply of missile interceptors comes under pressure. Regional officials told The Associated Press that Riyadh has approached Britain, France, Egypt and Pakistan about air-defense support. U.S. assistance has so far remained focused on intelligence sharing and planning support rather than direct military intervention.

The renewed Saudi-Houthi conflict had been comparatively quiet in recent years but intensified after the Houthis declared a naval blockade against Saudi Arabia in July 2026 and expanded operations against Saudi-backed forces.
