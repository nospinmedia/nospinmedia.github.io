Trump administration proposes major rollback of oil and gas methane rules

EPA projects $45 billion in savings and increased domestic production

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 28, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

President Trump and the Environmental Protection Agency have proposed a broad rollback of Biden-era regulations limiting methane and other air pollution from U.S. oil and natural gas operations, arguing the changes would reduce industry costs and increase domestic energy production.

Trump announced the proposal in Lubbock, Texas, on October 27, alongside EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin. The administration estimates the revisions would produce approximately $45 billion in regulatory savings while allowing an additional 210 million barrels of oil and about 1.4 trillion cubic feet of natural gas to be produced.

The proposal targets portions of EPA regulations finalized in 2024 under the Clean Air Act. Those rules established methane standards for new and modified oil and gas facilities and required states to develop plans for reducing emissions from existing operations.

Among the proposed changes, EPA would eliminate its Super Emitter Program, which allows approved third parties using remote-sensing technology to report potentially large methane releases. The agency would also revise requirements affecting marginal wells, associated-gas flaring, control devices, covers and closed-vent systems. EPA says the existing requirements impose unnecessary costs and can force lower-producing wells to close.

The action is a proposal rather than an immediate elimination of the methane regulations. EPA plans a public hearing and a comment period of at least 45 days after the proposal is published in the Federal Register before moving toward a final rule.

Methane is a powerful greenhouse gas and a major contributor to global warming. The 2024 regulations were designed to reduce methane along with volatile organic compounds and other pollutants released during oil and gas production. Environmental organizations argue that weakening the standards will increase emissions and reduce incentives for companies to identify and repair leaks.

The administration says the regulatory changes will increase energy supplies and ultimately reduce costs for consumers. Those projected savings and production increases are EPA estimates, and the final economic and environmental effects will depend on which provisions survive the rulemaking process and potential legal challenges.
