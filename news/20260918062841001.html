USPS stops work on system tied to Trump's mail-voting restrictions

Supreme Court keeps new ballot requirements out of 2026 midterms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 18, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The U.S. Postal Service has stopped developing a computer system central to President Trump's effort to impose new requirements on mail voting after the Supreme Court left a nationwide injunction against the rules in place. Postmaster General David Steiner confirmed the halt on September 17, saying USPS is taking no further action while the injunction remains in effect.

The blocked rule would have required states and local election officials to redesign ballot envelopes with specific features, including a specialized barcode unique to each voter, submit envelope designs to USPS for approval and upload voter information to a new Postal Service portal. Under the rule, ballot mail that failed to comply with the requirements would not have been accepted by USPS and would have been returned to election officials for correction. The administration said the changes were intended to help enforce federal law, address election-integrity concerns and improve postal operations.

Federal courts blocked the requirements after states challenged USPS's authority to impose them and warned that compliance was practically impossible so close to the November 3, 2026 midterm elections. Some jurisdictions had already ordered or begun mailing ballots. On September 14, the Supreme Court declined to lift the injunction. Justice Brett Kavanaugh, concurring with the decision, wrote that even if USPS ultimately has statutory authority for such a rule, implementing it during the 2026 election would be arbitrary and capricious because state and local officials lacked sufficient time to reasonably comply.

Steiner declined to take a position on Trump's policy itself, describing USPS as an independent establishment within the executive branch and saying his focus was on implementing requirements placed on the agency. USPS will continue handling election mail under existing procedures for the midterms, although it plans to encourage optional use of barcodes by offering lower processing fees.
