Farage says he 'wasn't even listening' during undercover donor meeting

Reform UK aides step aside as authorities examine foreign-funding allegations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 6, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Nigel Farage says he "frankly wasn't even listening" during part of an undercover meeting at the center of allegations over foreign funding of Reform UK, as two senior party officials step aside and authorities examine whether British political-finance laws may have been breached. The controversy followed an investigation by Channel 4 News and investigative group Verbatim, whose reporters posed as a wealthy American financier and his UK-based son while secretly recording meetings with party figures.

Reform UK said senior Farage aide Dan Jukes and policy chief James Orr stepped down from their positions pending an internal investigation. The undercover operation involved three opinion polls commissioned for Reform that cost approximately £32,500 and were paid for by a U.S. company controlled by the undercover operation. British election law generally restricts political parties from accepting donations or benefits from impermissible foreign sources.

Footage also captured discussions about a proposed £500,000 contribution from the supposed American donor. During one recorded meeting, Farage praised polling results financed by the undercover operation, while other footage showed aides discussing possible funding arrangements. Farage has denied wrongdoing, said Reform had accepted no illegal donations and characterized some of his aides' recorded remarks as "loose pub talk." Asked about his own participation, he said he had not been paying attention to portions of the conversation and emphasized that he was not responsible for the party's compliance procedures.

The allegations have drawn scrutiny from law enforcement and election regulators. Rival political parties referred the matter to police, while the Electoral Commission said it was assessing information surrounding the allegations. No finding that Reform UK or Farage violated election law has been announced. The party's internal investigation also remains underway.

The dispute erupted as Reform UK held its annual conference in Birmingham, forcing the party to address the funding allegations while Farage and other senior figures attempted to focus attention on its policy platform.
