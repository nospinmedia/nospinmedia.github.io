Trump calls pre-election GDP figures 'fake'

President demands investigation of his own Commerce Department

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 27, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

President Trump accused his own Commerce Department of releasing "FAKE" economic growth figures before the November midterm elections and demanded an immediate investigation, offering no evidence that the government's original estimate was intentionally manipulated.

The accusation followed Wednesday's scheduled revision by the Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis, which raised annualized third-quarter economic growth from 3.4% to 4.3%. The original estimate was released October 29, five days before the November 3 elections. At the time, the White House celebrated the 3.4% estimate, describing it as the economy's "strongest pace in a year."

In his Thanksgiving Day Truth Social post, Trump questioned why the higher figure had not been available before voters went to the polls and described the difference as a "massive, and purposeful, error." He also claimed the revised figures had been released three weeks late.

The BEA's November 25 second estimate, however, was issued according to its previously published release calendar. GDP estimates are routinely revised as more complete economic data become available. The agency's next scheduled revision, its third estimate for the quarter, is due December 23.

The 0.9-percentage-point upward revision was driven primarily by stronger estimates of business investment, particularly software, and consumer spending on goods. The revised 4.3% growth rate also represented an acceleration from 2.2% annualized growth during the second quarter.

The size of the revision was larger than typical. A May 2026 study co-authored by BEA Director Vipin Arora found that revisions made within roughly 90 days of an initial GDP estimate have averaged about 0.52 percentage points this century.

Official BEA data showed the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index increased at a 2.1% annualized rate in the third quarter, while the index excluding food and energy increased 2.5%.

Trump has previously challenged federal economic statistics, including dismissing a senior Labor Department official following a weak 2025 jobs report and alleging without evidence that employment data had been manipulated.
