Trump gave four White House aides cash holiday gifts

Three employees disclosed payments of $45,000 each

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 9, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Newly released financial disclosures show President Trump gave substantial personal cash gifts to four White House employees, including $45,000 each to aides Natalie Harp, Margo Martin and Chamberlain Harris and $20,000 to Walt Nauta. The payments, listed on the employees' disclosure forms as "Cash Gift for Holidays," are drawing attention from government ethics specialists over how federal rules apply to unusually large gifts from a superior to subordinates.

Harp, Martin and Harris each earn $150,000 annually in their White House positions, meaning Trump's $45,000 payments equaled nearly one-third of their government salaries. Nauta, the White House director of Oval Office operations, earns $175,000. All four have worked with Trump for years, including during periods when he was out of office.

A White House official said the payments reflected Trump's longstanding practice of giving Christmas gifts to people around him and were unrelated to the recipients' government duties. The administration maintains that the gifts comply with applicable ethics requirements.

Government ethics experts have offered differing assessments. Richard Painter, who served as the White House's chief ethics lawyer under President George W. Bush, said the payments appear to raise concerns under a federal law prohibiting outside supplementation of a government employee's salary. Don Fox, a former acting director of the Office of Government Ethics, said the available facts do not clearly establish that the payments violate that prohibition. Fox nevertheless said payments of this size from a superior to subordinate employees present unusual ethics concerns.

The disclosures do not by themselves establish that Trump or the aides violated federal law. The reporting instead raises a legal and ethics question about whether personal gifts from a president to salaried government employees can be distinguished from payments connected to their federal service.
