Supreme Court takes up Trump's mandatory immigrant detention policy

Ruling could determine whether thousands detained by ICE are entitled to bond hearings

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 2, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court agreed Thursday to decide whether the Trump administration can require immigrants who entered the country illegally to remain detained during deportation proceedings without receiving a bond hearing, taking up a dispute that has produced conflicting rulings in federal appeals courts.

The case, Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha, involves Ricardo Aparecido Barbosa da Cunha, a Brazilian citizen who entered the United States illegally more than 20 years ago and has no criminal convictions, according to court records. He was arrested by ICE during a traffic stop in Massachusetts in 2025 and denied a bond hearing under the administration's mandatory-detention policy. A federal judge later ordered that he receive a hearing, and he was released on bond in October 2025.

The dispute centers on the administration's interpretation of federal immigration law governing people considered "applicants for admission." Under the policy adopted by immigration authorities in July 2025, people who entered the country illegally can be held without bond while their removal cases proceed, even if they have lived in the United States for years. Previously, many long-term undocumented residents could ask an immigration judge for release by showing that they were not a flight risk or danger to the public.

Federal appeals courts are divided over the policy. The 5th and 8th U.S. Circuit Courts of Appeals have accepted the administration's interpretation, while nine other federal appeals courts have ruled that certain immigrants must remain eligible for bond hearings. The 2nd Circuit, which considered Barbosa da Cunha's case, rejected the government's position in April.

The policy has generated an extraordinary volume of litigation. More than 73,000 federal lawsuits challenging immigration detention were filed during 2026, according to federal court data cited by Reuters. A Supreme Court ruling could resolve the conflicting standards now being applied around the country and determine whether thousands of people detained during removal proceedings can seek release on bond.
