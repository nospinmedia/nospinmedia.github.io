Saudi Arabia prepares major offensive against Houthis

More than 100,000 Yemeni troops could join push for Red Sea strait

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 3, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Saudi Arabia is preparing a major offensive against Iran-backed Houthi forces in Yemen aimed at reversing their recent gains and regaining control around the strategically important Bab el-Mandeb Strait, according to regional and Western officials. More than 100,000 Yemeni troops could be mobilized depending on the scale of the operation, with Saudi airstrikes supporting the ground forces.

Reuters reported that Saudi planners are considering two options: a concentrated operation around Bab el-Mandeb, or a broader campaign involving synchronized attacks in Al-Bayda, Marib, Taiz and Al-Jawf. Six people familiar with the preparations told Reuters the operation could begin within weeks, although estimates ranged from within a week to sometime after the November 3 U.S. midterm elections. Saudi, Yemeni, Houthi and U.S. military representatives had not publicly confirmed the plans when Reuters published its report.

Bab el-Mandeb forms the southern entrance to the Red Sea and connects shipping between Asia and the Mediterranean through the Suez Canal. Its importance has increased as the Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed by the Iran conflict. Houthi forces advanced along Yemen's coast last month and seized control of the Bab el-Mandeb area, increasing their leverage over a major international shipping route.

The United States is providing Saudi Arabia with military advisers, intelligence and targeting information but is not currently expected to participate directly in the offensive. Axios separately reported that President Trump declined Saudi requests for direct U.S. participation, including American airstrikes, while continuing non-combat support.

Pakistan and Turkey have also supplied military equipment used by Saudi Arabia, while Pakistan has advisers supporting Yemeni forces. Reuters reported that Pakistani troops deployed to Saudi Arabia are there for defensive duties and have not been authorized to enter Yemen for combat. France has separately announced defensive support to help protect Saudi infrastructure.

The planned offensive would represent a major escalation in Yemen after years in which a 2022 truce largely restrained fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis.
