Firefighting Helicopter Crashes Near Richfield, Utah

Flames block rescuers from reaching the two aboard

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 8, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

A Sikorsky S-64 Skycrane firefighting helicopter crashed Friday near Richfield, Utah, while battling the Widemouth 2 Fire. Officials said the two people aboard could not be reached because active flames and rugged terrain made the crash site unsafe. The accident occurred in remote mountains within Fishlake National Forest, where incident managers described fire conditions as volatile and access as extremely limited. Authorities said they would not risk additional responders until conditions improve.

The fire was sparked by lightning on July 27, 2026, near Kanosh and had grown to about 165 square miles, with 19% containment by Friday morning. More than 600 personnel and 7 helicopters were assigned to the incident. Tyler Hecht, deputy incident commander with the Great Basin Incident Management Team, said crews had not yet reached the wreckage and briefly paused suppression efforts before resuming operations. Sevier County Sheriff Nathan Curtis said responders were held back because fast-moving flames and steep terrain made access too dangerous. Officials also reported the crash ignited a new fire near the impact site.

The Federal Aviation Administration and National Transportation Safety Board opened investigations and said teams planned to reach the area by Saturday night if fire conditions allow. Red flag weather, including extreme heat, low humidity, and erratic winds, remained in effect Friday. Utah officials reported eight large active wildfires burning more than 331,000 acres statewide. Governor Spencer Cox called the crash a sobering reminder of the risks faced by wildland firefighters.

Evacuations and at least six structure losses have been reported in the Widemouth 2 fire area as crews continue working to contain the blaze across timber, brush, and grass. Officials urged residents to follow evacuation alerts and avoid speculation about the cause of the crash until investigators can safely examine the wreckage.
