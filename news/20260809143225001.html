Türkiye Says Mecca Defense Pact Isn't Aimed at Iran

Mutual-defense agreement links Saudi Arabia, Türkiye and Pakistan

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 10, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Türkiye's Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said on August 8, 2026 that the newly signed Mecca Joint Defense Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan is a defensive arrangement and is not directed at Iran or any other country. Fidan said nations that do not threaten the three partners have nothing to fear, emphasizing that the agreement is intended to strengthen regional security rather than establish an alliance against a specific adversary.

The agreement was signed in Mecca on August 7 by Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. Its central provision states that an armed attack against one member will be treated as an attack against all three. Fidan compared the mutual-defense principle to NATO's Article 5, while noting that any response would depend on the circumstances of an attack and require a formal request from the affected member.

The pact also establishes regular meetings among foreign and defense officials, creates political and military coordination committees, and calls for a permanent secretariat in Saudi Arabia to oversee intelligence sharing, military training, logistics, defense-industrial cooperation, and future planning. Fidan said Egypt could become a future participant, describing it as a natural regional partner if technical discussions are successfully completed. The agreement also states that it is open to additional countries that support its defensive principles.

Iranian officials criticized the agreement after it was announced, while the three signatories maintained that it is neither a military bloc nor a sectarian alliance. The pact complements existing bilateral and multilateral security relationships rather than replacing them. Together, the three countries combine Saudi Arabia's economic influence, Pakistan's military experience, and Türkiye's NATO-connected defense capabilities in what officials describe as a framework for regional deterrence and cooperation.
