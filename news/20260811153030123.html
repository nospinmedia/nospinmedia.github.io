Trump Says Iran Must Pay Compensation In Any Deal

New U.S. demand complicates Hormuz talks as oil prices rise

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 11, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

President Donald Trump said on August 10, 2026 that he has instructed U.S. negotiators to seek compensation from Iran in any future agreement to end the conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The announcement came after Iranian officials reiterated demands that the United States provide compensation for damage caused during the recent conflict, adding another obstacle to negotiations aimed at restoring commercial shipping through the strategic waterway.

In a social media statement, Trump said Iran should compensate the United States and the families of Americans killed or injured in attacks linked to Tehran, directing U.S. negotiators to include those demands in future talks. Iranian officials have continued to insist that any broader agreement address sanctions relief, access to frozen assets, and compensation for war damage. Oman remains involved as a mediator, but both sides have introduced additional conditions that have narrowed prospects for a near-term agreement.

Financial markets reacted to the latest developments. Brent crude rose about 5% as investors weighed the increasing likelihood that negotiations could take longer than previously expected. The Strait of Hormuz normally carries roughly one-fifth of the world's seaborne oil, making any prolonged disruption significant for global energy markets and fuel prices.

Negotiations continue through Omani mediation, but officials on both sides have acknowledged that additional conditions have complicated efforts to reach a settlement. As of August 11, 2026, neither Washington nor Tehran has announced a timetable for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and no final agreement has been confirmed.
