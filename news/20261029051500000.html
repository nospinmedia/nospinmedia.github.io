Data leak exposes more than 40,000 Homeland Security workers

Records may identify personnel in undercover and surveillance roles

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

A transparency group has published records identifying more than 40,000 Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors, exposing names, contact information, job titles and internal organizational details across several federal agencies. DHS says it is investigating what it described as a potential breach and is taking steps to protect its personnel.

The records were released by Distributed Denial of Secrets, or DDoSecrets, after being provided by an entity calling itself the Department of Peace. The data covers personnel at agencies including U.S. Customs and Border Protection, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, Homeland Security Investigations and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. More than 21,000 records are associated with CBP and about 6,600 with ICE, according to reporting on the dataset.

The information includes names, phone numbers, email addresses, job titles and internal email groups. Some records contain details associated with special operations, surveillance, computer forensics and undercover work, potentially identifying personnel performing sensitive assignments. Reuters independently confirmed some information by comparing records with other sources and contacting DHS employees, but said it could not authenticate the entire dataset.

The Department of Peace claims the information was obtained through an Okta single sign-on service used by DHS. That account has not been independently verified. Okta said it has found no evidence that its own systems or data were compromised. DHS has not publicly detailed how the information was obtained or whether an unauthorized party directly accessed department systems.

The disclosure raises both cybersecurity and personnel-safety concerns because internal organizational information can reveal relationships, assignments and operational structures that are not normally public. DHS said it is working to address the situation and ensure affected employees are protected while the investigation continues.
