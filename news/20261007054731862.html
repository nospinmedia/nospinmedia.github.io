Chicago woman shot by Border Patrol agent sues U.S. government

Charges against her were dropped after evidence challenged federal account

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 7, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Marimar Martinez, a 31-year-old U.S. citizen who was shot by a federal immigration officer during a Chicago enforcement operation last year, has sued the federal government, alleging the agent used unjustified force and that authorities falsely portrayed her as the aggressor afterward.

The shooting occurred on October 4, 2025, during the Trump administration's Operation Midway Blitz immigration crackdown. Martinez had been following federal agents through Chicago while honking her horn and warning residents about their presence. Customs and Border Protection Agent Charles Exum opened fire after the agents' SUV and Martinez's vehicle collided. Exum fired five shots, leaving Martinez with seven gunshot wounds.

Federal authorities initially accused Martinez of ramming the agents' vehicle and said Exum fired because she was driving toward him. The administration also publicly described Martinez as a 'domestic terrorist' and emphasized that she possessed a handgun. Her attorney says the gun remained inside her purse and that Martinez had both a firearm license and a concealed-carry permit.

Federal prosecutors charged Martinez with assaulting a federal officer, but the government permanently dismissed the case in November 2025. Evidence disclosed during the criminal proceedings, including body-camera footage and forensic analysis, appeared to contradict parts of the government's initial account. Martinez's attorneys say their reconstruction shows Exum fired three rounds through the windshield, another through the front passenger window and a final shot through the rear passenger window as her vehicle moved away.

The lawsuit, filed under the Federal Tort Claims Act, accuses the government of battery, negligence and malicious prosecution. Martinez's lawyers are seeking monetary damages and continue to call for criminal charges against Exum. The Justice Department declined to comment on the pending litigation. Exum is currently on administrative leave, according to CBP.

Martinez said she brought the case partly to prevent similar incidents involving federal immigration officers from happening to others.
