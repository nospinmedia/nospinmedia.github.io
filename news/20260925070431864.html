New Jersey governor tells lieutenant governor to resign by Friday

Investigation substantiates sexual harassment and ethics violations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 25, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill has called on Lt. Gov. Dale Caldwell to resign by Friday after an independent investigation substantiated sexual harassment and multiple violations of state ethics rules.

The investigation was launched after the governor's chief ethics officer received an anonymous complaint in May 2026. The outside law firm Lowenstein Sandler conducted the inquiry, which examined whether Caldwell violated New Jersey's workplace discrimination policy, the governor's Code of Conduct and the state's Uniform Ethics Code.

Investigators substantiated a sexual harassment allegation involving a female staffer. According to the report, Caldwell made a romantic advance toward the staffer's friend and, after being rejected, made a sexual remark to the staffer. Investigators concluded that the comment was sexual, derogatory and sufficiently serious to violate the state's workplace policy.

The report also found ethics violations involving a state employee with whom Caldwell had a romantic relationship. Investigators said Caldwell repeatedly sought a promotion for the employee without disclosing their relationship. Caldwell was additionally found to have brought personal guests to ticketed events without paying for their admission or receiving required ethics approval.

Sherrill said the findings showed a pattern of misconduct that continued despite warnings and training. She said Caldwell had engaged in serious, repeated violations of state policy and gave him until Friday, September 25, to resign.

Caldwell's attorney Thomas Calcagni disputes the findings and says the lieutenant governor was not given a meaningful opportunity to respond to each allegation. His attorney also disputes the characterization that Caldwell improperly used his office to secure a promotion, noting that the employee did not ultimately receive one.

Caldwell, 66, took office in January and also serves as New Jersey secretary of state. As of early Friday, the public reporting reviewed for this story did not establish that he had resigned.
