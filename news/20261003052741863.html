G7 agrees to release 100 million barrels from emergency reserves

Diesel release begins immediately after U.S. pressure

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 3, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

The Group of Seven countries have agreed to a coordinated release of 100 million barrels of diesel, crude oil and other petroleum products from emergency reserves as governments try to ease severe pressure on global fuel supplies and prices. The G7 said the release will begin immediately, run for four months and include a substantial amount of diesel within the first 20 days.

The agreement follows pressure from the Trump administration on European countries to tap their emergency diesel inventories. The administration had warned that the United States could restrict diesel exports if Europe did not contribute more emergency supplies. President Trump said Friday that the United States would not impose a diesel export ban following the agreement. The G7 also pledged not to impose restrictions on energy and energy-product exports among its members.

The release will be coordinated through the International Energy Agency, which also organized a record emergency draw earlier this year after the war with Iran disrupted global energy supplies. That earlier action released roughly 400 million barrels. The new G7 agreement specifically emphasizes diesel because supplies of the transportation and heating fuel have become particularly tight.

The G7 did not provide a country-by-country breakdown or specify exactly how much of the 100 million barrels will consist of diesel versus crude oil and other petroleum products. That distinction matters because diesel shortages cannot necessarily be resolved simply by releasing additional crude oil. The agreement instead calls for the release to be front-loaded with substantial diesel volumes and allows discussions over additional diesel releases if needed.

The action could add supply to strained fuel markets, but the agreement does not guarantee a particular reduction in prices. Fuel prices remain affected by the broader disruption to global energy markets and the continuing conflict involving Iran.

The agreement is a significant development from earlier U.S. pressure on Europe: governments have now committed to an actual coordinated emergency release rather than merely discussing one.
