Drone Strike Sets Russian Cargo Ship Ablaze In Baltic Sea

16 Crew Evacuate As Burning Vessel Drifts Into Finnish Waters

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 22, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

A sanctioned Russian cargo ship caught fire after being struck by two drones in the Gulf of Finland, forcing all 16 crew members to evacuate before the damaged vessel drifted into Finnish territorial waters and prompted Finland to tighten security in the region.

The Sparta II, a 397-foot Russian-flagged cargo ship, was traveling from St. Petersburg to the Russian Baltic exclave of Kaliningrad when it was struck on Saturday, November 21. The vessel is owned by Oboronlogistika, a logistics company linked to Russia's Defense Ministry, and is under U.S. and Ukrainian sanctions.

Leningrad region Governor Aleksandr Drozdenko said two drones hit the ship in international waters west of Gogland Island. The resulting fire left the vessel drifting until it entered Finnish waters and dropped anchor off Loviisa, a coastal town in southern Finland. Finnish coast guard vessels used water cannons to fight the blaze and cool containers on the ship. Finnish authorities said they planned to tow the vessel out of the country's territorial waters.

All 16 crew members abandoned the Sparta II in lifeboats and transferred to another Russian vessel. Finnish military and border officials said Ukraine was likely responsible for the drone strike, although Ukraine had not publicly claimed responsibility.

Finland imposed temporary aviation and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland as authorities worked to secure the area. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo warned that the war was "spilling over into our neighbouring areas" while saying Ukraine has the right to defend itself against Russia.

The incident was followed by another potential security breach. Finnish authorities said a Russian helicopter was suspected of entering more than 4 kilometers (2.5 miles) into Finnish airspace, potentially in connection with operations around the damaged vessel.

Finnish President Alexander Stubb called the situation in the Gulf of Finland "serious" and said Finland had briefed key international partners and NATO allies about the incident.
