Dolly Parton Dies at 80 in Nashville

Country music legend leaves generations of songs, films and philanthropy

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 25, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Dolly Parton, the country music singer-songwriter, actor, businesswoman and philanthropist whose career stretched across more than six decades, died peacefully in Nashville, Tennessee, on August 25, 2026, according to her publicist. She was 80 years old. Her family also announced the death through her nephew Bryan Seaver, who had served as her head of security for more than two decades. A cause of death was not disclosed.

Born January 19, 1946, in rural Tennessee as one of 12 children, Parton rose from poverty to become one of the most recognizable figures in American entertainment. She wrote hundreds of songs, including "Jolene," "Coat of Many Colors" and "I Will Always Love You," and her recordings generated more than 100 million worldwide sales and more than 1 billion online streams. She earned 55 Grammy nominations and 10 wins, was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame, and later entered the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame.

Parton's success extended well beyond country music. She starred with Jane Fonda and Lily Tomlin in the 1980 comedy 9 to 5, whose title song became another major hit, and later appeared in films including The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas and Steel Magnolias. She also built Dollywood into a major East Tennessee tourism business while retaining control over much of her songwriting and publishing work throughout a career in an industry historically dominated by men.

Her philanthropy became nearly as prominent as her entertainment career. Parton created the Imagination Library to provide free books to children, initially inspired by her father's difficulty learning to read. After deadly 2016 wildfires in the Smoky Mountains, her foundation provided monthly financial assistance to families who lost homes. She also donated $1 million to Vanderbilt University medical research during the COVID-19 pandemic. Her husband of nearly 60 years, Carl Dean, died in March 2025 at age 82.

President Trump ordered American flags lowered following Parton's death as tributes spread across music, entertainment and public life.
