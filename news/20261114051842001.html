Ukraine launches nearly 1,200 drones at Russia

European security enters expanding U.S.-led peace talks

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 14, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Ukraine launched almost 1,200 long-range drones at targets across Russia overnight into Friday in one of its largest aerial attacks since Russia's full-scale invasion began nearly five years ago, as negotiations aimed at ending the war expanded to include broader questions about European security.

Russia's Defense Ministry said its air defenses downed 1,198 Ukrainian drones between late Thursday and early Friday. Local Russian officials said strikes across three regions killed seven people, including a 12-year-old, and wounded 17. Ukraine's General Staff said its forces struck an oil refinery in Volgograd, where a fire was reported.

Russia simultaneously continued attacks on Ukraine. Ukrainian authorities said Russia launched 117 drones overnight, killing at least two people, including a 17-year-old. A separate Russian strike on two five-story apartment buildings in Zaporizhzhia wounded 25 people, including six children, according to officials. Russian attacks on Ukraine's energy infrastructure also caused power outages across about half of the country's regions.

The attacks came as U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner continued diplomatic efforts following talks with Russian and Ukrainian delegations in Abu Dhabi and subsequent meetings with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin.

Kushner said Russia had agreed to include European representatives in upcoming negotiations, allowing talks to address broader postwar European security arrangements alongside territorial disputes between Russia and Ukraine. European officials have approached that expansion cautiously. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Europe already has a security architecture through NATO, while EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said a ceasefire should come before negotiations over Europe's broader security structure.
