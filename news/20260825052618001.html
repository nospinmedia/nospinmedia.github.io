Trump Administration Plans Record Visa Revocations

Up to 200,000 asylum applicants could lose visitor visas

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 25, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

The State Department is preparing what could become the largest single mass visa revocation in U.S. history, targeting up to 200,000 foreign nationals who entered the country on temporary B-1 business or B-2 tourist visas before applying for asylum. According to State Department documents and U.S. officials, the revocations are expected to be announced in the coming weeks and would be carried out in coordination with the Department of Homeland Security (DHS).

State Department spokesperson Tommy Pigott said the administration is working with DHS to identify nonimmigrant visa holders who entered the United States as temporary visitors but later sought asylum to remain in the country. Officials said revoking the visas would not automatically trigger deportation proceedings. Instead, most individuals with pending asylum cases would lose their visitor classification while their asylum claims continue through the immigration system.

The proposal is part of the Trump administration's broader effort to tighten immigration enforcement and reduce what officials describe as abuse of the asylum process. Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau argued that asylum should not become a means of bypassing immigration laws, saying the system has been overwhelmed by what he characterized as unfounded claims. Current applicants for B-1 and B-2 visas are already required to affirm they do not intend to seek asylum and must demonstrate they plan to return to their home countries after their visit.

The administration said the effort builds on a wider campaign that has resulted in more than 175,000 visa revocations over the past 18 months involving criminal allegations, security concerns and other violations. Immigration attorneys and advocacy organizations are expected to challenge the new policy, arguing that revoking visitor visas based on asylum applications conflicts with protections established under federal immigration law. If implemented as planned, the policy would represent one of the administration's most significant immigration actions ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.
