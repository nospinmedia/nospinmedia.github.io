U.S. envoys head to Moscow and Kyiv for new peace push

Zelenskyy calls for temporary air truce during talks

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected to travel to Moscow this weekend before heading to Kyiv, reviving a U.S.-led effort to negotiate an end to Russia's war in Ukraine after months of limited diplomatic progress. President Trump said the two negotiators were carrying what he described as an "idea for peace," while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine was preparing for substantive discussions with the American delegation.

Russia's state news agency TASS reported that Witkoff and Kushner were expected in Moscow on September 5 and 6, although Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov declined Friday to confirm the reported schedule. Zelenskyy said the U.S. side had confirmed plans for meetings in both Moscow and Kyiv and stressed that continued American involvement was important. The trip would also mark the first visit to Kyiv by Witkoff and Kushner in their current Ukraine mediation effort.

Zelenskyy called for a temporary halt to aerial attacks during the diplomatic mission and urged Russia to reciprocate. The proposal comes as both countries continue launching strikes deep into each other's territory. Russia has intensified attacks on Kyiv in recent days, including a September 4 drone strike on the headquarters of Ukraine's Security Service of Ukraine (SBU), while Ukrainian drones have continued targeting sites inside Russia.

Major obstacles to an agreement remain unresolved. Russian President Vladimir Putin recently said negotiations had been "frozen," while Russian officials have continued to identify territorial questions as central to any lasting settlement. Ukraine has resisted demands that it surrender territory occupied or claimed by Russia. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha, however, said he saw a "new dynamic" in diplomatic efforts as political contacts intensified.

The renewed mediation comes more than four years after Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022, with fighting continuing along the eastern front despite repeated international efforts to negotiate a settlement.
