Eight sailors attempted suicide during extended Navy deployment

USS Abraham Lincoln strike group spent more than 250 consecutive days at sea

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 24, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Eight U.S. Navy personnel assigned to the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier strike group attempted suicide during its extended deployment, which included combat operations in the war with Iran, according to Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao. No service members died by suicide during the deployment.

Cao disclosed the figure in a letter to Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand of New York, who had sought information about morale and mental health following reports of difficult conditions aboard the carrier. The eight attempts occurred across the wider strike group rather than solely aboard the Lincoln and included personnel from the carrier, its air wing, strike group and destroyer squadron staffs, escorting destroyers and embarked aviation squadrons. A defense official told CBS News that two of the eight attempts occurred during active fighting with Iran.

The strike group's deployment began in November 2025 and included more than 250 consecutive days at sea without a port call. Between roughly 6,000 and 7,000 sailors served across the strike group. Reports during the deployment raised concerns about mental health, food and supply problems and plumbing issues. The Navy has said personnel had access to mental health support and has disputed claims that conditions amounted to a widespread crisis.

Cao's letter said the Lincoln deployed with five chaplains, an integrated prevention counselor, a psychologist, a clinical social worker, three behavioral health technicians and an expeditionary facility dog. He also confirmed that one sailor attempted to go overboard in March but was stopped by shipmates, while another sailor went overboard in August and was quickly recovered and treated.

The Lincoln left the Middle East after being relieved by the USS George Washington and stopped in Thailand in early September. It is now heading toward its home port in San Diego. Cao said the carrier's exact return schedule remains classified.
