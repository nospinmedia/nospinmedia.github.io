Kushner Holds Rare Talks With Hamas In Egypt

Netanyahu meeting follows as Gaza peace plan faces key test

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 17, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

U.S. envoy Jared Kushner held rare direct talks with Hamas political leader Khalil al-Hayya in Egypt on August 16 as part of an effort to advance a U.S.-backed roadmap aimed at ending the conflict in Gaza. The discussions included mediators from Egypt, Qatar, and Turkey and focused on possible benchmarks for Hamas disarmament and the future administration of Gaza. Kushner traveled to Jerusalem on August 17 for follow-up talks with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The diplomatic effort comes after Netanyahu rejected key elements of the proposed 15-point peace framework, arguing that Israeli forces should not withdraw from Gaza until Hamas is fully disarmed. Hamas has indicated it could accept a phased process but says any disarmament must be tied to Israeli commitments on withdrawals and implementation of the broader agreement. The differing positions have left negotiators attempting to bridge major gaps before any formal accord can move forward.

The proposal under discussion would gradually transfer Gaza's civilian administration to a Palestinian governing body while introducing international oversight during the transition. Officials involved in the negotiations say sequencing remains the central obstacle, with both sides disagreeing over whether disarmament or military withdrawals should occur first. Regional governments have urged continued negotiations, warning that failure to reach an agreement could further delay reconstruction efforts and increase instability.

The meetings in Egypt and Jerusalem represent one of the highest-level diplomatic efforts in recent months to revive negotiations over Gaza's future. While no breakthrough has been announced, officials described the discussions as an attempt to determine whether enough common ground exists to continue negotiations in the coming weeks.
