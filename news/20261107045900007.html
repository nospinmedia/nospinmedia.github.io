U.S. adds 165,000 jobs in October

Hiring beats forecasts as unemployment falls to 4.1%

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 7, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

U.S. employers added 165,000 jobs in October, substantially exceeding economists' expectations as the unemployment rate declined to 4.1%, according to the latest federal employment report released Friday, November 6.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that nonfarm payroll growth accelerated during the month. Economists had generally expected about 65,000 new jobs, making the reported increase roughly 100,000 higher than the consensus forecast. The unemployment rate fell from 4.2% in September to 4.1% in October.

The report also showed stronger hiring in previous months than initially estimated. Employment gains for August and September were revised upward by a combined 84,000 jobs, indicating that payroll growth entering the fourth quarter was stronger than earlier government estimates had shown.

Hiring in October was spread across several areas of the economy. Among the industries contributing to the increase were professional and business services, health care, transportation and warehousing, and manufacturing, according to the federal data.

The monthly employment report is closely watched because it provides one of the broadest measures of conditions in the U.S. labor market. Job creation and unemployment figures can influence consumer confidence, business decisions and expectations about future economic policy. The October numbers showed employers continuing to expand payrolls despite concerns about whether economic growth and hiring would weaken.

The figures are preliminary and can change as the Labor Department receives additional information from employers. The upward revisions to August and September illustrate how initial monthly estimates can shift as more complete data become available.

Taken together, the October gain, lower unemployment rate and revisions to earlier months indicate a stronger labor market than economists had forecast heading into the final months of 2026.
