U.S. deportations send LGBTQ migrants to countries where homosexuality is criminalized

Reuters documents 40 cases as legal protections face new limits

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 6, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The Trump administration's expanded use of third-country deportations has sent LGBTQ migrants to nations where homosexuality is criminalized or where they face hostility and potential violence, according to a Reuters examination of 40 cases. Some of the migrants had previously received U.S. legal protection from being returned to their home countries because judges found they could face persecution or torture there.

Reuters identified migrants from countries including Iraq, Jamaica, Russia and several African nations who were removed to third countries under agreements the administration has reached with foreign governments. Some remain detained or otherwise confined in receiving countries, while others have returned to their countries of origin or gone into hiding. The administration has substantially expanded agreements allowing the United States to deport migrants to countries other than their own when their home countries will not accept them or when legal protections prevent direct removal.

One case documented by Reuters involved Abdou Latifou, a gay man from Togo who had received protection from deportation to his home country. The United States instead sent him to Sierra Leone, where same-sex sexual activity can carry severe criminal penalties. He was later returned to Togo and eventually fled to Ivory Coast. Other LGBTQ migrants have been sent to countries where they have no family, cultural or linguistic ties.

The administration says third-country deportations are lawful and necessary to enforce final removal orders. The Department of Homeland Security has maintained that migrants receive due process and that the government can seek alternative destinations when deportation to a person's home country is unavailable. The U.S. Supreme Court has allowed the administration to continue third-country removals while litigation over the policy proceeds.

CNN has separately documented LGBTQ migrants sent to third countries, including a 21-year-old bisexual woman from Ghana who received U.S. protection but was later deported to Cameroon, where same-sex relationships are criminalized.
