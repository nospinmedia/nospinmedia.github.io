CDC: Jalapeño Salmonella Outbreak Hits 27 States

Chipotle and QDOBA pull peppers; 345 sick, 36 hospitalized

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 6, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

A multistate outbreak of Salmonella Javiana linked to fresh jalapeño peppers has expanded to 27 states, with at least 345 illnesses and 36 hospitalizations, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. On August 5, 2026, the CDC said two major chains — Chipotle Mexican Grill and QDOBA — removed implicated peppers as investigators traced the contamination to crops grown in Sinaloa, Mexico and distributed in the U.S. by Coast Citrus Distributors.

Federal officials said most cases are concentrated in Colorado and Minnesota, with each reporting at least 110 patients. Minnesota health interviews show exposures at Mexican‑style restaurants between June 14 and July 14, aligning with the national case window. The Food and Drug Administration is conducting a traceback to determine whether contaminated jalapeños also entered grocery retail channels; consumers are advised to ask sellers about pepper sources until the check is complete. The FDA said Coast Citrus Distributors agreed to recall remaining product associated with the outbreak, while no deaths have been reported.

Both Chipotle and QDOBA said they proactively replaced or removed fresh jalapeños nationwide. After those steps, federal health officials indicated that neither chain is considered an ongoing risk to customers in this outbreak. Typical salmonella symptoms include diarrhea, fever, and cramps starting within hours to a few days; most people recover without treatment, though young children, older adults, and people with weakened immune systems face higher risk of severe illness.

Investigators noted that the peppers were imported from Mexico and supplied by Coast Citrus Distributors, providing a single distribution pathway consistent with a multistate pattern. While epidemiologic evidence and product distribution align behind fresh jalapeños as the likely vehicle, officials continue testing and traceback to clarify any additional points of sale. The last major U.S. jalapeño‑linked salmonella episode occurred in 2008, underscoring the vulnerability of raw peppers to contamination and the importance of rapid supplier recalls to interrupt transmission.
