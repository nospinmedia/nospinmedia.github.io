FDA Upgrades 19 Million Egg Recall To Highest Risk Level

Consumers in six states urged to check Texas-produced eggs

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has elevated a recall of more than 19 million shell eggs to a Class I designation—its highest risk level—warning there is a reasonable probability that consuming the affected eggs could cause serious health consequences or death. The action follows an ongoing Salmonella Enteritidis outbreak that has sickened at least 98 people across 17 states, resulting in 26 hospitalizations. No deaths have been reported.

The recalled eggs were voluntarily recalled on July 22 by Midwest Poultry Services, L.P. and were produced at two company farms in Texas between June 6 and July 3, 2026. The recall covers 1,589,577 dozen white shell eggs and brown cage-free shell eggs sold under the Kroger, Simple Truth, Brookshire's, Country Morning, and Sunups brand names. The eggs were distributed to retailers and food-service customers in Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, New Mexico, Oklahoma, and Texas, including Kroger stores in Texas and Louisiana and Brookshire Grocery stores across the six-state distribution area.

Consumers should check cartons for plant codes P-1950 or 0840962, Julian dates 157 through 184, and sell-by or best-by dates between July 20 and August 17, 2026. If those markings are present, the FDA advises consumers not to eat the eggs. Instead, they should return them to the retailer for a refund or discard them and thoroughly wash and sanitize any surfaces or containers that came into contact with the eggs.

Federal investigators traced the outbreak, in part, to the recalled eggs after environmental testing at the Texas farms detected Salmonella Enteritidis matching the outbreak strain through whole-genome sequencing. The FDA cautioned that the investigation remains ongoing and that additional sources of illness are still being examined. Health officials say young children, older adults, pregnant women, and people with weakened immune systems face the greatest risk of severe illness from Salmonella infections.
