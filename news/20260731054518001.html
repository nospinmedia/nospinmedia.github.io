Saudi Arabia Announces 14-Nation Maritime Defense Coalition To Protect Red Sea Shipping

Regional Initiative Aims To Secure Key Energy And Trade Routes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 31, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

Saudi Arabia announced plans to lead a new multinational maritime defense coalition designed to strengthen security for commercial shipping and energy transportation routes through the Red Sea, Bab el-Mandeb Strait and the Gulf of Aden. The initiative was unveiled following a defense meeting in Riyadh attended by military representatives from 43 countries and the European Union, with 14 nations issuing a joint statement supporting the proposed coalition.

According to the Saudi Ministry of Defense, the coalition will be headquartered in Riyadh and will focus on improving maritime security cooperation, intelligence sharing and coordination to protect international navigation and global trade. Countries publicly supporting the initiative include Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Jordan, Turkey, Pakistan, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Sudan, Djibouti, Somalia, Bangladesh, Nigeria and Yemen's internationally recognized government.

The announcement follows escalating attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthi movement against Saudi-linked shipping and the group's declaration of a naval blockade targeting Saudi Arabia. The Bab el-Mandeb Strait has become increasingly important to global energy markets as conflict and security concerns have reduced traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, forcing exporters to rely more heavily on Red Sea shipping routes.

Reuters reported that Oman and the United Arab Emirates were not among the countries supporting the initial declaration. Saudi officials said additional nations could join the coalition as governments complete their internal approval processes. The coalition is intended to complement broader international efforts to protect maritime commerce rather than replace existing security missions.

Analysts say the initiative reflects growing concern that continued attacks on commercial vessels could disrupt one of the world's most important maritime trade corridors. The Red Sea carries a significant share of global energy exports and international container traffic, making security in the region increasingly important for global supply chains and oil markets.
