U.S., Canada Race to Avert 50% Tariffs on $20B in Goods

Midnight Section 338 Deadline Hits 12:01 a.m. Aug. 19

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

U.S. and Canadian negotiators are in eleventh‑hour talks in Washington to head off 50% tariffs on about $20 billion in Canadian exports set to take effect at 12:01 a.m. Wednesday, August 19, 2026. Ordered by President Trump under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, the duties represent an extraordinary escalation and would mark the statute’s first known use in modern trade disputes.

On Monday, United States Trade Representative Jamieson Greer met in Washington with Dominic LeBlanc, Canada’s minister responsible for Canada‑U.S. trade, as both sides searched for a compromise before the deadline. The White House says the move responds to Canadian policies it argues disadvantage U.S. commerce, citing autos, alcohol and dairy. The targeted list covers roughly 5% of Canadian exports to the United States, according to public briefings, and the tariffs become operative one minute after midnight on Wednesday.

The levies would apply even to goods that qualify as originating under the United States‑Mexico‑Canada Agreement (USMCA), a significant break from past practice that could snarl tightly integrated supply chains. Affected categories include dairy products, alcoholic beverages, motor vehicles, and a range of other manufactured goods. Tariffs are paid by U.S. importers and can be passed through to customers, raising the risk of higher consumer prices and immediate compliance challenges for businesses with cross‑border inventory in transit.

Ottawa is pressing for broader tariff relief beyond the new Section 338 measures, seeking progress on longstanding U.S. duties on steel, aluminum, and softwood lumber. Prime Minister Mark Carney described the negotiations as “intense and delicate,” while officials on both sides have signaled that options remain on the table and that talks are continuing into the night. If no agreement is reached before the clock strikes 12:01 a.m. Aug. 19, Canada is weighing responses while trying to shield key sectors from further disruption.
