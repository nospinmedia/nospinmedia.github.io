USPS Reports $2.5B Quarterly Loss

Revenue Grows, But Financial Pressures Persist

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 9, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

United States Postal Service reported a net loss of $2.5 billion for its fiscal third quarter ended June 30, 2026, even as total operating revenue increased 6.1% year over year to $19.9 billion. Postmaster General David Steiner said the results reflected operational progress but warned the agency remains in a “severe liquidity crisis,” emphasizing that long-term financial stability will require regulatory and legislative changes.

Total operating expenses reached $22.5 billion, while the agency's non-GAAP “controllable” loss improved to $1.0 billion, down from $1.6 billion a year earlier. First-Class Mail volume continued to decline, while package revenue increased despite lower shipping volume, reflecting higher pricing. USPS credited revenue growth to package pricing, previous increases for First-Class Mail and Marketing Mail, an 8% transportation surcharge that took effect April 26, 2026, and the increase in the Forever stamp price to 82 cents in July 2026.

Steiner has told lawmakers that USPS's business model remains structurally imbalanced. Maintaining six-day delivery to roughly 170 million addresses costs about $3.4 billion annually, while about 70% of delivery routes and roughly 58% of the agency's approximately 18,000 post offices operate at a loss. To preserve cash, USPS suspended employer contributions to the Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS) beginning in April, a move expected to conserve about $2.5 billion through September 30, 2026, alongside temporary pension payment relief approved by the Postal Regulatory Commission.

USPS said long-term financial stability will depend on congressional action, including changes to borrowing authority and retirement funding requirements. The agency also warned that proposed unique ZIP Code legislation could add roughly $800 million in annual operating costs if enacted. While management says service and cost controls are improving, officials maintain additional reforms will be necessary to preserve universal six-day mail delivery.
