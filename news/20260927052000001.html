Nor’easter floods Northeast as New York and New Jersey declare emergencies

Power outages, damaging winds and dangerous coastal flooding hit the region

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 27, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

A powerful nor’easter battered the Northeast over the weekend, bringing coastal flooding, heavy rain, damaging winds and power outages from New Jersey and New York into southern New England. New York Gov. Kathy Hochul declared a state of emergency for New York City, Long Island and Westchester County, while New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill declared an emergency in several coastal and flood-prone counties as officials prepared for repeated high-tide flooding.

Conditions became especially dangerous along the coast on September 26, where wind gusts exceeded 60 mph in parts of New Jersey and Long Island. CBS News reported a gust of 81 mph in Montauk, while parts of the Jersey Shore received more than 2 inches of rain by Saturday afternoon. Coastal flood alerts covered the region, with forecasters warning that some locations could experience 2 to 3 feet of flooding above normally dry ground. High surf, beach erosion and dangerous rip currents were expected to continue through the weekend.

Flooding had already made some New Jersey roads impassable, and officials in Manasquan recommended voluntary evacuation for people in non-elevated homes in low-lying areas. In New York, water rescue teams were deployed to Nassau and Suffolk counties, thousands of utility workers were positioned to respond to outages, and ferry service in Suffolk County was suspended as officials warned residents to stay away from the water.

The storm also disrupted major weekend events. The Global Citizen Festival in Central Park and the New York Ironman Triathlon at Jones Beach were canceled, while other sporting events and concerts were postponed or rescheduled because of the weather.
