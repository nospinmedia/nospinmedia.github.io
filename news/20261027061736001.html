Trump offers $1 million for voter fraud tips

Reward requires information that leads to a criminal conviction

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 27, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump has announced a program offering rewards of up to $1 million for information about voter fraud that results in a criminal conviction, introducing a large financial incentive for reporting alleged election misconduct shortly before the 2026 midterm elections.

Under the initiative, people who provide information about alleged voter fraud could qualify for a payment if their information ultimately leads to a conviction. The offer is tied to successful criminal cases rather than simply submitting an allegation or identifying a suspected voting irregularity.

Trump announced the initiative as his administration and political allies continue emphasizing election security ahead of the midterms. The reward adds a private financial incentive to existing systems through which suspected election crimes can be reported to election officials and law enforcement agencies.

The announcement comes amid continuing national disputes over election administration, including mail-in ballots, voter eligibility, ballot handling and election monitoring. Those issues have generated lawsuits and political disagreements in multiple states as officials prepare for the November elections.

The size of the proposed reward is unusual for an election-related tip program. Rewards are commonly used by law enforcement and private organizations to encourage information about crimes, but Trump's initiative specifically links payments of as much as $1 million to information about voter fraud that produces a criminal conviction.

The requirement for a conviction establishes a significantly higher threshold than an allegation alone. A tip would need to produce evidence sufficient for prosecutors to pursue a criminal case and ultimately obtain a guilty verdict or plea before qualifying under the announced terms.

The program arrives as election officials, campaigns and political organizations are increasing monitoring and legal preparations ahead of voting. The announcement itself does not establish that voter fraud has occurred in the upcoming election, and individual allegations would still require investigation and evidence before criminal charges could be brought.
