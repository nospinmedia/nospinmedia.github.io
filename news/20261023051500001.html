Powerful earthquake kills at least 16 in Tibet

More than 12,000 relocated as hundreds of aftershocks follow

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 23, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck a remote, high-altitude area of China's Tibet Autonomous Region late October 22, killing at least 16 people and injuring 65, according to the latest local government figures reported by Reuters. Hundreds of homes collapsed and more than 12,000 people have been relocated as rescue and relief operations continue.

The earthquake struck at about 10:33 p.m. local time near Nagqu in northern Tibet. The U.S. Geological Survey placed the epicenter about 119 miles east-northeast of Nagqu and estimated the quake's depth at about 11 miles. Chinese measurements placed it at a shallower depth of about 6 miles. The affected area is sparsely populated and sits at an average elevation of roughly 15,700 feet, adding challenges for rescue crews working in cold conditions.

Local officials reported that at least 339 homes collapsed and livestock structures were also damaged. Rockfalls and debris affected roads, while communications were disrupted in some areas. Railway service through the affected region was temporarily halted as authorities assessed damage.

The China Earthquake Networks Center reported that more than 400 aftershocks had been recorded by Friday morning. Emergency crews, police and other responders were sent into affected communities to search damaged buildings and move residents to safer locations. Relief supplies included prefabricated housing, thousands of tents and folding beds, flashlights and winter clothing.

Chinese President Xi Jinping called for extensive rescue and medical efforts. Officials said damage assessments and search operations were continuing, meaning casualty and damage figures could change as crews reach isolated communities.

The earthquake is the strongest to strike China since January 2024, according to Reuters. Tibet and surrounding areas are highly seismically active, and a separate earthquake in Tibet's Tingri County killed at least 126 people in January 2025.
