Press Groups Sue President Trump Over Paid Truth Social Feed

Federal lawsuit challenges early-access service for official posts

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 13, 2026 at 10:17 AM ET

August 12, 2026 — Two press-freedom organizations filed a federal lawsuit against President Trump, the Executive Office of the President, and senior White House aides, seeking to halt a new Truth Social service that sells paying subscribers millisecond-priority access to presidential posts. The complaint, filed by The Intercept and the Freedom of the Press Foundation in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, challenges Truth API, a premium data feed operated by Trump Media & Technology Group.

The service costs up to $100,000 per month, with discounted long-term plans beginning around $60,000. Plaintiffs argue the arrangement gives wealthy subscribers earlier access to official presidential communications that can influence financial markets. The lawsuit alleges the practice violates the First Amendment by denying equal access to government communications and the Fifth Amendment by conditioning access to certain public information on costly subscription fees. The plaintiffs are asking the court to block the administration from posting official government information exclusively on Truth Social while the paid early-access service remains available.

According to public statements by Trump Media, interim CEO Kevin McGurn said the company had signed 10 customers, primarily high-frequency trading firms, and that the feed delivers information in milliseconds. The company has defended the product as a standard commercial data-licensing service and denied attempting to restrict public access to the president's statements.

The filing follows additional scrutiny from lawmakers. On July 28, 2026, Senators Elizabeth Warren and Adam Schiff asked the Securities and Exchange Commission to examine whether selling priority access to presidential posts could undermine market integrity. AP reporting says the lawsuit also names Deputy Chief of Staff Dan Scavino and Executive Assistant Natalie J. Harp as defendants. The court has not yet ruled on the request for a preliminary injunction.
