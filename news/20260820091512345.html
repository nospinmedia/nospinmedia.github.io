Treasury Doubles Long-Bond Buybacks To $4B Per Operation

30-year yields retreat from 19-year highs after surprise move

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 20, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The U.S. Department of the Treasury moved to stabilize long-term government bond trading on August 19, 2026, announcing it will at least double the size of its long-end liquidity buyback operations to $4 billion per auction, up from $2 billion. The step, ordered by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, targets older, less-liquid nominal coupon securities in the 10-year to 20-year and 20-year to 30-year sectors where trading conditions have recently been strained.

Markets rallied immediately after the announcement. The benchmark 30-year Treasury yield slipped from an intraday high near 5.34%, a 19-year peak, to roughly 5.19% later in the session, easing some pressure on long-term borrowing costs that influence mortgages, business financing, and other forms of consumer credit.

Officials said the change takes effect September 9, 2026, and will remain in place through November 4, 2026, the remainder of the current refunding quarter. The overall quarterly liquidity-buyback authorization remains $38 billion, with existing capacity shifted toward longer-dated securities rather than increasing the total amount. Treasury officials said the goal is to improve market functioning by reducing illiquidity in older bonds, not to alter the government's overall borrowing plans.

Analysts said the larger buybacks could provide short-term relief to the most volatile part of the Treasury market, but whether long-term interest rates continue to decline will depend largely on future inflation data and upcoming Federal Reserve policy decisions. Recent volatility in long-dated Treasury yields has tightened financial conditions across the economy, affecting mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs, and other forms of long-term credit.
