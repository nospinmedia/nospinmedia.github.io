Congo Ebola outbreak surpasses 8,000 confirmed cases

3,901 deaths make it the country's deadliest outbreak

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 29, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

The ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has surpassed 8,000 confirmed cases, becoming the country's largest and deadliest Ebola outbreak on record as health authorities struggle to interrupt transmission across a growing geographic area.

As of September 26, the World Health Organization reported 8,067 confirmed cases and 3,901 deaths in Congo, a case-fatality rate of about 48%. The outbreak, declared in May 2026, is caused by the Bundibugyo virus and has spread to 63 health zones across seven provinces. It is now the world's second-largest recorded Ebola outbreak, behind the 2014–2016 West Africa epidemic, which caused more than 28,000 cases and 11,000 deaths.

Containing the outbreak remains difficult. WHO has cited delays in detecting cases and getting patients into appropriate care, while conflict, insecurity, population displacement and limited access to basic services have complicated surveillance and contact tracing. Fifty health workers have died, according to reporting from The Associated Press. In Ituri, the province hardest hit by the outbreak, use of routine health services has fallen 13% as the epidemic strains the health system and fear keeps some residents away from medical facilities.

Misinformation and distrust have created additional obstacles. Health workers and facilities have faced attacks, while officials continue efforts to persuade communities to report illnesses, cooperate with contact tracing and follow safe burial practices. WHO says the Bundibugyo species involved in the outbreak currently has no licensed vaccine or specific treatment, although candidate vaccines and treatments are being studied.

The outbreak also continues to expand geographically. WHO says recently affected areas include locations near international borders, increasing concern about cross-border transmission. Although infections have declined in some areas, health officials say sustained community transmission and unidentified chains of infection mean the outbreak is not yet under control.
