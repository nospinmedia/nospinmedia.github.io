Joe diGenova abruptly quits DOJ probe into Trump foes

Lead prosecutor says more time was needed to build cases

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 13, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Joe diGenova, the veteran prosecutor leading a sweeping Justice Department investigation into whether officials from the Obama and Biden administrations conspired against President Trump, abruptly resigned on September 10 amid disagreements over the pace of the inquiry. DiGenova, 81, told CBS News that he submitted his resignation to Attorney General Todd Blanche at 3 p.m. Thursday. His wife, attorney Victoria Toensing, who also worked with the prosecution team, said she would leave the department the following day.

DiGenova did not publicly identify a specific dispute that caused his departure. He separately told The Washington Post that he believed evidence existed to build cases but that time was needed to develop them, adding that people differed over how long that should take. His resignation comes after months of pressure within the administration for faster progress in investigations involving people President Trump has accused of wrongdoing. No indictments have been announced in the conspiracy investigation.

The inquiry is being conducted from Fort Pierce, Florida, and examines an unusual legal theory that officials engaged in a years-long conspiracy to violate Trump's rights through investigations beginning with scrutiny of possible connections between his 2016 campaign and Russia. Among the former officials receiving investigative scrutiny are former CIA Director John Brennan and former Director of National Intelligence James Clapper. Brennan's attorneys have said they were informed he is a target of an investigation involving whether he made false statements to Congress, allegations he denies.

The investigation had recently become more active. Lawyers for some witnesses were notified that their clients would receive grand jury subpoenas, while investigators sought voluntary interviews with former FBI agents involved in the investigation of Trump's retention of classified documents. DiGenova had taken leadership of the probe after career national security prosecutor Maria Medetis Long left the investigation following concerns about its direction.

Despite the leadership change, diGenova said he expects the investigation to continue under U.S. Attorney Jason Reding Quiñones in South Florida.
