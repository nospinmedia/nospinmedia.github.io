Iran says seven oil tankers were hit in Strait of Hormuz

President Trump disputes Tehran's account as tensions remain high

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 23, 2026 at 10:15 AM ET

Iran says seven oil tankers were attacked and damaged in the Strait of Hormuz, adding to a series of incidents around the strategically important waterway as tensions between Iran and the United States remain high. The circumstances surrounding the latest reported attacks have not been independently confirmed.

Iranian authorities said the tankers were struck over a period of several hours and blamed hostile forces for the attacks. Officials did not immediately provide publicly verifiable evidence establishing who carried them out. The reports come amid a broader series of attacks and security incidents involving commercial vessels in and around the Persian Gulf.

President Trump disputed Iran's account, saying Tehran was fabricating reports of attacks on ships in an effort to push global oil prices higher. He did not provide evidence supporting that accusation. Iran has rejected the U.S. claims and warned that additional American military action could bring a wider response.

The conflicting accounts come as commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz faces heightened security concerns. The narrow waterway connects the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and is one of the world's most important energy routes, carrying roughly one-fifth of global petroleum liquids consumption in recent years. Disruptions or threats to shipping there can therefore affect oil markets far beyond the Middle East.

The latest reports represent a further development in an already escalating pattern of maritime incidents. No Spin Media previously reported that attacks on vessels around the Strait of Hormuz had reached a wartime high, according to maritime monitoring data. The new Iranian claim identifies seven additional tankers as damaged but does not resolve uncertainty over responsibility for the attacks.

Shipping companies and governments continue to monitor the region as the risk of further incidents remains elevated. The competing claims from Washington and Tehran also make independent verification of individual attacks especially important as the situation develops.
