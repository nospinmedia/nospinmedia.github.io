Iran and Oman advance temporary Hormuz shipping corridor

Mine-clearing plan moves forward as Tehran demands U.S. lift blockade

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Iran and Oman are moving toward a temporary shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz, pairing the proposed route with a joint mine-clearing project as the two countries seek to restore safer commercial navigation through the strategically important waterway. The proposal followed talks in Tehran between Omani Foreign Minister Badr al-Busaidi and Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, but no temporary or permanent corridor has yet been formally established.

A joint statement from the two governments said the ministers discussed a phased framework that would create a temporary navigational corridor and establish a joint project to clear mines from the strait. Technical negotiations are expected to continue over a permanent shipping route, future administration of the waterway, information sharing, traffic management and security services. The two sides also said countries bordering the Persian Gulf should be included in further discussions.

Oman's foreign minister said he was hopeful that practical arrangements for a temporary corridor could be announced soon. Iran, however, has attached a major condition to reopening the waterway. Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said on August 26 that the United States must first fulfill commitments Iran says Washington has violated, including lifting its blockade of the strait. Most commercial traffic through Hormuz remains sharply restricted amid the continuing U.S.-Iran confrontation.

The diplomatic effort comes alongside conflicting claims over the condition of the waterway. President Trump said on August 25 that the U.S. Navy had informed him that all mines in international waters of the Strait of Hormuz had been removed or detonated. Trump also warned that vessels attempting to place new mines would be destroyed. Iran and Oman nevertheless continue to describe mine-clearing as part of their proposed framework, underscoring that significant disagreements remain over security and control of the strait.
