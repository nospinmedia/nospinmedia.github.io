U.S. diesel hits highest price ever recorded

National average reaches record $5.85 a gallon

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 4, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The average price of diesel fuel in the United States reached an all-time record of about $5.85 a gallon on September 4, surpassing the previous national peak reached during the energy shock of 2022. The milestone means diesel has never been this expensive in the national price series being reported by major fuel trackers, although prices adjusted for inflation have been higher during earlier energy crises. The new nominal-dollar record comes after GasBuddy's live national average crossed $5.820 on September 3, narrowly exceeding its previous $5.819 record from June 17, 2022, before prices moved still higher Friday.

The increase matters far beyond drivers of diesel vehicles. Diesel powers much of the country's commercial trucking, agricultural equipment, construction machinery and other freight operations. Higher fuel expenses can therefore work their way into the cost of groceries and other consumer products as businesses pay more to harvest, refrigerate, transport and deliver goods. The timing is particularly difficult for agriculture as the fall harvest increases demand for diesel-powered farm equipment and trucks.

The record comes amid an unusually tight supply environment. U.S. distillate inventories, which include diesel and heating oil, averaged their lowest August level since 1982, according to federal energy data cited by Reuters. East Coast distillate inventories fell to a record-low 19.3 million barrels, while U.S. refineries were already operating at about 98% of capacity during the week ending August 28. The diesel crack spread, a measure of the difference between crude-oil costs and the value of refined diesel, reached a record $108.02 per barrel, another indication of severe tightness in the market.

Global disruptions are adding pressure. The war involving the United States, Israel and Iran has disrupted energy flows through the Middle East, while Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries and restrictions on Russian diesel exports have further constrained international supplies. Reuters reported that U.S. diesel prices have risen roughly 55% since the Iran war began in February. With inventories already unusually low, analysts warn that additional demand from the harvest and the approaching heating season could keep pressure on prices.

For Americans, the significance of $5.85 is straightforward: the national diesel average has moved beyond the nominal record established during the 2022 energy crisis, creating another potential source of transportation and consumer-price pressure.
