FBI Investigates Suspected Breach Of National Crime Database

Critical Law Enforcement System Contains Warrants And Criminal Records

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 21, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The FBI is investigating a suspected cyberattack targeting the National Crime Information Center (NCIC), a nationwide system used by federal, state and local law enforcement agencies to share sensitive criminal justice information. The intrusion was first reported on Friday, November 20, by CNN and NBC News, citing people briefed on the investigation. The FBI had not publicly established who was responsible or the full extent of any unauthorized access.

The NCIC is a major operational tool for U.S. law enforcement. It contains information including arrest warrants, criminal histories and background-check records, allowing officers across the country to retrieve information during investigations and encounters. A former FBI official told CNN that officers rely on the database every day to identify wanted fugitives and verify warrants, making the accuracy and integrity of its records particularly important. Investigators have not publicly confirmed whether information was stolen, altered or otherwise compromised.

The suspected intrusion would be the second reported breach involving an FBI system in recent months. In September, the digital extortion group ShinyHunters compromised an FBI recruitment website, exposing personal information belonging to current and former FBI employees. Authorities have not publicly established that the same attackers were responsible for both incidents.

The FBI investigation follows another federal cyber incident reported Friday. Hackers breached a U.S. Department of Agriculture system used to distribute payments to farmers earlier in the week. Officials said no other USDA systems were known to have been targeted as of Friday. No connection between the USDA and FBI incidents has been established.

The incidents come amid broader scrutiny of federal cybersecurity and technology reliability. President Trump fired National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross on Thursday, November 19, and the administration tapped Defense Department Chief Information Officer Kirsten Davies to take over the role. Recent disruptions have also included a major Microsoft outage and the nationwide FAA ground stop, although the FAA failure was attributed to technical problems rather than a cyberattack.

For the NCIC investigation, authorities have not publicly identified an attacker, explained how the suspected intrusion occurred or confirmed that sensitive law-enforcement records were removed or manipulated.
