Russian strikes hit Ukraine’s energy network ahead of talks

Missiles and drones cause outages as nine are wounded

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Russian missile and drone attacks struck Ukraine’s energy and transportation infrastructure ahead of planned talks involving Ukraine, Russia and the United States, wounding at least nine people and causing electricity and water disruptions in several areas.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia launched 172 drones and 36 ballistic missiles during the overnight attack on November 6-7, targeting 13 Ukrainian regions. Ukrainian officials said the strikes damaged electrical substations and other energy infrastructure, while Kyiv experienced power and water outages. The capital’s Northern Bridge, a major route across the Dnipro River, was also damaged and temporarily closed for repairs.

One of the damaged substations in the Kyiv region connects the Rivne and Khmelnytskyi nuclear power plants with Kyiv and central Ukraine. Substations were also damaged in the Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr, Rivne, Kharkiv and Dnipropetrovsk regions, according to Ukrainian officials.

The attacks are part of a broader Russian campaign against Ukraine’s energy system as colder weather approaches. Analysts cited by CNN said Russia has intensified attacks on energy infrastructure earlier than in previous years and broadened the range of economic targets. Zelenskyy accused Moscow of increasing the pressure ahead of negotiations. Russia says its strikes target facilities connected to Ukraine’s military and energy sectors.

The latest barrage comes shortly before delegations from Ukraine, Russia and the United States are scheduled to participate in trilateral negotiations in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, on November 10-11. The talks are expected to address possible steps toward ending the war, while fighting and long-range attacks continue.

Ukraine has also renewed calls for additional air-defense support as Russian ballistic missile and drone attacks continue to challenge its ability to protect critical infrastructure.
