Australia Bars AI-Generated Songs From Official Charts

New ARIA rules require music to be substantially human-made

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 25, 2026 at 6:51 PM ET

The Australian Recording Industry Association (ARIA) has adopted new rules that make wholly AI-generated songs ineligible for Australia's official music charts, becoming one of the first national chart organizations to establish formal eligibility standards for generative artificial intelligence. The updated Charts Code of Practice takes effect with charts published on August 28, with an official chart date of August 31, and is intended to preserve recognition for recordings primarily created by human artists.

Under the revised policy, songs must be substantially human made to qualify for ARIA's rankings. The organization said human creators must be responsible for the songwriting and perform the lead vocals and principal musical elements. AI remains permitted as a production tool for tasks such as audio editing, mixing, mastering, synthesizer programming and other technical functions, provided it does not replace the primary creative contributions that determine authorship.

ARIA Chief Executive Annabelle Herd said the changes are designed to ensure the charts continue recognizing human artistic achievement as AI-generated music becomes increasingly sophisticated. The association said it will align its approach with evolving international guidance developed by organizations including the International Federation of the Phonographic Industry (IFPI). The decision follows growing debate across the music industry over copyright, artist consent and the commercial impact of generative AI systems trained on existing recordings.

The policy comes after AI-generated songs and digitally recreated performances attracted widespread attention and raised questions about whether recordings created largely by algorithms should compete directly with human performers. Music industry organizations have warned that expanding use of generative AI could significantly affect future earnings for songwriters, performers and producers if clear standards for authorship and licensing are not established.
