Trump-Backed World Liberty Wins Conditional U.S. Bank Charter

Federal approval would bring $4 billion USD1 stablecoin under OCC supervision

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 10:30 AM ET

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) granted preliminary conditional approval on August 14, 2026, for World Liberty Trust Company, National Association, allowing the Trump-family-backed cryptocurrency venture to move closer to operating a federally supervised national trust bank. If it receives final approval, the new institution would issue, redeem and safeguard USD1, World Liberty Financial's dollar-backed stablecoin, which has grown to roughly $4 billion in circulation.

The proposed trust bank would not operate like a conventional retail bank. It would not accept customer deposits or make traditional loans. Instead, World Liberty Trust plans to provide stablecoin issuance and redemption, digital-asset custody, settlement and related services primarily for institutional customers. USD1 reserves are currently held by BitGo, but World Liberty plans to bring issuance and custody operations under its own federally regulated entity once all OCC requirements are satisfied.

The approval remains conditional. Before opening, World Liberty Trust must meet OCC requirements that include maintaining at least $20 million in Tier 1 capital, completing required compliance and internal-control systems, obtaining regulatory approval for its final operating plan and management structure, and passing a pre-opening examination. The charter would place the company under continuing OCC supervision rather than requiring separate operating licenses in individual states for activities covered by the national charter.

World Liberty Financial was launched in 2024 and is closely associated with President Trump and members of his family. The OCC's review has attracted scrutiny from Democratic lawmakers and banking groups that raised conflict-of-interest and financial-stability concerns. The company and the administration have rejected allegations of improper influence, while the OCC has said applications are evaluated under established chartering standards and ethics requirements.

The preliminary approval does not authorize the bank to begin operations immediately; final OCC authorization is still required.
