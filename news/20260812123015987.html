Severe Storms Leave Widespread Damage Across Midwest

Recovery continues after tornadoes, flooding and major travel disruptions

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 8:03 AM ET

Severe thunderstorms swept across the Midwest on August 11, 2026, bringing destructive winds, torrential rainfall, suspected tornadoes, and widespread damage across Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, and Kentucky. The storms temporarily halted flights at Chicago O'Hare International Airport and Chicago Midway International Airport, damaged homes and businesses, and left hundreds of thousands of utility customers without electricity as crews began restoring service.

Communities across the region reported downed trees, damaged buildings, flooded roads, and extensive power line damage. Meteorologists identified several suspected tornadoes in the southern Chicago suburbs and parts of northwest Indiana, including communities such as Tinley Park, Flossmoor, and Homewood, where emergency crews responded to structural damage. Airport operations gradually resumed after weather conditions improved, although many flights continued to experience delays and cancellations.

The storms were also linked to multiple fatalities under investigation. Ohio Gov. Mike DeWine said a resident of Roseville died during a medical emergency after floodwaters delayed emergency responders. In Portage, Indiana, authorities are investigating whether severe weather contributed to a home explosion that killed one person. Officials cautioned that the cause of the explosion has not yet been determined.

Utility crews continued restoring power and local officials began assessing storm damage while the National Weather Service warned that additional rounds of severe weather remained possible across parts of the region. Residents were urged to avoid flooded roadways, stay clear of downed power lines, and monitor updated forecasts as recovery efforts continued.
