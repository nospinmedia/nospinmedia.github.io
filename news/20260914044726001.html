Unvaccinated Pennsylvania woman dies after measles infection

State outbreak has reached 676 cases across 37 counties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 14, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

An unvaccinated 40-year-old woman in Jefferson County, Pennsylvania, died after developing complications associated with measles, according to county officials, as the state's outbreak continues to spread. The Jefferson County Coroner's Office said the woman died on September 12, 2026, after suffering respiratory failure and had underlying lung conditions including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and asthma.

County officials said the woman had been exposed to someone with a laboratory-confirmed measles infection before becoming ill. Jefferson County Coroner Greg Furlong classified the death as measles-related, while the Pennsylvania Department of Health said it is conducting an epidemiological review to determine whether the case meets the state's criteria for a measles-associated death. If confirmed by the state, it would become Pennsylvania's third measles-associated death of 2026.

The state's first two measles-associated deaths of the year involved infants in Lancaster County and were announced in August. Neither infant was old enough to receive the routinely recommended first dose of the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine. One infant died from measles infection while the other died from a ruptured spleen, with measles listed as a contributing condition.

Pennsylvania reported 676 confirmed measles cases across 37 counties as of September 11, including 124 hospitalizations. Only four confirmed cases involved people appropriately vaccinated for their age, according to state health officials. The Department of Health says two doses of the MMR vaccine are about 97% effective at preventing measles. The disease can cause serious complications including pneumonia and brain swelling, and state officials say nearly 20% of Pennsylvania patients in the current outbreak have required hospitalization.
