Trump promises $5,000 payments if Republicans keep Congress

Proposal would cost more than $1 trillion and require congressional approval

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 10, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

President Trump says every adult U.S. citizen would receive a $5,000 payment if Republicans retain control of both the House and Senate in the November 3 midterm elections, tying what he called a "Trump Dividend" directly to the election outcome. Trump announced the proposal Wednesday during the Republican midterm convention in Dallas and said recipients would be required to spend the money in the United States.

Trump provided few details about how the payments would work or how they would be funded. With nearly 270 million adults living in the United States, the Associated Press estimates that sending $5,000 to every eligible adult citizen would cost more than $1 trillion. Congress would have to authorize the spending because a president cannot independently distribute federal money on that scale.

Vice President JD Vance later suggested that wealthy Americans might not receive the payments, which would differ from Trump's statement that every adult citizen would qualify. Vance also suggested tariff revenue could help finance the proposal. The White House had not released a detailed eligibility formula or financing plan following Trump's announcement.

The federal government is already running an annual budget deficit approaching $1.8 trillion, while the national debt recently exceeded $40 trillion. Tariff revenue collected by the government would be substantially less than the amount required for universal $5,000 payments under the proposal described by Trump.

Trump previously proposed smaller tariff-funded dividends, but Wednesday's announcement explicitly conditioned the new $5,000 payment on Republicans winning both chambers of Congress. Sen. Bernie Moreno of Ohio said he would prepare legislation so Congress could consider the dividend immediately after the election if Republicans prevail.

The proposal is a campaign promise rather than an enacted federal benefit, and no payments have been authorized or scheduled.
