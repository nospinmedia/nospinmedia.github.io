Poland Says Evidence Points To Russian Cruise Missile That Crashed Deep Inside NATO Territory

Tusk Says Military Was Prepared To Shoot It Down During Massive Ukraine Attack

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 30, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said evidence indicates a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile entered Polish airspace during Russia's latest large-scale missile attack on Ukraine before crashing in an open field approximately 100 kilometers (62 miles) inside Poland. The missile came down near the village of Tarnawa-Kolonia in Biłgoraj County, leaving a large crater but causing no injuries or damage to nearby buildings. Emergency responders, bomb disposal teams, and military personnel secured the area while investigators examined the wreckage.

According to Tusk, Polish military radar tracked the missile after it crossed into NATO airspace during the overnight assault on western Ukraine. Poland's Armed Forces Operational Command scrambled F-16 fighter jets and activated air defense systems as Russian missiles and drones approached the country's eastern border. Tusk said Polish forces were prepared to intercept the missile if necessary, but it lost altitude and crashed before defensive action became necessary. Officials emphasized that the investigation remains ongoing, although preliminary evidence strongly points to the missile being Russian.

The incident occurred during one of Russia's largest recent missile and drone attacks against Ukraine, prompting heightened military readiness across Poland and neighboring NATO members. Polish defense officials remain in close contact with allied governments while investigators continue analyzing missile fragments to formally determine the weapon's origin.

Prime Minister Tusk canceled scheduled travel to participate in emergency national security meetings with senior defense officials, including Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz and President Karol Nawrocki. The airspace violation renewed concerns about the risks posed to neighboring NATO countries as Russia continues launching long-range missile attacks near the alliance's eastern frontier.
