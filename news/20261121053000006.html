U.K. And EU Sign Biggest Deal Since Brexit

Food Checks, Carbon Markets And Youth Travel Reshape Post-Brexit Ties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 21, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The United Kingdom and European Union have sealed their most significant package of agreements since Brexit, covering food trade, carbon markets and expanded opportunities for young people to live, work and study across the English Channel.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham led the U.K. delegation at the Brussels summit on Friday, November 20, completing agreements intended to reduce some of the economic and practical barriers created after Britain's departure from the EU. The summit had previously been postponed from July following the resignation of former Prime Minister Keir Starmer.

A new Sanitary and Phytosanitary agreement will eliminate most border checks and health certificates on food, plant and animal products moving between Britain and the EU. It will also remove many "Not for EU" labels introduced after Brexit. The British government estimates the changes could eventually add up to £5.1 billion a year to the U.K. economy.

The two sides also agreed to link their Emissions Trading Systems, aligning their carbon markets and allowing covered British and EU exports, including steel and aluminum, to avoid certain carbon border charges. The U.K. government estimates the arrangement could add up to £3.8 billion annually to the British economy and affect approximately £6.5 billion in exports.

For younger people, the new Youth Experience Scheme will allow up to 100,000 people under age 30 from each side to live, work or study in the other's territory for as long as three years. The program has numerical and eligibility limits and does not restore the unrestricted freedom of movement that existed before Brexit.

One significant issue remains unresolved. Britain and the EU gave themselves another six months to negotiate whether EU students attending British universities should qualify for lower domestic tuition rates rather than international fees.

The agreements do not reverse Brexit or return Britain to the EU's single market or customs union. Instead, they establish closer cooperation in selected areas while keeping the U.K. outside the bloc.
