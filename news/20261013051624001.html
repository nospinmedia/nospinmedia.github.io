Federal Agents Search Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington Home

DOJ Confirms Criminal Probe as Democrats Question Timing Before Midterms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 13, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Federal agents searched the Washington, D.C., home of U.S. Representative Ilhan Omar on Monday, October 12, 2026, as part of an ongoing criminal investigation. The operation, conducted by Homeland Security Investigations (HSI), immediately sparked political controversy because it targeted a sitting member of Congress just three weeks before the November midterm elections.

The Department of Justice (DOJ) confirmed that investigators executed a judicially authorized search warrant but declined to disclose the investigation's specific purpose or scope. Federal agents blocked off the street surrounding Omar's residence, and officers were observed carrying boxes marked Seized Property Evidence from the property. Authorities have not publicly detailed what evidence was collected or announced criminal charges against Omar.

The Minnesota Democrat, who represents Minnesota's 5th Congressional District, condemned the operation as politically motivated. Omar accused President Trump of pursuing a campaign of intimidation against her, declaring, "I will not be silenced." She has repeatedly denied allegations concerning her immigration history and marriage that have circulated among conservative critics.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries and other senior Democrats criticized the search, questioning why federal authorities conducted such a visible investigative operation so close to an election. The Justice Department has historically maintained guidelines discouraging overt investigative actions involving political candidates within 60 days of an election, although those guidelines do not constitute an absolute prohibition.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin previously acknowledged an ongoing investigation that he suggested involved possible irregularities in Omar's family's immigration paperwork. Vice President JD Vance has also publicly accused Omar of immigration fraud, allegations she denies. However, federal prosecutors have not publicly established that those allegations form the basis of the current search.

Asked about the operation, President Trump denied involvement, saying he had "nothing to do" with the search and that the investigation was up to the Justice Department.

The investigation remains ongoing, with no public explanation of the evidence supporting the warrant or whether prosecutors intend to pursue charges.
