Iran plans new restricted zone near Strait of Hormuz

U.S.-Iran tanker strikes deepen shipping confrontation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 7, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Iran says it plans to establish a new restricted maritime zone near the Strait of Hormuz, adding another layer of uncertainty to one of the world's most important oil-shipping routes after a weekend of direct attacks involving Iranian forces, U.S. warships and commercial tankers. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said Tehran would publish details of the zone and a new international shipping corridor as tensions with the United States continue to rise.

The announcement follows a sharp military escalation on September 5. U.S. Central Command said Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps fired ballistic missiles toward a U.S. aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer operating in regional waters. CENTCOM said both warships evaded the attacks and no American personnel were injured. U.S. forces then struck three Iranian crude-oil carriers, permanently disabling the M/T Downy near Kharg Island and the M/T Stark 1 near Jask, while completely destroying the unladen M/T Kylo, also known as the Noxen, in the Gulf of Oman after its crew was directed to abandon ship.

Iran subsequently said its forces targeted three oil tankers traveling what Tehran considers an unauthorized route through the Strait of Hormuz, along with three vessels it described as U.S.-affiliated. Iran also claimed it struck an unmanned American military vessel attempting to enter the strait, but CENTCOM denied that claim. Iranian officials have warned that further American attacks could bring a stronger response.

The Strait of Hormuz remains critical to global energy supplies, with roughly 9 million barrels of oil per day moving through the waterway according to current reporting. The combination of competing maritime restrictions, military strikes and threats against commercial shipping has increased concern that another round of attacks could further disrupt Gulf traffic and energy markets.
