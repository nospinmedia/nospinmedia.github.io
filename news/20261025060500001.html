France erupts in protests again as 400,000 take to streets

Nearly 1,000 arrested following another weekend of nationwide unrest

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 25, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

More than 400,000 people demonstrated across France on Saturday in another major round of nationwide protests, with authorities reporting nearly 1,000 arrests as clashes broke out between police and some demonstrators.

The demonstrations follow another period of serious unrest in France that saw widespread rioting, looting and more than 2,400 arrests. Authorities responded to the latest protests by deploying approximately 70,000 police officers and gendarmes across the country in an effort to prevent a repeat of the earlier violence.

Saturday's demonstrations included protests over education funding, living costs and economic inequality. Students have called for more teachers, improved school facilities and additional financial assistance, while workers and other demonstrators have raised broader concerns about household expenses and economic conditions.

The French Interior Ministry estimated nationwide participation at more than 400,000, while labor organizers offered substantially higher estimates. In Paris, tens of thousands joined demonstrations that were largely peaceful for much of the day before clashes developed involving some protesters and police.

Authorities used tear gas and water cannons in Paris as officers confronted people throwing objects and fireworks. Similar confrontations were reported elsewhere in the country. The arrests occurred across France and do not mean that those detained were representative of the hundreds of thousands of people who participated peacefully.

The renewed demonstrations add to mounting pressure on President Emmanuel Macron's government as France experiences repeated episodes of large-scale protest and unrest. Macron is serving the final months of his presidency and is constitutionally barred from seeking a third consecutive term in the 2027 presidential election.
