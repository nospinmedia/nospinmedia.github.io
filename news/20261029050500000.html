Spain train collision kills at least 68 people

Investigators examine why stopped signal was overridden

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

At least 68 people were killed and about 80 injured after two high-speed trains collided in Spain's Toledo province on October 28, making it the country's second deadly high-speed rail collision this year. Rescue crews continued searching the wreckage Thursday because officials said they could not rule out finding additional victims.

The collision occurred near Urda, south of Madrid, when a state-run Renfe high-speed train struck the rear of a stationary Iryo train on the route toward Seville. Renfe said 397 passengers were aboard its train, while 147 were traveling on the Iryo train. Several carriages derailed, with three plunging down an embankment. Rail service connecting Madrid with parts of southern Spain was suspended following the crash.

Investigators are focusing on what happened after a computer communications failure disrupted the signaling system along a 63-kilometer stretch of track. Transport Minister Óscar Puente said the failure caused trains to stop automatically. The Renfe train was subsequently authorized by the control center to resume traveling at normal speed even though the Iryo train remained stopped ahead. Puente said the train should either not have been cleared to proceed or should have been ordered to travel at low speed. Authorities have not completed their investigation into the sequence of events.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared three days of national mourning and promised a full investigation. The crash follows another high-speed collision in January 2026 that killed 46 people. Officials have said the two accidents appear unrelated, but the disasters have renewed scrutiny of rail safety on Spain's extensive high-speed network.

Wednesday's collision was Spain's deadliest rail disaster since a 2013 derailment that killed 80 people.
