Murder Trial Opens for Keefe D in 1996 Tupac Shakur Killing

Jury selection begins in Las Vegas nearly 30 years after rapper's death

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 11, 2026 at 2:46 PM ET

Jury selection began on August 10, 2026, in Clark County District Court in Las Vegas for the murder trial of Duane "Keffe D" Davis, who is charged in connection with the 1996 killing of rapper Tupac Shakur. Davis, 63, has pleaded not guilty to one count of murder with the use of a deadly weapon with the intent to promote, further or assist a criminal gang and has remained in custody since his 2023 arrest.

The case is being heard by Judge Carli Kierny and is expected to last about a month. Prosecutors do not allege Davis fired the fatal shots but contend he helped organize the attack following a confrontation involving his nephew, Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson, and Shakur inside the MGM Grand hours before the shooting. Under Nevada law, a person may be convicted for participating in or directing a criminal act even if they were not the shooter.

Prosecutors plan to rely heavily on Davis's own statements, including his 2019 memoir, Compton Street Legend, and recorded interviews in which he discussed the events surrounding the killing. Defense attorneys argue those statements were exaggerated for entertainment purposes and should not be viewed as factual admissions. The court previously ruled that portions of the memoir and a 2008 police interview may be presented as evidence during the trial.

If convicted, Davis could face life in prison. The trial marks the first criminal prosecution stemming from one of the most closely watched unsolved killings in modern music history, nearly three decades after Shakur died from gunshot wounds sustained in the drive-by shooting.
