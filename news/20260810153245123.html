Netanyahu Rejects 15-Point Gaza Plan

Israel says no troop withdrawal until Hamas disarms

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 10, 2026 at 11:58 AM ET

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu rejected a new 15-point Gaza plan backed by President Trump, saying Israeli forces will not withdraw from the territory until Hamas is fully disarmed. Netanyahu told his Cabinet on August 9, 2026 that Israel does not accept the document, while confirming that discussions with the United States are continuing over elements of the proposal.

The disagreement centers largely on the timing of disarmament and Israeli troop withdrawals. The plan calls for Hamas to begin giving up its weapons while Israel halts attacks and starts withdrawing from territory it controls. Israeli forces currently control more than half of Gaza, a Palestinian enclave of about 2 million people. Netanyahu said withdrawal would not begin until Hamas is genuinely disarmed, including its heavy and lighter weapons. U.S. envoy Nikolay Mladenov has said Israeli forces would withdraw sector by sector after weapons taken from Hamas are rendered unusable.

Hamas says it remains committed to the roadmap. Senior Hamas official Basem Naim said the group expects mediators and the United States to press Israel to follow the agreement. Reuters reported that Hamas has avoided describing its commitment as "disarmament," instead saying weapons would be handed over for storage under a U.S.-backed Palestinian technocratic administration. Hamas also maintains that implementation depends on Israel beginning its own commitments, including withdrawal and halting attacks.

The dispute creates another obstacle for efforts to advance the fragile ceasefire that has remained in effect since October 2025. Netanyahu is also facing domestic political pressure ahead of Israel's election scheduled for October 27, 2026, with far-right members of his government opposing concessions involving Gaza. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich publicly backed Netanyahu's position and argued that Israeli forces should not withdraw while Hamas remains armed.

The United States and Israel remain in talks, but the central question is unresolved: whether Hamas must complete disarmament before Israeli forces begin leaving Gaza, or whether those steps should proceed together.
