DOJ seeks charges against key January 6 witness Cassidy Hutchinson

Former Trump aide faces investigation over whether she lied to Congress

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 9, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The U.S. Department of Justice is seeking criminal charges against Cassidy Hutchinson, a former White House aide whose testimony became a central part of the congressional investigation into the January 6, 2021, Capitol attack. Federal prosecutors have been presenting evidence to a grand jury in Washington, D.C., examining whether Hutchinson made false statements to Congress.

Two sources familiar with the investigation confirmed the grand jury activity to CBS News on October 8, 2026. The potential charges concern Hutchinson's congressional testimony, although investigators have not publicly identified which specific statements are under scrutiny. No indictment had been announced at the time of the reporting.

Hutchinson served as an aide to Mark Meadows, President Trump's White House chief of staff during his first administration. She attracted national attention in June 2022 when she testified publicly before the House committee investigating the Capitol attack.

Among her allegations, Hutchinson said President Trump and Meadows had been warned that some supporters attending the January 6 rally were carrying weapons. She testified that Trump wanted security officials to remove magnetometers used to screen attendees.

Hutchinson also described an account she said she received from then-Deputy White House Chief of Staff Tony Ornato. According to her testimony, Trump became angry when Secret Service personnel declined to take him to the Capitol after his speech and allegedly lunged toward the steering wheel of his vehicle.

That account was subsequently disputed by Trump's driver, who testified under oath that the president had not attempted to grab the steering wheel. Hutchinson's description of the incident was based on what she said Ornato told her, rather than something she personally witnessed.

The investigation is being handled by the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division, rather than the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Columbia, which would ordinarily handle an investigation involving testimony before Congress. Sources confirmed the unusual assignment but did not explain why the division was selected.

According to media reports, Republican members of Congress referred concerns about Hutchinson's testimony to the Justice Department. The department declined to confirm or deny the existence of an investigation when contacted by reporters.

The investigation raises questions about the accuracy of congressional testimony and the standards used when prosecutors pursue criminal charges against witnesses in politically sensitive investigations.

Hutchinson has not been convicted of wrongdoing, and presenting evidence to a grand jury does not establish that a crime occurred. Prosecutors would need to establish the elements of any offense, including whether a disputed statement was knowingly false.
