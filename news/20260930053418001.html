U.S. consumer confidence falls to lowest level since 2014

High prices and growing job concerns weigh on Americans

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 30, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Americans' confidence in the economy fell sharply in September, reaching its lowest level since April 2014 as consumers expressed increasing concern about high prices, fuel costs and a weakening job outlook. The Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index dropped 6.7 points to 81.9, down from a revised 88.6 in August, marking the third consecutive monthly decline.

Both major components of the survey deteriorated. The Present Situation Index, measuring consumers' assessment of current business and labor-market conditions, fell 7.9 points to 109.3. The Expectations Index, which measures the six-month outlook for income, business and employment, dropped 5.9 points to 63.6. The Conference Board said consumers became more negative about current business conditions and increasingly expected business and employment conditions to weaken in the months ahead.

Prices remained one of the biggest concerns. Consumers' written responses increasingly mentioned the high cost of goods and services, particularly oil and gasoline prices. Average expected inflation over the next 12 months rose 0.3 percentage points to 6.1%, while 68.4% of respondents expected interest rates to increase over the coming year. The survey was conducted from September 1 through September 23, a period that included a Federal Reserve interest-rate increase and continued geopolitical tensions affecting energy markets.

Views of the job market also weakened. 23.6% of respondents said jobs were plentiful, down from 24.5% in August, while 21.9% said jobs were hard to get, up from 20.3%. Looking six months ahead, 28.4% expected fewer jobs to be available, compared with 26.1% the previous month.

Household finances showed another sign of strain. For only the second time since the Conference Board began asking the question four years ago, more consumers described their current family finances as bad than good. Plans to purchase homes and automobiles also declined on a six-month moving-average basis.
