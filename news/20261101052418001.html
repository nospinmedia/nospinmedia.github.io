Iranian negotiators meet Qatari mediators as U.S. weighs new strikes

Talks focus on ending Gulf war and reopening Strait of Hormuz

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 1, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Iranian negotiators met with Qatari mediators in Doha as regional governments sought a diplomatic path out of the Gulf war and tried to prevent another round of U.S. military strikes. The Doha meeting followed more than 12 hours of Iranian-Qatari talks in Tehran the previous day, according to Reuters.

The discussions are being coordinated through regular contacts with Washington. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said President Donald Trump had not decided whether to launch new strikes against Iran and would prefer a diplomatic resolution, while saying the United States remained capable of taking military action if necessary. Iran, meanwhile, has warned that renewed attacks would bring retaliation against U.S. and Israeli targets in neighboring countries.

The diplomatic effort includes competing proposals. Qatar had presented a seven-day confidence-building plan under which Washington and Tehran would take daily steps before broader peace negotiations. An Iranian official said Tehran considered that proposal inadequate. Iran has promoted its own plan calling for the United States to lift its blockade of Iranian ports, unfreeze Iranian assets and approve an Iranian-backed shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz before the waterway is reopened.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran was also studying a letter from Vice President JD Vance and described the U.S. attempt to open dialogue as a positive step. More recent Iranian statements said talks with Qatar had produced discussions of a mechanism that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days if Washington accepts Iran's conditions, although no final agreement has been announced.

The diplomacy is unfolding as both governments prepare for the possibility that fighting could resume after the November 3 U.S. midterm elections.
