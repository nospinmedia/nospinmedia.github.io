Republicans confront cost-of-living concerns at Dallas convention

Oil above $100 adds pressure to GOP affordability message

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 10, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Republicans gathered for their national midterm convention in Dallas while some lawmakers and candidates privately expressed concern that rising costs could complicate the party's economic message less than two months before the November 3 elections. The concerns intensified as oil reached $100 per barrel amid the war with Iran and the United States escalated its trade dispute with Canada.

In a closed-door meeting Wednesday, Republican Senate candidates including Sen. Jon Husted of Ohio, Michael Whatley of North Carolina and Rep. Mike Collins of Georgia met with leaders of a Senate Republican-aligned super PAC to discuss competitive races and voter concerns about the economy. Collins later told Politico that Republicans could not ignore gasoline prices while defending the administration's approach toward Iran.

Sen. Rick Scott of Florida told Politico that Republicans needed to address the expenses families face for housing, food, health care and child care. Scott said Congress has a role in addressing those pressures and acknowledged that higher gasoline prices were an economic consequence of the conflict with Iran. Another Republican lawmaker attending the convention, speaking anonymously, told Politico that the party was "going the wrong direction" and warned that little time remained before the election.

The affordability issue is also part of the convention's official agenda. Republican organizers scheduled programming focused on lower taxes and the cost of living, along with prescription-drug costs, manufacturing and tax policies. The White House and Republican National Committee argue that administration policies are lowering costs and have blamed Democrats for the affordability problems facing households.

Politico also reported that some Republicans are concerned about the economic effects of the Canada trade dispute on agriculture and manufacturing, along with higher energy prices. Other Republican candidates plan to use convention appearances to argue that their policies would leave families with more disposable income.
