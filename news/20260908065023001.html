Russia and North Korea open their first road bridge

New crossing expands trade and travel between allies

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 8, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Russia and North Korea have opened the first road bridge connecting the two countries, creating a new route for vehicles, freight and travelers as Moscow and Pyongyang continue expanding their economic and military relationship.

The two-lane bridge, which stretches about 1 kilometer across the Tumen River, links Russia's Khasan area with North Korea's Rason region. The first trucks crossed Monday during ceremonies on both sides of the border. Russian Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin and North Korean Premier Pak Thae Song participated by video link and formally authorized traffic to begin.

Russian officials said the newly constructed border crossing will eventually be capable of handling up to 300 vehicles and more than 2,300 people per day. The bridge is named for Yakov Novichenko, a Soviet Red Army officer credited with protecting North Korean founder Kim Il Sung from a grenade during an assassination attempt in 1946.

Until now, the neighbors had direct rail and air connections but no dedicated road crossing. Their railway bridge over the Tumen River dates to 1959. Construction of the new road bridge began in April 2025 after the project was agreed during Russian President Vladimir Putin's 2024 visit to North Korea.

The opening comes as relations between Russia and North Korea have deepened significantly during the war in Ukraine. North Korea has supplied Russia with ammunition and troops, including forces deployed to Russia's Kursk region, while Moscow has strengthened economic and military cooperation with Pyongyang. Both countries are subject to extensive international sanctions.

Officials from both governments say the new crossing will increase trade, tourism and other exchanges. Its ability to move trucks directly across the border also creates a substantially larger logistics route between the countries than their previous transportation links provided.
