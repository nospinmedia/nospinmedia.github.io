DOJ escalates investigation into Alaska Senate race

Prosecutors seek to isolate ballots ranking second Dan Sullivan

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 31, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The Justice Department is intensifying its investigation into Alaska's closely watched U.S. Senate race, confirming an ongoing criminal probe while separately asking a federal judge to order election officials to preserve and segregate ballots ranking a candidate who shares a name with Republican incumbent Sen. Dan S. Sullivan.

U.S. Attorney Michael Heyman said Friday that the criminal investigation has been underway for several months. He said recent reporting alleging that people associated with Democratic candidate Mary Peltola's campaign helped recruit retired teacher Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. prompted a "significant surge of law enforcement assets" to Alaska. Heyman said investigators have not determined that a crime occurred and declined to identify potential crimes or targets of the investigation.

Federal prosecutors have issued grand jury subpoenas and sought records as part of the inquiry. An FBI agent also visited a business in Daniel J. Sullivan's hometown of Petersburg and questioned a campaign donor about whether he had been confused about which Sullivan he was supporting. The donor told the Associated Press that he had not been confused.

In a separate civil case, the Justice Department asked a federal judge Friday to require Alaska officials to preserve and segregate ballots ranking Daniel J. Sullivan so potential voter confusion could be investigated if necessary. The government has also sought expedited information from Peltola's campaign and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee.

Alaska officials oppose the request. They say ballots are commingled after being cast and cannot be separated as requested without jeopardizing ballot secrecy. They also note that the ballot identifies the incumbent as Dan S. Sullivan, Republican and incumbent, while his challenger appears as Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., nonpartisan, and that ranked-choice voting allows voters to rank both candidates.

Peltola and national Democrats deny involvement in recruiting Daniel J. Sullivan. He has also denied running at Democrats' direction. No one has been charged with a crime.
