Kim Jong Un Displays Missile Capable of Reaching U.S.

North Korea Unveils Hwasong-20 as Trump Considers New Talks

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 13, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un has showcased an intercontinental ballistic missile capable of reaching the United States at a newly opened military museum in Pyongyang, highlighting the country's expanding weapons program as President Trump considers another meeting with the North Korean leader.

Photographs released Tuesday, October 13, 2026, by North Korea's state-run Korean Central News Agency (KCNA) showed Kim attending the opening of the Defence Revolution Museum on Sunday alongside his wife, Ri Sol Ju, and daughter, Ju Ae. The exhibition featured weapons developed under Kim's leadership, including military drones and the Hwasong-20 intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM).

The Hwasong-20 was displayed on a mobile launcher beside illustrations suggesting a multiple-warhead configuration. According to Shin Jong-woo, secretary-general of the Korea Defence and Security Forum, accompanying maps depicted potential missile ranges extending across the continental United States and reaching the Middle East, Australia and South America.

The display suggests North Korea is emphasizing its ambitions to develop missiles capable of carrying multiple independently targeted warheads. Such technology could complicate missile defense operations, although the exhibition does not establish that North Korea has successfully deployed an operational multiple-warhead system.

The museum opening followed another period of heightened military activity on the Korean Peninsula. On Monday, October 12, North Korea launched multiple ballistic missiles toward waters off its eastern coast. South Korea's military reported that one missile type traveled more than 900 kilometers, or approximately 559 miles.

North Korean state media subsequently confirmed a tactical ballistic missile test intended to evaluate improvements to an unspecified component. The launch marked Pyongyang's fourth missile test within a month, following South Korea's recent demonstration of a domestically developed hypersonic missile.

Meanwhile, President Trump indicated that he might meet Kim during a planned visit to Asia for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in China in November. No formal meeting has been announced.

Military analysts say the permanent museum could serve two purposes: preserving Kim's weapons-development achievements and demonstrating North Korea's military capabilities ahead of possible diplomatic negotiations.

The developments leave Washington and its regional allies confronting North Korea's continued missile expansion while the possibility of renewed direct diplomacy remains open.
