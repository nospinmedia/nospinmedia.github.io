Binance sets aside $6.34 billion after record crypto theft

About $3 billion frozen as investigators point to North Korea

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 31, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance says it has set aside more than $6.34 billion of company resources to cover losses from a massive cyberattack as blockchain investigators increasingly point to a North Korean hacking group as the likely perpetrator.

Attackers moved approximately $6.36 billion from Binance wallets across 10 blockchains on October 29, according to blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs. Most of the assets were removed during a roughly 20-minute period. The incident is believed to be the largest cryptocurrency theft on record, far exceeding the approximately $1.5 billion stolen from Bybit in 2025.

Binance initially said its investigation indicated that a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop may have allowed an attacker to access a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. The company said it found no evidence that wallet private keys were leaked and later said investigators had identified additional points of compromise.

TRM Labs attributed the attack with high confidence to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-sponsored hacking unit, based on on-chain and off-chain evidence and similarities to previous attacks. Other blockchain researchers have also said North Korean involvement is likely. Binance and law enforcement authorities have not publicly made their own attribution.

Binance says industry partners have frozen roughly $3 billion of the affected assets. Stablecoin issuers and blockchain organizations moved quickly to prevent portions of the stolen cryptocurrency from being transferred, while attackers converted other assets into cryptocurrencies that cannot be centrally frozen.

The exchange says user funds are safe and that it will cover confirmed losses using company resources. Binance has reserved more than $6.34 billion for that purpose and says it plans to publicly verify customer holdings through its proof-of-reserves system. Withdrawals were temporarily suspended while security checks and recovery work continued.
