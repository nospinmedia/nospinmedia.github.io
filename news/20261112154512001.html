Democrats plan Trump administration probes as White House pushes back

Incoming committee leaders outline investigations as White House warns some actions could face scrutiny

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 12, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

House Democrats preparing to take control of key committees in January are outlining investigations into the Trump administration, while the White House is warning that some investigative actions could cross legal boundaries and themselves face scrutiny.

One planned inquiry focuses on the administration's handling of U.S. intelligence agencies. Rep. Jim Himes, who is expected to chair the House Intelligence Committee, told Reuters that Democrats intend to examine what he described as political pressure on intelligence analysts, retaliatory firings and declining morale. He said the committee will also investigate relationships with defense and intelligence contractors and U.S. covert operations abroad.

During President Trump's second term, hundreds of intelligence personnel have been fired or encouraged to leave. Former Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard reduced staffing at her agency by about 40%, while CIA Director John Ratcliffe previously announced plans to eliminate more than 1,000 positions over several years. The administration has described personnel reductions as part of efforts to restructure the intelligence community.

Democrats are planning broader oversight as well. Rep. Robert Garcia, expected to lead the House Oversight Committee, has discussed investigations involving the administration's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein investigation, immigration enforcement, White House construction projects, cryptocurrency activities and business dealings involving President Trump and his family.

White House counsel Will Scharf responded in a letter to Garcia, arguing that congressional investigations must have a legitimate legislative purpose. Scharf warned that threats intended to intimidate executive branch officials or investigative processes designed merely to instill fear "may cross the line into blatant criminality." He also said unlawful subpoenas intended to deprive people of constitutional rights could warrant investigation.

Scharf's November 9 letter followed Garcia's warning after Democrats won control of the House that Trump administration officials should "lawyer up" in preparation for congressional investigations. Garcia has said Democrats intend to conduct aggressive oversight when they take control in January.

Current House Oversight Committee Chairman James Comer, R-Ky., criticized Garcia's "lawyer up" remark as being in "bad taste," but said the committee is independent and should serve as a check and balance on the executive branch regardless of which party controls the White House.

Garcia rejected the White House warning, saying Congress has broad constitutional authority to conduct investigations and oversight. Attorney General Todd Blanche separately acknowledged that Congress has oversight authority and said the Justice Department would continue responding to congressional subpoenas and requests.

The dispute sets up a potentially significant confrontation over congressional oversight and executive authority when Democrats assume control of the House in January.
