First Russian Diesel Shipment Under Trump-Putin Deal Heads for U.S.

Tanker Sets Sail as Ukraine and U.S. Lawmakers Challenge Resumed Energy Trade

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 24, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The first tanker carrying Russian diesel to the United States under a deal announced by President Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin is heading across the Atlantic, marking a concrete step in the resumption of direct Russian fuel shipments to the U.S.

Shipping data reviewed by Reuters showed the tanker Seaways Pecos carrying roughly 730,000 barrels of diesel from Russia's Baltic port of Primorsk. The vessel is expected to arrive in the United States in November, although its specific U.S. destination was not publicly identified.

The shipment follows an agreement announced after talks between President Trump and Putin that allows limited imports of Russian petroleum products into the United States. The administration has presented the arrangement as one way to increase fuel supplies while Americans face elevated diesel prices.

Ukraine has strongly opposed the imports. Ukrainian officials argue that purchases of Russian petroleum provide revenue to Moscow while Russia continues its war against Ukraine. Kyiv has urged the United States and other countries to maintain economic pressure on Russia rather than expand energy trade.

The deal has also drawn opposition in Congress. Lawmakers from both parties have questioned the decision to resume Russian diesel imports, including senators from New Hampshire, Maine and Connecticut who have challenged the administration over the policy.

Russian petroleum imports have been largely barred from the United States since 2022, when the U.S. prohibited imports of Russian crude oil and several petroleum products following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The new arrangement therefore represents a significant change in U.S.-Russia energy trade.

The departure of the first tanker moves the issue beyond a proposed policy change: Russian diesel is now physically on its way toward the United States as political arguments continue over fuel prices, sanctions and financing Russia's war.
