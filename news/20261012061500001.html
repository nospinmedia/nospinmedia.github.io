Hurricane Simon Batters Mexico's Pacific Coast

115 MPH Winds Threaten Resorts as Flooding Forces Evacuations

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 12, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Hurricane Simon battered Mexico's Pacific coast with destructive winds and torrential rainfall as authorities evacuated vulnerable communities, closed transportation facilities, and warned of potentially catastrophic flooding. The storm, which previously reached Category 4 strength, weakened to a Category 3 hurricane while continuing to threaten coastal towns and major tourist destinations, including Puerto Vallarta and Manzanillo.

According to the National Hurricane Center, Simon remained just offshore of west-central Mexico late Sunday, October 11, with maximum sustained winds of approximately 115 mph. The hurricane was moving northwest at roughly 5 mph, keeping dangerous conditions near the coast for an extended period. Hurricane-force winds extended approximately 15 miles from its center, while tropical-storm-force winds reached considerably farther.

Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum urged coastal residents to remain indoors, follow emergency instructions, and seek shelter when necessary. Authorities suspended classes, restricted access to beaches, halted public transportation in affected areas, and opened emergency shelters. Puerto Vallarta International Airport was closed as the storm threatened the popular tourist region.

In Manzanillo, heavy rainfall flooded streets and damaged homes, prompting evacuations assisted by military personnel. Mexican authorities placed 28 municipalities under their highest alert level and another 93 municipalities under high alert as emergency teams prepared for additional flooding and infrastructure damage.

Forecasters warned that the greatest danger could extend well beyond the hurricane's strongest winds. Parts of Michoacán, Colima, and Jalisco could receive isolated rainfall totals approaching 30 inches, creating potentially life-threatening flash floods and mudslides, particularly where steep mountain terrain meets densely populated coastal communities. Dangerous surf and storm surge also threatened beaches, ports, and low-lying neighborhoods.

Although Simon weakened from its earlier peak intensity, the National Hurricane Center emphasized that reduced wind speeds did not eliminate the danger. Continued rainfall, flooding, and landslides remained serious concerns as the hurricane moved slowly along Mexico's western coastline.
