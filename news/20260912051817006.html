North Korea fires multiple short-range ballistic missiles

Launches follow conclusion of U.S.-South Korea-Japan drills

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

North Korea fired multiple short-range ballistic missiles from its eastern coast early September 12, one day after the United States, South Korea and Japan concluded a five-day trilateral military exercise. South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said the missiles were launched from the Wonsan area at about 5:20 a.m. and traveled approximately 250 kilometers (155 miles) before falling into the sea off North Korea's eastern coast.

South Korean and U.S. intelligence authorities tracked the launches from their early stages and shared information with Japan, according to the Joint Chiefs. South Korea said it increased surveillance for possible additional launches while maintaining its combined defense posture with the United States. The launches were North Korea's first ballistic missile tests since August 20, when it fired another barrage during U.S.-South Korean military exercises.

South Korea's presidential Office of National Security convened an emergency meeting involving defense and military officials after Saturday's launches. The office called the ballistic missile activity a violation of United Nations Security Council resolutions, assessed its effect on the country's security situation and called for North Korea to immediately halt such actions. The U.S. Pacific Command said the launches did not pose an immediate threat to U.S. personnel, territory or allies but said Washington was consulting closely with regional partners.

The launches came immediately after the three allies completed Freedom Edge 26, a five-day exercise held in international waters east and south of Jeju Island. The drills included ballistic missile defense, anti-submarine warfare, maritime interdiction, air defense and other operations intended to improve cooperation among the three militaries.

North Korean Defense Minister Kim Song Gi criticized Freedom Edge when the exercise began and warned that Pyongyang would take countermeasures. North Korea has long characterized U.S.-led military exercises in the region as preparations for conflict, while the United States and its allies describe the exercises as defensive.
