Trump asks New York appeals court to overturn 34 felony convictions

Judges hear arguments over election law and presidential immunity

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 6, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

President Trump is asking a New York appeals court to overturn his 34 felony convictions in the Manhattan hush-money case, with his attorneys arguing Thursday that federal election law and presidential immunity issues undermined the prosecution and trial.

A five-judge panel of the New York Supreme Court Appellate Division, First Department heard oral arguments on November 5 but did not immediately rule. Trump was convicted by a Manhattan jury in May 2024 of 34 counts of falsifying business records connected to reimbursements for a $130,000 payment made to adult-film actor Stormy Daniels before the 2016 presidential election. Trump has denied Daniels' account and pleaded not guilty to the charges.

Trump's attorneys argued that federal campaign-finance law should have prevented Manhattan prosecutors from using an alleged violation of election law to elevate the business-record charges from misdemeanors to felonies. The defense also challenged evidence introduced at trial involving Trump's actions while he was president, citing the U.S. Supreme Court's 2024 presidential immunity ruling.

Prosecutors from the Manhattan District Attorney's Office urged the court to uphold the convictions. They argued that New York had authority to prosecute falsified business records and that the evidence presented to jurors supported the verdict. Prosecutors also disputed the defense's contention that federal election law displaced the state's prosecution.

The appeal is the latest stage of a case that produced the first criminal conviction of a current or former U.S. president. Trump was sentenced in January 2025 to an unconditional discharge, meaning he received no jail time, probation or fine, while the convictions remained on his record.

The appellate judges reserved their decision after Thursday's arguments. A ruling could uphold the convictions, overturn them or potentially order additional proceedings, and further appeals could follow regardless of the outcome.
