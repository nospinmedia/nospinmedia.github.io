Washington resident charged with helping Canadian school shooter

Prosecutors allege months of advice, money and livestream planning

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 7, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Federal authorities have arrested James Cody Bryant, 30, of Bellingham, Washington, accusing Bryant of helping the gunman responsible for the February 10, 2026 mass shooting in Tumbler Ridge, British Columbia. Bryant is charged with conspiracy to murder persons in a foreign country, an offense that carries a potential sentence of up to life in prison.

According to a federal criminal complaint, Bryant communicated frequently with 18-year-old Jesse Van Rootselaar for at least six months before the attack. Prosecutors allege Bryant provided tactical advice, sent money and offered technical assistance while knowing Van Rootselaar was planning mass violence. Authorities also allege Bryant agreed to help livestream the attack to increase its visibility and assisted with testing the livestream equipment beforehand.

Van Rootselaar first killed her mother and stepbrother at their home before driving to Tumbler Ridge Secondary School, according to authorities. At the school, she killed five children ages 12 and 13 and one educational assistant and wounded others before dying from an apparent self-inflicted gunshot wound. Eight victims were killed in the attacks, in addition to Van Rootselaar's death.

The complaint alleges Bryant discussed possible targets and tactics with Van Rootselaar, sent money that was used for items connected to the attack and advised her about concealing online communications. Prosecutors say Bryant also knew enough about the planned violence to potentially alert authorities but did not do so. On the day of the shooting, Bryant allegedly remained in contact with Van Rootselaar and helped troubleshoot the vehicle she used to reach the school.

The case is being investigated by the FBI, Homeland Security Investigations and Canadian authorities, including the Royal Canadian Mounted Police. The criminal allegations against Bryant have not been proven in court.
