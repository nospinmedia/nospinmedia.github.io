Ex-CIA official pleads guilty in $194 million fraud scheme

FBI found 298 gold bars, $2 million cash and luxury watches at his home

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 7, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Former senior CIA official David J. Rush pleaded guilty Tuesday to one count of wire fraud, admitting he used fabricated classified government programs to obtain nearly $194 million in federal funds and assets for his own benefit. Rush, 49, faces up to 20 years in federal prison.

According to the Justice Department, Rush held a senior executive-level government position and a Top Secret/Sensitive Compartmented Information clearance. Prosecutors said he created fictitious government authorities, including a fake Special Access Program, and used the appearance of highly classified operations to persuade colleagues and contractors to authorize expenditures that benefited him personally.

The scheme included approximately $145 million in wire transfers that Rush used to acquire four luxury properties in Palm Beach and Hobe Sound, Florida. Prosecutors said he separately invented a purported sensitive government activity to obtain hundreds of gold bars. When the FBI searched his Ashburn, Virginia, home on May 19, agents recovered 298 gold bars acquired at a government cost of approximately $46 million, about $2.1 million in cash, more than €104,000, and numerous luxury watches.

Rush also admitted providing classified information identifying a clandestine U.S. intelligence source to an unauthorized foreign official, according to prosecutors. Investigators found he had falsely claimed academic degrees and misrepresented his military experience while advancing through government service. He had served in the Navy but falsely claimed, among other things, that he had been a Navy pilot and held degrees from Clemson University and Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute.

Under his plea agreement, Rush agreed to forfeit the gold, currency, four Florida properties, 30 watches and two 2026 BMW Alpina vehicles. The government says his conduct caused approximately $193.6 million in losses. His sentencing is scheduled for January 28, 2027, in Alexandria, Virginia.
