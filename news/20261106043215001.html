Nasdaq loses $2.5 trillion in AI-driven sell-off

Democratic midterm wins intensify data-center concerns

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 6, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

U.S. technology stocks suffered a sharp reversal on Thursday, November 5, with the Nasdaq Composite falling 5.5% as investors reassessed the outlook for artificial intelligence spending and data-center construction following Democratic victories in the midterm elections. The decline pushed the Nasdaq into correction territory, nearly 12% below its October 6 record.

The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4%, erasing roughly $2.5 trillion in market value during the session. Semiconductor companies were among the hardest hit. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 12%, while Nvidia fell 9% and AMD declined 17%. AI infrastructure companies also suffered heavy losses, including CoreWeave, which fell 10%, and Nebius, which dropped 15%.

The sell-off followed a post-election rally a day earlier and reflected several overlapping concerns. Investors were already questioning whether the enormous amounts being spent on AI infrastructure would generate sufficient returns. Democratic election gains added another risk: greater scrutiny of data centers and potential restrictions on new projects because of their electricity and water demands. Democratic candidates in several states campaigned on proposals to pause or more tightly regulate data-center development.

The political concern is not limited to federal legislation. State and local governments control many of the permits and approvals needed for data-center construction. UBS estimates that roughly 30% of planned U.S. data-center capacity additions for 2028 could face risks from permitting delays, local opposition or other constraints. Democrats will control Congress beginning in January, although President Trump retains veto power, limiting their ability to enact major federal policy changes without Republican support.

The technology retreat also spread into overseas markets, adding to concerns that a pullback in AI investment could affect companies and economies well beyond the United States.
