Iran sends Washington terms for ending war

Tehran awaits President Trump's response

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 20, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Iran says it has formally sent Washington conditions for ending the war and re-entering negotiations, opening a potential diplomatic path as President Trump considers the next phase of the conflict. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said Qatar delivered Tehran's terms to the United States and Iran is now awaiting Trump's response.

Rezaei publicly identified three demands: an end to the war "on all fronts," the release of Iranian funds frozen abroad, and an end to the U.S. naval blockade. He said Qatar and Pakistan remain involved as mediators after a previous memorandum of understanding between Washington and Tehran expired in August. Rezaei also warned that Iran remains prepared for further fighting if diplomacy fails and said Iranian military planners consider another U.S. attack possible.

The diplomatic message follows Trump's comments on September 16 that the United States was "hopefully toward the end" of the war and that Iran wanted to make a deal. Trump also said he had heard from Iran directly, although he did not explain the nature of that contact. Tehran's latest statement provides a more concrete description of the conditions Iran says it wants addressed before an agreement can be reached.

One notable change is that reparations, which Iran had previously sought during negotiations, were not among the three conditions Rezaei publicly identified. He also said Iran has not decided to withdraw from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty, although he warned that additional sanctions or further international action against Iran's nuclear program could affect that position.

The exchange does not mean a ceasefire or settlement has been reached. Iran says its terms have been delivered, but Washington had not publicly accepted them in the reporting reviewed for this story.
