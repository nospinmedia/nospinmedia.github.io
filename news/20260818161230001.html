New York Judge Delays Luigi Mangione Murder Trial

Double jeopardy challenge pushes Sept. 8 start off the calendar

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 6:47 PM ET

A New York judge has postponed Luigi Mangione’s state murder trial, vacating the Sept. 8, 2026 start while the court weighs a double jeopardy challenge prompted by his recent federal guilty plea. The ruling pauses the Manhattan case over the killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson, reflecting New York’s broader protections against successive prosecutions.

Justice Gregory Carro canceled the September trial date and set briefing deadlines: prosecutors must respond by Oct. 9, 2026, with a defense reply due Oct. 30, 2026. Oral arguments are scheduled for Dec. 10, 2026. Federal sentencing remains set for Dec. 18, 2026; prosecutors said they will seek life in prison, though federal sentencing guidelines recommend 24 to 30 years. Mangione, 28, previously pleaded guilty in federal court to two stalking charges tied to the Dec. 4, 2024 shooting. He still faces New York charges including second-degree murder and firearms offenses.

The defense argues the state prosecution is barred by New York’s double jeopardy law because the federal plea covers the same course of conduct. The Manhattan District Attorney’s Office, led by Alvin Bragg, counters that the state charges involve different legal elements and should proceed. Assistant District Attorney Joel Seidemann has argued that the federal plea does not preclude the state homicide case. Carro’s order pauses state proceedings until he rules on the motion, and any decision could be appealed.

During his federal plea, Mangione admitted following Thompson to a UnitedHealth Group investor conference and fatally shooting him outside a Midtown Manhattan hotel on Dec. 4, 2024. The outcome of the double jeopardy challenge will determine whether New York may proceed with its separate homicide prosecution.
