Hackers breach unclassified CIA intelligence system

Officials say classified networks were not compromised

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 25, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Hackers breached an unclassified computer system used by the CIA and Office of the Director of National Intelligence, but officials say the intrusion did not compromise classified information or critical capabilities.

The affected system is used for open-source intelligence, or OSINT, which involves collecting and analyzing publicly and commercially available information. That can include material from the internet, news reports, social media, commercial satellite imagery and private data providers. Follow-up reporting indicates the shared intelligence environment was also used by the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA), expanding the known scope of agencies affected by the intrusion.

The breach has been resolved, according to people familiar with the incident, and officials said no other systems are currently at risk. Authorities have not publicly identified who carried out the intrusion or disclosed exactly what information may have been accessed. Although the underlying material on an OSINT system is unclassified, the way intelligence agencies search, organize and analyze that information can potentially reveal what subjects analysts are studying and where intelligence priorities are focused.

The disclosure follows several other recent federal cybersecurity and technology incidents. The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center, while the Department of Agriculture recently took systems offline following a separate security incident. The National Security Agency also experienced a multiday disruption to a classified communications network that officials attributed to software patching rather than a cyberattack.

Those incidents should not be treated as evidence of a single coordinated attack. Administration officials have said the recent wave of government and private-sector technology problems includes a mixture of actual cyber intrusions and unrelated technical failures. There is currently no established connection between the CIA/ODNI intrusion and the FBI incident.

The latest breach therefore adds another confirmed intrusion to the government's recent cybersecurity problems, while remaining distinct from the unrelated outages that have occurred during the same period. Investigators have not publicly confirmed the identity of the attackers or the method they used to gain access.
