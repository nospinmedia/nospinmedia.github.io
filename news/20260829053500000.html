Chinese Chipmaker CXMT Sues Pentagon Over Military Blacklist

Memory giant challenges national security designation in U.S. court

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 29, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China's largest producer of dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) chips, filed a lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia seeking to overturn the Department of Defense's designation of the company as a Chinese military enterprise. The complaint argues the Pentagon acted arbitrarily, lacked sufficient evidence to support the designation, and violated the company's constitutional due process rights by placing it on the military blacklist without an adequate opportunity to respond.

The lawsuit names Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinberg, and Assistant Secretary of Defense for Industrial Base Policy Michael Cardinale as defendants. CXMT maintains that it develops and manufactures memory chips solely for commercial markets, including smartphones, personal computers, automobiles and other consumer electronics. The company argues the designation has damaged its international reputation, disrupted business relationships and created uncertainty for customers and suppliers around the world.

The case challenges the Pentagon's authority under Section 1260H of the National Defense Authorization Act, which requires the Defense Department to identify companies determined to be linked to or supporting China's People's Liberation Army. CXMT was first added to the list in January 2025, and the designation was reaffirmed during the Trump administration's June 2026 review of Chinese military companies. While the designation itself does not immediately prohibit private companies from doing business with CXMT, it can lead to procurement restrictions, additional sanctions and increased scrutiny by governments and commercial customers.

The lawsuit is the latest legal challenge involving U.S. national security restrictions on Chinese technology firms. Other Chinese companies, including Alibaba and WuXi AppTec, have also sought judicial review after receiving similar designations. The case highlights continuing tensions between Washington and Beijing over semiconductor technology, supply-chain security and the role of Chinese firms in industries considered strategically important to U.S. national security.
