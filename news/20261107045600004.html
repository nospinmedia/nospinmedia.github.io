U.S. measles cases surpass 5,000

CDC reports highest annual total in 35 years

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 7, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

The United States has recorded more than 5,000 measles cases in 2026, reaching the country's highest annual total in 35 years, according to updated data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

The CDC reported 5,182 cases as of Friday, November 6, more than twice the 2,289 cases recorded during all of 2025. Of this year's total, 5,164 cases were reported across 45 states and Washington, D.C., while another 18 cases involved international visitors. The last year with a higher U.S. total was 1991.

Children and teenagers account for nearly 60% of confirmed infections. The CDC data also show that approximately 96% of cases involve people who were unvaccinated or whose vaccination status was unknown. About 562 patients, representing roughly 11% of reported cases, have required hospitalization.

Several states are experiencing substantial outbreaks. Pennsylvania has reported 1,487 cases, while Ohio has recorded 311, New York has reported 210, and Indiana has recorded 134. Only Arkansas, Hawaii, Mississippi, Nevada and New Hampshire had reported no measles cases during 2026 at the time of the federal update.

Measles is a highly contagious respiratory disease that can cause serious complications, particularly among young children and people with weakened immune systems. Public health officials recommend the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine, commonly known as the MMR vaccine, as the primary protection against infection and outbreaks.

The 2026 increase continues a sharp resurgence in U.S. measles activity and comes after the country nearly eliminated domestic transmission decades ago. Federal and state health officials continue monitoring outbreaks and vaccination coverage as cases spread across most of the country.
