AfD wins Saxony-Anhalt with record 43.8% showing

Party falls three seats short of outright majority

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 7, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Germany's far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) won a decisive victory in the Saxony-Anhalt state election on September 6, taking 43.8% of the vote and more than doubling its support from five years ago. Final results gave the AfD 39 seats in the 83-member state parliament, leaving it three seats short of the 42 needed for an absolute majority and complicating its effort to form a state government on its own.

The result represented the strongest state-election performance in the AfD's 13-year history. Its candidate for state premier, Ulrich Siegmund, 35, said the party had received a mandate to govern but rejected the possibility of operating a minority government. Other major parties have maintained their longstanding Brandmauer, or firewall, against governing with the AfD, leaving the formation of a stable administration uncertain despite the party's commanding first-place finish.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democratic Union (CDU) suffered a major reversal after governing Saxony-Anhalt for 24 years. The CDU received 17.2% and won 15 seats, down from 37.1% in the previous state election. The Social Democrats received 9.3%, the Greens 8.9% and the Left Party 8.6%, with each winning eight seats. The Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance (BSW) received 5.3% and secured five seats. Voter turnout reached 77.8%.

The AfD's Saxony-Anhalt branch is classified as a proven right-wing extremist organization by the state's domestic intelligence agency, a characterization the party rejects as politically motivated. Forming an alternative government could also prove difficult because the CDU refuses to govern with either the AfD or the Left Party. Germany's states control important areas including education and security and participate in the Bundesrat, the country's upper legislative chamber.
