Trump says he convinced Nvidia to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO

Historic $950 billion stock surge comes as Congress prepares hearings

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 24, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

President Trump says he personally intervened with Nvidia's board and helped convince the company to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, reversing Huang's departure just 11 days after revelations involving extramarital affairs and a woman suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence.

Huang resigned on November 12 after reports of multiple affairs, including a relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman linked to Singapore-based cloud company Megaspeed International. U.S. officials have investigated suspected connections between Alice Huang and Chinese intelligence, adding national-security concerns to an already sensitive debate over Nvidia's advanced AI chips and China. The allegations surrounding those intelligence ties remain under investigation.

Nvidia announced Huang's return on Monday, November 23, saying its board voted unanimously to reinstate him as president and CEO and restore him to the board. Harvey C. Jones remains Nvidia's board chair. The company said directors met with Huang and were satisfied with his candor and ability to lead. Nvidia also said the board received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and concluded that Huang's reinstatement posed no national-security risk.

Trump has openly taken credit for the reversal. He said he spoke with Nvidia's board and persuaded the company to bring Huang back, arguing that Huang's leadership was important to America's position in artificial intelligence. The intervention followed a sharp decline in Nvidia shares and broader turmoil in AI-related stocks after Huang's resignation.

Wall Street's response to his return was dramatic. Nvidia shares jumped 31.5% Monday, adding roughly $950 billion in market value in what was reported as the largest one-day increase in dollar value for a U.S.-listed company. The rally also helped lift the broader stock market.

At the same time, the Trump administration placed Megaspeed on a U.S. trade blacklist, keeping the China-related national-security questions surrounding the controversy alive even as the administration supported Huang's return.

Trump's involvement has drawn criticism from lawmakers in both parties, with members of Congress questioning the president's direct involvement in the leadership of a publicly traded company and seeking answers about Nvidia's China business, advanced-chip exports and the government's assurances to the board.

Huang is scheduled for a closed-door, transcribed interview on December 1 before the House Intelligence Committee, House Foreign Affairs Committee and House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter is scheduled to appear before the committees on December 2.

The scrutiny will then move into public view. Huang is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3 at a hearing titled Protecting America's AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls.

The result is an unusual convergence of corporate governance, presidential influence, national security and enormous financial stakes: Huang is back running the world's leading AI-chip company after Trump pushed for his return, investors added nearly $1 trillion to Nvidia's value in a day, and Congress is preparing to question Huang about the issues that surrounded his departure.
