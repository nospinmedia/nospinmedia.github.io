New student visa rules take effect this month

Universities prepare as international enrollment continues to decline

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 1, 2026 at 10:32 AM ET

U.S. colleges and universities are preparing for significant changes to international student immigration rules as the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) moves to replace the long-standing "Duration of Status" (D/S) system with fixed admission periods beginning September 15, 2026. Federal officials recently briefed schools on compliance requirements ahead of the rule's implementation, while universities have urged international students to return to the United States before the new policy takes effect.

Under the new regulations, most F-1 student and J-1 exchange visitor visa holders will receive a fixed expiration date on their Form I-94, generally capped at four years, instead of remaining in the country for the duration of their approved academic program. Students needing additional time to complete their studies will generally be required to apply for extensions through U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS). The rule also shortens certain post-completion grace periods and changes several compliance requirements for schools and students.

The policy change comes as international enrollment shows signs of slowing. Common App reported a 10% decline in first-year international applicants during the 2025-2026 admissions cycle, including a 15% decrease from India and a 17% decline across Africa. A separate analysis by NAFSA: Association of International Educators projected the United States could lose as many as 111,000 international students during the 2026-2027 academic year, potentially reducing higher education revenue by $3.4 billion and affecting nearly 40,000 jobs.

Many universities, including Harvard University, Columbia University, and Georgetown University, have issued travel advisories encouraging international students to return before the September 15 implementation date to avoid potential complications under the new system. Higher education organizations continue to monitor how the policy will affect enrollment, research programs, and the ability of U.S. institutions to attract students from around the world.
