Democratic state gains put national popular vote plan within reach

Five battleground states could push compact past 270 electoral votes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 11, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Democratic victories in the 2026 midterm elections have created a possible path for the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact to reach the 270 electoral votes needed to take effect, potentially changing how participating states choose electors in presidential elections.

The compact has been enacted by 18 states and Washington, D.C., representing 222 electoral votes. Under the agreement, participating states would award their electoral votes to the presidential candidate who receives the most votes nationwide, regardless of which candidate wins within each member state. The compact does not take effect until participating jurisdictions collectively control at least 270 electoral votes.

Democrats emerged from the midterms with control of the governorship and both legislative chambers in Arizona, Michigan, Nevada, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin, five states that have not joined the compact and together hold 61 electoral votes. Minnesota also will have unified Democratic control but is already a compact member. If all five nonmember states joined, the compact would reach 283 electoral votes.

That outcome is not assured. Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro has expressed support for moving toward a national popular vote, while incoming Wisconsin Gov. David Crowley has said he is interested in discussing the issue with lawmakers. Leaders in Michigan, Arizona and Nevada have been less definitive about whether or when they would pursue the compact. Nevada lawmakers previously approved joining it in 2019, but then-Gov. Steve Sisolak, a Democrat, vetoed the measure, arguing it could reduce the influence of smaller states.

Supporters argue that a national popular vote would make every presidential vote count equally and reduce campaigns' focus on a small group of battleground states. Opponents argue the compact would diminish states' individual influence and raise constitutional concerns.

Legal challenges are expected if the compact reaches 270 votes. Among the unresolved questions are whether participating states would require congressional approval under the Constitution's Compact Clause and whether differences among state ballots could raise equal-protection issues. Those questions could ultimately reach the U.S. Supreme Court.
