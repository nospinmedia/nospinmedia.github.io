Trump Threatens Jail for California Officials in Mail-Ballot Dispute

DOJ Warns Officials They Could Face Criminal Liability for Counting Contested Ballots

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 17, 2026 at 1:15 PM ET

President Trump is threatening California election officials with prison as his administration escalates a dispute over more than 20,000 mail ballots it says were sent to people who are likely noncitizens.

The Justice Department warned Los Angeles County and California officials that they could face criminal liability if they knowingly facilitate illegal voting by counting ballots from people who are not U.S. citizens. Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter wrote that noncitizen voting in a federal election is a crime and knowingly facilitating it is also unlawful.

Trump amplified the warning Friday, October 16, writing on social media that officials involved would go to jail for a long period of time. The warning came one day after the Justice Department sued California, asking a federal judge to prevent the state from counting ballots from the disputed group until their citizenship status can be verified.

Federal officials say the list was created by comparing voter information with internal immigration records. The Justice Department previously delivered the names on a password-protected thumb drive and gave California officials 48 hours to review them.

California officials dispute the federal government's approach. Los Angeles County Counsel Dawyn R. Harrison said the county could not comply because federal officials had not adequately explained how the list was compiled. California Secretary of State Shirley Weber has accused the Justice Department of trying to improperly access protected voter information and purge voter rolls, saying the effort creates confusion and undermines public confidence in elections.

Voting by noncitizens in federal elections is illegal. However, citizenship-verification systems have previously misidentified some naturalized U.S. citizens as noncitizens, and studies have found actual noncitizen voting to be rare.

The dispute comes less than three weeks before the November 3 midterm elections and adds a new criminal-enforcement dimension to an existing federal lawsuit over the ballots.
