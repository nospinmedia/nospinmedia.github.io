Bahrain intercepts Iranian aerial attacks after nationwide alert

IRGC says six drones targeted U.S. radar site

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 15, 2026 at 6:15 AM ET

Bahrain said its air defenses intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks on Sunday after authorities issued nationwide warnings telling residents to remain calm and move to safe locations, while Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed it launched six drones at a U.S. military radar installation in the kingdom.

The IRGC said the drones targeted a U.S. air-defense radar at the Al-Rifaa helicopter base, which hosts American forces, describing the operation as retaliation for U.S. military actions against Iran. Bahrain confirmed its defenses engaged Iranian aerial threats before they reached their targets, but neither Bahrain nor the United States immediately confirmed that the American radar installation had been hit. No casualties or damage from the claimed strike were immediately reported.

The Bahrain Defence Force accused Iran of continuing what it called a "systematic hostile approach" against the kingdom and said its air-defense systems intercepted and destroyed multiple incoming threats. Military units remained at the highest level of readiness. Bahrain's Interior Ministry activated warning sirens and issued alerts directing residents to remain in safe locations and follow official instructions. The warnings marked Bahrain's first nationwide threat alert since September.

Bahrain's Foreign Ministry condemned the attacks as "blatant aggression against Bahrain's sovereignty and territorial integrity." The ministry said the attacks violated UN Security Council Resolution 2817 and stated that Bahrain reserves its right to individual and collective self-defense under Article 51 of the UN Charter.

The confrontation follows another incident two days earlier, when Iran claimed it attacked a U.S. early-warning radar facility in Bahrain. The U.S. military said American forces intercepted two Iranian one-way attack drones in that incident and reported no injuries or damage. Bahrain hosts the headquarters of the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet, making the kingdom a central base for American naval operations across the Persian Gulf and surrounding waterways.

The escalation extended into Saudi Arabia on Sunday. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defenses intercepted two ballistic missiles attributed to Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis that were headed toward Riyadh. Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki also said defenses destroyed three drones targeting Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province, home to much of the kingdom's oil and gas infrastructure.

Iran's claimed attack has therefore not been confirmed as a successful strike: Iran says six drones targeted the U.S. radar site, while Bahrain says its defenses destroyed incoming Iranian attacks before they achieved their objectives.
