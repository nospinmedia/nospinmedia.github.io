U.S. Ambassador Calls West Bank Settler Siege 'Terror'

Israeli Military Removes Two Illegal Outposts Near Qusra

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The United States issued an unusually strong public condemnation of Israeli settler violence after several days of reported blockades around Palestinian homes in Qusra, south of Nablus, in the occupied West Bank. U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee described the siege as a "horrific act of terror" and referred to those responsible as "Israeli terrorists," saying the U.S. Embassy was coordinating with Israeli authorities as the situation unfolded.

According to residents, the confrontation began on August 9, 2026, when dozens of settlers established encampments around three Palestinian homes, restricting movement and repeatedly cutting electricity and water service. One of the homes belongs to Loui Ridi, a Palestinian-American from Ohio, while his brother, Qusai Abu Rida, remained inside with family members during much of the standoff. On August 11, the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) declared the area a closed military zone, calling the siege "illegal, reprehensible, and unacceptable." Two days later, the military said troops dismantled two illegal settler outposts near Qusra and a neighboring village, detained one Israeli suspect, and deployed additional forces to the area.

Local officials said Israeli soldiers temporarily evacuated more than a dozen nearby homes for operational purposes, while residents reported utilities were disrupted again after temporary restoration efforts. Israeli authorities said inspections found families had access to essential supplies, but residents and humanitarian organizations described deteriorating conditions caused by intermittent access to food, water, and electricity. The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs has recorded more settler-related incidents in Qusra this year than in any other West Bank community.

The incident adds diplomatic pressure as the Trump administration balances support for Israel with growing international concern over settler violence in the West Bank. Israeli officials said security operations in the area remain ongoing, while residents and human rights organizations warned that previous enforcement actions have not always prevented settlers from returning after dispersal.
