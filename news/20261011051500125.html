Drone strike kills 41 displaced civilians in Sudan

Eight children among dead as UN demands accountability

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 11, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

A drone strike in Sudan's Blue Nile region killed at least 41 displaced civilians, including eight children, on Friday, October 9, 2026, according to the Sudanese human rights organization Emergency Lawyers. The group blamed the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) for the attack near the Ethiopian border, where families fleeing Sudan's civil war had sought refuge. The United Nations condemned the reported killings and called for accountability.

The strike occurred in the Abu Shandi area of Wad al-Mahi locality, where displaced families had gathered near a settlement sheltering people uprooted by fighting. Local reporting indicated that civilians were near a water facility and an area used for small-scale gold mining when the drone struck. Many of those killed were women and children.

The reported death toll increased as information emerged from the remote area. Local authorities initially reported approximately 30 deaths, while Emergency Lawyers subsequently placed the toll at 41. Dozens of additional people were reportedly wounded. The casualty figures remain subject to verification as humanitarian organizations assess the aftermath.

The RSF has been accused of carrying out the strike, but responsibility has not been independently established. The paramilitary group had not publicly responded to the allegations in the initial reporting. Sudanese civil society organizations have called for an independent investigation into the attack and the protection of civilians in conflict-affected communities.

Denise Brown, the United Nations resident and humanitarian coordinator in Sudan, condemned the reported attack and emphasized that those responsible for violations of international humanitarian law must be held accountable. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) warned that the affected area had already experienced sustained attacks in recent weeks, forcing thousands of civilians from their homes.

Sudan's civil war began in April 2023, when fighting erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces. According to conflict monitoring estimates, at least 150,000 people have been killed, although the actual toll may be substantially higher.

The conflict has displaced more than 12.4 million people, while approximately 33.7 million Sudanese, including 17.3 million children, urgently need humanitarian assistance. An estimated 825,000 children face severe acute malnutrition, illustrating the scale of the country's humanitarian emergency.
