Pentagon Publishes Fifth UAP File Drop: 41 New Records

AC-130J “cold orbs” footage and 2002 Afghanistan triangle detailed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 8, 2026 at 11:32 AM ET

The Pentagon on August 7, 2026 published its fifth tranche of declassified unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) files, adding 41 documents, images, and videos to the government’s public archive. The release continues a transparency push initiated by President Trump under an executive order signed in January 2026, with officials saying more postings will follow on a rolling basis.

New materials include six videos from a September 2021 incident over the Gulf of Oman recorded aboard an AC-130J gunship during a live‑fire exercise. An accompanying intelligence report says operators observed “approximately 25” “cold orbs” maneuvering at roughly 250–1,300 mph and appearing to react when the gunship fired its main cannon; the case remains unresolved by the All‑domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO). Another filing summarizes a 2002 account over Bagram, Afghanistan, where a former military pilot described a massive, silent triangular object that “blacked out” the stars overhead—estimated at about 500 feet across—based on a redacted FBI interview form.

According to the release, the 41 files span 1950–2026 and draw from multiple agencies, including the Pentagon, FBI, CIA, State Department, and the Executive Office of the President. A Pentagon spokesman said additional files will be posted “on a rolling basis,” underscoring that these records document incidents the government has not definitively resolved. Several items feature artist renderings tied to witness interviews, and new sensor clips attributed to U.S. Central Command.

Friday’s publication follows earlier drops on May 8, 2026 and subsequent monthly releases. Prior sets included files related to a 2015 incident at the Pantex nuclear facility near Amarillo, Texas, and video associated with the 2023 Lake Huron shootdown—illustrating that the archive mixes historical records with recent sensor data. Officials say this iterative process will continue as interagency reviews clear additional materials for public release, while analysts caution that many videos lack the context needed for definitive identification.
