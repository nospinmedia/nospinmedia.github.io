President Trump calls for Ukraine to replace Zelenskyy amid Russian diesel dispute

Kyiv rejects criticism as Russian strikes kill at least 20 civilians

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 11, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

President Trump called for Ukraine to replace President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on Saturday, October 10, 2026, escalating a diplomatic dispute over Washington's decision to ease sanctions on Russian diesel exports. The remarks came as Russian airstrikes killed at least 20 people in Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia region, including three children.

Speaking to reporters outside the White House, Trump suggested Ukraine needed a leader willing to negotiate a peace agreement with Russia. He accused Zelenskyy of missing opportunities to end the war and blamed Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries for worsening global diesel shortages.

Trump also argued that Ukraine's strikes were creating problems for American farmers and consumers already struggling with elevated fuel prices.

The confrontation followed a Friday telephone conversation between Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin, after which Washington announced a temporary easing of sanctions on Russian energy exports.

Under the arrangement, Russia agreed to release 300,000 metric tons of diesel immediately, followed by another 500,000 tons in November and additional supplies afterward. Trump said as much as 4.8 million tons could eventually become available, depending on Russian refinery capacity.

Zelenskyy condemned the agreement, warning that renewed revenue from Russian energy exports could help finance Moscow's military operations. He argued that reducing economic pressure would encourage Russia to continue its attacks rather than negotiate an end to the conflict.

Ukraine's foreign minister, Andriy Sybiga, rejected Trump's call for new leadership, emphasizing that Ukrainians have the right to determine their own president.

The disagreement unfolded as Russian aerial bombs struck residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia, killing at least 20 people and injuring others. Ukrainian officials reported additional missile attacks against Kyiv.

The diesel agreement has also drawn criticism from American political figures, including former Vice President Mike Pence, who urged Trump to reverse the decision. Meanwhile, Germany reaffirmed its commitment to maintaining sanctions against Russia.

The dispute highlights growing differences between Washington and Kyiv over economic sanctions, Ukraine's military strategy and the conditions for ending the war.
