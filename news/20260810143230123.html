Louisiana Warns Beachgoers After 5 Dead From Vibrio

Florida Reports 14 Cases, 2 Deaths So Far This Year

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 11, 2026 at 10:32 AM ET

Health officials are urging caution along the Gulf Coast after an increase in Vibrio vulnificus infections during the warm-weather season. The Louisiana Department of Health has reported 9 confirmed cases in 2026, with all nine patients hospitalized and 5 deaths. At the same point during the previous decade, Louisiana averaged about 7 cases and 1 death, according to state data cited by the Associated Press.

Neighboring Florida, which typically reports more Vibrio infections than most states, has reported 14 Vibrio vulnificus cases and 2 deaths so far in 2026, according to the latest figures cited by AP. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says Vibrio bacteria naturally live in coastal waters and are found in higher concentrations during warmer months, particularly from May through October. About 1 in 5 people who develop a Vibrio vulnificus infection die, sometimes within a day or two of becoming ill.

People can become infected when an open wound is exposed to salt water or brackish water, or through contact with raw seafood. Infection can also occur from eating raw or undercooked shellfish, particularly oysters. Open wounds can include cuts and scrapes as well as recent surgical incisions, piercings and tattoos.

Severe illness is more likely among people with certain underlying conditions, including liver disease, diabetes and weakened immune systems. Wound infections can progress rapidly, causing redness, swelling, severe pain, discoloration and blistering. The CDC advises people to seek medical attention immediately if symptoms of a serious wound infection develop after exposure to coastal water.

### How to reduce the risk

- Keep open wounds and cuts out of salt water and brackish water when possible. - Cover wounds with a waterproof bandage if contact with coastal water is possible. - Wash wounds thoroughly with soap and clean running water after exposure to coastal water or raw seafood. - Avoid eating raw or undercooked oysters and other shellfish; proper cooking kills harmful Vibrio bacteria. - Seek medical care quickly for rapidly worsening redness, swelling, pain, discoloration, fever or blistering after possible exposure.

Vibrio vulnificus infections remain relatively rare, but their potential severity makes prevention especially important for people at higher risk during the Gulf Coast's warm-water season.
