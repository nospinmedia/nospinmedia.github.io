Singapore Expands Tax Breaks and Visa Program for Fund Managers

New incentives aim to strengthen financial hub against Hong Kong

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 19, 2026 at 7:38 PM ET

The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) announced a package of tax, immigration and investment measures on August 19, 2026, aimed at strengthening Singapore's position as a global asset management center amid increasing competition from Hong Kong. The initiative combines targeted tax incentives, expanded visa eligibility and a new government-backed investment program designed to attract fund managers and encourage firms to expand operations in Singapore.

The reforms include tax exemptions on qualifying profit-related returns from fund management services beginning with the 2027 Year of Assessment, a specialized pathway under the Overseas Networks & Expertise (ONE) Pass that better recognizes performance-based compensation when evaluating visa eligibility, and a state-backed program that will provide investment capital to selected hedge fund managers establishing or growing operations in the country.

Singapore says the changes are intended to ensure its immigration and tax policies remain competitive as financial firms increasingly compare regional business environments. Officials noted that the country's asset management industry oversees nearly S$7 trillion in assets and represents a significant share of Singapore's financial sector. The announcement follows recent policy moves by Hong Kong to offer tax incentives targeting investment professionals, intensifying competition between Asia's two leading financial hubs.

Additional legislative details are expected in Singapore's 2027 Budget, but officials say the package reflects a broader strategy to attract high-value financial talent and investment activity while maintaining the country's position as a leading international finance center. Analysts say the long-term success of the initiative will depend on whether the incentives encourage firms to expand operations in Singapore rather than competing financial markets.
