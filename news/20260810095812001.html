Trump Names Will Scharf As White House Counsel

Staff secretary will replace David Warrington on Sept. 1

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 10, 2026 at 12:00 PM ET

President Trump announced that White House Staff Secretary Will Scharf will become White House Counsel effective September 1, 2026, replacing David Warrington, who is leaving the administration to return to the private sector. Scharf currently oversees the flow of documents to the president and previously served as a federal prosecutor before joining Trump's legal team.

Trump said Scharf represented him in several legal matters, including the presidential immunity case decided by the U.S. Supreme Court. During Trump's first term, Scharf also worked on judicial confirmations, including those of Justices Brett Kavanaugh and Amy Coney Barrett. In July 2025, Trump appointed him chairman of the National Capital Planning Commission, where he later presided over approval of the administration's proposed $400 million White House ballroom project.

The White House Counsel serves as the president's chief legal adviser on official matters, providing guidance on executive actions, legislation, judicial nominations, presidential pardons, congressional oversight, and lawsuits involving the presidency. Scharf will assume the additional title of Assistant to the President when he takes office.

The leadership change comes as the administration continues defending several major policy initiatives in federal court. Separately, construction of the proposed White House ballroom remains the subject of ongoing litigation after a federal appeals court halted above-ground work pending further legal review. Warrington, who previously served as Trump's personal attorney and 2024 campaign lawyer, has held the White House Counsel position since the beginning of the administration.
