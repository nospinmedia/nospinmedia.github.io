Aisha Wahab Wins Special Election for California House Seat

First Afghan American Elected to Congress

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 21, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

California State Sen. Aisha Wahab won the special election to represent California's 14th Congressional District, securing the remainder of former Rep. Eric Swalwell's term through January 2027. The Associated Press projected Wahab the winner after vote totals showed her defeating Bay Area Rapid Transit Board President Melissa Hernandez by roughly 53.1% to 46.9%. The victory makes Wahab the first Afghan American ever elected to the U.S. Congress, marking a historic milestone while preserving Democratic control of the East Bay district.

Wahab, a progressive Democrat who previously served on the Hayward City Council before joining the California Senate, campaigned on housing affordability, public safety, and expanding economic opportunity. She overcame a late surge of outside spending that dramatically narrowed what had once been a commanding lead. Groups affiliated with the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) invested millions of dollars supporting Hernandez and opposing Wahab, turning the race into one of the most expensive House special elections of the year.

The seat became vacant after Swalwell resigned from Congress in April 2026 while denying allegations of sexual assault and misconduct that prompted multiple investigations. Because California conducted separate elections for the unexpired term and the next full term, Wahab and Hernandez will face each other again in the November 3, 2026, general election, which will determine who represents the district for the full two-year congressional term beginning in January 2027. The district will also include newly redrawn boundaries that add voters from Dublin, potentially reshaping the political landscape.

Wahab's election is expected to strengthen the Democratic caucus in the House while highlighting ongoing ideological divisions within the party between progressive and more moderate factions. Her victory also demonstrates that heavy outside spending does not necessarily determine election outcomes, even in closely contested races.
