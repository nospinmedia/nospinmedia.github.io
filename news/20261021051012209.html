Trump administration says more than 300,000 U.S. visas revoked

State Department reports accelerated enforcement during second term

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 21, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The Trump administration says it has revoked more than 300,000 U.S. visas since President Trump returned to office in January 2025, part of a broader expansion of immigration screening and enforcement affecting people who had previously received permission to travel to the United States.

The U.S. State Department said the pace of revocations is faster than under previous administrations. The total has risen substantially from the more than 175,000 visas the department said had been revoked by August 2025. Officials say visas have been canceled for reasons including criminal activity, immigration or visa fraud, national security concerns and violations of visa conditions.

The administration has also expanded screening of visa applicants and existing visa holders, including reviews of social media activity. In September 2026, the State Department announced that enhanced online-presence reviews would be expanded to additional visa categories beginning October 1, adding foreign media representatives and certain professional workers and dependents to categories already subject to such screening.

Some of the administration's visa actions have generated legal and civil-liberties disputes. Immigration and rights advocates have argued that expanded screening and revocation policies can threaten due process and suppress protected expression. Administration officials maintain that holding a U.S. visa is a privilege and that continuous screening is necessary to protect national security and public safety.

The broader immigration crackdown has extended beyond revocations. The administration has restricted or suspended visa issuance for nationals of numerous countries, tightened screening requirements and imposed additional limitations on some student, worker and immigrant visa programs.

The 300,000-plus figure represents visas revoked after they had been issued, rather than visa applications that were denied or visas that were never issued because of separate travel or processing restrictions.
