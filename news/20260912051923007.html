CIA releases 71 pre-9/11 intelligence briefs

Files reveal warnings about bin Laden and al-Qaeda plots

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 12, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

The CIA has declassified 71 President's Daily Brief items concerning Osama bin Laden and al-Qaeda, providing a new look at intelligence presented to Presidents Bill Clinton and George W. Bush before the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks. Released on the 25th anniversary of 9/11, the collection spans more than 100 pages and includes assessments of al-Qaeda's plans, capabilities and efforts to attack the United States.

The documents cover intelligence from the years before the attacks, including warnings about bin Laden's interest in weapons of mass destruction and possible attacks on American targets. One briefing from February 13, 1998, reported that bin Laden was seeking chemical, biological and radiological capabilities. Another assessment from September 1998 discussed threats against Washington, D.C., including intelligence indicating that terrorists associated with bin Laden might consider using an aircraft packed with explosives against a U.S. city.

The newly public material also documents the U.S. government's efforts to pressure the Taliban, which controlled Afghanistan and provided sanctuary to bin Laden. An April 1998 briefing described the Taliban's refusal to surrender him despite U.S. efforts to secure his expulsion. Other documents followed al-Qaeda's financing, personnel, overseas activities and evolving plans as American intelligence agencies attempted to understand the growing threat.

CIA Director John Ratcliffe said the release provides additional transparency about intelligence available to senior U.S. policymakers before the attacks. The documents do not establish that intelligence agencies knew the specific operational details of the September 11 attacks in advance. Instead, they illustrate the broader and sometimes fragmentary warnings officials received about al-Qaeda's intention and capability to conduct major attacks against U.S. interests.

The release accompanies additional historical material made public around the anniversary, including records connected to the 9/11 Commission's investigation. The September 11 attacks killed nearly 3,000 people after hijackers crashed passenger aircraft into the World Trade Center and Pentagon, while a fourth aircraft, United Flight 93, crashed in Pennsylvania after passengers and crew fought the hijackers.
