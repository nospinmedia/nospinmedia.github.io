Russia votes under massive Ukrainian drone attack

Anti-war candidates barred as voting extends into occupied Ukraine

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 20, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Russia entered the final day of its State Duma parliamentary election on September 20 while facing the largest Ukrainian drone attack yet reported against Moscow. The vote is Russia's first parliamentary election since its 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine and, for the first time, includes occupied portions of Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia that Moscow claims as Russian territory despite international rejection of those annexations.

The wartime election is taking place in a heavily restricted political environment. Most anti-war candidates were barred from running, while Russia's Supreme Court removed the liberal Yabloko party from the nationwide party ballot after a challenge alleging electoral violations, which Yabloko denied. Some Yabloko candidates remain in individual constituency races. Senior opposition figures have received prison sentences or gone into exile, and Russian laws allow lengthy prison terms for statements authorities consider false information about or discrediting of the military.

Independent scrutiny is also limited. Russia did not invite election monitors from the Organization for Security and Cooperation in Europe, although authorities say hundreds of thousands of domestic observers and more than 1,100 international observers and experts are registered. Yabloko and the Communist Party have alleged violations including observers being denied access, sealed ballot boxes being opened and turnout information being withheld. Those allegations have not been independently established.

Voting in occupied Ukrainian territory has drawn additional condemnation. Ukrainian officials and the European Union reject Russia's authority to conduct elections there. Ukrainian human-rights activists have alleged that some residents were pressured to participate through threats involving employment or social benefits. Russian authorities say more than 3.5 million voters live in the four regions it claimed to annex in 2022.

The final day coincided with a massive Ukrainian attack involving more than 1,000 drones, including hundreds aimed toward Moscow. Russian officials said two people were killed and accused Ukraine of attempting to disrupt the election. Ukraine confirmed striking Russian oil and logistics infrastructure but did not confirm that disrupting the vote was its objective.
