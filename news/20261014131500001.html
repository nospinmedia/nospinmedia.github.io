FBI Arrests OpenAI Engineer Over Hidden Recording Device

Federal Investigators Say Employee Used AI Tools to Plan Surveillance of Executive Meetings

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 14, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The FBI has arrested a former OpenAI engineer accused of secretly installing a sophisticated recording device inside an executive conference room at the company's San Francisco offices. Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national, was arrested on October 9, 2026, and appeared in federal court on Tuesday, October 13, following an investigation into an alleged attempt to intercept confidential company discussions.

According to the U.S. Department of Justice, OpenAI security personnel discovered the concealed device on September 30, connected to a USB port behind a television in a conference room used for sensitive executive meetings. Investigators said the device had been wrapped in toilet paper to conceal it and contained encrypted data. The company determined that it had likely been installed around September 28.

Federal prosecutors allege that Alfozan, who joined OpenAI in July 2025, used internal productivity tools to research company buildings, executive meeting locations, conference room schedules, and other information potentially useful for surveillance. A chatbot account associated with his spouse also contained inquiries about building continuous audio recorders, configuring wireless connectivity, improving remote listening capabilities, and encrypting stored recordings. Investigators linked some of those inquiries to technical identifiers associated with the recovered device.

Surveillance footage reportedly showed Alfozan near the restricted conference room on several occasions. On October 5, cameras captured him entering the room and appearing to examine the area behind the television where the device had been installed. That same day, security personnel discovered a second similar recording device inside an unattended bag containing his identification and personal belongings.

OpenAI confirmed that it terminated Alfozan's employment and contacted federal investigators. The company said it used its own artificial intelligence models to help analyze the equipment and identify the suspected employee. It also reported finding no indications that the individual improperly accessed or removed AI model weights, source code, or customer information. That finding does not establish whether the recording device captured confidential conversations.

FBI Director Kash Patel described the investigation as part of broader efforts to protect sensitive American artificial intelligence research. However, authorities have not publicly established a foreign intelligence connection or identified an outside organization directing the alleged activity. Alfozan's Saudi nationality does not, by itself, establish such a connection.

Alfozan faces a federal charge of using or attempting to use an electronic device to intercept oral communications. If convicted, he could receive up to five years in prison, a $250,000 fine, and three years of supervised release. His next court appearance is scheduled for October 19. The allegations remain unproven, and he is presumed innocent unless convicted.
