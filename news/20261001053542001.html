Tennessee execution fails after two lethal injections

Christa Pike remains alive as governor orders outside review

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 1, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Christa Gail Pike remained alive after Tennessee attempted to execute her by lethal injection Wednesday night, an extraordinary development hours after the U.S. Supreme Court overturned a lower-court stay and allowed the execution to proceed. Pike, 50, was transported to an off-site medical facility after the state was unable to complete what would have been Tennessee's first execution of a woman in more than 200 years.

Pike received two rounds of lethal-injection drugs but continued breathing, according to witnesses and her attorneys. The Tennessee Department of Correction said it had followed every step of the state's established execution protocol and that the protocol did not permit additional procedures beyond those carried out. The department subsequently confirmed Pike had been taken to a medical facility.

The failed execution prompted Gov. Bill Lee to suspend Tennessee executions for the remainder of the year and order an independent review of what happened. The development shifts attention from the legal battle over whether Pike's execution could proceed to questions about Tennessee's lethal-injection procedures and why the drugs failed to cause her death as intended.

Earlier Wednesday, the 6th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals had stayed Pike's execution while considering her pending legal arguments. Tennessee appealed, and the U.S. Supreme Court vacated that stay. Justices Sonia Sotomayor, Elena Kagan and Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented. The Supreme Court's order cleared the way for Tennessee to proceed later that evening.

Pike was sentenced to death for the 1995 murder of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer in Knoxville, committed when Pike was 18. Her attorneys had argued that severe childhood sexual abuse and other mitigating evidence were not adequately presented during the penalty phase of her trial. Gov. Lee denied Pike's request for clemency before the scheduled execution.

Because Pike survived the attempt, Tennessee would need additional legal proceedings before another execution could be scheduled. The state's independent review is now expected to examine the execution protocol and how Wednesday night's procedure failed.
