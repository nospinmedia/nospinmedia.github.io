New Forensic Analysis Challenges Epstein Suicide Ruling

Expert says injury patterns more closely fit homicidal strangulation

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 17, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A forensic medicine professor who spent months reviewing Jeffrey Epstein's autopsy records and photographs says the evidence is more consistent with homicidal strangulation than suicide, challenging the official finding that Epstein died by suicide at Manhattan's Metropolitan Correctional Center on August 10, 2019.

Dr. Michael Freeman, editor-in-chief of the Journal of Forensic and Legal Medicine, presented his findings in London on Monday, November 16. Freeman worked with Swedish forensic medicine professor Anders Eriksson and researcher Itiel Dror, who specializes in bias in forensic decision-making. Freeman said he was not paid for the review by Epstein's brother, Mark Epstein, who provided the materials.

Freeman created three-dimensional biomechanical models using autopsy photographs, documented injuries and an account of Epstein being found hanging from a bedpost. He said some injuries were compatible with hanging but argued that multiple neck markings, deep hemorrhaging in Epstein's left shoulder, a back injury and marks interpreted as possible fingernail scratches were not adequately explained by suicide. He concluded the overall pattern was more consistent with strangulation by another person.

Freeman did not physically examine Epstein's body, and his analysis has not been accepted for peer-reviewed publication. An academic journal rejected the report, and Freeman said he plans to resubmit it. He has previously served as a cause-of-death expert in other high-profile cases, including the George Floyd murder trial.

The New York City Office of Chief Medical Examiner continues to stand by its 2019 suicide determination. Dr. Kristin Roman, who performed Epstein's autopsy, previously told federal investigators she was not permitted to inspect his jail cell and was not provided photographs showing his body hanging. Roman also reported finding no signs of a struggle and no debris beneath Epstein's fingernails.

Federal investigations have documented serious failures surrounding Epstein's death, but authorities have not changed the official finding.
