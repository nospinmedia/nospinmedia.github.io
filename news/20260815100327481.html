Magnitude 7.7 Quake Strikes Indonesia’s Flores Region

At least 38 dead as rescuers confront landslides and damaged roads

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A powerful magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck Indonesia’s Flores region early August 15, 2026, killing at least 38 people, injuring more than a dozen others and damaging buildings across several districts in East Nusa Tenggara. Indonesian authorities initially issued tsunami warnings for several provinces and urged coastal residents to move to higher ground, but the warnings were later lifted after monitoring showed no significant sea-level changes threatening coastal communities.

The U.S. Geological Survey said the earthquake struck at 5:58 a.m. local time at a depth of 10 kilometers, with its epicenter approximately 68 kilometers north-northwest of Ende. Rescue teams were deployed across affected communities as officials assessed collapsed buildings, damaged homes and disrupted infrastructure. Indonesia’s disaster agency reported 38 deaths, while 2 people were seriously injured, 11 suffered lighter injuries, and roughly 2,000 residents were evacuated from affected areas.

Landslides triggered by the shaking blocked portions of the Trans-Flores Highway, complicating access for emergency crews moving between communities. Power outages and communications disruptions also slowed damage assessments. Hospitals in several areas moved patients and medical equipment outdoors as a precaution while engineers checked buildings for structural damage. Authorities warned residents to stay away from damaged structures as aftershocks continued following the main earthquake.

The quake was felt across much of Flores Island and in neighboring parts of Indonesia. The region has a long history of destructive seismic activity because Indonesia sits along the Pacific Ring of Fire. In 1992, an earthquake and tsunami on Flores killed about 2,500 people, contributing to the rapid coastal evacuations ordered after Saturday’s quake.

Search-and-rescue operations and damage assessments remain underway, meaning casualty and damage figures could change as teams reach communities affected by landslides and communications failures.
