Father of deployed USS Lincoln sailor released after immigration case dismissed

Rapid turnaround raises questions about enforcement involving military families

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 27, 2026 at 9:26 AM ET

Luis Manuel Aviles Roa, the father of an active-duty U.S. Navy sailor serving aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln, has been released from federal immigration custody after an immigration judge dismissed his removal case only hours after it was filed. Aviles Roa, a 48-year-old native of Nicaragua who has lived in Key West, Florida, for 19 years, was arrested during a Border Patrol traffic stop on August 22 and transferred to an immigration detention center in Miami. His family confirmed he returned home on August 25 following the court's decision.

The case drew national attention after his son, Joshua Aviles, posted on social media that he learned of his father's arrest while serving aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln, which recently completed a record-setting deployment of more than 200 consecutive days at sea. Joshua wrote that his father had a valid work permit, driver's license and Social Security card while awaiting a decision on his application for permanent legal residency. The Department of Homeland Security said Aviles Roa had entered the United States unlawfully and initially stated he would remain in custody pending removal proceedings.

According to Department of Justice records, an immigration judge dismissed the case on August 24, just hours after it reached the Miami immigration court. Neither the Department of Homeland Security nor U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement has publicly explained why the case was dismissed or why Aviles Roa was released so quickly. Immigration attorneys described the rapid filing, dismissal and release as highly unusual compared with typical immigration proceedings.

The case has intensified scrutiny of immigration enforcement involving the immediate family members of active-duty military personnel. Members of Congress have requested information from the Department of Homeland Security about enforcement practices affecting military families, while administration officials have maintained that immigration laws are enforced regardless of a person's family connections or military service. The Navy confirmed Joshua Aviles serves aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln, which is now returning to the United States after its extended deployment.
