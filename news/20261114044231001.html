FAA lifts nationwide ground stop

Duffy calls for $50 billion aviation overhaul after outage

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 14, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

The Federal Aviation Administration lifted its nationwide ground stop Friday, November 13, after telecommunications problems disrupted flight departures across the United States. Administration officials said aging switches and routers became overloaded, but the precise technical root cause remains under investigation.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said U.S. airspace reopened at 8:15 p.m. Eastern Time, more than four hours after the nationwide stop began. The FAA said it tested its systems before allowing departures to resume. Thousands of flights were canceled or delayed, with additional cancellations extending into Saturday.

CBS News, citing multiple sources, reported that an update to a communications network operated by L3Harris caused a reboot and outage. Officials described instability affecting radar data and communications at air traffic control facilities. Aircraft already airborne continued toward landing while departures were restricted.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said investigators had found zero evidence of a cyberattack, although the investigation remained ongoing. Officials said the disruption was not connected to the preceding Microsoft outage.

The incident was followed by a larger funding demand. At Friday evening’s briefing, after the ground stop was lifted, Duffy said he and President Trump had requested $30 billion for aviation improvements. He then called for $50 billion, saying, “I want $50 billion.”

Earlier Friday, the administration had sought $30.1 billion for a package covering air traffic control modernization, control towers and airport improvements. Congress previously approved $12.5 billion over five years for air traffic control upgrades and additional controller hiring. Duffy’s call for $50 billion was a funding request, not an approved appropriation.

Airlines continued working to reposition aircraft and crews, with the reopening allowing flights to resume gradually rather than immediately restoring normal schedules. Passengers were advised to check directly with their airlines.
