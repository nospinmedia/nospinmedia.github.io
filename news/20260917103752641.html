EU proposes Canada as its first ‘associate member’

New partnership could deepen trade, defense and energy ties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 17, 2026 at 6:00 PM ET

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has proposed opening the door for Canada to become the European Union's first "associate member," an unprecedented status that does not currently exist under EU treaties. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney welcomed the prospect of a stronger alliance, although neither side is proposing that Canada become a full member of the 27-nation EU.

Von der Leyen announced the idea during her annual State of the Union address to the European Parliament in Strasbourg on September 16, with Carney in attendance. She said the EU and Canada should build a new partnership encompassing economic security, advanced manufacturing, technology, defense industries, artificial intelligence, energy, critical minerals, batteries, quantum technology, cybersecurity and the Arctic.

Exactly what associate membership would mean remains unresolved. No such membership category currently exists in EU treaties, and von der Leyen's proposal would need support from EU member governments before moving forward. Creating a formal new status through changes to EU treaties would require agreement among all 27 member states and domestic ratification. Canada and the EU already have extensive economic ties through the Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement, known as CETA.

Carney has described Canada's goal as developing a "unique alliance" with Europe rather than pursuing full EU membership. His office said Canada and Europe should strengthen their strategic autonomy through deeper cooperation across key industries and security capabilities. Canada also formally applied this week to join the UK-led Joint Expeditionary Force, a 10-country European rapid-response military coalition.

The proposal has also drawn a response from President Trump, who said on September 17 that he could impose additional tariffs or restrict some trade with Europe if he considered an EU-Canada arrangement hostile to U.S. interests. The European Commission said the proposed partnership is intended to strengthen Canada and Europe and is not directed against another country.
