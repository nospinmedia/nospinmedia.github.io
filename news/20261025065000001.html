Trump says he has the ‘right to run again’ in 2028

President acknowledges Constitution leaves ‘not a lot of wiggle room’

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 25, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

President Donald Trump says he believes he has the “right to run again” in 2028, even while acknowledging that the constitutional restriction preventing him from being elected president a third time is unusually clear.

Trump made the comments Thursday during a campaign rally in Sioux City, Iowa, where he was campaigning for Republican candidates ahead of the November 3 midterm elections.

“I think the law says that I'm not allowed to run again,” Trump told the crowd. He said he had studied the relevant language “forever,” describing it as “quite concise” and adding that “there's not a lot of wiggle room.”

But Trump then offered his own reason for why he believes he should be able to seek the presidency again. Referring to the 2020 presidential election, Trump repeated his claim that the election had been stolen from him and said: “So I think I have the right to run again.” He repeated the statement before again alleging that the 2020 election was rigged.

Trump's claim that he won the 2020 election is not supported by evidence that changed the outcome. Joe Biden won the 2020 presidential election, while Trump won presidential elections in 2016 and 2024.

The constitutional restriction Trump referenced is the 22nd Amendment, which states that “No person shall be elected to the office of the President more than twice.” The amendment does not provide an exception allowing another presidential run because a candidate believes an earlier election was improperly decided.

Trump has raised the possibility of another term before, sometimes joking about it and sometimes discussing possible ways it could happen. His latest comments are notable because he both acknowledged how explicit the constitutional restriction is and then said he nevertheless believes the circumstances surrounding 2020 give him the right to run again.

Trump also told supporters during the Iowa appearance that he has two years remaining in his current term, while urging them to vote Republican in the upcoming midterms.
