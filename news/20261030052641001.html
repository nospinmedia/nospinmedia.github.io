U.S. economy grows 3.4% in third quarter

Strong spending comes alongside inflation and falling savings

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 30, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The U.S. economy grew at a 3.4% annualized rate from July through September, accelerating from 2.2% in the second quarter as consumer spending and business investment remained strong. The Bureau of Economic Analysis said investment, consumer spending and government spending contributed to the increase, while declining exports and rising imports weighed on growth.

Consumers, whose spending accounts for more than two-thirds of economic activity, increased spending at a 3.8% annualized rate during the quarter. Business equipment investment jumped 20.9%, with information-processing equipment such as computers and peripherals accounting for much of the increase. Separate AP reporting found business investment excluding housing rose 12.7%, including a 37.4% increase in information-processing equipment. Housing investment, meanwhile, declined 3.3% amid high mortgage rates.

The strong headline GDP figure came with signs of pressure on household finances. The personal saving rate declined from 4.4% in the second quarter to 4.1% in the third, before falling to 3.8% in September. After adjusting for inflation and taxes, disposable household income declined 0.3% in September, while inflation-adjusted consumer spending was unchanged that month.

Inflation also remained above the Federal Reserve's 2% target. The Personal Consumption Expenditures price index increased 3.6% from a year earlier in September, up from 3.4% in August. The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate at 3.75% to 4.00% this week while saying inflation remained elevated.

The 3.4% GDP figure is an advance estimate and will be revised as additional information becomes available. The BEA is scheduled to publish its second estimate for third-quarter GDP on November 25.
