Judge seeks outside review of government statements in Trump golf case

Former U.S. attorney could examine whether DOJ lawyers misled court

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

A federal judge wants a former U.S. attorney to investigate whether Justice Department lawyers misled her about the Trump administration’s plans to close and redevelop Washington’s East Potomac Golf Links, escalating a court inquiry into the government’s handling of the case.

U.S. District Judge Ana C. Reyes said she intends to appoint Jessie K. Liu, who served as U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia from 2017 to 2020, as a special master. Liu would examine the circumstances surrounding statements made by government attorneys and help determine whether sanctions or other court action may be warranted.

The dispute centers on the administration’s plans for the federally owned public golf facility. During court proceedings, government lawyers maintained that there was no established timeline for closing the course. The administration subsequently filed notice that the facility would close on November 15 for a redevelopment project expected to last about 15 months.

At an October 30 hearing, Reyes sharply questioned the government’s representations and whether Justice Department attorneys had provided accurate information to the court. The judge has ordered federal officials and government lawyers involved in the matter to preserve emails, notes and other records connected to decisions about the golf course.

The proposed special master would review that evidence and investigate the sequence of communications and decisions surrounding the closure. Appointment of a special master would allow someone outside the immediate litigation to conduct fact-finding and report findings to the judge.

The inquiry does not establish that Justice Department attorneys intentionally misled the court. Its purpose is to determine what government officials and lawyers knew, when they knew it and whether representations made during the litigation were accurate.
