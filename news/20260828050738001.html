U.S. nears long-term deal for Venezuelan oilfields

Talks target access to 17 fields holding 90B barrels

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 28, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

The Trump administration is in advanced negotiations with Venezuela's interim government on a landmark energy agreement that would give American companies long-term access to develop 17 oil fields containing an estimated 90 billion barrels of proven crude reserves. According to officials familiar with the talks, the proposed framework would allow U.S. energy firms to rehabilitate aging production sites in the Orinoco Belt and around Lake Maracaibo through long-term leases, joint ventures or competitive operating agreements rather than transferring ownership of Venezuela's underground oil reserves.

The negotiations are being led by Secretary of State Marco Rubio and senior administration officials, with Energy Secretary Chris Wright expected to travel to Caracas in early September if discussions continue to progress. The proposal would guarantee long-term crude supplies for U.S. refiners while providing Venezuela with investment, technical expertise and revenue to restore production after years of economic decline and underinvestment. Venezuela possesses the world's largest proven oil reserves, but production has fallen sharply over the past decade.

The discussions follow major political changes in Venezuela earlier this year and represent one of the most significant shifts in U.S.-Venezuelan relations in decades. Officials view the agreement as a way to strengthen Western Hemisphere energy security and reduce reliance on supplies vulnerable to disruptions elsewhere in the world. The proposal remains under negotiation, and no final agreement has been announced.

Legal experts caution that any agreement will need to comply with Venezuela's Hydrocarbons Law and the 1999 Constitution, which reserve ownership of subsoil resources to the Venezuelan state. While foreign companies have previously participated in joint ventures, the structure of any new agreement could face legal and political scrutiny inside Venezuela. Separate reports also indicate Venezuelan officials are weighing the country's future relationship with OPEC, although no decision has been announced.
