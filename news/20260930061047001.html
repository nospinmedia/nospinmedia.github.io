Pentagon plans 20% cut to general and admiral positions

Military ordered to complete reductions by January 1

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 30, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is set to announce that the Pentagon will cut 20% of positions reserved for generals and admirals, doubling a reduction ordered last year as part of a broader restructuring of senior military leadership. Pentagon officials confirmed the plan ahead of Hegseth's September 30 State of the Force address at Marine Corps Base Quantico in Virginia.

The military currently has roughly 800 general and flag officer positions, meaning a 20% reduction would affect about 160 positions. The services have been directed to complete the reductions by January 1, 2027. Officials told Reuters that some positions will be eliminated while others will be downgraded to lower ranks rather than removing 20% of the people currently serving as generals and admirals.

The new target doubles a 10% overall reduction in general and flag officer positions that Hegseth ordered in 2025. That earlier directive separately called for at least a 20% reduction in four-star positions across the active-duty force and a similar reduction in general officers in the National Guard. It remains unclear how much of those earlier reductions have already been completed.

The Pentagon says the changes are intended to reduce redundant bureaucracy and create a more efficient military leadership structure. Hegseth previously described the effort as "less generals, more GIs" and said reducing senior positions was not intended as punishment for individual officers. The latest plan comes after President Trump and Hegseth fired or pushed more than two dozen senior military leaders into retirement since the administration took office.

The proposal has also drawn scrutiny on Capitol Hill. Sen. Jack Reed, the ranking Democrat on the Senate Armed Services Committee, said Congress should be consulted before major leadership positions are removed and questioned whether further reductions could affect military readiness during ongoing overseas operations.
