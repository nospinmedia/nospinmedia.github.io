Appeals Court Halts Army Plan To Detonate 5 Tons Of Explosive

EPA Permit Paused As Iowa Community Raises Lead-Contamination Concerns

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 21, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

A federal appeals court has halted the U.S. Army's plan to detonate nearly 5 tons of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant, putting the operation on hold while judges consider a challenge to the environmental permit allowing the blast.

Late Thursday, November 19, the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals issued an administrative stay temporarily blocking an emergency permit granted by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. The stay remains in place while the court considers an emergency motion filed by environmental groups, including the Sierra Club and Earthjustice.

The Army is storing 68 barrels, containing roughly 9,700 to 10,000 pounds, of lead azide at the facility near Middletown, Iowa. The explosive has been kept in earthen-covered bunkers for more than 20 years in a mixture of ethanol and water intended to keep it stable. Inspections earlier this year found that liquid had evaporated and some of the lead azide had recrystallized, making the material dangerously sensitive to friction and shock.

The Army initially planned the controlled open-air detonation for Friday, November 20, then postponed it until Monday, November 23. The appeals court order now prevents the blast from proceeding while the legal challenge remains under review.

The operation had prompted significant precautions in surrounding communities. Nearby schools announced closures, while residents were advised to remain indoors or temporarily leave the area and clean outdoor surfaces afterward because the blast was expected to produce a plume containing lead particles. Iowa Gov. Kim Reynolds and other state officials also raised concerns about the amount of advance notice provided.

Army officials say leaving the deteriorating explosive in storage presents its own danger. The Army rejected chemical neutralization alternatives after determining that moving or extensively handling the unstable barrels could cause an accidental explosion and potentially kill workers.

The ruling materially changes the situation reported earlier by NSM: the planned detonation is now on hold indefinitely pending further court action.
