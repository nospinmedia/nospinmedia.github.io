Russia Releases U.S. Marine Veteran Robert Gilman

Humanitarian release ends more than four years in Russian custody

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 12, 2026 at 12:18 PM ET

Russia released former U.S. Marine Robert Gilman on August 11, 2026, ending more than four years of detention in a case the U.S. State Department had classified as a wrongful detention. President Trump announced the release, and U.S. officials said it was granted on humanitarian grounds without a prisoner exchange, financial payment, or policy concession by the United States.

Gilman, a 32-year-old teacher from Lowell, Massachusetts, was arrested in Voronezh, Russia, in January 2022 after an altercation aboard a train. He was initially sentenced to 3½ years for assaulting a police officer, but additional convictions involving alleged assaults on detention staff increased his prison term to 10 years. U.S. officials repeatedly called for his release, arguing his treatment and continued imprisonment were unjustified.

Advocates and family members said Gilman's health had deteriorated significantly during his imprisonment. Global Reach, an organization assisting with the case, reported he spent weeks in a severely impaired condition and required hospitalization and a feeding tube before his release. U.S. officials said they remain concerned about reports of mistreatment and indicated Gilman will continue receiving medical evaluation and care following his return to the United States.

The release comes as Washington continues efforts to secure the freedom of other Americans detained in Russia. Administration officials emphasized that Gilman's return was a unilateral humanitarian action rather than part of a negotiated prisoner swap, distinguishing it from several previous U.S.-Russia detainee agreements. U.S. officials say at least eight Americans remain in Russian custody, with diplomatic efforts continuing to seek their release.
