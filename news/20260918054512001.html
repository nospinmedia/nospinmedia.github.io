White House withdraws Trump's nominee to lead ICE

Senate hearing stalled as Rand Paul sought answers on civilian deaths

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 18, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The White House has withdrawn President Trump's nomination of Richard "Lance" Schroyer to lead U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, leaving the agency without a Senate-confirmed director as it carries out a major expansion of immigration enforcement. The administration announced the withdrawal on September 17 but did not provide a reason for the decision.

Schroyer, a former Oklahoma Highway Patrol major and U.S. Marine, was nominated in June and has served as a senior adviser to Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin. Schroyer had nearly three decades of law-enforcement experience and helped expand Oklahoma's participation in the federal 287(g) program, which allows trained state and local officers to perform certain immigration-enforcement functions. He had not previously worked for ICE or led a major federal law-enforcement agency.

His nomination had stalled before the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee, where Chairman Rand Paul of Kentucky had declined to hold a confirmation hearing until the Department of Homeland Security provided information he requested about civilian deaths during immigration-enforcement operations. Paul told the Associated Press that he did not have a concern with Schroyer himself and was still waiting for an update on the internal investigations.

The withdrawal comes as ICE faces scrutiny over its enforcement operations and rapid expansion. The agency recorded nearly 51,000 arrests in August, according to the Associated Press, the highest monthly total of President Trump's second term. David Venturella continues to serve as acting director after former acting director Todd Lyons left the agency earlier this year.

ICE has operated without a Senate-confirmed director since 2017, and the administration has not announced another nominee to replace Schroyer.
