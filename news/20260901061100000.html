Army Secretary Dan Driscoll resigns from Pentagon post

Departure adds to leadership turnover at Defense Department

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 1, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Secretary of the Army Dan Driscoll has resigned from his position after submitting his resignation to President Trump on August 31, 2026, becoming the highest-ranking civilian Army official to leave the Pentagon during the administration. The White House confirmed the resignation, with spokeswoman Anna Kelly thanking Driscoll for his service and highlighting his work on military readiness, overseas operations, and diplomatic efforts involving the war in Ukraine.

Driscoll, an Iraq War veteran who was confirmed by the U.S. Senate in February 2025, had overseen the Army during a period of modernization initiatives and global operational demands. Multiple reports said his departure followed months of disagreements with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over leadership decisions, service autonomy, and modernization priorities. Neither Driscoll nor the Pentagon publicly detailed the specific reasons for his resignation.

His departure comes after several senior leadership changes within the Army. Earlier this year, Gen. Randy George retired as Army chief of staff, followed by the departure of Gen. Christopher Donahue, commander of U.S. Army Europe and Africa. Driscoll had also championed modernization efforts, including the Army Transformation Initiative and the FUZE rapid technology acquisition program, both intended to accelerate the fielding of new capabilities for soldiers.

The resignation leaves the Army without its Senate-confirmed civilian leader while the administration considers a successor. Defense analysts said the leadership transition comes at a significant time as the Army continues modernization efforts, supports overseas deployments, and manages evolving global security challenges. Pentagon officials have not announced when a nomination for a new Army secretary will be submitted to the Senate.
