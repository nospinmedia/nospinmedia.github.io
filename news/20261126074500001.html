FedEx cargo jet overruns runway at Miami airport

No injuries reported as incident disrupts Thanksgiving travel

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 26, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A FedEx cargo jet overran a runway at Miami International Airport Wednesday night, temporarily leaving the airport with only one operating runway and triggering a ground stop during the busy Thanksgiving travel period.

The McDonnell Douglas MD-11F, operating as FedEx Flight 910, had arrived from Memphis when it overran Runway 26R at about 8:40 p.m. Wednesday, November 25. The 34-year-old aircraft, registration N605FE, came to a stop near the airport perimeter following the runway excursion.

Two pilots were aboard the freighter, and both exited safely. Authorities reported no injuries, fire or smoke. Emergency personnel from Miami-Dade Fire Rescue and other local agencies responded, while FedEx said it was cooperating with authorities investigating the incident.

Rain was reported around the airport at the time of the overrun. The cause, however, has not been determined, and there has been no official finding that weather caused the aircraft to leave the runway.

The incident initially closed all of Miami International’s runways except Runway 9R and prompted the Federal Aviation Administration to impose a ground stop on Miami-bound flights. The ground stop was lifted later Wednesday night and additional runways reopened, although Runway 26R remained closed Thursday morning as crews worked to remove the disabled aircraft.

The disruption came on the night before Thanksgiving, during one of the busiest travel periods of the year. More than 1,000 flights were affected by delays at the airport, with average delays reported at about 44 minutes during the disruption.

The incident also follows another cargo-plane runway excursion at Miami International in September 2026, when a Boeing 767-300 freighter operating for Amazon Air ran off Runway 30 in an accident that killed five people.

Investigators will examine the aircraft, runway conditions and other operational information to determine what caused Wednesday’s overrun.
