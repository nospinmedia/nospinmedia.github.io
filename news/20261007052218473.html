Disney and ABC challenge FCC over early license reviews

Court fight tests government power over broadcasters

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 7, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

The Walt Disney Company and ABC are asking a federal court to stop the Federal Communications Commission from accelerating reviews of licenses for all eight ABC-owned television stations, arguing the unusual action amounts to government retaliation for programming and news coverage protected by the First Amendment.

The case was heard Tuesday before U.S. District Judge Loren L. AliKhan in Washington, D.C. Broadcast networks themselves do not require FCC licenses, but individual stations need them to use the public airwaves. Those licenses normally undergo staggered renewal reviews. Under Chairman Brendan Carr, however, the FCC ordered ABC's eight owned stations to undergo reviews years ahead of their normal schedules. Disney sued the agency in August 2026 seeking to block the accelerated process.

Disney argues the reviews followed political pressure over ABC programming. Carr ordered the accelerated license reviews one day after President Trump and first lady Melania Trump publicly called for ABC to fire Jimmy Kimmel. President Trump has also repeatedly called for ABC stations to lose their licenses over programming he considers unfair. Disney argues that timing and other statements show the government is using regulatory authority to pressure the company over editorial decisions.

Carr denies that characterization. He says the accelerated reviews stem from the FCC's investigation into Disney's diversity, equity and inclusion practices, not pressure from the White House. The government also argues Disney has not suffered irreparable harm because its stations' licenses remain valid.

Disney attorney Beth Wilkinson told the court the FCC's actions are already having a chilling effect on ABC's editorial decisions. One example involved Jimmy Kimmel Live!, which kept an interview with Texas Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico off broadcast television and instead released it on YouTube amid concerns about FCC equal-time rules. ABC has also said the regulatory environment has made The View more cautious about booking political candidates.

Judge AliKhan did not immediately rule. She ordered additional written arguments, with the government due October 9 and ABC due October 14, meaning a decision is not expected before mid-October.
