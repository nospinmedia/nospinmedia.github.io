56 Decomposing Bodies Found At Chicago Funeral Home

State Suspends Director’s License As Forensic IDs Begin

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 8, 2026 at 5:17 PM ET

Authorities in Chicago have opened a criminal investigation after forensic teams recovered 56 decomposing bodies at South Chicago Chapel on the city’s Far South Side. The Cook County Medical Examiner’s Office said investigators and forensic pathologists were dispatched on August 6, 2026 to document conditions and begin identifying remains, a process officials said could take days.

Regulators moved swiftly against the funeral home’s leadership. The Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation issued an emergency suspension of funeral director Johanna Morgan’s license on Wednesday, August 5, citing an immediate danger to public safety. In the suspension order, the department alleged remains were kept in an unrefrigerated area under “deplorable conditions,” including rodent and maggot infestations. The funeral home lists its address as 2939 E. 95th St., Chicago (60617). The Chicago Police Department said its preliminary statement incorrectly described the incident as a hazardous-materials response; officials clarified that no such declaration was made by the fire department.

Investigators are cross-referencing death certificates, facility logs, and medical records to confirm identities and notify families. No arrests have been announced. Officials emphasized that families seeking to transfer a loved one’s remains may do so as identifications are confirmed and custody is lawfully established. Authorities also indicated that potential violations of the Illinois Funeral Directors and Embalmers Licensing Act are under review alongside any criminal charges that may be supported by evidence.

State and county officials noted prior regulatory issues connected to the funeral home’s operators. Public records show Johanna Morgan incorporated South Chicago Chapel in 2022, while her husband, Clark X. Morgan, is listed as the company’s president. Regulators revoked Clark X. Morgan’s funeral director license and fined him in 2024 following misconduct tied to Heights Crematory in suburban Chicago, where investigators previously found improperly stored remains. Illinois Comptroller Susana Mendoza called the latest discovery “heartbreaking and an abomination,” adding that agencies are coordinating to ensure proper handling, identification, and return of remains to families.
