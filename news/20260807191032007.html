New Mexico Judge Orders Meta to Pay $567M for Youth Mental Health

Ruling adds to earlier $375M verdict and mandates teen safety changes

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 7, 2026 at 2:30 PM ET

Meta Platforms Inc. was ordered to pay $567 million into a youth mental health abatement fund after a New Mexico court found that Facebook and Instagram contributed to harms affecting young users. The ruling by Chief Judge Bryan Biedscheid follows a March 2026 trial in which the state argued that aspects of Meta's platform design created a public nuisance. Meta said it plans to appeal.

The court directed $420 million toward youth treatment services over the next five years, with the remaining funds supporting screening, prevention, public awareness, and independent oversight. The order comes in addition to a $375 million civil penalty imposed in March 2026 after a jury found 75,000 violations of the New Mexico Unfair Practices Act, bringing Meta's total liability in the case to $942 million. The lawsuit was brought by New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez.

Beyond the financial penalties, the court ordered Meta to implement additional safety measures for users in New Mexico. Those requirements include expanding safety information shown to teens, strengthening AI-based age assurance, requesting proof of age when accounts are believed to belong to children under 13, creating a reporting portal for schools or child-safety organizations to flag suspected underage accounts, filing compliance reports twice each year, and deleting personal data collected from users under 13 when required.

The ruling recognizes limits imposed by the federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and does not require Meta to use data-collection methods prohibited under federal law. Meta argues that its existing safeguards continue to improve and that the court's remedies exceed what current technology can reasonably accomplish. The company intends to challenge the decision through the appeals process.
