U.S. monitors suspected plague case after Russian lab worker dies

Nearly 200 contacts observed as Moscow says tests remain negative

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 5, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

The U.S. government is monitoring a suspected pneumonic plague case in Siberia after a 28-year-old employee of Russia's Irkutsk Anti-Plague Research Institute died from severe pneumonia, prompting Russian authorities to place nearly 200 contacts under medical observation.

The worker, identified in Russian media as Daria Shipilova, became ill after local reports alleged she broke a test tube containing the bacterium that causes pneumonic plague on September 25. She was hospitalized in Shelekhov, near Irkutsk, on September 29 with severe pneumonia and died overnight between October 1 and October 2. The reported laboratory accident and its connection to her illness have not been independently confirmed.

Russia's public-health agency Rospotrebnadzor has not confirmed that Shipilova had plague. It said she was diagnosed with pneumonia of unknown origin and that expanded laboratory testing found no microorganisms connected to her professional work. Officials said people identified as close contacts remained without symptoms and had tested negative, while describing the epidemiological situation in Irkutsk and Shelekhov as stable.

Authorities nevertheless implemented precautionary measures. The hospital where Shipilova was treated was placed under quarantine, some departments stopped admitting or discharging patients, and nearly 200 people were reportedly placed under medical observation. Regional officials also convened an emergency anti-epidemic commission after what the governor described as a suspected particularly dangerous infection.

A U.S. State Department official said Washington is monitoring the reports with the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and other federal agencies and urged Russian authorities to provide timely and accurate information. Pneumonic plague is a bacterial infection that can be deadly but is treatable with antibiotics, particularly when treatment begins quickly.
