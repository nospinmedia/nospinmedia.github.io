Tesla puts steering-wheel-free Cybercab into Austin passenger service

NHTSA evaluates rollout as robotaxi reaches public roads

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 4, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Tesla has begun offering rides in its purpose-built Cybercab in limited areas of Austin, Texas, putting passengers into a two-seat autonomous vehicle built without a steering wheel or pedals. The September 3 rollout prompted attention from the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, which said it is in contact with Tesla and is evaluating the situation as the unconventional vehicle begins operating on public roads.

The Cybercab represents a significant change from the Model Y vehicles Tesla has primarily used for its existing robotaxi network. Designed specifically for autonomous operation, the Cybercab has no steering wheel, pedals or traditional side mirrors and relies on Tesla's camera-based autonomous-driving technology rather than the lidar and radar systems used by some competitors. Tesla said rides are initially available only in limited parts of Austin, making the launch a restricted deployment rather than a broad nationwide rollout.

Federal safety standards typically require equipment such as manual driving controls and mirrors, creating regulatory questions for vehicles designed exclusively for autonomous operation. NHTSA has not announced a formal enforcement action or investigation into the Cybercab launch; the agency told Reuters only that it is communicating with Tesla and evaluating the deployment. Federal rules also limit how manufacturers can commercially deploy vehicles that do not satisfy applicable safety standards unless they qualify through another compliance pathway or receive an exemption.

Texas records showed Tesla had 420 autonomous vehicles registered in the state immediately before the launch, including 45 Cybercabs, according to Reuters. Alphabet's Waymo had 988 vehicles registered in Texas. Tesla began Cybercab production in April 2026, but the company has not said how quickly it expects the specialized vehicles to replace Model Ys within its robotaxi fleet. CEO Elon Musk has described autonomous ride-hailing as central to Tesla's future, while the company still faces regulatory and technical hurdles as it tries to expand the service beyond Texas and other existing markets.
