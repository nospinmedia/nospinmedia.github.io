DOJ Says Kennedy Center Could Face Demolition Without Renovation

Court filing ties repairs and donor support to Trump's overhaul plan

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 26, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

The U.S. Department of Justice argued in a federal court filing that the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts could ultimately face demolition if a judge permanently blocks a planned $250 million renovation backed by President Trump. Administration lawyers described the nearly 55-year-old landmark as structurally deteriorating and financially struggling, warning that without major repairs and continued fundraising efforts the building could eventually become unsafe and require replacement.

The filing was submitted in ongoing litigation brought by Rep. Joyce Beatty (D-Ohio), who is challenging the Trump-appointed Kennedy Center board's renewed effort to recognize the president on the building's facade. Earlier this year, U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper ruled that only Congress has the authority to rename the national memorial honoring President John F. Kennedy, leading to the removal of Trump's name from the building. The current proposal would instead add the inscription "Restored and Renovated by President Donald J. Trump" while renaming the surrounding grounds President Donald J. Trump Plaza.

Justice Department attorneys argued that Trump's fundraising efforts and public recognition are essential to attracting private donations needed to complete long-delayed structural repairs. The filing warned that if renovation plans are blocked, the center could continue into what lawyers described as a financial and structural "death spiral," potentially requiring the building to be demolished and replaced with another performance venue, such as an outdoor amphitheater overlooking the Potomac River. Attorneys representing Beatty rejected that argument, calling it an improper attempt to pressure the court into approving the administration's proposal.

A federal judge is expected to consider whether the revised recognition plan complies with prior court rulings while the broader dispute over the Kennedy Center's future continues. The outcome could determine whether the renovation proceeds as proposed and whether the administration's latest naming plan survives judicial review.
