Trump Floats U.S. Claim Over Strait of Hormuz

Iran rejects remark as dispute over strategic waterway deepens

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 15, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

President Trump said on August 14, 2026, that he would "pretty soon" declare the Strait of Hormuz a territory of the United States, tying the idea to Washington's continuing blockade during the war with Iran. Speaking at a law-enforcement event on Long Island, New York, Trump argued that the United States effectively controls passage through the strategic waterway because ships cannot move through unless Washington allows them to.

The seriousness of the remark and whether it represents a new administration policy were not immediately clear. Kazem Gharibabadi, Iran's deputy foreign minister for legal and international affairs, quickly rejected Trump's statement, saying Iran would not be intimidated by threats or displays of force and maintaining that decisions about opening or closing the strait would remain under Iranian authority.

The Strait of Hormuz, bordered by Iran and Oman, is one of the world's most important energy chokepoints, with roughly 20% of global seaborne oil supplies typically passing through it. Traffic has fallen sharply during the nearly six-month conflict, and the United States has been blockading Iranian shipping and ports as part of its campaign to pressure Tehran. Iran has also imposed restrictions on commercial traffic and sought greater control over how vessels transit the waterway.

Trump had already claimed earlier in the week that the United States had "total control" of the strait and suggested Washington might keep it. Both Washington and Tehran have previously floated proposals involving tolls or fees for commercial traffic, although international maritime rules generally protect passage through internationally navigable straits. Meanwhile, negotiations remain stalled, with Iranian officials saying they have not agreed to resume direct talks with the United States and separate discussions with Oman focusing on possible shipping routes through the strait.
