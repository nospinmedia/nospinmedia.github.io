Eisenkot gets first chance to form Israeli government

Netanyahu remains prime minister during coalition talks

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 8, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

Israeli President Isaac Herzog has selected opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to receive the first opportunity to form Israel’s next government after an election left the country without a clear governing majority.

Herzog announced the decision on Saturday, November 7, following consultations with political parties after Israel’s October 27 election. The presidential mandate is scheduled to be formally presented to Eisenkot on Sunday, November 8, at the President’s Residence in Jerusalem.

During the consultations, parties representing 67 seats in the 120-seat Knesset recommended Eisenkot for the mandate, compared with 46 seats supporting Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Eisenkot’s support included opposition parties as well as the Arab-majority Ra’am and Joint List. The recommendation count helps explain Herzog’s decision even though Netanyahu’s Likud won more individual seats than Eisenkot’s Yashar party in the election.

Eisenkot, a former chief of staff of the Israel Defense Forces, now faces the more difficult task of converting those recommendations into a coalition capable of governing. Support for receiving the presidential mandate does not necessarily mean every recommending party will join his government.

Under Israel’s government-formation process, Eisenkot initially has 28 days to secure enough parliamentary support to form a government. Herzog can grant an extension of up to 14 additional days. If Eisenkot cannot assemble a coalition, the process can continue with another lawmaker receiving an opportunity to try.

Netanyahu remains prime minister while negotiations proceed, meaning Herzog’s decision does not itself remove him from office or guarantee that Eisenkot will become prime minister.
