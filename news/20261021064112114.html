Food importer challenges Trump's 50% tariffs on Canadian goods

Lawsuit argues administration relied on a superseded 1930 trade law

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 21, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

A New Jersey food importer has sued the Trump administration over its 50% tariffs on Canadian goods, arguing that President Trump exceeded his authority by relying on a nearly century-old provision of U.S. trade law.

Atalanta Corporation, part of Gellert Global Group, filed the lawsuit in the U.S. Court of International Trade. The company imports Canadian food products, including specialty cheeses, and says the new duties have substantially increased its costs. The case challenges Trump's use of Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 to impose the tariffs.

Section 338 allows a president to impose additional duties of up to 50% against countries found to discriminate against U.S. commerce. The provision had never previously been used to impose tariffs. The administration turned to the law after earlier tariffs imposed under emergency powers faced legal challenges.

The lawsuit argues that Congress effectively superseded Section 338 when it enacted more modern trade laws, including Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, which established procedures for investigating and responding to unfair foreign trade practices. The importer also argues that the administration failed to follow requirements governing when tariffs imposed under Section 338 can take effect.

The company is asking the trade court to block collection of the Canadian tariffs, declare them unlawful and order refunds of duties already collected, with interest. The case adds another legal challenge to the administration's effort to use presidential authority to reshape U.S. trade policy.

The lawsuit does not immediately suspend the tariffs, which remain in effect while the court considers the challenge.
