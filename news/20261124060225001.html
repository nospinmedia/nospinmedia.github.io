Trump gets new path to potentially erase hush-money conviction

Federal appeals court revives bid to move historic case out of New York state court

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 24, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

President Trump has gained a new legal route to potentially erase his New York hush-money conviction after a federal appeals court revived his effort to move the case from state court into the federal court system.

A three-judge panel of the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals on Monday, November 23, reversed a lower-court ruling that blocked Trump's attempt to transfer the case. The judges ordered that the matter be returned to federal district court for prompt reconsideration.

The ruling does not overturn Trump's conviction, nor did the appeals court decide that presidential immunity applies. Instead, it gives Trump another opportunity to move the case into federal court, where his lawyers intend to argue that the verdict should be thrown out because prosecutors used evidence involving official presidential acts.

Trump was convicted in May 2024 on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records connected to efforts to conceal a $130,000 payment to Stormy Daniels before the 2016 election. At his January 2025 sentencing, Trump received an unconditional discharge, meaning he received no prison time, probation or fine while the felony convictions remained on his record.

Trump sought to move the prosecution to federal court after his 2023 indictment and tried again following his conviction and the U.S. Supreme Court's July 2024 presidential immunity ruling. His attorneys argue Manhattan prosecutors used evidence at trial that should have been protected, including testimony involving Oval Office conversations, White House workflow and social media activity.

U.S. District Judge Alvin Hellerstein rejected Trump's efforts, concluding in part that paying hush money or covering up an embarrassing episode was not protected by presidential immunity. Hellerstein died earlier in 2026 at age 92, so the 2nd Circuit directed that the case be reassigned to a different federal judge in Manhattan's Southern District of New York.

The appellate panel — Judges Raymond J. Lohier Jr., John M. Walker Jr. and José A. Cabranes — found that Hellerstein erred by treating removal of a state criminal case as categorically unavailable after conviction and by concluding Trump lacked good cause for his later removal attempt.

The decision also intersects with Trump's separate appeal in New York state court, where the Appellate Division heard arguments on November 5. Trump's attorneys notified that court of the federal ruling and sought a pause while the jurisdictional issue is resolved.

The next step is not dismissal. A newly assigned federal judge must first determine whether Trump's case can move into federal court. Only then could Trump pursue his presidential-immunity argument seeking to erase the conviction.
