Nevada Judge Dismisses 2020 Alternate Elector Case

Prosecutors Failed To Show Required Criminal Intent

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 14, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A Nevada judge dismissed criminal charges against six Republicans accused of submitting documents asserting that President Trump won the state's 2020 presidential election. Clark County District Court Judge Mary Kay Holthus ruled that prosecutors failed to establish sufficient evidence that the defendants acted with the specific intent required under Nevada's forgery statutes. The judge also found that prosecutors failed to present certain potentially exculpatory evidence to the grand jury before the indictment was issued.

The dismissed charges included felony forgery and offering a false instrument for filing or record. The defendants — Michael McDonald, Jesse Law, Jim DeGraffenreid, Jim Hindle III, Shawn Meehan, and Eileen Rice — argued they met publicly in Carson City on December 14, 2020, to preserve pending election litigation rather than to deceive government officials. Judge Holthus concluded the state had not established the criminal intent necessary for the case to proceed.

The ruling represents another setback for Nevada Attorney General Aaron Ford, whose office obtained the indictment in December 2023. Judge Holthus previously dismissed the case in 2024 after finding it had been filed in the wrong county, but the Nevada Supreme Court later reinstated the prosecution and returned it to district court for further proceedings. Following Thursday's ruling, Ford announced plans to appeal the latest dismissal to the Nevada Supreme Court.

Joe Biden carried Nevada in the 2020 election by 33,596 votes, receiving all six of the state's electoral votes. Nevada is one of several battleground states where prosecutors have pursued criminal cases involving alternate elector slates submitted after the 2020 election, with outcomes differing across jurisdictions and additional appeals still pending.
