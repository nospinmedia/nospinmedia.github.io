Florida confirms dengue death as outbreak grows

152 locally acquired cases make 2026 state's second-highest year

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 19, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Florida has confirmed its first dengue-related death of 2026 as the state experiences one of its largest outbreaks of the mosquito-borne virus in decades. The Florida Department of Health confirmed the death on Friday, September 18, but did not release details about the patient. The Tampa Bay Times previously reported that an elderly Tampa woman died on September 4.

Florida has recorded 152 locally acquired dengue infections so far this year, making 2026 the state's second-highest year for locally transmitted cases. Florida's largest recent annual total was nearly 200 cases in 2023. More than 130 cases this year have been reported in Hillsborough County, which normally records only a handful of locally acquired dengue infections.

Dengue is caused by a virus transmitted through infected mosquitoes. Many people who become infected develop no symptoms, while others experience fever, headache and flu-like illness. Severe dengue can cause serious bleeding, shock and death. Because some infected people never become sick or seek medical care, reported case totals can underestimate the actual number of infections.

The current outbreak is concentrated north of Tampa, where mosquito-control workers have been spraying during the day and night and checking properties for standing water where mosquitoes can reproduce. Recent mosquito sampling has not detected the virus, which local officials said could indicate that the risk of new infections is declining.

Most U.S. dengue cases historically have involved travelers infected abroad or locally acquired infections in Puerto Rico, but Florida has experienced recurring local transmission since an outbreak in Key West in 2009 and 2010. Experts say warmer conditions and the presence of mosquito species capable of carrying dengue can increase opportunities for local transmission.
