Earth tops 2°C warming for seven straight days

Global temperature streak marks a first as single-day record also falls

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 27, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Earth's average surface temperature exceeded pre-industrial levels by more than 2 degrees Celsius (3.6 degrees Fahrenheit) for seven consecutive days, the first time such a weeklong streak has been recorded, according to data from the European Union's Copernicus Climate Change Service.

Copernicus data showed daily global temperatures remained more than 2°C above the 1850–1900 pre-industrial baseline each day from November 18 through November 24. In total, 10 days during November 2026 reached or exceeded the 2°C mark, including November 3, 4 and 13.

Within the seven-day streak, November 23 registered the highest single-day global temperature anomaly on record at 2.25°C above pre-industrial levels. The previous daily record was 2.05°C, set in November 2023.

Scientists stressed that individual days above 2°C do not mean the world has breached the Paris Agreement's long-term temperature goals. Copernicus currently estimates long-term global warming at about 1.44°C above pre-industrial levels. A simple extrapolation of the recent warming trend would put the long-term 1.5°C threshold around 2029, although the timing remains uncertain and depends on future temperatures and emissions.

Scientists attributed the latest spike to the combination of long-term warming from greenhouse gas emissions and an exceptionally strong El Niño. Monthly average sea-surface temperatures in the tropical Pacific's Niño 3.4 region reached 29.94°C, making the current event the strongest by that measure since the 1870s.

Samantha Burgess, Strategic Lead for Climate at the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts, described the streak as a preview of where the climate is heading. She emphasized that El Niño is temporary, while underlying warming associated with greenhouse gas emissions will persist unless emissions decline.

Copernicus projects 2026 is very likely to finish as the second-warmest calendar year on record and the second full calendar year averaging more than 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.

The milestone follows the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, which ended November 22 without a main political agreement after governments remained divided over fossil-fuel language.
