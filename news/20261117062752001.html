NYC Disbands NYPD Unit Used For Protests And Counterterrorism

New structure emphasizes de-escalation while police union raises safety concerns

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 17, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch announced on Monday, November 16, that the department's Strategic Response Group will be disbanded, separating counterterrorism operations from the city's response to demonstrations. The roughly 500-officer unit is scheduled to be formally dissolved in spring 2027 as officers are reassigned and civilian personnel are hired and trained.

Created in 2015, the SRG combined counterterrorism responsibilities with rapid-response crowd management. It was deployed during the 2020 racial justice protests, demonstrations at Columbia University and anti-ICE protests. Civil rights organizations accused SRG officers of excessive force and mass arrests, and the 2023 Payne v. City of New York settlement established reforms governing NYPD treatment of protesters, journalists and legal observers.

Under the new structure, SRG counterterrorism functions will move to the NYPD's Intelligence and Counterterrorism Bureau. Protest response will shift to a new Patrol Support Division, supported by a 30-officer First Amendment Support Team and a civilian-led Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division whose personnel will have neither weapons nor arrest powers.

New rules prohibit kettling, tear gas and long guns during First Amendment activities. Pepper spray cannot be used as a general crowd-management tool, acoustic devices will be limited to 90 decibels, and officers may not use bicycles as weapons except to prevent death or physical injury. City officials said the restructuring will not change overall NYPD headcount or require additional funding.

New York Civil Liberties Union Executive Director Donna Lieberman called the dissolution "a victory for our First Amendment rights." Police Benevolent Association President Patrick Hendry opposed the move, citing a March 2026 incident near Gracie Mansion involving alleged ISIS supporters accused of attempting to detonate pipe bombs during a protest as an example of dangers officers can face.
