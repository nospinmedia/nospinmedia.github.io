Patrick Stewart, beloved Star Trek captain, dies at 86

Actor behind Jean-Luc Picard and Professor X leaves six-decade legacy

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 4:57 PM ET

Sir Patrick Stewart, the acclaimed British actor whose portrayal of Captain Jean-Luc Picard helped define a generation of Star Trek, has died at 86. His family confirmed the news on Thursday, October 29, 2026.

Stewart died Wednesday at his home in Los Angeles, following what his family described as a brief experience with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia. He was surrounded by loved ones, including his wife, Sunny Ozell.

Born on July 13, 1940, in Yorkshire, England, Stewart began his career in theater, developing a lifelong connection to Shakespeare. He joined the Royal Shakespeare Company in the 1960s and established himself as an accomplished stage performer before achieving international television fame.

In 1987, Stewart took command of the USS Enterprise as Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation. Across seven seasons, several feature films and the later series Star Trek: Picard, his portrayal emphasized diplomacy, intellectual curiosity, moral responsibility and leadership. The character became one of science fiction's most recognizable figures.

Stewart also introduced Professor Charles Xavier to movie audiences in the X-Men franchise, beginning in 2000. His portrayal of the mutant leader extended across multiple films, including Logan in 2017.

Despite his success in film and television, Stewart remained devoted to theater, performing Shakespeare and appearing in major productions alongside longtime friend Ian McKellen.

In 2010, Queen Elizabeth II knighted Stewart for his contributions to drama. His career spanned more than six decades and reached audiences across theater, television and cinema.

For generations of viewers, Stewart's performances brought depth and humanity to characters confronting difficult moral choices.
