Russian bombing kills 26 at Ukraine aid site

Rescuers and police among those killed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 3, 2026 at 2:31 PM ET

Russian forces bombed a school being used as a humanitarian aid distribution point and shelter in Komyshuvakha, a village in southeastern Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia region, killing 26 people, according to Ukrainian emergency services and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

The school was no longer holding classes because of its proximity to the front line and was being used to distribute humanitarian assistance each week. Zelenskyy said Russian forces initially dropped several aerial bombs on the school as people were arriving and then carried out a second strike on the shelter where residents had gathered. Ukrainian officials said two rescue workers and four police officers were among those killed.

Emergency crews continued searching the rubble for survivors. Five people were hospitalized, including two rescue workers, while Ukrainian authorities said 27 people remained missing. Rescue operations were complicated by additional Russian drone activity in the area, according to Ukrainian officials. The casualty figures have changed as search operations have progressed, with earlier reports placing the death toll below 26.

The attack occurred shortly after a separate six-day localized ceasefire expired in the neighboring Kherson region. That pause had allowed humanitarian aid deliveries and evacuations in Russian-occupied territory. Zelenskyy said the bombing demonstrated that Russia was not seeking de-escalation. Russia had not publicly provided its own account of the Komyshuvakha strikes at the time of the latest reporting.

U.S.-mediated talks between Ukraine and Russia are scheduled for November 10-11 in Abu Dhabi, with security arrangements, de-escalation and humanitarian issues expected to be discussed.
