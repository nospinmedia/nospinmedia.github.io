Judge permanently dismisses Trump's lawsuit against Wall Street Journal

Court says amended complaint failed to plausibly show actual malice

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 1, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

A federal judge has dismissed President Donald Trump's defamation lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal with prejudice, preventing him from amending or refiling the case in the same court. The lawsuit challenged the newspaper's reporting about a birthday letter it said bore Trump's name and was prepared for Jeffrey Epstein's 50th birthday in 2003.

U.S. District Judge Darrin P. Gayles issued the 20-page ruling in Miami on October 31, finding that Trump's amended complaint did not plausibly establish that the defendants acted with actual malice. That standard requires a public figure pursuing a defamation claim to show that a statement was published with knowledge that it was false or with reckless disregard for whether it was true.

The Journal reported in 2025 that a sexually suggestive letter bearing Trump's name appeared in an album assembled for Epstein. Trump has denied writing or signing it and called it fabricated. Gayles rejected the argument that publishing after Trump's denial helped establish actual malice, explaining that accepting that theory would effectively allow public figures to block unfavorable reporting simply by denying an allegation before publication.

The underlying album later became part of a congressional investigation. The House Oversight Committee obtained a redacted copy from Epstein's estate in September 2025, providing lawmakers access to the material at the center of the dispute.

Gayles had previously dismissed Trump's original complaint in April 2026 but allowed his attorneys to amend it. He concluded that the revised allegations still failed to meet the required legal standard and dismissed the case with prejudice. The renewed lawsuit sought $10 billion in damages and named the Journal, News Corp, reporters and Rupert Murdoch among the defendants.

Dow Jones, the Journal's publisher, welcomed the decision. Trump's legal team has indicated that the ruling could be appealed.
