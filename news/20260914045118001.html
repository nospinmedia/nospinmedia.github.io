‘Cat in the Hat’ threat trend leads to arrests

AI-generated posts target students and schools

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 14, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

A disturbing ‘Cat in the Hat’ social media trend has prompted arrests, criminal charges and warnings from schools and police departments across the United States after some posts moved beyond creepy imagery to name specific students, schools and communities. The trend commonly uses altered or AI-generated images and videos depicting a sinister version of the Dr. Seuss character lurking near homes, streets or schools, sometimes accompanied by threatening messages.

In Hutchinson, Kansas, police identified a 15-year-old Hutchinson High School student as the creator of a local TikTok account connected to the trend. The teenager was charged with two counts of criminal threat after investigators examined posts containing ominous threats directed toward students. Hutchinson police said that even when such posts are intended as jokes, causing people to fear for their safety can result in criminal charges.

Authorities have responded to similar incidents elsewhere. In Warren County, Tennessee, a high school student was charged with making threats of mass violence on school property, a Class E felony, following an investigation involving the Warren County Sheriff's Office, McMinnville Police Department and Homeland Security Investigations. Tennessee officials have also investigated posts containing the names of real children and, in some cases, images or information identifying their homes. School districts in multiple states have increased security or issued warnings as the trend has spread.

Officials emphasize that not every ‘Cat in the Hat’ post represents a credible plan for violence. The concern is that threatening posts can create fear, disrupt schools and require law enforcement resources to determine whether a genuine danger exists. Authorities have urged students and parents to report threatening material directly to schools or police rather than reposting it and potentially spreading the disruption further.
