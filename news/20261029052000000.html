Labor and civil rights leader Dolores Huerta dies at 96

United Farm Workers co-founder spent decades organizing for farmworkers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Dolores Huerta, the labor organizer and civil rights activist who co-founded what became the United Farm Workers with César Chávez, died on October 28 at age 96. Her foundation said Huerta died of natural causes at her home in Bakersfield, California, surrounded by family.

Born Dolores Fernández on April 10, 1930, in Dawson, New Mexico, Huerta became one of the most influential organizers in the American farmworker movement. She and Chávez founded the National Farm Workers Association in 1962. The organization later merged with another union and eventually became the United Farm Workers. Huerta played a central role in organizing strikes and nationwide consumer boycotts seeking higher wages and improved working conditions for agricultural laborers.

Huerta was also a key negotiator for farmworkers. During the movement that grew from the Delano grape strike, she helped negotiate contracts between growers and workers. She later helped campaign for California's Agricultural Labor Relations Act of 1975, which established collective-bargaining rights for farmworkers in the state.

Her influence extended beyond organized labor to women's rights, immigration issues and political participation. Huerta is also closely associated with the phrase “Sí, se puede,” meaning “Yes, it can be done” or “Yes, we can.” She developed the slogan while organizing in Arizona in 1972, and it became one of the best-known phrases associated with the farmworker movement.

Huerta received the Presidential Medal of Freedom from President Barack Obama in 2012. Even in her later years, she remained active through the Dolores Huerta Foundation, which she founded to support community organizing and civic participation.
