Democrats nominate Hakeem Jeffries for House speaker

251-11 vote puts him on track to make history in January

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 19, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries to become the next speaker of the U.S. House of Representatives, approving his nomination 251-11 in a closed-door vote on Wednesday, November 18. If elected when the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027, the New York Democrat would become the first Black speaker in U.S. history.

Jeffries ran unopposed but requested a formal secret-ballot tally rather than having the caucus approve his nomination by acclamation, allowing returning and incoming Democratic members to register their votes. He must still win the formal election on the House floor in January. Democrats are set to enter the new Congress with a large majority following their midterm gains.

The 11 votes against Jeffries reflected continuing disagreements within the Democratic caucus rather than an organized leadership challenge. Progressive lawmakers have sought commitments from leadership on policy and oversight priorities. No progressive candidate ultimately challenged Jeffries, with some lawmakers, including Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez of New York, pointing to the significance of the opportunity for Jeffries to become the first Black speaker while continuing to press leadership on policy issues.

The elections established the broader Democratic leadership team expected to run the House. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts was selected to become majority leader and would be the first woman to hold that position. Pete Aguilar of California was chosen as majority whip. Democrats also elected Ted Lieu of California as Democratic Caucus chair and Lauren Underwood of Illinois as caucus vice chair, while Joe Neguse of Colorado was elected assistant speaker.

Jeffries has led House Democrats since 2023, when he succeeded Nancy Pelosi as the party's top House leader and became the first Black lawmaker to lead a party in Congress.

Republicans held their leadership election earlier, selecting Jim Jordan of Ohio over current Majority Leader Steve Scalise of Louisiana in a 106-73 secret-ballot vote on November 10. Speaker Mike Johnson did not seek the minority leadership position.
