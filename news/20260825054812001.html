Ontario Premier Threatens U.S. Power and Mineral Cutoffs

Doug Ford says 'everything is on the table' in tariff fight

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 25, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

Ontario Premier Doug Ford said his province is prepared to consider cutting electricity exports and restricting shipments of critical minerals to the United States if trade tensions continue to escalate. Speaking on August 24, Ford said "everything is on the table" after the Trump administration imposed new 50% tariffs on Canadian goods and signaled that additional duties on products including automobiles and steel could follow. He urged Prime Minister Mark Carney and Canada's provinces to respond with a coordinated national strategy.

Ford said Ontario possesses significant economic leverage because it supplies electricity to several northern U.S. states and plays an important role in North America's critical mineral supply chain. Ontario exports power through transmission connections serving Michigan, New York and Minnesota, while the province is also a major producer and processor of minerals such as nickel and other materials used in manufacturing, defense and clean energy technologies. He argued those assets should remain available as potential negotiating tools if trade negotiations fail to ease tensions.

The comments come as Canada prepares retaliatory measures in response to the latest U.S. tariffs. Federal officials have announced plans for additional countermeasures, while provincial governments are evaluating actions within their own authority. Ford previously introduced, and later withdrew, a surcharge on electricity exports during an earlier round of trade tensions, but said stronger measures remain possible if the dispute worsens. No decision has been made to halt exports, and Ontario continues to supply electricity to U.S. customers.

Economists have cautioned that broad restrictions on electricity or critical mineral exports could increase costs for businesses and consumers on both sides of the border while disrupting integrated North American supply chains. Officials in both countries have indicated they remain open to negotiations even as each side prepares additional trade responses.
