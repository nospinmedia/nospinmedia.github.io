2,100-Year-Old Roman Shipwreck Found Off Sicily

Hundreds of wine amphorae mapped near Mazara del Vallo

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 11, 2026 at 5:54 AM ET

Italian authorities and divers have identified a 2,100-year-old Roman shipwreck about 3 miles off Mazara del Vallo on Sicily’s southwest coast, a site Italy’s culture minister Alessandro Giuli called “one of the most important underwater archaeological discoveries of recent years.” Officials say the cargo preserves insights into late Roman Republic trade along Mediterranean routes.

Initial documentation places the wreck at roughly 46 meters (151 feet) deep with an estimated hull size near 21 meters long and 6 meters wide. Divers reported roughly 500 amphorae on the seabed, most classified as Dressel 1A, vessels widely used to transport wine between the 2nd and 1st centuries BC. Authorities said the location was flagged after a tip from local fishers; divers — including Giacomo De Mola and Igor Bisulli — then recorded extensive video and notified Sicilian cultural officials. In a post on Aug. 8, 2026, the Ministry of Culture highlighted the find and praised the cultural heritage police for their role.

Key site facts: - Depth: 46 m (151 ft) - Distance from shore: ~3 miles off Mazara del Vallo - Estimated hull: ~21 m x 6 m - Cargo: about 500 amphorae, mostly Dressel 1A (wine transport)

Sicily’s Soprintendenza del Mare plans further non-invasive surveys and protective measures in the coming months. Investigators aim to refine the wreck’s dating, determine the amphorae’s origin and intended destination, and assess the preservation state to guide any future recovery. Officials emphasized that mapping and photogrammetry will precede any intervention to maintain the site’s integrity and deter looting.

Dressel 1A amphorae are valuable to researchers because their markings and distribution help reconstruct commercial networks linking Italy with other Mediterranean producers and markets during the late Republic. While early estimates point to a wine cargo, authorities have not released a full artifact inventory, and precise coordinates remain undisclosed to safeguard the site. As survey work proceeds, officials caution that specific counts and measurements could be updated as documentation advances.
