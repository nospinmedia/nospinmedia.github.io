Death toll reaches 67 after mass crossing into Spain's Ceuta

More than 48,000 migrants return to Morocco as border crisis eases

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 1, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

At least 67 people have died after tens of thousands of migrants crossed from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta, according to the Spanish government, as authorities reported that more than 48,000 people had since returned across the border. The deaths were attributed to drownings and a stampede during the unprecedented surge, which overwhelmed the small territory and prompted Spain to deploy soldiers alongside police.

Spain's Interior Ministry estimated that roughly 50,000 to 60,000 migrants entered Ceuta between July 30 and July 31, many by swimming around coastal barriers or crossing through overwhelmed border points. Most later returned voluntarily to Morocco after Spanish security forces prevented them from traveling onward to mainland Europe. Officials said the overnight situation into August 1 remained largely under control, although soldiers intercepted additional swimmers attempting to reach the enclave.

Emergency crews continued searching coastal waters and processing fatalities as the government began installing a 500-meter containment barrier extending from the Tarajal breakwater. Ceuta, one of the European Union's only land borders with Africa, has a permanent population of about 84,000, meaning the influx temporarily approached the size of the city's normal population and severely strained shelters, medical services and public facilities.

Spanish officials said cooperation with Morocco helped restore control, but the crisis renewed questions about conditions driving migration and how European governments handle people who enter irregularly. Reports indicated that social media rumors claiming the border was open contributed to the surge, while many migrants described unemployment, poverty and limited prospects in Morocco as motivations for attempting the crossing.

The latest confirmed death toll is higher than the 57 deaths reported in earlier accounts, reflecting continuing recovery and identification work. Authorities warned that the situation remained unstable despite the large number of returns and the sharp decline in new arrivals.
