U.S. cancels major Marine exercise with South Korea

Iran deployments force pause in key amphibious drills

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 24, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

The United States has canceled the biennial Ssangyong joint amphibious landing exercise scheduled for next month with South Korea, citing limited availability of U.S. forces because of ongoing military commitments related to the war in Iran. South Korea's Marine Corps said the U.S. Marine Corps formally notified its counterpart in June that personnel and amphibious assets needed for the large division-level exercise would not be available.

Ssangyong, meaning "Twin Dragons," is one of the allies' largest combined Marine exercises and typically involves 10,000 to 20,000 troops practicing amphibious assaults, logistics and combined operations. A South Korean Marine Corps spokesperson said both countries remain in close consultations about resuming the exercise in the future, while South Korea plans to proceed with its own division-level amphibious training later this year. U.S. Forces Korea did not immediately comment on the cancellation.

The decision follows President Trump's recent order to significantly reduce the annual Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises, which ended early after the administration cited costs and South Korea's decision not to participate in U.S. military operations related to Iran. Trump also argued the exercises sent an unnecessarily hostile signal to North Korea. Despite the reduction in allied training, Pyongyang last week launched 10 short-range ballistic missiles into waters east of the Korean Peninsula and again condemned the joint drills as rehearsals for invasion.

Security analysts have long viewed large-scale combined exercises as a central element of deterrence and interoperability between U.S. and South Korean forces. The cancellation highlights how expanding U.S. military commitments in the Middle East are affecting force availability in the Indo-Pacific, raising questions about balancing global operational demands while maintaining readiness in regions facing persistent security threats.
