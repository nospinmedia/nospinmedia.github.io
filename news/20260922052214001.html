Two killed in helicopter crash while fighting Yosemite wildfire

Investigators will reach wreckage when fire conditions allow

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 22, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

Two members of a helicopter crew were killed when their aircraft crashed while helping fight the Dome Fire in Yosemite National Park, according to the U.S. Forest Service. The helicopter went down Sunday afternoon while conducting wildfire suppression operations as hundreds of firefighters continued battling the blaze.

The Eurocopter AS32 crashed shortly before 3 p.m. Pacific Time on Sunday, September 20, according to the Federal Aviation Administration. The two crew members were the only people aboard. The aircraft was operated by Precision, LLC, also known as Flyprecision, and its crew had been contracted to the Forest Service's Price Valley Rappel Crew in Idaho. Pilot Greg King was identified Monday as one of those killed.

The FAA, National Transportation Safety Board and Forest Service are investigating the crash. The NTSB said its investigators had not yet reached the wreckage and would travel to the site when wildfire conditions allowed. The agency expects to issue a preliminary report within 30 days. No cause for the crash has been announced.

The accident occurred during active suppression of the Dome Fire, which had grown to about 3,222 acres and was 15% contained Monday morning. More than 500 firefighters were working the fire, which has been burning through remote terrain in and around areas previously affected by the 2017 Empire Fire. Dead standing trees and difficult access have added hazards for crews, while aircraft have played an important role in the response.

The deaths underscore the risks faced by aviation crews supporting wildfire operations, where pilots may work close to rugged terrain, smoke and active fire. Investigators will now examine the aircraft and circumstances surrounding the crash to determine what happened.
