Israel marks three years since Oct. 7 attack

Memorials and protests renew demands for accountability

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 7, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Israelis marked three years since the October 7, 2023 Hamas-led attack with memorial ceremonies, vigils and protests, as survivors and families of victims renewed demands for accountability over the security failures that preceded the deadliest day in Israel's history.

The attack killed about 1,200 people and resulted in 251 people being taken hostage in Gaza. Commemorations on Wednesday began at 6:29 a.m., the time the assault started three years earlier. Survivors and bereaved families gathered at Kibbutz Re'im, near the site of the Nova music festival, where hundreds were killed and dozens abducted. Memorial events were scheduled at hundreds of locations across Israel, while the October Council, representing survivors and bereaved families, organized a nationwide moment of remembrance.

This year's anniversary is the first with no Israeli hostages remaining in Gaza. The attack triggered a war that has devastated the territory. Gaza health authorities say more than 74,000 Palestinians have been killed since the war began. A U.S.-brokered ceasefire reached in October 2025 has not produced a lasting resolution, and major disputes remain over Hamas's disarmament, Israeli military control and reconstruction of Gaza.

The anniversary also highlighted continuing political divisions inside Israel. Protesters and families have demanded an independent state commission of inquiry into the failures surrounding the October 7 attack. Demonstrations and memorial protests were planned in Tel Aviv, Caesarea and Jerusalem, including a vehicle convoy organizers called the 'Route of Abandonment.' A large grassroots memorial organized by the Kumu movement was scheduled for Yarkon Park in Tel Aviv.

The commemorations come weeks before Israel's national election, with the October 7 attack and questions of responsibility remaining central political issues. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has faced sustained demands for an independent investigation while arguing that responsibility for the failures should be examined after the conflict.
