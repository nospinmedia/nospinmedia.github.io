Texas Capitol attack suspect taken into federal custody

Authorities say alleged mass shooting was planned for Oct. 1

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 1, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A 40-year-old Texas man accused of planning a violent attack on the Texas State Capitol is in federal custody after state and federal authorities moved to stop the alleged plot before its planned October 1 date. The Texas Department of Public Safety identified the suspect as Benny Caldera Jr. of Converse, near San Antonio.

DPS said it received credible information Tuesday evening about a planned attack and worked overnight with the FBI and local law enforcement. A DPS SWAT team arrested Caldera at his Converse home at about 3 a.m. Wednesday. He was initially booked into the Bexar County Jail and charged with the felony offense of making a terroristic threat against a public servant. Court records listed a $75,000 bond, but DPS later said Caldera was taken into federal custody by the FBI to face additional federal charges.

An arrest warrant affidavit obtained by NBC's Dallas-Fort Worth station says a friend told investigators that Caldera had threatened to go to the Capitol and "shoot it up." ABC News separately reported, citing an official briefed on the investigation, that Caldera allegedly had been collecting guns and ammunition. The same official said investigators were seeking access to electronic devices as they examined whether anyone else may have been involved. Authorities have not publicly established a motive, and the investigation remains active.

A notice sent to Texas senators described Caldera as the main suspect and referenced suspected co-conspirators. However, DPS has not announced additional arrests, and reporting from the Texas Tribune noted that the agency's public statement did not identify other suspects. The Capitol remained open for normal operations, with an increased DPS presence around the building.

DPS said there was no reason to believe an ongoing threat remained to legislators, Capitol employees or the public following the arrest. Gov. Greg Abbott credited DPS and the FBI for their response.
