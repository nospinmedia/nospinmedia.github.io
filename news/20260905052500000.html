Veterans seek emergency halt to Trump's 250-foot D.C. arch

Judge reinforces notice requirement as excavation plans advance

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 5, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

Three military veterans and an architectural historian asked a federal judge Friday to stop the Trump administration from beginning construction of a planned 250-foot triumphal arch near Arlington National Cemetery, after Interior Secretary Doug Burgum announced excavation work would begin within two weeks. The challengers argue the administration lacks congressional authorization for the project and asked U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan for a temporary restraining order.

The proposed Great Triumphal Arch and Military Observation Deck would rise at Memorial Circle, between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery. The administration says the monument would commemorate the nation's 250th anniversary and has argued that congressional legislation dating to 1925, connected to development of the Arlington Memorial Bridge area, provides sufficient authority. The plaintiffs dispute that interpretation and contend Congress must specifically authorize the arch before construction proceeds.

Chutkan reinforced an existing requirement that the administration provide advance notice before beginning work on the monument. The government said Friday that it would provide at least 48 hours' notice before activity beyond archaeological assessments begins. Interior officials have characterized the initially planned excavation as testing for underground artifacts rather than construction of the arch itself.

The project has received preliminary federal reviews but has not obtained final approval from the National Capital Planning Commission. A National Park Service report released in August supported the project while concluding that the structure would have adverse effects on historic views and the setting of numerous Washington landmarks. Among the affected vistas is the alignment connecting the Lincoln Memorial, Arlington Memorial Bridge and Arlington House.

The administration has asked Chutkan to dismiss the lawsuit, arguing in part that the plaintiffs lack legal standing. The challengers say allowing work to begin before the court resolves their claims could make the project increasingly difficult to stop if they ultimately prevail.
