Norte Fire Burns Homes as Thousands Flee Southern California

Orange County Wildfire Grows to 570 Acres With No Containment

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 17, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

A fast-moving wildfire in Orange County, California, burned homes and forced thousands of residents to evacuate as strong Santa Ana winds pushed flames through communities in San Clemente and San Juan Capistrano.

The Norte Fire began late Friday morning, October 16, near Portico Del Norte and Eminencia Del Norte in San Clemente. By Friday night, Cal Fire reported the blaze had grown to 570 acres and remained 0% contained. The cause remained under investigation.

At least six homes were damaged or destroyed as the fire moved into residential areas. Approximately 16,000 people were ordered to evacuate, according to reporting from the Associated Press. Evacuation orders covered several zones in San Clemente and San Juan Capistrano, and emergency shelters were opened for displaced residents.

Two firefighters were hospitalized with heat-related injuries, according to the Associated Press. No civilian deaths were reported as of early Saturday. Schools in affected areas dismissed students early or closed as crews worked to protect neighborhoods.

The fire is one of several burning across Southern California during a period of hot, dry and windy weather. Santa Ana winds with powerful gusts helped fires spread rapidly, while utilities shut off electricity to customers in some areas as a precaution against additional fires being sparked by electrical equipment.

Governor Gavin Newsom announced that California secured a Fire Management Assistance Grant from FEMA for the Norte Fire. The federal program can reimburse eligible firefighting costs as state and local crews continue suppression efforts.

Fire conditions remain dangerous, and acreage, containment and evacuation figures may change as crews provide new assessments.
