FBI investigates massive driver's license data breach

153 million U.S. and Canadian IDs reportedly exposed

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) has opened an investigation into reports that digital scans of more than 153 million driver's licenses from the United States and Canada are being offered for sale on a dark web marketplace. The records were first detailed by cybersecurity journalist Brian Krebs, who reported that the data appeared on a newly launched cybercrime service known as Nexus, raising concerns about one of the largest known exposures of government-issued identification documents in North America.

According to the reporting, the leaked database contains front-and-back scans of driver's licenses, including security imaging used for identity verification. The records are believed to have originated from IDScan.net, a Louisiana-based identity verification company whose technology is used by businesses in industries including retail, hospitality and transportation to verify customer identification. Reuters reported that the FBI confirmed it is investigating the incident but declined to provide additional details about the scope or source of the breach.

Krebs reported that the marketplace also advertised millions of additional identification records, including state identification cards and passports, and released sample records belonging to public officials to demonstrate the authenticity of the database. Those claims have not been independently verified by the FBI. IDScan.net said it is investigating the allegations and has stated there is no evidence at this time that its production systems were compromised, while continuing to work with law enforcement and cybersecurity specialists.

Security experts warn that breaches involving government-issued identification documents present unique long-term risks because, unlike passwords, driver's licenses and other identity documents cannot simply be changed after exposure. Criminals can potentially use stolen identification records in identity theft schemes, financial fraud, or attempts to bypass identity verification systems. Federal investigators are continuing to determine how the records were obtained, whether additional organizations were affected, and the full number of individuals whose information may have been exposed.
