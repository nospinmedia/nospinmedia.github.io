Trump urges Ukraine to stop strikes on Russian refineries

President links attacks to global diesel shortage

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 14, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

President Trump urged Ukraine to stop attacking Russian oil refineries and other diesel infrastructure, arguing that the strikes are contributing to a global fuel shortage that has sent diesel prices sharply higher. Speaking to reporters in Doonbeg, Ireland, on September 13, 2026, Trump said he had told Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy that Kyiv should choose other targets rather than facilities producing diesel fuel.

Ukraine has repeatedly attacked Russia's refining industry with long-range drones as part of an effort to disrupt fuel supplies and revenue supporting Moscow's war. On September 13, Ukrainian forces struck the Slavyansky oil refinery in Russia's Krasnodar region and the TANECO refinery in Tatarstan, according to Ukraine's General Staff. TANECO processes more than 16 million metric tons of oil annually, while the Slavyansky refinery has annual processing capacity of about 5.2 million metric tons. Fires were reported at both facilities.

Trump said the attacks were "hurting the world" by reducing available diesel supplies. His request comes as fuel markets are also being squeezed by major disruptions in the Middle East, including reduced shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. That makes the extent to which Ukrainian strikes alone are responsible for higher U.S. diesel prices difficult to isolate. Ukraine, meanwhile, says Russian refineries are legitimate targets because they provide fuel for Russian military operations.

The request places additional U.S. pressure on Kyiv over how it conducts the war. Zelenskyy said on September 11 that Ukraine remains interested in an energy truce protecting infrastructure on both sides, but also said Ukrainian forces would continue responding to Russian attacks on Ukrainian energy facilities. Russia rejected a U.S.-backed proposal for a mutual pause in aerial attacks earlier in the week.
