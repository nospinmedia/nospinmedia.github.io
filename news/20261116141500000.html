Army whistleblower says warnings were ignored before deadly Iran strike

Six U.S. troops died at Kuwait post allegedly lacking dedicated anti-drone protection

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 16, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

An Army major who survived an Iranian drone strike that killed six U.S. service members has filed a whistleblower complaint with Congress, alleging military leaders ignored warnings about inadequate defenses at the American command post in Kuwait before the attack.

Maj. Stephen Ramsbottom submitted the complaint to the House and Senate Armed Services Committees on November 13. The filing concerns the March 1 attack on Port Shuaiba, which killed six Army Reserve soldiers from the 103rd Sustainment Command, the first U.S. service members killed in the war.

Ramsbottom said the command post was essentially a "trailer park" surrounded by concrete barriers designed to protect against car bombs but lacking warning sirens and dedicated overhead defenses against drones. The complaint also alleges the facility had no physician, no fixed aid station and only one ambulance.

According to Ramsbottom, soldiers raised concerns about aerial protection before the attack. He said that when he discussed the issue with two generals, one superior responded, "We don't have to worry about protection." Ramsbottom said an anti-drone system was sought during the week before the strike but never arrived.

The account contrasts with statements made after the attack by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, who said U.S. forces had strong air defenses and described the tactical operations center that was struck as fortified.

The Pentagon opened an internal investigation on March 3. That investigation concluded in June, with families privately briefed in July, but its report has not been publicly released. Ramsbottom's attorneys are separately seeking an independent investigation.

Sen. Richard Blumenthal sent Hegseth a letter on November 15 requesting a classified briefing and all force-protection assessments for Port Shuaiba. The request comes as Democrats prepare broader investigations into the administration's handling of the Iran war after taking control of Congress in January.

Ramsbottom has characterized the attack as a tactical failure involving planning throughout the chain of command, including himself. His complaint does not establish wrongdoing, but it adds new congressional scrutiny to whether known vulnerabilities were addressed before the deadly strike.
