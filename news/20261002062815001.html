ICE bans high-speed chases and tightens rules for vehicle stops

New guidance requires trained officers, body cameras and marked enforcement vehicles

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 2, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers have been ordered not to engage in high-speed vehicle pursuits and can conduct traffic stops only after completing specific training, under new internal guidance issued after several encounters involving ICE officers and drivers ended in shootings.

A memo sent to officers in ICE's Enforcement and Removal Operations division says agents are not authorized to chase vehicles that refuse to stop. Instead, officers are instructed to record identifying information about the vehicle for follow-up investigation. The memo was signed by ICE Executive Associate Director Marcos D. Charles and issued this week.

The guidance also requires vehicles used to initiate stops to have functioning lights and sirens, and officers at the scene must wear and activate body cameras. Only officers who have completed one of five specified ICE training courses may conduct vehicle stops. Officers without that training can participate only in a supporting role and cannot drive vehicles involved in the stop. Specially trained officers can still use tactics such as pinning vehicles or deploying tire-deflating spike strips.

The changes follow a series of confrontations during ICE vehicle stops. ICE officers have fired weapons during more than a dozen vehicle encounters since last year, killing at least four people and wounding several others. After fatal shootings involving drivers in Maine and Texas in July, officers were briefly directed to pause most vehicle stops while additional training was prepared. President Trump subsequently directed ICE to continue conducting the stops.

The new policy does not end ICE traffic stops or immigration arrests. ICE said vehicle stops and arrests will continue while describing the memo as guidance intended to improve safety during enforcement operations.

The rules were issued as a federal court in Austin, Texas, examined a September vehicle stop in which ICE officers pursued and shot delivery driver Wilber Rafael Garces Perez. Testimony indicated that several aspects of that encounter would not have complied with the newly issued requirements.
