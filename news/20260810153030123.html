NBA Hall Of Famer Don Nelson Dies At 86

Second-winningest coach; family says he died Sunday morning

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 10, 2026 at 11:59 AM ET

Don Nelson, the NBA’s second-winningest head coach and a five-time Boston Celtics champion as a player, died on August 9, 2026, at 86. His family said he passed away peacefully on Sunday morning surrounded by loved ones and did not disclose a cause or location. Tributes from across the league credited his inventive mind and decades of influence on modern basketball.

Across 31 seasons, Nelson compiled 1,335 regular-season wins and earned three NBA Coach of the Year awards. He is one of only two coaches to record at least 250 victories with three franchises — the Milwaukee Bucks, Golden State Warriors, and Dallas Mavericks. Known for an up-tempo “Nellie Ball” approach and for popularizing the “point forward,” he reached the playoffs 18 times and guided the Warriors’ “We Believe” team that upset top-seeded Dallas in 2007. During his final season on the bench in 2009–10, he mentored rookie Stephen Curry. Nelson was inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in 2012 and received the Chuck Daly Lifetime Achievement Award in 2025.

Nelson’s front-office and developmental fingerprints also run deep. In Dallas, he and longtime assistant-turned-executive Donnie Nelson persuaded Dirk Nowitzki to join the Mavericks after draft-day maneuvering in 1998, and he helped shape Steve Nash into an elite point guard before Nash’s later MVP seasons. League leaders and former players praised Nelson as a fearless innovator whose willingness to experiment — smaller lineups, interchangeable roles, and pace-and-space concepts — helped shape today’s offenses. Officials have not announced memorial plans as of August 10, 2026.

Before coaching, the 6-foot-6 forward enjoyed a 14-year playing career, winning five titles with the Celtics and becoming a fan favorite for his reliability and clutch shooting. His No. 19 was retired by Boston in 1978. Nelson retired from coaching in 2010 as the NBA’s all-time wins leader; he now ranks second after Gregg Popovich surpassed him in 2022.
