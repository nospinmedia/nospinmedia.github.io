Paramount clears major hurdle to Warner Bros. takeover

Settlement requires more films, worker funding and news safeguards

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 22, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

Paramount Skydance has settled lawsuits brought by 12 states and the Writers Guild of America, removing a major legal obstacle to its takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery and bringing together some of the biggest names in movies, television, streaming and news.

The state settlement, which still requires court approval, imposes enforceable conditions for five years. Paramount agreed to release 30 films annually during the first two years and 32 annually during the following three years, including at least four independent films each year. The company also committed at least an additional $1.5 billion to U.S. film production over five years.

The agreement includes a $47.5 million Workforce Fund for training and career development for workers displaced by the merger. Paramount must honor existing collective bargaining agreements and negotiate in good faith with unions. The Writers Guild separately agreed to end its challenge after Paramount committed $17.5 million to the union's health fund and agreed not to lay off writers at CBS News Broadcast for five years.

The settlement also addresses competition and news operations. Paramount and Warner Bros. basic cable channels must be negotiated separately for five years, and the merged company must continue offering a free streaming service such as Pluto TV at its current level of service and quality. An independent News Editorial Independence Board will be established to help protect editorial independence at CNN and CBS.

The states had sued in July, arguing the combination could reduce competition, raise prices and hurt entertainment workers and consumers. The coalition includes California, Arizona, Colorado, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Oregon and Washington. An independent monitor will oversee compliance with the settlement.
