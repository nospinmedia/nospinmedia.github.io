Western Europe Sets Hottest June–July On Record

July extends record heat as seas and temperatures reach new highs

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 11, 2026 at 8:46 PM ET

Western Europe recorded its hottest combined June–July period since modern records began, according to the Copernicus Climate Change Service, as prolonged heat waves, drought, and wildfires continued across much of the region. Copernicus said the average temperature for Western Europe reached 21.62°C, surpassing the previous June–July record set in 2022. Scientists said persistent high-pressure systems, exceptionally dry soils, and long-term climate warming combined to produce the prolonged heat.

The record-setting summer has also produced new national temperature extremes. On August 5, 2026, Slovakia reached 41.4°C in Kamenica nad Hronom, while neighboring Austria recorded 41.2°C in Bad Deutsch-Altenburg, setting new national highs. Emergency officials across parts of Western and Central Europe have continued issuing heat advisories as elevated temperatures increase health risks and fuel wildfire activity.

The oceans are also retaining record amounts of heat. Copernicus reported that the average sea surface temperature outside the polar regions reached 20.96°C during July 2026, the highest ever recorded for that month and above the previous July record set in 2023. Globally, the average surface air temperature measured 16.90°C, tying July 2024 as the second-warmest July on record. Scientists said unusually warm oceans provide additional energy that can reinforce heat waves and other extreme weather patterns.

The new findings build on Copernicus's report issued in July, which found June 2026 was Western Europe's hottest June on record. The latest data show the exceptional warmth continued through July, establishing a new two-month regional record rather than simply repeating June's milestone. Scientists say long-term climate warming remains the primary driver of the trend, while natural climate variability can influence how individual heat events develop.
