Fire at South Carolina nuclear site triggers emergency response

Officials report no contamination detected outside laboratory

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 29, 2026 at 11:45 AM ET

A fire at the Savannah River Site in South Carolina triggered an emergency response on October 28, but officials said monitoring found no contamination outside the affected laboratory and no danger to the surrounding public.

The fire occurred at the Savannah River National Laboratory, part of the sprawling federal nuclear complex near Aiken. Emergency crews responded to the laboratory after the fire was reported, and officials said the incident was brought under control. The facility is operated for the U.S. Department of Energy and conducts research related to nuclear materials, environmental cleanup and national security.

One worker was injured during the incident and received medical attention, according to officials. Authorities did not report additional injuries. Emergency personnel conducted monitoring following the fire to determine whether radioactive or other hazardous material had escaped from the affected area.

Officials said testing detected no contamination outside the laboratory, and there was no indication that the incident posed a threat beyond the Savannah River Site. The emergency response nevertheless reflected the additional precautions required when a fire or other incident occurs at a facility that handles nuclear and hazardous materials.

The Savannah River Site was established during the Cold War to produce nuclear materials for the nation's weapons program. Today, the approximately 310-square-mile federal complex supports nuclear materials management, environmental cleanup, research and other Department of Energy missions.

Officials were investigating the cause of the fire and assessing the affected laboratory. The available reporting did not establish that radioactive material was released from the building, and authorities continued monitoring the site following the incident.
