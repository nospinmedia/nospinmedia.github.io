Paraguay lawmakers reject president's resignation

Peña faces impeachment trial as bank crisis deepens

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 6, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Paraguayan President Santiago Peña remains in office after lawmakers rejected his attempt to resign and instead moved forward with impeachment proceedings over the collapse of Ueno Bank, a digital lender that served millions of Paraguayans and held hundreds of millions of dollars in public funds.

Peña submitted his resignation on Thursday, November 5, as lawmakers prepared to consider impeachment. Minutes after his announcement, the Chamber of Deputies voted 78-0 to approve the impeachment case and send it to the Senate. Both chambers subsequently declined to accept Peña's resignation, leaving him president while the impeachment process continues.

Lawmakers opposing acceptance of the resignation said removing Peña through impeachment could affect his eligibility for a constitutionally granted lifetime Senate seat and associated legal protections. Paraguay's attorney general has ordered three criminal investigations examining government oversight of Ueno, the handling of its collapse and the placement of public money in the bank. Peña has denied wrongdoing and said he will cooperate with investigators.

Ueno claimed about 3 million customers and managed roughly $850 million in public funds. Paraguay's central bank intervened after finding serious solvency problems and alleged irregularities involving loans to companies connected to the bank's controlling shareholder. Customers have been unable to access accounts, triggering protests and a government effort to return protected deposits.

Prosecutors have charged 27 bank officials and associates, while several former executives have been ordered held in custody as investigations continue. Authorities say the problems are concentrated at Ueno and do not indicate broader weakness across Paraguay's banking system.

Peña previously owned a stake in Grupo Vázquez, which controlled Ueno, but says he sold that investment in March 2025 and had no direct ownership in the bank. His presidential term was scheduled to continue until 2028.
