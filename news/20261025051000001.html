Trump says U.S. could strike Iran as soon as Sunday

President also leaves door open to a deal with Tehran

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 25, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

President Trump raised the possibility of renewed U.S. military action against Iran as soon as Sunday while also saying Washington could instead reach an agreement with Tehran, a notable shift from his statement earlier this month that the United States would not attack Iran before the November 3 midterm elections.

Speaking to reporters Saturday after voting early in Florida, Trump was asked about possible further military action against Iran. He declined to disclose his plans and suggested action could come as soon as the following day. Trump also said he might have to "finish it off" or that Iran could make a deal, leaving both military action and diplomacy publicly on the table. He did not announce that an attack had been ordered or provide a timetable for military action.

The comments come as the U.S. military maintains preparations for possible renewed operations. U.S. Central Command has developed strike options that could be used if authorized by the White House, according to reporting by The Hill. A Pentagon official said the Defense Department's role is to develop and present military options to the president and declined to speculate about potential future operations.

The possibility of near-term action represents a change from Trump's public position on October 8, when he said the United States would not attack Iran before the midterm elections. At the same time, diplomatic efforts have continued. Iran's ambassador to Pakistan said Saturday that Tehran remains willing to resume talks under a Pakistan-brokered framework and has proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz if Washington ends its naval blockade. The two governments remain divided over the conditions for a broader agreement.

Tensions remain high around the strait and elsewhere in the region, but as of Sunday morning the White House had announced neither a new military strike nor a diplomatic agreement.
