U.S. Takes Majority Stake in Venezuela Oil Venture

Agreement covers fields holding 65B barrels

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 29, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

President Trump announced an agreement with Venezuela giving the U.S. government a 55% interest in a new oil venture covering 17 Venezuelan fields containing an estimated 65 billion barrels of proven crude reserves. A U.S. official said Venezuela's interim President Delcy Rodríguez granted the private joint venture a 100-year concession, with the American stake combining equity ownership and the ability to obtain oil produced by the venture at cost.

The agreement was negotiated by senior U.S. officials including Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth following the removal of Nicolás Maduro from power in January 2026. The private company that will operate alongside the U.S. government has not been publicly identified. A U.S. official described the venture as the world's second-largest corporate holder of proven oil reserves behind Saudi Aramco, while Venezuela as a country holds more than 300 billion barrels of proven reserves, the largest national total in the world according to U.S. government data.

Rubio said the arrangement is expected to attract nearly $100 billion in private investment and support thousands of jobs. Rodríguez separately estimated that the project could ultimately generate more than $209 billion in tax revenue for Venezuela. Those figures are projections rather than guaranteed returns, and significantly increasing Venezuelan production could take years because the country's petroleum industry has suffered from aging infrastructure, years of underinvestment and political instability.

The agreement also comes as Washington confronts tight global energy supplies and depleted emergency reserves. U.S. crude held in the Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to 289.7 million barrels during the week ending August 21, its lowest level since 1982, amid disruptions linked to the Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz. The Venezuela agreement could provide the United States with a large long-term source of crude, but analysts have raised questions about its legal durability under Venezuelan law and whether enough private investment will materialize to develop the fields as planned.
