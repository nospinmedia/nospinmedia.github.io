FTC and 22 states sue Amazon over advertising auctions

Regulators allege hidden pricing scheme cost sellers more than $20 billion

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 1, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and attorneys general from 22 states filed a federal lawsuit accusing Amazon.com Inc. of secretly manipulating its online advertising auctions, alleging the company extracted more than $20 billion in improper charges from businesses that relied on sponsored search listings. The 181-page complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington, contends that Amazon misrepresented how its advertising marketplace determined winning bids while collecting substantially higher payments from advertisers than promised.

According to the complaint, Amazon told advertisers its platform operated as a traditional second-price auction, where the winning bidder pays only slightly more than the second-highest bid. Regulators allege the company instead introduced undisclosed internal pricing mechanisms known as "soft reserve prices", causing many advertisers to pay significantly more than expected. The FTC alleges that by 2024, winning advertisers were charged their maximum bid about 80% of the time, affecting more than 1.2 million advertising accounts, including over 500,000 small and medium-sized businesses.

FTC Chairman Andrew N. Ferguson said the alleged practices increased advertising costs for merchants, with many businesses ultimately passing those expenses on to consumers through higher retail prices. The lawsuit alleges violations of Section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act as well as multiple state consumer protection laws. The coalition is seeking civil penalties, restitution for affected advertisers, and court-ordered changes to Amazon's advertising business.

Amazon denied the allegations, calling the lawsuit "misguided" and arguing that its advertising systems are designed to improve relevance for shoppers rather than maximize charges to sellers. The company also said average winning bids declined substantially between 2019 and 2025, disputing regulators' claims that advertisers were systematically overcharged. The case is expected to become one of the most significant legal challenges yet to Amazon's rapidly growing advertising business.
