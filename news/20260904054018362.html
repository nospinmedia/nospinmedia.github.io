Missouri Supreme Court blocks GOP congressional map

November election returns to 2022 districts as voters get final say

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 4, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The Missouri Supreme Court unanimously blocked the state's new Republican-drawn congressional map on September 3, ordering officials to use Missouri's 2022 congressional districts for the November 3 midterm election and allowing voters to decide the fate of the replacement map through a statewide referendum. The ruling preserves, for now, a congressional configuration that currently sends six Republicans and two Democrats to the U.S. House.

The dispute centers on House Bill 1, enacted during a special legislative session in September 2025 after President Trump encouraged Republican-led states to redraw congressional districts before the 2026 midterms. Missouri's new map was designed with the potential to produce a 7-1 Republican advantage, largely by substantially reshaping the Kansas City-based 5th Congressional District represented by Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver. Opponents organized a referendum campaign through People Not Politicians and submitted more than 305,000 signatures seeking a statewide vote on the law.

Secretary of State Denny Hoskins rejected the referendum petition in August, arguing that Missouri's constitutional referendum power did not extend to congressional redistricting. The state Supreme Court disagreed. Writing for the unanimous court, Judge Ginger Gooch concluded that the referendum petition was legally sufficient and timely and ordered Hoskins to certify it for the November ballot. The court also prohibited implementation of the 2025 map unless voters ultimately approve it.

The ruling creates an unusual election situation. Missouri conducted its August 2026 congressional primaries using the 2025 districts, but under the Supreme Court's order, the November general election will use the 2022 boundaries. Republican state officials argue that changing district lines between the primary and general election creates constitutional and administrative problems. Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway said the state will seek emergency relief from the U.S. Supreme Court, meaning the map could still change if the justices intervene before November.

The decision also has national implications because control of the U.S. House could hinge on a small number of seats. Missouri's attempted redraw was part of a broader mid-decade redistricting battle in which parties have sought additional congressional advantages before the midterms.
