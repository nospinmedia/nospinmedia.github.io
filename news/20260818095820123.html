Judge Blocks Trump Move to Put FBI HQ in D.C.’s Reagan Building

Ruling preserves 2023 Greenbelt selection and halts fund reprogramming

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 18, 2026 at 2:54 PM ET

A federal judge in Maryland on August 17, 2026 blocked President Trump’s administration from relocating the FBI headquarters to the Ronald Reagan Building and International Trade Center in Washington, D.C., finding the government’s reversal of a prior relocation plan was unlawful. U.S. District Judge Theodore D. Chuang issued a 47-page opinion that stops officials from moving forward with the Reagan Building plan and from redirecting congressionally appropriated funds tied to that effort.

The ruling sides with Maryland and Prince George’s County, which sued after the General Services Administration (GSA), under new leadership, pivoted in July 2025 from a previously approved suburban site. During the Biden administration, the GSA had selected Greenbelt, Maryland, in November 2023, following 2022 congressional direction to choose among three suburban locations. Judge Chuang wrote that the administration’s shift was “arbitrary and capricious and not in accordance with law,” and the injunction prevents renovating the Reagan complex or repurposing existing funds for that move.

An FBI spokesperson criticized the decision, while Maryland Attorney General Anthony G. Brown welcomed the ruling, saying it restores the project’s path back to Greenbelt. The decision pauses the administration’s effort—championed by FBI Director Kash Patel—to keep headquarters in downtown Washington while litigation continues over the relocation dispute. The ruling leaves the previously approved Greenbelt project in place unless a higher court or Congress changes the outcome.
