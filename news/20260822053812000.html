U.S. Sends 161 Deportees to Haiti After TPS Ends

Flight Lands in Cap-Haïtien as Gang Violence Limits Air Travel

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 22, 2026 at 8:45 PM ET

The United States deported 161 people to Haiti on August 20, marking the first deportation flight to the country since the Trump administration ended Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for roughly 350,000 Haitians. The Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) charter departed from Alexandria, Louisiana, and landed at Cap-Haïtien International Airport in northern Haiti rather than the country's primary airport in Port-au-Prince, where severe gang violence continues to disrupt transportation and security.

The deportees included people who had held TPS as well as Haitians who had completed prison sentences in the United States, according to Jean Négot Bonheur Delva, director general of Haiti's National Office of Migration. Other reporting indicated the group also included people in different stages of the U.S. immigration system. Some passengers had been born outside Haiti but were considered Haitian nationals through their parents. Upon arrival, Haitian authorities released the returnees and provided food and limited financial assistance, including about $76 to help some travel toward their home communities.

The flight comes after federal courts cleared the administration to terminate the humanitarian protection that had allowed hundreds of thousands of Haitians to legally live and work in the United States. TPS was created under the Immigration Act of 1990 to temporarily protect nationals of countries facing armed conflict, natural disasters or other extraordinary conditions that make safe return difficult. Haiti remains under a Level 4: Do Not Travel advisory from the U.S. State Department, and U.S. commercial flights to Port-au-Prince remain restricted because of the security situation.

Haitian and immigration advocates have raised concerns about returning people to a country where armed groups control large portions of the capital and surrounding transportation routes. Haitian migration officials have also been told to prepare for an increase in U.S. deportation flights, potentially reaching two flights per week, as immigration enforcement expands following the termination of TPS protections.
