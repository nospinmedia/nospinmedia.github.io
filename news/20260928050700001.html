5 arrested near U.K. air base used for Iran strikes

Trump says suspects sought to do 'big damage'

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 28, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

British counterterrorism police are investigating five men arrested near RAF Fairford after three suspicious vehicles were reported traveling toward the military air base early September 27. The base in Gloucestershire, England, hosts U.S. Air Force personnel and has been used by American bombers in operations against Iran.

Police said they received a call at about 12:45 a.m. concerning the three vehicles. Armed officers responded and arrested five men in the nearby Whelford area on suspicion of offenses under the Explosives Act. The men were subsequently further arrested on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act under Section 5 of the Terrorism Act. All five remained in custody as counterterrorism officers worked to determine the circumstances surrounding the incident.

Authorities established a 400-meter cordon around the vehicles while the British Army's Explosive Ordnance Disposal unit examined them. About 85 households were advised to evacuate as a precaution, and a nearby leisure center was opened for residents who could not stay elsewhere. Police said the incident was contained but cautioned against speculation while the investigation continued.

President Trump later praised the British response and said the suspects had been looking to do "big damage." Trump also said British and American authorities had the group under investigation before the arrests. However, reporting from Britain indicated the police operation was triggered by a public report of the suspicious vehicles, and British authorities had not publicly confirmed Trump's account of prior surveillance.

Investigators are examining whether the suspected plot had links to Iran, but authorities have not publicly identified who they believe was behind it. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps previously warned that bases used for attacks on Iranian territory could be considered targets after RAF Fairford was used for U.S. operations against Iran.
