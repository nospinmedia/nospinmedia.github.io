Serbia’s Vučić concedes defeat to student-led movement

Student List projected to win 135–140 of parliament’s 250 seats

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 26, 2026 at 6:16 AM ET

Serbia’s longtime political leader Aleksandar Vučić has conceded defeat in a snap parliamentary election after a student-led movement turned nearly two years of anti-corruption protests into a major electoral victory.

Vučić said final results were not yet available, but results from a sample made the outcome clear. He estimated the Student List would win between 135 and 140 seats in Serbia’s 250-seat parliament, compared with about 105 seats for his political camp. The result would give the student-backed movement a parliamentary majority if confirmed by the final count.

The election represents a major reversal for Vučić, who had dominated Serbian politics as either prime minister or president since 2014. He resigned as president on September 27, 2026, before campaigning to return to government as prime minister. The snap parliamentary election was called in September after sustained political pressure from a student movement that had grown into the most serious challenge to his longtime rule.

The movement began after a railway station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad in November 2024, killing 16 people. Students and other demonstrators demanded accountability and protested what they described as government corruption and weakening democratic institutions. Serbian authorities and Vučić have denied allegations of widespread corruption and authoritarianism.

The protests gradually gained broader public support, and the Student List fielded 250 candidates from varied backgrounds, including professors, lawyers, farmers and actors. Tens of thousands of supporters gathered in Belgrade after the election results emerged, celebrating what would be Serbia’s biggest political transition in more than a decade.

The next step is the finalization of the election results and the formation of a new government, with the student-backed candidates facing the transition from a protest movement to governing Serbia.
