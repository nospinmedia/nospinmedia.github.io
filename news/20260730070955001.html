U.S. Blocks New Foreign-Made Humanoid And Robot Dog Models Over Security Concerns

FCC Restricts New Equipment Authorizations As U.S.-China Technology Tensions Continue

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished July 30, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) voted to place foreign-produced advanced humanoid robots, quadruped 'robot dog' systems, and certain connected power inverters on its Covered List, preventing new models from receiving the equipment authorization required for legal importation, marketing, and sale in the United States. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said the action is intended to reduce national security and supply chain risks associated with connected devices produced by companies considered to pose security concerns.

The order applies to new device models seeking FCC approval and does not automatically prohibit previously authorized products already being imported, sold, or operated within the United States. Manufacturers may request conditional approval through designated federal agencies if they can demonstrate that a product does not present an unacceptable national security risk.

Federal officials said the decision reflects growing concern that increasingly sophisticated connected robots and critical infrastructure equipment could expose sensitive communications networks or other essential systems to foreign interference. The order also expands restrictions on certain foreign-built power inverters used in renewable energy installations and large electrical systems.

The move is expected to have its greatest impact on Chinese manufacturers, which currently dominate the global commercial market for advanced humanoid robotics. Chinese officials condemned the decision, accusing the United States of using national security as a justification for trade protectionism and warning that Beijing would take measures to protect the interests of its domestic technology companies.

The action represents another escalation in the broader technology competition between Washington and Beijing, where export controls, semiconductor restrictions, artificial intelligence regulations, and telecommunications security have increasingly become central issues in the relationship between the world's two largest economies. Analysts said the new restrictions could encourage additional investment in domestic robotics manufacturing while further reshaping global technology supply chains.
