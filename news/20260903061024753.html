U.S. forces sink vessel tied to cartel logistics

Operation targets Pacific trafficking network

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 3, 2026 at 5:15 PM ET

U.S. Southern Command (SOUTHCOM) announced that American forces boarded, searched, and deliberately sank a vessel in the Eastern Pacific Ocean that officials said was being used to support maritime drug trafficking operations linked to Los Choneros, an Ecuadorian criminal organization designated by the United States as a Foreign Terrorist Organization (FTO). The operation was conducted in coordination with the government of Ecuador.

According to SOUTHCOM, U.S. Marines and sailors operating from the USS San Antonio (LPD 17) intercepted the vessel in international waters after intelligence indicated it was functioning as an offshore refueling platform for trafficking operations. Boarding teams secured the ship without reported injuries, detained multiple people aboard, and transferred them to Ecuadorian authorities for further legal proceedings. After the search was completed and personnel removed, U.S. forces intentionally sank the vessel to prevent it from being used again.

Military officials described the ship as an important logistics asset that helped extend the operating range of trafficking vessels moving narcotics through the eastern Pacific. SOUTHCOM said eliminating floating refueling platforms is intended to disrupt supply networks that enable organized criminal groups to transport drugs over long distances while avoiding frequent returns to shore. The operation forms part of expanding security cooperation between the United States and Ecuador aimed at reducing transnational organized crime in the region.

The announcement comes as the United States and several partner nations continue increasing maritime interdiction efforts against drug trafficking organizations operating throughout the Americas. Officials said the operation reflects a strategy of targeting not only narcotics shipments but also the infrastructure that supports trafficking networks, including vessels used for refueling, resupply, and logistics.
