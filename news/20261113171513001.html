ICE arrests and deportations reach record highs

Federal data shows sharp increase in immigration enforcement during fiscal 2026

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 13, 2026 at 5:16 PM ET

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement recorded the highest annual totals of arrests and deportations in the agency's 23-year history during fiscal year 2026, according to federal figures released by the Department of Homeland Security.

ICE carried out 483,200 deportations between October 2025 and September 2026, surpassing the previous annual high of 442,640 set the year before. The agency also recorded more than 491,000 immigration arrests, including 474,850 civil administrative arrests. That compares with about 239,890 arrests the previous fiscal year and 113,430 in fiscal 2024.

Monthly arrests reached 50,925 in August, followed by more than 48,000 in September. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin credited expanded resources and the Trump administration's enforcement policies for the increase. Congress has provided approximately $45 billion to expand immigration detention capacity and nearly $30 billion for arrests, transportation and personnel.

Independent analyses provide additional context for the federal totals. Syracuse University's Transactional Records Access Clearinghouse found that formal removals accounted for 63% of ICE deportations during July and August, with voluntary departures and returns making up the remainder.

Reporting citing the Migration Policy Institute and Deportation Data Project also found that 71% of deported immigrants who had lived in the United States for at least two years had no criminal convictions. The administration has emphasized the removal of people with criminal records, while the data indicates enforcement has also reached a broader population of immigrants without convictions.

The record applies specifically to ICE's history. Broader measures of U.S. immigration removals and returns, including enforcement at the border, reached higher levels during some earlier periods before ICE was created in 2003.
