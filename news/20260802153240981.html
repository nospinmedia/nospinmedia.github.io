Israeli Strikes Kill At Least 17 In Gaza

Jerusalem rebuffs pause as it flags concerns over Trump’s disarmament plan

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 2, 2026 at 2:57 PM ET

Israeli airstrikes across the Gaza Strip on August 2, 2026 killed at least 17 people, underscoring that combat operations remain active even as diplomacy over Gaza’s future intensifies. The attacks came days after President Trump touted progress on a disarmament framework negotiated by the U.S.-led Board of Peace, but an Israeli government spokesperson said Israel had conveyed “serious security concerns” about the proposal and offered no indication of a halt to strikes.

Medical officials reported fatalities in Gaza City, Deir al-Balah, and Khan Younis. Among the dead were three members of one family killed in a residential strike and a 4-year-old child whose body was taken to Nasser Hospital in southern Gaza, according to hospital staff. Palestinian health authorities said additional casualties and injuries were recorded after drone and air attacks on apartments, vehicles, and tents for displaced families. The Israel Defense Forces said recent operations targeted militant infrastructure and weapons storehouses.

The continuing bombardment highlights a widening gap between Washington’s push to implement a negotiated path and Jerusalem’s insistence that verifiable disarmament come first. Doron Spielman, spokesperson for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s office, said Israel has raised detailed objections with mediators, including former UK Prime Minister Tony Blair, now a senior figure with the Board of Peace. U.S. officials have said the plan seeks phased steps that would ultimately see weapons decommissioned and pave the way for reconstruction and troop redeployments, but emphasized that implementation details remain under discussion. Gaza’s Health Ministry says the broader war has killed more than 73,000 people since October 7, 2023, a figure Israel disputes.

Trump’s proposal—described by U.S. officials as a 20-point framework—centers on the phased disarmament of Hamas and other armed groups alongside monitored security arrangements meant to enable reconstruction and a gradual Israeli redeployment. Sunday’s strikes, however, signal that no operative pause is in effect and that Israeli leaders remain unconvinced the current plan provides sufficient guarantees to prevent a rapid militant rearmament once forces pull back.
