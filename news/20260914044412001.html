Russian drone strikes train near Polish border

Diplomatic train passed through minutes earlier

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished September 14, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

A Russian drone struck a passenger train near Ukraine's border with Poland on September 13, 2026, shortly after a separate diplomatic train carrying former British Prime Minister Boris Johnson, former Swedish Prime Minister Carl Bildt and European security advisers had passed through the area. The attack occurred near the Yahodyn-Dorohusk border crossing, about 2 kilometers (1.2 miles) from Poland, a NATO and European Union member.

Ukraine's state railway operator Ukrzaliznytsia said the diplomatic train had deliberately departed Yahodyn earlier than scheduled because timetables were being adjusted in response to Russian attack threats. A drone subsequently struck the locomotive of another passenger train traveling on the Kyiv-Warsaw route. Railway personnel had received advance warning of the approaching drone and evacuated passengers before the strike. No casualties were reported. Another train carrying former CIA Director David Petraeus was still at Yahodyn station when the attack occurred.

Ukrzaliznytsia said it was "highly probable" that the diplomatic train had been the drone's intended target, although that assessment has not been independently established. Bildt said his train had passed through only minutes earlier. The officials were returning from the Yalta European Strategy conference in Kyiv, an annual gathering involving political, security and business figures.

A separate Russian drone also struck a truck less than 1 mile from Polish territory, prompting authorities to temporarily close the Yahodyn-Dorohusk crossing. The attacks were part of a wider Russian barrage across Ukraine involving more than 450 drones and missiles. Ukrainian forces said they intercepted 405 drones and six missiles. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha described the strikes near Poland as Russian attacks reaching the doors of the EU and NATO, while Polish authorities increased scrutiny of the border area.
