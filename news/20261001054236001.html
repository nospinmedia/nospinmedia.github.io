Pennsylvania reports fifth measles-associated death

Outbreak reaches 943 cases across 39 counties

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 1, 2026 at 3:46 PM ET

Pennsylvania has reported its fifth measles-associated death of 2026 as the state's outbreak grew to 943 confirmed cases across 39 counties, according to the Pennsylvania Department of Health. The latest person to die was an unvaccinated resident of Lancaster County who had tested positive for measles. State officials did not release the person's age or other identifying information, citing privacy protections.

The latest death is the third reported in Lancaster County, the center of Pennsylvania's outbreak. The state's other measles-associated deaths involved an 18-year-old Mifflin County resident, a 40-year-old Jefferson County woman and two infants from Lancaster County. All five people were unvaccinated, although the two infants were too young to receive the vaccine under routine recommendations. Pennsylvania's first measles-associated deaths this year were also its first reported in 35 years.

The outbreak has continued expanding rapidly. Pennsylvania reported 40 additional cases in two days, bringing the statewide total to 943. At least 176 people have been hospitalized, and only four of the 943 confirmed cases involved people who had been vaccinated, according to state health officials. Pennsylvania currently has more reported measles cases than any other state.

Health officials have responded with expanded vaccination efforts. State healthcare centers have administered more than 5,500 MMR vaccine doses since April in communities affected by the outbreak, while nurses at pop-up clinics have administered more than 8,500 doses. Two doses of the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine are about 97% effective at preventing measles, according to public health authorities.

Pennsylvania and federal officials differ over how measles deaths are counted. The state classifies five deaths as measles-associated, while the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention had federally confirmed only two measles-related deaths nationwide using mortality data from the National Center for Health Statistics. The federal count may change as additional death records are reviewed.
