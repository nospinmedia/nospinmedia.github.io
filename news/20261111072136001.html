House blocks effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth

Lawmakers vote 211-204 to send eight impeachment articles to committee

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished November 11, 2026 at 1:16 PM ET

The Republican-led House has blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, voting 211-204 to send eight articles of impeachment to the House Judiciary Committee rather than allowing an immediate vote on them. The procedural move effectively ends the effort for the remainder of the current Congress.

The impeachment resolution was introduced by Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky, a Republican who has frequently clashed with the Trump administration over military policy. Massie was the only Republican to vote against referring the resolution to committee, joining Democrats who were present in opposing the procedural move. Eleven Democrats and seven Republicans did not vote.

Massie's eight articles accused Hegseth of misconduct involving U.S. military operations and executive authority, including actions connected to Iran and Yemen and maritime strikes. The allegations also challenged the administration's handling of congressional oversight and its legal authority for some military actions. Referral to the Judiciary Committee prevents the articles from receiving an immediate up-or-down impeachment vote on the House floor.

Before the House acted, Hegseth dismissed the impeachment effort during his first formal Pentagon news conference in more than six months, calling it a political stunt and defending the administration's military decisions. The House vote came shortly afterward, resolving the immediate threat of impeachment that Hegseth had publicly played down.

The issue could return after control of the House changes in January 2027. Democrats won the chamber in the 2026 midterm elections, and Democratic leaders have indicated that oversight of the Defense Department and the Trump administration will remain a priority in the next Congress. Any future impeachment effort, however, would require lawmakers to introduce and advance a new resolution after the new Congress convenes.

The vote marks a clear conclusion to the impeachment push in the current Republican-controlled House while leaving open the possibility of renewed scrutiny under the incoming Democratic majority.
