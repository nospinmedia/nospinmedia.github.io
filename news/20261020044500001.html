France imposes curfews after worst rioting in years

More than 2,400 arrests follow widespread violence and looting

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 20, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

French authorities have imposed localized curfews in parts of Paris and several other cities following a weekend of widespread rioting, looting and arson that resulted in more than 2,400 arrests and damage to thousands of businesses.

Paris police imposed an overnight curfew from 11 p.m. Monday to 6 a.m. Tuesday around Place de la République and the Les Halles shopping district, while demonstrations in those areas were prohibited beginning at 8 p.m. Other communities, including Lille and Nice, imposed restrictions targeting minors, while authorities in Marseille banned demonstrations. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu backed limited local curfews but said a citywide Paris curfew was not planned.

The unrest grew out of demonstrations over the rising cost of living, fuel prices and concerns about public services and education. Protests that began largely peacefully escalated into some of France's worst violence in years. About 3,000 businesses were damaged nationwide, and nearly 1,000 police officers and firefighters were injured. One protester was also reported killed during the unrest.

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez offered his resignation after acknowledging failures in the security response, but President Emmanuel Macron declined it and called for changes in police tactics. Lecornu said authorities would restore order and left open the possibility of stronger emergency measures if violence continued.

The unrest has also increased political pressure on Macron's government ahead of France's presidential election in roughly six months. Opposition parties have criticized the government's handling of the violence, while officials have announced assistance for affected businesses and continued cleanup and security operations in damaged areas.
