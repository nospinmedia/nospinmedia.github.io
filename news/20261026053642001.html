Trump invokes 9/11 while attacking El-Sayed in Michigan Senate race

President compares opposing Democrat to Flight 93 passengers fighting hijackers

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished October 26, 2026 at 10:16 AM ET

President Trump invoked the September 11, 2001 attacks while campaigning against Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan, comparing the effort to defeat him at the polls with the actions of passengers who fought hijackers aboard United Flight 93.

Speaking at a rally in Port Huron, Michigan, on October 25, Trump called El-Sayed a "jihadist" and a "Muslim Brotherhood member" and said Michigan voters needed to fight to keep him out of the Senate. Trump also said El-Sayed would have to pass through a metal detector before entering the Senate. El-Sayed has denied having any affiliation with the Muslim Brotherhood, and Reuters reported that Republican opponent Mike Rogers has alleged such ties without providing evidence.

Trump specifically invoked Todd Beamer, a Michigan native who was among the Flight 93 passengers who fought back against hijackers on 9/11. Trump told the crowd that Michigan voters should similarly stand up and fight to prevent El-Sayed from reaching the Capitol. Some people in the audience shouted "traitor" and "deport," although El-Sayed was born in Michigan.

The remarks drew criticism from Bishara Bahbah, a founder of the group formerly known as Arab Americans for Trump, who called Trump's comments unacceptable and said Arab and Muslim voters would remember them on Election Day. El-Sayed has described challenges to his loyalty to the United States as "shameful." Rogers said separately that he has never called El-Sayed a terrorist, although he has continued questioning the Democrat's political associations.

El-Sayed and Rogers are competing for the open Senate seat being vacated by retiring Democratic Sen. Gary Peters. The November 3 contest is among the Senate races that could affect control of the chamber. If elected, El-Sayed would become the first Muslim elected to the U.S. Senate.
