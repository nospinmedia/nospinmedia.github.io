FCC eliminates 39% national TV ownership cap

No new nationwide percentage limit replaces it

By No Spin Media NewsroomPublished August 7, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

The Federal Communications Commission voted 2–1 on August 6 to eliminate the rule that generally barred a single television station owner from reaching more than 39% of U.S. television households. Republican FCC Chairman Brendan Carr and Commissioner Olivia Trusty supported the change, while Democratic Commissioner Anna M. Gomez dissented.

The FCC did not replace the 39% limit with a higher numerical cap. Instead, it eliminated the national percentage ceiling entirely and replaced it with case-by-case review of proposed transactions. That means a broadcaster can seek acquisitions giving its stations reach beyond 39% — potentially 50%, 60%, 80% or more of U.S. television households — without violating a fixed national FCC percentage cap. Approval is not automatic: the FCC can still approve or reject individual transactions under its public-interest review, including consideration of competition, localism and viewpoint diversity. Separate rules limiting ownership within individual local television markets were not eliminated by this order.

The change could significantly affect consolidation among large station groups. In March 2026, the FCC approved Nexstar Media Group's acquisition of Tegna after waiving the 39% rule. Reuters reported that the combined company would reach approximately 80% of U.S. television households if the transaction survives pending court challenges. Under the FCC's new national ownership framework, exceeding 39% by itself would no longer prevent a similar transaction from being considered.

The repeal is also headed toward a legal fight over whether the FCC has authority to eliminate the ceiling. Congress directed the agency in the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2004 to set the national audience-reach limit at 39% and excluded that limit from the FCC's regular ownership-rule review. Gomez argues those provisions mean only Congress can eliminate the cap, while Carr's FCC maintains the agency has authority to modify its ownership rule. Republican Sen. Ted Cruz, chairman of the Senate Commerce Committee, has also questioned whether the FCC can change the limit without congressional action. Free Press, which opposes further media consolidation, said it intends to challenge the repeal in court.
