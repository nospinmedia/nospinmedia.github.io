🚒 Maine cites six fire departments after deadly Searsmont mill explosionNearly $108,000 in penalties waived as departments correct safety problems 👇

HealthNovember 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 11, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Maine workplace safety officials have cited six fire departments over deficiencies identified during their response to the deadly Robbins Lumber explosion in Searsmont in May. The departments in Appleton, Belmont, Lincolnville, Montville, Morrill and Searsmont were cited after a state investigation found they lacked an effective system for tracking firefighters' locations during the emergency.

The Maine Department of Labor's Bureau of Labor Standards also cited five departments for deficiencies involving personal protective equipment. State officials said the departments have corrected the cited problems and are continuing training and improvements to personnel accountability systems.

The departments initially faced nearly $108,000 in proposed civil penalties, but the state suspended almost all of them so the towns could direct resources toward safety improvements. Montville was the exception, paying $1,000 of a proposed $20,000 penalty because its respirator fit-testing violation repeated a citation from 2025. Belmont's $11,000 in proposed penalties were suspended after the department adopted the Passport Accountability System and revised its medical evaluation procedures.

The findings stem from the May 15 fire and explosion at Robbins Lumber. Investigators determined that a fire developed around a silo containing sawdust and wood shavings. Water introduced during firefighting operations added oxygen and disturbed combustible dust, contributing to conditions that produced the explosion while firefighters, emergency medical personnel and mill employees were nearby.

Morrill firefighter Andrew Cross, 27, died at the scene. Searsmont Assistant Fire Chief Wayne Woodbury later died from his injuries, as did mill co-owner Alden Robbins. At least 10 other people were injured, and nearly 300 firefighters from 46 departments eventually responded.

Morrill First Selectman Randy Place said state labor officials worked with the town as it addressed the findings while grieving Cross's death. The citations focus on firefighter workplace safety, and officials said the departments cooperated with the state and corrected the identified deficiencies.
