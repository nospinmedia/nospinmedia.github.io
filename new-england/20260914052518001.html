🌧️ Flash flooding swamps coastal ConnecticutHeavy rain halts trains and closes Tweed terminal 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 14, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Torrential rain triggered widespread flash flooding across coastal Connecticut on September 13, 2026, inundating roads, disrupting rail service and forcing emergency crews to rescue people from stranded vehicles. Some communities received approximately 6 to 7 inches of rain, with particularly severe flooding reported across Fairfield and New Haven counties.

In Westport, officials reported approximately 7 inches of rain, including periods when precipitation fell at several inches per hour. Emergency crews conducted 13 vehicle rescues as rapidly rising water trapped motorists on flooded roads. Officials repeatedly warned drivers not to enter standing or moving water. Flooding also affected neighboring communities, including Bridgeport, where firefighters used boats during rescue operations as water entered streets and residential areas.

Transportation across the Connecticut shoreline was heavily disrupted. Metro-North Railroad suspended New Haven Line service between South Norwalk and New Haven because of flooding on and around the tracks. Amtrak also reported disruptions along the Northeast Corridor. Flooded roadways forced closures and restrictions on portions of major routes, including Interstate 95 and Route 15, while numerous local streets became temporarily impassable.

At Tweed New Haven Airport, flooding forced officials to close the commercial departures terminal and tell departing passengers not to travel to the airport. Arriving flights were still being handled while airport crews assessed conditions. Governor Ned Lamont urged residents to avoid flooded roads and unnecessary travel as emergency personnel responded throughout the affected region.

Conditions began improving as the heaviest rain moved away, but officials continued monitoring flooded roads, transportation infrastructure and electrical hazards as water receded. The storm demonstrated how quickly intense rainfall can overwhelm drainage systems in Connecticut's densely developed coastal communities.
