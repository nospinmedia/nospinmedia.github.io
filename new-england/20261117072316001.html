⚖️ Judge Sets March 29 Retrial For Lindsay ClancyDefense plans appeal that could affect timing of second murder trial 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthNovember 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 17, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Plymouth Superior Court Judge William Sullivan scheduled Lindsay Clancy's murder retrial to begin on March 29, 2027, during a hearing in Plymouth, Massachusetts, on Monday, November 16. Prosecutors confirmed they intend to retry Clancy on the original first-degree murder indictments in the January 2023 deaths of her three young children in Duxbury.

Clancy, a former labor and delivery nurse, is accused of strangling Cora Clancy, 5, Dawson Clancy, 3, and Callan Clancy, 8 months, inside the family's home. Her first trial ended in a mistrial on September 4, 2026, after the 12-member jury was unable to reach a unanimous verdict following days of deliberations.

Assistant District Attorney Jennifer Sprague represented the prosecution at Monday's hearing. Prosecutors had requested a May 2027 trial date because of scheduling conflicts, but Sullivan instead selected March 29. Defense attorney Kevin Reddington noted that prosecutors are pursuing the original first-degree murder charges rather than lesser charges.

The judge also scheduled a hearing for December 30, 2026, to address logistical issues including courtroom security and media arrangements. A final pretrial conference is scheduled for February 18, 2027.

The March date is not necessarily final. Defense attorney Martin Weinberg told the court that Clancy's legal team plans to appeal Sullivan's recent denial of its motion to dismiss the charges on double-jeopardy grounds to the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court. An appellate ruling or stay could affect the timing of the retrial.

The central dispute remains Clancy's criminal responsibility at the time of the killings. Prosecutors have argued that her actions were deliberate and planned. Her defense maintains that she was suffering from severe postpartum mental illness, including psychosis, and was not criminally responsible. Clancy remains in state custody while receiving medical and psychiatric care at Tewksbury Hospital.
