📜 Massachusetts Enacts PROTECT Act Limiting State Cooperation With ICENew law restricts civil immigration enforcement at schools, hospitals, and courthouses 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EducationAugust 7, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 7, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey signed the PROTECT Act into law on August 5, 2026, establishing new statewide limits on cooperation with federal civil immigration enforcement. Formally titled "An Act promoting rule of law, oversight, trust and equal constitutional treatment," the law prohibits state and local agencies from assisting certain civil immigration enforcement activities at designated sensitive locations, including public schools, courthouses, hospitals, nursing homes, and childcare centers.

The law also prohibits Massachusetts law enforcement agencies from entering into new Section 287(g) agreements that authorize local officers to perform certain federal immigration enforcement duties. In addition, employers generally must provide workers with at least 48 hours' notice after receiving federal employment-eligibility audit notices, and individuals are granted a civil cause of action under Massachusetts law for specified civil-rights violations covered by the statute.

Governor Healey signed the measure during a ceremony at La Colaborativa in Chelsea, joined by lawmakers, healthcare professionals, labor leaders, and community advocates. Supporters said the law strengthens trust between immigrant communities and public institutions by encouraging residents to seek medical care, attend school, and access the courts without fear of civil immigration enforcement. Opponents argue the measure could hinder cooperation with federal immigration authorities and are expected to continue challenging similar policies in other states.

The statute preserves cooperation between Massachusetts law enforcement and federal authorities in criminal investigations and continues to require compliance with valid judicial warrants issued by state or federal courts. The law takes effect under the implementation schedule established in the legislation.
