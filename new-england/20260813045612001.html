🗳️ Amanda Janoo Wins Vermont Democratic PrimaryFirst-time candidate will face Gov. Phil Scott in November 👇

EconomyAugust 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 13, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Political newcomer Amanda Janoo won Vermont’s Democratic primary for governor on August 11, defeating Aly Richards in a close contest and advancing to a November matchup with five-term Republican Gov. Phil Scott. The Associated Press called the race for Janoo late Tuesday, and Richards conceded shortly afterward.

Janoo, a 38-year-old economist who had never previously run for office, held about 49% of the vote as of 11 p.m., compared with roughly 46% for Richards. Janoo grew up in Strafford and now lives in Burlington, and has worked with think tanks, the United Nations and international development organizations. She campaigned as the more progressive and anti-establishment choice, proposing policies that included a surcharge on personal income above $500,000 to help fund free primary care and new state regulations affecting large data centers.

Richards, the former head of the childcare advocacy organization Let’s Grow Kids, entered the primary with backing from prominent Vermont Democrats and stronger institutional support. She also benefited from more than $200,000 in late digital advertising purchased by an outside political action committee supporting her candidacy. Richards conceded Tuesday night and said she would support Janoo in the general election.

Janoo now faces a difficult challenge against Scott, who ran unopposed in the Republican primary. Scott has won five terms as governor and received 73% of the vote in his 2024 reelection. Another victory would put him on track to become Vermont’s longest-serving governor since statehood. Vermont and neighboring New Hampshire are the only states that elect governors every two years.

The result gives Vermont Democrats another first-time statewide candidate as they try to unseat a Republican governor who has repeatedly drawn support from Democratic and independent voters.
