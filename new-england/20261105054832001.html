🗳️ LePage declares victory in Maine's still-uncalled House raceOutstanding votes leave 2nd District result unresolved 👇

PoliticsNovember 5, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 5, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Former Maine Gov. Paul LePage declared victory Wednesday in the race for Maine's 2nd Congressional District, but the contest against Democrat Matt Dunlap remained officially uncalled as votes continued to be counted.

LePage, a Republican, said his campaign had contacted communities with outstanding results and concluded there was no remaining path for Dunlap to overtake him. The Associated Press had not called the race, and Dunlap had not conceded as of Wednesday evening.

The contest remained extremely close as returns came in. LePage had trailed by about 1,200 votes early Wednesday, but results from smaller, Republican-leaning communities gradually erased Dunlap's advantage. By Wednesday afternoon, LePage had moved narrowly ahead, with additional ballots still outstanding.

Most of the remaining votes were expected from rural areas that have generally favored Republican candidates. The district's larger population centers, including Bangor, Lewiston and Augusta, had already reported results and gave Dunlap substantial margins.

The race could also involve Maine's ranked-choice voting system. A candidate must receive more than 50% of first-choice votes to win outright. With write-in votes also being counted, the final margin will determine whether a ranked-choice tabulation is necessary.

The seat is open because Democratic Rep. Jared Golden did not seek reelection. If LePage ultimately wins, the former two-term governor would return to elected office for the first time since leaving the Blaine House in 2019 and would flip a Democratic-held House seat to Republicans.

Until remaining ballots are counted and election officials complete the process, LePage's declaration is a campaign claim rather than an official determination of the winner.
