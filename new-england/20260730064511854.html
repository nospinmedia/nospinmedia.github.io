⚖️ Three Maine Laws Limiting State Cooperation With ICE Take Effect 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EducationJuly 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published July 30, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Three new Maine laws limiting how state and local agencies cooperate with federal immigration enforcement officially took effect on July 29, establishing new rules for law enforcement, public schools, healthcare providers and landlords. Supporters say the measures protect the rights and privacy of Maine residents, while opponents argue they could make federal immigration enforcement more difficult.

The legislation, commonly referred to by supporters as the "ICE Out of Maine" laws, prohibits state and local law enforcement officers from making civil immigration arrests without judicial authority, limits when landlords may disclose certain tenant information to immigration officials, and requires public schools and state-funded healthcare facilities to follow new procedures before providing access to federal immigration agents. In many situations, agencies must require a judicial warrant before granting access to non-public areas or confidential records.

The laws took effect as public attention remains focused on the fatal shooting of Johan Sebastian Duran Guerrero by ICE agent David Brouillette during an attempted arrest in Biddeford earlier this month. The shooting remains under investigation by state and federal authorities. Separately, investigative reporting by The Maine Monitor examined prior immigration enforcement operations involving Brouillette, including allegations from immigrants and attorneys regarding earlier vehicle stops and arrests. Those allegations have not been adjudicated in court, and the ongoing investigations into the Biddeford shooting have not reached final conclusions.

Following the shooting, the Department of Homeland Security announced a nationwide policy requiring ICE officers to wear body cameras during vehicle stops, while officials also said they are reviewing agency policies and procedures.

Supporters of the new Maine laws, including the ACLU of Maine, say the measures help ensure that state resources are focused on local responsibilities rather than federal civil immigration enforcement. Critics contend the restrictions could hinder cooperation between local agencies and federal authorities in some situations.
