🏠 Vermont homelessness declines statewide but rises around BurlingtonAnnual count finds increases among children, unsheltered residents and BIPOC Vermonters 👇

EconomyNovember 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 11, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Vermont recorded a modest decline in homelessness this year, but new data show increases among children, people without shelter, BIPOC residents and people in Chittenden County. The state's annual Point-in-Time count identified 3,286 people experiencing homelessness on January 28, exactly 100 fewer than in 2025 and about 200 fewer than in 2024.

The statewide decline was nearly 3%, but Chittenden County moved in the opposite direction. The county, which includes Burlington, recorded 834 homeless people, an increase of about 1% from last year and the highest total of any Vermont county. The number of homeless children increased 5%, with 667 people under age 18 identified, while unsheltered homelessness also rose 5% to 284 people.

The 2026 report also found that homelessness among Black, Indigenous and People of Color increased 9% compared with 2025. Housing advocates have highlighted the disparity as another measure showing that the overall statewide decline was not experienced equally across Vermont's population.

Separate Housing Inventory Count data provide a snapshot of the state's capacity to respond. Vermont had 3,359 temporary housing and shelter beds, including 2,120 hotel and motel beds and 259 beds in domestic and sexual violence shelters.

Housing advocates caution that the Point-in-Time count is a one-night snapshot rather than a complete census. Kim Anetsberger, director of Lamoille Community House and co-chair of the Balance of State Continuum of Care, said the downward movement was encouraging while emphasizing that substantial pressures remain.

The results arrive as Vermont continues adjusting its emergency housing system, including the General Assistance motel voucher program and winter shelter operations. Advocates say the statewide decline should therefore be considered alongside demographic disparities, regional increases and continuing demand for temporary housing.
