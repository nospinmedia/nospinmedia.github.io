🏠 Rhode Island creates home hardening grant programNew law aims to strengthen homes against hurricanes and lower insurance losses 👇

File photoSatellite image of a mature tropical cyclone over the ocean showing a well-defined eye and spiral cloud bands. No specific location or event is implied.Credit: NASA
EnvironmentAugust 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 3, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Rhode Island has established the Strengthen Rhody Homes Program, creating a statewide framework to help homeowners reinforce their properties against hurricanes and other severe storms through grants and other financial assistance. The legislation authorizes the Rhode Island Department of Business Regulation to administer the program and pursue federal, state, private and nonprofit funding to support eligible mitigation projects.

The measure, enacted as Senate Bill 3150A, was sponsored by Sen. V. Susan Sosnowski and Rep. Brian Patrick Kennedy and signed into law during the 2026 legislative session. The program is modeled after home-hardening initiatives in other hurricane-prone states that encourage property owners to make improvements designed to reduce storm damage and improve long-term resilience.

Eligible projects may include strengthening roof systems, improving roof-to-wall connections, installing impact-resistant windows and doors, reinforcing garage doors and completing other upgrades intended to reduce damage from high winds and wind-driven rain. State officials said the program is expected to rely on recognized mitigation standards and licensed contractors as grant requirements are developed. The Department of Business Regulation must establish program rules, identify funding sources and develop an application process before grants become available.

Supporters say strengthening homes before storms strike can reduce repair costs, improve public safety and help stabilize the state's property insurance market by lowering future claims. Housing advocates and lawmakers have also argued that mitigation investments are generally less expensive than rebuilding after major disasters and may improve access to affordable homeowners insurance in vulnerable coastal communities.

The law does not immediately provide grant funding. Instead, it creates the legal framework allowing the department to seek and distribute available funding as it becomes available and implement the program through future regulations.
