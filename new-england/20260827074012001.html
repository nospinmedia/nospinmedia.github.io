🗳️ Maine governor candidate proposes asking for ICE at polling placesLeaked audio sparks legal and political backlash 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
PoliticsAugust 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 27, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Maine Republican gubernatorial nominee Bobby Charles is facing criticism after saying he plans to ask White House border czar Tom Homan to deploy U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents and U.S. Marshals to polling places before the November 2026 election. Audio from an August 20 meeting with Maine Young Republicans in Arundel, first reported by the Bangor Daily News and later obtained by The Associated Press, captured Charles saying he would seek a federal law enforcement presence at voting locations three to four weeks before Election Day.

Following publication of the recording, Charles defended his remarks, saying he wanted state and local election officials to have access to federal resources if needed to prevent illegal voting, violence or voter intimidation. He said legally eligible citizens should be able to vote freely while those not legally entitled to vote should not be permitted to cast ballots. His campaign said he intends to pursue every lawful option to protect election integrity.

The proposal drew immediate opposition from Maine Secretary of State Shenna Bellows, who said there is no evidence of widespread noncitizen voting in Maine and emphasized that armed federal officers generally are prohibited from serving as a routine presence at polling places under federal law. Bellows said Maine voters should expect elections that are secure, accessible and free from intimidation. The Maine Democratic Party also criticized the proposal, calling it an attempt to discourage voter participation.

Charles, who previously served in the administration of President George W. Bush, won the Republican nomination for governor and will face Democratic nominee Hannah Pingree in November's general election. The controversy has placed election administration and immigration enforcement at the center of Maine's gubernatorial campaign as candidates debate how best to protect both election security and voter access.
