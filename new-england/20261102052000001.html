📊 Pingree holds 12-point lead in final Maine governor pollCharles trails 49%-37% as voters prepare for Election Day 👇

EducationNovember 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 2, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Democrat Hannah Pingree holds a double-digit lead in the final New York Times/Portland Press Herald/Siena College poll of Maine's governor's race, with relatively few voters still undecided ahead of Election Day on Tuesday.

The survey found Pingree supported by 49% of likely voters, compared with 37% for Republican Bobby Charles and 8% for independent Rick Bennett. Another 6% either were undecided or declined to answer. The poll surveyed 714 likely Maine voters from October 27 through November 1 and has a margin of error of ±4.7 percentage points.

The result continues a pattern seen in most polling since the June primaries. A September Times/Press Herald/Siena survey showed Pingree leading Charles 48%-36%, with Bennett also at 8%. However, an NPR/PBS News/Marist poll released in late October produced a different result, showing Pingree and Charles tied at 44% among likely voters.

Pingree, a former speaker of the Maine House of Representatives who later led Gov. Janet Mills' policy office, is seeking to keep the Blaine House under Democratic control after Mills leaves office because of term limits. Charles is a lawyer and former U.S. assistant secretary of state, while Bennett is a state senator and former chair of the Maine Republican Party.

Affordability has been a major issue throughout the campaign, including housing and heating costs. The candidates have also debated state spending, taxes, health care and Maine's relationship with the federal government.

Unlike Maine's federal congressional elections, the governor's race does not use ranked-choice voting. The candidate receiving the most votes Tuesday will win, even without an outright majority.

The poll measures voter preferences shortly before Election Day but does not determine the election's outcome.
