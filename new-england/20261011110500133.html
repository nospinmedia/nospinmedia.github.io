🚨 Massachusetts man killed by police after allegedly trying to hit pedestrians32-year-old identified as investigators examine deadly Stoughton confrontation 👇

HealthOctober 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 11, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A 32-year-old man was shot and killed by police in Stoughton, Massachusetts, early Saturday, October 10, 2026, after authorities said he attempted to strike pedestrians with his vehicle near a local pub. The Norfolk County District Attorney's Office identified the deceased man as Salaheddine Yagoub, who had no known address.

According to investigators, the Holbrook Regional Emergency Communications Center received multiple 911 calls around 12:33 a.m. reporting a possibly armed man outside a business at 37 Monk Street.

Authorities said callers reported that the man entered a black Mazda CX-5 and attempted to hit several pedestrians while driving in the area. Stoughton police officers responded and encountered Yagoub during the confrontation.

Police shot Yagoub, although officials have not publicly established the complete sequence of events immediately preceding the use of deadly force.

Officers administered first aid before emergency personnel transported him to a nearby hospital, where he was pronounced dead.

Residents described hearing multiple gunshots during the incident. One nearby resident told CBS Boston that a bullet struck his kitchen window. Another witness reported hearing officers ordering someone to drop a gun before shots were fired. Those accounts have not yet been established as official investigative findings.

The shooting occurred near The Public House, a popular neighborhood pub. The business said the confrontation did not involve its operations and thanked employees who helped keep customers calm. The establishment closed Saturday following the incident and announced plans to reopen Sunday.

The Massachusetts State Police Detective Unit, assigned to the Norfolk County District Attorney's Office, is investigating the shooting.

Authorities have not publicly identified the officers who fired their weapons or disclosed how many shots were fired. Investigators have also not confirmed whether Yagoub possessed a firearm during the confrontation.

The district attorney's office said there was no continuing threat to the public.

The investigation will examine the reported danger to pedestrians, the circumstances officers encountered when they arrived, and whether the use of deadly force was justified.
