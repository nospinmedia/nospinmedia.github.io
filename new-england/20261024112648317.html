🗳️ Justice Department Sending Election Monitors to Portland and LewistonFederal Officials Will Observe Maine Polls During November 3 Election 👇

Illustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
PoliticsOctober 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 24, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The U.S. Department of Justice plans to send federal election monitors to Portland and Lewiston, Maine, for the November 3 election, placing federal personnel at polling locations in two of the state's largest cities.

The Justice Department regularly deploys monitors around the country during elections to evaluate compliance with federal voting-rights laws. The Maine deployment is part of that broader federal monitoring effort rather than a takeover of local election administration.

Federal monitors can observe activity at polling places and speak with election officials and voters about potential voting-rights issues. Local election workers will continue to administer the election under Maine law, including voter check-in, ballot handling and vote counting.

The decision comes during an election in which Maine voters are choosing between Republican Sen. Susan Collins and Democratic challenger Troy Jackson in a closely watched U.S. Senate contest, along with congressional, state and local races.

The federal presence does not by itself establish that wrongdoing has occurred in either Portland or Lewiston. Justice Department election monitoring has been used for decades in jurisdictions across the country to assess whether voters are able to cast ballots without discrimination or other violations of federal law.

Portland and Lewiston are among Maine's largest and most diverse communities. Federal voting-rights protections can cover issues including discrimination based on race or language, accessibility for voters with disabilities and improper interference with a person's ability to vote.

The monitors' role is observational. They can document potential problems and report their findings to the Justice Department, which can take further legal action if officials determine federal voting laws have been violated.

The deployment adds another layer of oversight to Maine's election process as voters prepare to cast ballots on November 3, but state and municipal officials remain responsible for conducting the election and counting the votes.
