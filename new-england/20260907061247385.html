🍁 Canadian counter-tariffs take effect TuesdayNew England border businesses face deepening U.S.-Canada trade fight 👇

File photoShipping containers are lifted by a crane at a commercial cargo port.Credit: PickPik
EconomySeptember 7, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 7, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

New Canadian counter-tariffs on $27.6 billion worth of U.S. goods are scheduled to take effect at 12:01 a.m. September 8, adding another source of uncertainty for businesses across northern New England that depend heavily on trade and travel with Canada. The Canadian government says the new duties will range from 15% to 50%, depending on the product, and will match recently imposed U.S. tariffs dollar-for-dollar and rate-for-rate.

Canada announced the countermeasures after the United States imposed 50% tariffs on $27.6 billion of Canadian goods beginning August 22. The Canadian tariffs target products including steel and aluminum, dairy products, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, plastics and electronics. Goods already in transit to Canada when the tariffs take effect are exempt from the new duties.

The escalation comes as northern New England businesses are already dealing with weaker Canadian travel and uncertainty surrounding cross-border commerce. Vermont and Maine have particularly close economic ties with neighboring Canadian provinces, while tourism destinations throughout the region depend on Canadian visitors during the fall foliage and winter recreation seasons. The new tariffs apply to traded goods rather than tourism itself, but they arrive amid broader U.S.-Canada tensions that have affected travel patterns.

An Associated Press review found that Canadian travel to the United States fell sharply during the trade dispute, prompting U.S. tourism organizations to use discounts and other promotions to encourage visitors to return. Canadian trips to the United States fell 25% in 2025, while associated spending declined by approximately $2.4 billion, according to figures cited by AP. The continued trade confrontation is now creating additional uncertainty as New England communities approach important fall and winter tourism periods.
