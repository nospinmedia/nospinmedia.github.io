⚡ Massachusetts blocks state permits for data centers without local approvalNew rules target energy costs, water use and community impact 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 9, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey signed an executive order requiring large new data center projects to secure community support and meet state environmental and energy standards before state agencies can issue permits, as Massachusetts responds to growing concerns about the infrastructure needed to support artificial intelligence and other computing demands.

Under Executive Order 658, signed September 8, state permitting agencies cannot approve data center projects with peak electricity demand exceeding 25 megawatts unless developers demonstrate compliance with Massachusetts' Data Center Framework and submit a community benefits agreement developed with key local stakeholders. The agreement must also go through review by the state's Office of Environmental Justice and Equity.

The order requires qualifying data centers to address their effects on electricity supplies, water resources, air quality and public health. Developers are expected to procure or develop enough new clean electricity to support their operations rather than shifting additional energy costs onto other customers. Data centers unable to immediately secure sufficient clean electricity will be required to make payments into a newly created Ratepayer Protection Fund until they meet the requirement.

Massachusetts environmental officials must establish that payment system by the end of 2026. The state must also publish detailed guidance for municipalities evaluating data center proposals and working with developers by December 31. The state will develop protocols governing water, wastewater and stormwater impacts, while state agencies are prohibited from entering into nondisclosure agreements with data center developers, a provision intended to make the approval process more transparent.

The order follows Healey's June decision to pause applications for Massachusetts' data center sales and use tax exemption while her administration developed stronger safeguards. Communities including Lowell and Westfield have imposed temporary restrictions on new data center development amid concerns about energy use, pollution, noise and other local impacts.

Healey's administration says it wants Massachusetts to benefit from continued growth in artificial intelligence and computing while preventing data center development from increasing costs or creating disproportionate burdens for surrounding communities.
