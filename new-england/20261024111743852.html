🤖 Boston Dynamics Workers Push Back on ICE's Planned Use of Robot DogsEmployees Urge Massachusetts Company to Cancel Sale of Spot Robots 👇

PoliticsOctober 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 24, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Employees at Boston Dynamics are urging the Massachusetts robotics company to cancel a planned sale of its four-legged Spot robots to U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, raising questions inside the company about how its technology should be used by federal law enforcement.

Workers circulated an internal petition opposing the transaction after learning of ICE's interest in the robots. Boston Dynamics, based in Waltham, Massachusetts, is best known for Spot, a dog-like mobile robot capable of navigating stairs, rough terrain and locations that may be difficult or dangerous for people to enter.

The employee opposition does not mean the robots are autonomous weapons or that ICE has used them in immigration raids. The publicly reported information concerns ICE's planned acquisition of Boston Dynamics robots, and details about exactly how the agency intends to deploy them remain limited.

Employees opposing the deal have asked company leadership to "cancel the sale" and expressed concern about the possibility that Boston Dynamics technology could become part of immigration-enforcement operations. Their objections come amid heightened public scrutiny of ICE operations and broader debate within the technology industry over government uses of advanced robotics.

Boston Dynamics has previously established principles governing how its robots should be used. The company has publicly opposed weaponizing general-purpose robots and has said its products should not be used to harm or intimidate people. At the same time, Spot robots have been used by police, public-safety agencies and other government organizations for tasks including inspecting potentially dangerous locations while keeping personnel at a distance.

The dispute therefore centers not simply on whether law enforcement can use Boston Dynamics technology, but on whether employees believe this particular customer and potential application are consistent with the company's stated principles.

The situation also illustrates a growing challenge for technology companies: engineers who build advanced systems increasingly have opinions about who should be allowed to buy them and how those products should be deployed after they leave the company.
