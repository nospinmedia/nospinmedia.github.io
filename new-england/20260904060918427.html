🗳️ NH officials prepare for federal monitors ahead of Tuesday primaryScanlan says state is ready to prevent disruptions at the polls 👇

LegalSeptember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 4, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

New Hampshire election officials say they are prepared to protect normal voting operations during the September 8 state primary as federal election monitors head to Manchester and Nashua and questions continue about whether federal immigration agents could appear near polling locations. Secretary of State David Scanlan said September 3 that he does not expect U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement activity to interfere with voting and that state officials are prepared to respond if problems arise.

The distinction between the federal agencies is important. The U.S. Department of Justice has announced election monitoring in Manchester and Nashua, New Hampshire's two largest cities, as part of its enforcement of federal voting-rights laws. Those monitors are not ICE agents and are not being sent to conduct immigration enforcement. Federal election monitoring has been used by administrations of both parties to observe compliance with voting-rights protections. New Hampshire officials have been communicating with local election administrators about what to expect when the monitors arrive.

Separate concerns about ICE followed national comments from Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin about circumstances in which immigration officers could potentially be present near polling locations. Federal officials have said ICE is not planning operations targeting polling places, although they have not categorically ruled out officers responding to a specific threat or carrying out a particular law-enforcement action. Scanlan said New Hampshire election officials have received no indication that ICE plans to deploy at polling places for Tuesday's primary.

Under New Hampshire law, local election moderators have substantial authority to maintain order inside polling locations. Scanlan said the New Hampshire Attorney General's Office will also be prepared to respond quickly to complaints or disruptions on Election Day. State and local officials have been working through procedures ahead of the primary, including preparations in Manchester and Nashua.

Scanlan is forecasting statewide primary turnout of approximately 28%. His office projects about 145,751 Republican primary ballots and 103,024 Democratic primary ballots will be cast. New Hampshire's large population of undeclared voters can choose either party's ballot in the primary and then return to undeclared status after voting.
