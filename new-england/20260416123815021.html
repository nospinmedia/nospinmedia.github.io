🏥 CT Reviews Doctor Tied to Fatal SurgeryProbe follows Florida indictment 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthApril 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published April 16, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Health officials in Connecticut are reviewing the background of a surgeon indicted in Florida after authorities said he mistakenly removed a patient’s liver during a procedure that was intended to remove the spleen, a mistake prosecutors say led to the patient’s death.

Federal and state investigators in Florida allege the surgeon removed the wrong organ during the operation and later attempted to justify the procedure in medical records by describing the organ as "enlarged." Prosecutors say the patient died shortly after the surgery due to the critical nature of the error.

Court filings identify the physician as Dr. Thomas Shaknovsky, who now faces criminal charges that include manslaughter and falsifying medical records. Authorities say the investigation is continuing as prosecutors review medical documentation and testimony from surgical staff.

Officials with the Connecticut Department of Public Health said they are reviewing the physician’s professional history to determine whether he ever held privileges, contracts, or temporary assignments at hospitals in the state. Regulators routinely examine such cases to confirm whether physicians facing criminal allegations practiced in other jurisdictions where patients could have been affected.

Medical safety experts say "wrong-site" or incorrect-organ surgeries are rare but remain a persistent risk in complex procedures. Hospitals and surgical organizations typically require detailed verification steps before operations, including imaging reviews and team checklists designed to confirm the correct procedure and anatomy.

Authorities emphasized that there is currently no indication the surgeon performed procedures in Connecticut linked to the case, but the review will continue as part of routine licensing oversight.

The case has drawn national attention as another example of how medical errors can trigger both criminal investigations and broader discussions about patient safety protocols.
