📋 Brown Shooting Reviews Find Response Saved LivesReports recommend communication upgrades and crime center link 👇

EducationAugust 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 30, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Two independent after-action reviews of the December 13, 2025, mass shooting at Brown University concluded that first responders acted quickly to save lives but identified significant communication and coordination problems that officials say should be addressed before another major emergency. The reports, released by the City of Providence and Brown University, examined the response to the attack inside the Barus & Holley engineering building, where two students were killed and nine others were wounded.

A 76-page review prepared by the Police Executive Research Forum (PERF) for the city praised police, firefighters and emergency medical personnel for beginning victim evacuations within approximately eight minutes and completing them within about 13 minutes. At the same time, the report issued 18 recommendations, citing overloaded radio channels, inconsistent communications among responding agencies and difficulties tracking injured victims who were transported to hospitals by police vehicles instead of ambulances.

Brown University released a separate assessment conducted by Teneo Risk, along with a security infrastructure review by COSECURE. That review concluded the attack was highly unlikely to have been prevented based on the information available before the shooting but identified opportunities to improve emergency coordination, dispatch operations and access to surveillance technology. Among the university's planned changes is linking campus security cameras with the Providence Police Department's Real Time Crime Center through a controlled agreement that would allow authorized investigators faster access to video during critical incidents.

City and university officials said the recommendations are intended to strengthen emergency preparedness rather than assign blame for the response. Both organizations have committed to implementing operational improvements, expanding coordination with partner agencies and reviewing emergency communication procedures to better manage future large-scale incidents.
