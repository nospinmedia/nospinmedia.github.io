🗳️ Maine races unresolved after primaryRanked-choice count delays final results 👇

PoliticsJune 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published June 13, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Several key races in Maine’s June 2026 primary elections remain unresolved as officials begin the state’s ranked-choice voting tabulation process, delaying final outcomes in closely contested contests. Election officials confirmed that initial results released on June 11–12, 2026 did not produce majority winners in multiple races, triggering additional rounds of vote redistribution.

Under Maine law, candidates must secure more than 50% of the vote to win outright. In races where no candidate reaches that threshold, ballots are reallocated based on voter rankings until a majority is achieved. Officials said the tabulation process is being conducted centrally in Augusta and could take several days due to narrow margins in statewide and congressional contests.

The delayed results highlight both the intended function and ongoing debate surrounding ranked-choice voting. Supporters argue it ensures broader voter consensus, while critics point to slower result timelines and public confusion during extended counts. State officials emphasized the system is operating as designed.

The outcome of these races is expected to shape Maine’s gubernatorial and congressional landscape, with final certified results pending completion of all tabulation rounds.
