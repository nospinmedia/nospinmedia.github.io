⚖️ DOJ asks Supreme Court to lift Boston judge’s block on USPS ballot rulesEmergency fight moves from Massachusetts court to high court 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalSeptember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 4, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The Justice Department asked the U.S. Supreme Court on September 3 to lift a temporary order issued by a federal judge in Boston that prevents the U.S. Postal Service from enforcing new requirements for mail ballots. Solicitor General D. John Sauer filed the emergency application seeking to put the rules into effect as states begin sending ballots for the November 3 midterm elections.

U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani of the District of Massachusetts imposed the 14-day temporary restraining order on August 27, finding that the Postal Service rule likely conflicts with states' constitutional authority to administer elections and would be extremely difficult to implement so close to voting. The Justice Department appealed to the Boston-based 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, but then went directly to the Supreme Court without waiting for the appeals court to rule. Talwani held another hearing Thursday as she considers whether to issue a longer preliminary injunction.

The Postal Service rule grew out of an executive order signed by President Trump in March. It requires state and local election officials using mail ballots to provide USPS with information about mail voters and use agency-approved outbound and return envelopes containing unique barcodes. Ballot mail that does not meet the requirements could be rejected and returned to election offices. The administration argues that the requirements regulate use of the federal mail system rather than state election procedures and are intended to protect against voter fraud. The administration has not presented evidence of widespread mail-voting fraud.

The timing has become central to the Massachusetts litigation. At least two Wisconsin municipalities have already mailed ballots, North Carolina begins sending ballots to certain voters on September 4, and additional states follow later this month. Election officials challenging the rule say envelopes have already been printed and that implementing the new system now could disrupt legitimate voting. A senior Postal Service official said Thursday that the agency was still refining its Federal Ballot Mail Portal and expected it to become available for voluntary use sometime next week.

Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson, who handles emergency matters arising from the 1st Circuit, directed the states and voting-rights groups challenging the rule to respond to the administration's Supreme Court request by Tuesday morning. The high court previously intervened in an earlier stage of the dispute but did not decide whether the mail-ballot requirements themselves are legal.
