🏥 Baystate Health to resume gender-affirming care for minorsWestern Massachusetts system reverses February cutoff after federal funding threats 👇

HealthNovember 25, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 25, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Baystate Health will resume prescribing gender-affirming medications to patients under 19 in Western Massachusetts, reversing a decision earlier this year to stop the treatments amid threats from the Trump administration to withhold federal funding from hospitals providing such care to minors.

The Springfield-based health system said prescriptions for treatments including hormone therapy and puberty blockers will resume December 15. Baystate said its physicians and other medical providers helped determine that restarting the services was in the best interests of patients and their families.

Baystate stopped providing gender-affirming medications to patients under 19 in February 2026, although counseling remained available. The decision followed Trump administration actions threatening federal funding for healthcare institutions providing gender-affirming medical treatment to minors.

The cutoff forced affected families in Western Massachusetts to seek prescriptions and treatment elsewhere. Some moved their care to specialized providers, including TransHealth in Northampton.

Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell welcomed Baystate's reversal. Campbell said her office had worked with the health system for months to encourage restoration of the services and emphasized that federal law does not prohibit gender-affirming care for minors in Massachusetts.

The decision does not mean every family affected by the earlier cutoff plans to return. Some parents told New England Public Media that they remain concerned about the reliability of care after Baystate's earlier decision and intend to continue using alternative providers.

The dispute reflects a broader conflict between federal policy and Massachusetts policy over medical treatment for transgender minors. Baystate faced the possibility of federal financial consequences for providing the treatments while Massachusetts officials maintained that the care remained lawful in the state.

For Western Massachusetts families who rely on Baystate, the practical change begins December 15, when the health system says it will again prescribe the medications to eligible patients under 19.
