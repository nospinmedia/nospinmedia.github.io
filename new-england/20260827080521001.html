⚡ Maine selects major wind and transmission projects for Aroostook CountyRegional cost-sharing could limit Maine's share of grid expansion costs 👇

EconomyAugust 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 27, 2026 at 5:20 PM ET

The Maine Public Utilities Commission has selected Clearway Energy Group and Avangrid, the parent company of Central Maine Power, to enter negotiations for a major wind and transmission development aimed at connecting northern Maine more directly to the ISO New England electric grid. Regulators chose Clearway's proposed 800-megawatt onshore wind project in Aroostook County and Avangrid's proposed 1,200-megawatt transmission line running from Pittsfield to Haynesville.

The decision does not authorize immediate construction. The commission must still finalize contract terms, and the projects face extensive engineering, environmental review, permitting and federal approvals. Maine intends to purchase approximately 600 megawatts of Clearway's proposed generation, leaving about 200 megawatts available for other participating states. Because the transmission line is designed for at least 1,200 megawatts, roughly 400 megawatts of additional capacity could later support other northern Maine generation projects.

Commissioners said Avangrid's proposal stood out because it would rely heavily on existing transmission corridors where the company already controls nearly all necessary rights-of-way, potentially reducing permitting and siting complications. The regional procurement includes Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island and Vermont, and Maine regulators estimate Maine customers would be responsible for less than 11% of the transmission project's costs. Commission Chair Philip Bartlett said the combined projects are projected to produce approximately $2.4 billion in economic output, create about 2,500 construction jobs, and save Maine electric customers at least $387 million over the projects' operating lives.

The selection revives a northern Maine renewable energy effort authorized by state lawmakers in 2021 after an earlier transmission and wind proposal collapsed in 2023 amid rising costs and contracting difficulties. Clearway's wind farm cannot move forward without the new transmission infrastructure, making successful contract negotiations and permitting critical to both projects.
