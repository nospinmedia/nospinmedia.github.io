⚖️ Supreme Court won't hear challenge to Maine's 72-hour gun waiting periodDecision leaves the state's firearm purchase law in effect 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalOctober 20, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 20, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court has declined to hear a challenge to Maine's 72-hour waiting period for firearm purchases, leaving the state law in effect while the underlying legal fight continues.

The justices turned away an appeal from gun rights groups and firearm dealers seeking to block enforcement of the law while their constitutional challenge proceeds. The Supreme Court's action does not amount to a ruling that Maine's waiting-period law is constitutional. Instead, it leaves in place a lower-court decision allowing the law to remain enforceable during the litigation.

Maine's law requires most firearm buyers to wait 72 hours between agreeing to purchase a gun and taking possession of it. Lawmakers approved the measure following the October 2023 Lewiston mass shooting, in which 18 people were killed and 13 others were wounded.

Gun rights organizations and several firearm dealers challenged the waiting period, arguing that it violates the Second Amendment by forcing people who are legally entitled to purchase firearms to wait three days before receiving them. They sought a preliminary injunction that would have prevented Maine from enforcing the law while the case moved through the courts.

The state has defended the waiting period as a public-safety measure intended to reduce impulsive shootings and suicides. Supporters argue that a delay between purchasing and receiving a firearm can provide time for someone experiencing a crisis to reconsider an immediate act of violence or self-harm.

A federal judge initially blocked enforcement of the law, but the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals later allowed Maine to enforce it while the case proceeds. The challengers then asked the Supreme Court to intervene.

By declining that request, the justices leave the appeals court's decision in place without resolving the broader constitutional question. The lawsuit challenging Maine's waiting period can continue in the lower courts.
