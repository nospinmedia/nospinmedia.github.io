🧳 TSA considers allowing full water bottles through securityNo New England airports have been named for planned trials yet 👇

New EnglandSeptember 12, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 12, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Transportation Security Administration is considering allowing some TSA PreCheck passengers to carry full water bottles through airport security, but travelers across New England should continue following the existing liquid limits for now. Senior administration officials told CBS News that TSA could test the change at a limited number of airports in the coming months, although no participating airports have been announced.

The potential program would initially be limited to airports equipped with newer computed tomography, or CT, scanners and automated detection technology capable of distinguishing water from other liquids and identifying potential threats. TSA says the technology exists, but the agency does not currently have enough equipment or funding to deploy it nationwide.

That means travelers using major New England airports including Boston Logan International Airport, Manchester-Boston Regional Airport, Portland International Jetport, Bangor International Airport, Patrick Leahy Burlington International Airport, Rhode Island T.F. Green International Airport, Bradley International Airport and Tweed New Haven Airport should not assume the rules have changed. None has been publicly identified as part of the proposed water-bottle test.

For now, TSA's nationwide 3-1-1 liquids rule remains in effect. Most liquids, gels and aerosols in carry-on luggage must be in containers of 3.4 ounces or less, placed inside a single quart-sized clear bag. Empty reusable bottles can still pass through checkpoints and be filled afterward.

Some New England airports are already receiving other security upgrades. Bradley International Airport completed an expanded TSA checkpoint this week with two additional screening lanes and PreCheck Touchless ID, while Boston Logan already offers facial-comparison technology for eligible PreCheck travelers. Those upgrades, however, do not mean either airport has been selected for the proposed water-bottle change.
