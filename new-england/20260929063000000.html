🏛️ UMaine removes George Mitchell’s name from center and scholarshipsTrustees cite Epstein ties and concerns about public trust 👇

EducationSeptember 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 29, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The University of Maine System Board of Trustees voted 9-5 Monday to remove former U.S. Sen. George Mitchell's name from a university research center, fund and scholarship program following a review of his connections to Jeffrey Epstein. One trustee abstained from the vote.

The decision renames the George J. Mitchell Center for Sustainability Solutions as the University of Maine Center for Sustainability Solutions. Its associated fund will receive a similar name change, while the George J. Mitchell Peace Scholarship will become the University of Maine Peace Scholarship. The changes took effect immediately, although replacing signs, websites and other materials could take several months.

UMaine created a task force to review the names after Mitchell appeared more than 300 times in recently released Epstein-related files. The task force unanimously recommended removing his name under a university policy allowing names to be changed when continued association with an honoree could compromise public trust or damage the university's reputation.

University President Joan Ferrini-Mundy emphasized that Mitchell has not been convicted of a crime. She said the task force nevertheless concluded that his continued contact with Epstein after Epstein's 2008 conviction, along with allegations involving Mitchell, met the university's threshold for reconsidering the naming honors. Mitchell has denied allegations of wrongdoing connected to Epstein.

The decision prompted substantial debate among trustees. Some who opposed the recommendation expressed concern about removing honors based on allegations that have not resulted in findings of wrongdoing. Trustees supporting the change argued that the question before them was not whether Mitchell was guilty of misconduct, but whether keeping his name on university programs could damage public trust in the institution.

Mitchell, 93, represented Maine in the U.S. Senate and served as Senate majority leader before becoming internationally known for his diplomatic work in Northern Ireland. His name and likeness have also been removed from several other institutions following disclosures in the Epstein files.
