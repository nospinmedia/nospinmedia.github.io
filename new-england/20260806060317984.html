⚡ Vermont Regulators End Clean Heat Standard ProceedingRequired legislative approval never occurred 👇

EconomyAugust 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 6, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Vermont Public Utility Commission has formally closed the proceeding created to develop the state's proposed Clean Heat Standard, ending the regulatory process after lawmakers failed to hold the additional vote required before implementation. The commission issued its final order on January 30, 2026, dismissing the advisory groups that had spent roughly two years developing the program.

The Clean Heat Standard originated in the 2023 Affordable Heat Act, also known as Act 18, which lawmakers enacted over Governor Phil Scott's veto. It directed regulators to design a credit marketplace intended to reduce greenhouse gas emissions from heating buildings. Fossil-fuel wholesalers would have been required to earn or purchase credits by supporting measures such as weatherization, heat-pump installations and conversions away from heating oil or propane.

Act 18 did not automatically authorize the completed program to take effect. The law required the Vermont General Assembly to review the proposed rules and vote affirmatively before implementation. Lawmakers did not take that vote during the 2025 legislative session, leaving the commission without authorization to proceed.

The commission had previously concluded that a Clean Heat Standard was theoretically workable but was not well suited to Vermont because of its administrative complexity and uncertain costs. Its modeling projected that the program could increase residential heating-oil and propane prices by approximately 8 to 9 cents per gallon during its first year and between 56 and 65 cents per gallon by 2035, depending on how the credit system operated.

Closing the case also ended the work of the Technical Advisory Group and Equity Advisory Group, which advised regulators on emissions calculations, eligible clean-heat measures and impacts on lower-income residents. Vermont still faces binding emissions-reduction requirements under the 2020 Global Warming Solutions Act, including another statutory target in 2030, but the state will need to pursue those reductions through existing programs or a different policy approved by lawmakers.
