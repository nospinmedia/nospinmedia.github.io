🦠 Flu Season Takes Early Toll Across New EnglandDeaths Mount As Respiratory Illness Activity Rises Ahead Of Thanksgiving 👇

HealthNovember 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 21, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Flu and other respiratory illnesses are already causing deaths across New England as the region heads toward Thanksgiving, with federal and state data showing influenza activity increasing earlier in the season.

The U.S. CDC reports that Region 1, which includes New England, is already above baseline for influenza-like illness. National surveillance shows influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 is the dominant identified strain, accounting for 84.8% of subtyped influenza A viruses so far this season.

Massachusetts has recorded 21 flu-related deaths this season, including its first pediatric flu death. Five influenza deaths were reported during the week of Nov. 8-14, according to state data, while influenza-like illness activity has reached moderate levels.

In New Hampshire, health officials report four flu-related deaths, along with six deaths related to COVID-19 and one related to RSV. Respiratory illness remains low in most counties, but Rockingham County is reporting high activity and Hillsborough County is at moderate activity.

Connecticut has recorded at least six influenza deaths, while lab-confirmed flu cases exceeded 1,040 during the first two weeks of November, more than three times the roughly 335 recorded during the same period in 2025. The state has also recorded nine COVID-19-related deaths since September.

In Maine, officials reported three respiratory-virus deaths in Washington County amid influenza A, influenza B and COVID-19 outbreaks. The deaths involved one employee and two contract workers affiliated with Worcester Resources, although health officials said the illnesses were unrelated to workplace activities and had not determined which virus caused each death.

Vermont and Rhode Island are also monitoring flu, COVID-19 and RSV, but currently published figures do not provide directly comparable season-to-date death totals for this regional count.
