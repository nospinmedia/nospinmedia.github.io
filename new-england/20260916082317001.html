🏥 Healey proposes expanded postpartum care across Massachusetts$2 million plan would offer nurse home visits to every new family 👇

HealthSeptember 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 16, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey is proposing expanded postpartum care that would give every family with a newborn the opportunity to receive a free home visit from a maternal and child health nurse. The proposal follows renewed attention to postpartum mental health after the Lindsay Clancy murder trial ended in a mistrial earlier this month.

Healey plans to ask the Legislature for $2 million to expand the state's Welcome Family program, which currently provides voluntary nurse visits to about 3,000 families annually. Massachusetts has roughly 68,000 births each year. Participation would remain voluntary, but the expansion is intended to make a visit available to every new parent or caregiver in the state. Nurses can answer questions about feeding, sleep and newborn care, monitor postpartum health, screen for depression and other mental health concerns, provide referrals and follow up with families.

Healey is also proposing another $250,000 for the Massachusetts Child Psychiatry Access Program for Moms, which helps health care providers identify and treat mental health and substance-use concerns during pregnancy and after birth. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health has also been directed to update state regulations governing perinatal mental health screenings to improve early identification of mood and anxiety disorders. The funding requests are expected to be included in a supplemental budget and require legislative approval.

The announcement comes after Clancy's high-profile trial focused attention on postpartum psychosis. Clancy's attorneys argued that she was suffering from postpartum psychosis when she killed her three children in Duxbury in 2023 and could not be held criminally responsible. Prosecutors argued that evidence showed she understood that her actions were wrong. Jurors were unable to reach a unanimous verdict, resulting in a mistrial on September 4.

State data show broader maternal mental health concerns extend beyond that case. Mental illness, including substance-use disorder, accounted for 38% of pregnancy-related deaths in Massachusetts from 2019 through 2024, and the state's maternal mortality review committee determined that 85% of pregnancy-related deaths were preventable.
