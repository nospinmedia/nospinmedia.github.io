🏛️ Bipartisan Senators Seek Audit Of New Hampshire University SystemReview targets falling enrollment, rising costs and proposed leadership merger 👇

IllustrationExecutives review a major corporate merger agreement.Credit: AI-generated illustration (No Spin Media)
EducationAugust 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 16, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A bipartisan group of New Hampshire state senators is asking for a performance audit of the University System of New Hampshire (USNH) as lawmakers weigh declining enrollment, rising operating costs and a proposed consolidation of senior leadership. Senate President Sharon Carson joined Sens. Tim Lang, Sue Prentiss and Donovan Fenton in an August 12 request asking the Joint Legislative Performance Audit and Oversight Committee to direct the state's Legislative Budget and Audit Division to conduct the review.

The senators want auditors to examine USNH's organizational and staffing structure along with a proposal to combine the positions of USNH chancellor and University of New Hampshire president. The USNH Board of Trustees voted 20-5 in June to recommend combining the jobs beginning July 1, 2027, if lawmakers and the governor approve the change. Under the proposal, the presidents of Keene State College and Plymouth State University would report to the person holding the combined position, while the schools would retain their individual names, missions, presidents and accreditation.

The request comes amid significant financial pressure. Enrollment at UNH, Keene State and Plymouth State fell from 24,394 students in 2015 to 19,915 in 2024, an 18% decline, while system operating expenses rose from $811 million in 2016 to about $925 million in fiscal 2025. State appropriations are projected to fall from $95 million in fiscal 2025 to $87 million in 2026 and $77 million in 2027. Keene State has faced particularly steep enrollment losses and an operating deficit exceeding $3 million.

USNH already receives an annual external financial audit, but the senators are seeking a broader performance review focused on efficiency, staffing and whether structural changes could reduce costs without damaging educational services or the economies of communities such as Keene and Plymouth. System officials argue consolidation could eliminate an executive layer, streamline administrative work and give the smaller colleges greater access to UNH resources.
