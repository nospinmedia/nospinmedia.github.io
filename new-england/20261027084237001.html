🚗 Massachusetts installs nearly 90 wrong-way detection systemsHundreds more are planned at highway ramps across the state 👇

New EnglandOctober 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 27, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Massachusetts has installed nearly 90 wrong-way driver detection systems at highway ramps across the state, with transportation officials planning hundreds more as part of an effort to prevent potentially deadly head-on crashes.

The Massachusetts Department of Transportation is placing the systems at ramps identified as higher-risk locations for drivers mistakenly entering highways in the wrong direction. The technology is designed both to warn the driver immediately and to alert authorities when a wrong-way entry occurs.

The systems use detection equipment, including cameras and thermal technology, to recognize vehicles traveling against the intended direction of traffic. When a wrong-way vehicle is detected, flashing Wrong Way signs are activated in an attempt to get the driver to stop and turn around before reaching the highway.

At the same time, an electronic notification is sent to MassDOT's Highway Operations Center, allowing transportation officials and Massachusetts State Police to respond. The combination is intended to provide an immediate warning to the motorist while giving authorities earlier notice if the vehicle continues toward highway traffic.

Wrong-way crashes are relatively uncommon compared with other types of traffic collisions, but they can be particularly dangerous because vehicles may collide head-on while traveling at highway speeds. Massachusetts has experienced several fatal wrong-way crashes in recent years, increasing attention on technology that could prevent drivers from reaching active highway lanes.

The nearly 90 installations represent only part of the state's planned network. MassDOT officials say hundreds of additional locations are expected to receive detection systems as the program expands across Massachusetts highways and interchanges.

The technology does not physically prevent a vehicle from entering a ramp in the wrong direction, making rapid detection, driver warnings and notification of authorities central to the state's approach.
