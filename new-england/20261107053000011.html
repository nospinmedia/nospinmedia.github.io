⚖️ Healey puts Massachusetts abortion law into immediate effectOpponents' repeal effort will still go before voters in 2028 👇

HealthNovember 7, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 7, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey used an emergency provision on Friday, November 6, to put a recently passed abortion law into effect immediately, preventing a successful referendum petition from automatically suspending the measure while voters wait to decide its future.

Opponents of the law collected enough valid signatures to qualify a repeal question for the November 2028 statewide ballot. Under the normal Massachusetts referendum process, reaching that threshold would have suspended the challenged law until voters decided whether to keep or repeal it.

Healey instead signed an emergency preamble, a power available under the Massachusetts Constitution that allows legislation deemed necessary for the immediate preservation of public convenience to take effect without the usual delay. The action means the abortion law can remain in force while the referendum process continues. It does not remove the repeal question from the 2028 ballot or prevent voters from ultimately overturning the law.

The legislation, originally signed by Healey in August 2026, changes Massachusetts law governing abortion care, including circumstances involving serious medical complications later in pregnancy. It expands protections for patients and medical providers and allows physicians greater discretion in certain complex cases.

Supporters of the law argued that allowing it to remain suspended for approximately two years would delay protections they consider medically necessary. Opponents argued that collecting the required signatures should have triggered the suspension contemplated by the state's referendum process and criticized Healey's decision to use emergency authority.

The immediate dispute is therefore separate from the larger question Massachusetts voters will eventually decide. Healey's action determines when the law takes effect, while the 2028 referendum will determine whether the law remains in place afterward.

Until that vote occurs, the newly enacted provisions will remain Massachusetts law unless they are changed through another legislative or legal action.
