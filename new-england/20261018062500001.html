🗳️ Elizabeth Warren Campaigns With Troy Jackson in PortlandMaine Democrats Focus on Turnout With Election Day Just Over Two Weeks Away 👇

HealthOctober 18, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 18, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Massachusetts Sen. Elizabeth Warren joined Democratic U.S. Senate nominee Troy Jackson and other Maine candidates in Portland on Saturday as Democrats used a nationwide No Kings day of action to encourage voters to participate in the November midterm elections.

Warren spoke at Deering Oaks Park alongside Jackson and Democratic gubernatorial candidate Hannah Pingree. Organizers specifically billed the gathering as a "Defeat Collins Rally," reflecting its focus on Jackson's campaign against Republican Sen. Susan Collins.

Much of Warren's appearance focused on Maine's U.S. Senate race. Warren urged voters to support Jackson and criticized President Trump and congressional Republicans. Jackson and other speakers also addressed health care, heating costs, immigration, abortion and the war involving Iran.

Jackson, a fifth-generation logger from northern Maine and former president of the Maine Senate, became the Democratic nominee in July after the withdrawal of previous nominee Graham Platner. He is challenging Collins, who is seeking a sixth term in the Senate. The two candidates completed their fourth and final debate on Thursday.

Following the Portland rally, Warren and Jackson traveled to Kennebunk for a private town hall with Tiffany Muller, president of End Citizens United. The event focused on the role of money in politics and continued Warren's campaign appearances on Jackson's behalf.

The Portland gathering was more election-focused than earlier No Kings demonstrations, with organizers and candidates emphasizing voter participation ahead of Election Day on November 3.

The Maine Senate contest has attracted national attention because Republicans currently control the U.S. Senate 53-47, making competitive races including Maine important to determining which party controls the chamber after the midterms.
