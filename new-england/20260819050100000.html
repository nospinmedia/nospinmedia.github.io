🏫 Maine Launches First Recovery High SchoolFort Kent program combines education and recovery support 👇

EducationAugust 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 19, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Maine has launched its first recovery high school, creating a new educational option for teenagers recovering from substance use disorders while allowing them to continue earning a high school diploma. The Upper St. John Valley Recovery High School in Fort Kent began operating for the 2026-27 school year on the campus of the University of Maine at Fort Kent, combining academic instruction with structured recovery support in a specialized setting.

The pilot program is operated through MSAD 27 and the Valley Unified Education Service Center. It was established with a $616,000 grant from the Maine Recovery Council, funded through the state's opioid settlement funds. The school is designed to serve up to 14 students, including eight who live in university housing during the school week and several additional day students. Staff include certified educators, recovery professionals, and residential supervisors who support students both academically and personally.

Unlike a treatment or rehabilitation facility, the school is intended for students who have already entered recovery and need an environment that reduces exposure to the triggers they may encounter when returning immediately to a traditional high school. Students continue working toward graduation while participating in recovery-focused programming, life skills development, and family involvement designed to support long-term success.

Educators involved in the program say the goal is to improve graduation outcomes while helping students build stable recovery habits during a critical stage of adolescence. Organizers also hope the Fort Kent pilot can serve as a model for similar programs elsewhere in Maine if demand and results support future expansion.
