👮 Massachusetts State Police academy to resume recruit training in OctoberOutside evaluator will monitor 103 reforms for five years 👇

EducationSeptember 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 6, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Massachusetts State Police plans to resume recruit training at its New Braintree academy on October 26, with an outside consulting firm monitoring an overhaul launched after an independent review identified extensive problems with the academy's training environment. The department selected PFM Group Consulting LLC to serve as an independent evaluator as it implements 103 recommendations made by the International Association of Chiefs of Police.

The incoming 93rd Recruit Training Troop had originally been scheduled to begin in June, but Superintendent Colonel Geoffrey Noble delayed the class while the department addressed recommendations involving curriculum, instructional practices, leadership, trainee wellness and institutional accountability. A 10-member working group identified 31 priority recommendations, with 22 required to be completed before recruits arrive and work on the remaining nine required to be underway.

PFM Group Consulting is expected to provide independent oversight for five years, reviewing implementation of the recommendations and advising State Police leadership on whether the reforms are producing measurable improvements. The department says the changes include revised curriculum and policies, new civilian leadership positions and a more consistent and evidence-based approach to recruit training.

The overhaul follows the September 2024 death of 25-year-old Enrique Delgado-Garcia, who suffered fatal injuries during a boxing exercise at the academy. Four State Police academy staff members were subsequently indicted on charges including involuntary manslaughter. They have pleaded not guilty, and the charges remain allegations. State Police permanently ended boxing and head-strike training following Delgado-Garcia's death.

An independent assessment released in May 2026 described the academy's culture as highly disciplined but also excessively punitive and called for stronger leadership, improved instructor oversight, better tracking of recruit injuries and expanded wellness support. State Police responded by postponing the next recruit class until priority reforms could be implemented.

The October restart will provide the first major test of whether those changes alter recruit training and safety at the academy.
