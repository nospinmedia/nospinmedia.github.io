📜 Massachusetts Legislature Approves Bill Changing Post-24-Week Abortion Standard 👇

HealthAugust 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 2, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The Massachusetts Legislature has approved legislation that would change the legal standard governing abortions performed after 24 weeks of pregnancy, sending the measure to Governor Maura Healey for consideration. The bill would replace the current statutory criteria for post-24-week abortions with a standard allowing physicians to provide care based on their professional medical judgment. Supporters said the proposal is intended to give physicians greater flexibility when treating patients facing complex or medically challenging pregnancies.

Current Massachusetts law generally permits abortions after 24 weeks only when a physician determines the pregnancy involves a fatal fetal anomaly or when continuing the pregnancy would pose a serious risk to the patient's physical or mental health. Under the approved legislation, those specific statutory conditions would be replaced with a physician's good-faith professional judgment, while licensed healthcare providers would remain subject to applicable medical practice standards and oversight.

Supporters argued that medical decisions involving complicated pregnancies should be made by patients and their physicians rather than being narrowly defined by state statute. They said advances in medicine and the wide range of serious pregnancy complications require physicians to have flexibility when determining the most appropriate course of care for individual patients.

Opponents contended that removing the existing statutory language would significantly broaden access to abortions later in pregnancy and reduce legal safeguards established under prior law. The proposal generated extensive debate during the closing days of the Legislature's formal session, reflecting the continuing national divide over abortion policy following changes in federal constitutional protections.

The bill received final legislative approval on July 31, 2026, but it will not become law unless Governor Healey signs it or otherwise allows it to take effect. If enacted, the updated standard would apply statewide to physicians providing abortion care after the 24-week threshold.
