🏥 Brattleboro hospital reverses plan to close birthing centerBoard preserves southeastern Vermont's last hospital delivery unit 👇

HealthSeptember 12, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 12, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Brattleboro Memorial Hospital has reversed its plan to close its birthing center, preserving the last hospital-based labor and delivery unit in southeastern Vermont. The hospital's board voted to continue obstetric and birthing services after months of uncertainty, community organizing and warnings that the unit's planned closure would significantly reduce maternity-care access in the region.

A major change came on September 8, when Brattleboro Memorial learned it had been approved for funding through Vermont's federally supported Rural Health Transformation Program. The hospital said the money will help it explore new models for delivering obstetric and maternity care. The amount of funding had not yet been finalized when the reversal was announced.

Financial pressures remain substantial. Brattleboro Memorial reported an operating loss of about $16 million in fiscal year 2025. After staffing, billing and other operational changes, the hospital now projects a loss of approximately $9.5 million for fiscal year 2026. Its proposed 2027 budget submitted to the Green Mountain Care Board projects that deficit narrowing further to $7.1 million as part of a multi-year turnaround plan.

The birthing center had been scheduled to close by the end of 2026 after hospital leaders concluded that maintaining round-the-clock staffing for fewer than one birth per day was financially unsustainable. Physicians, midwives and community members responded with rallies and fundraising efforts. Supporters recently secured about $325,000 in pledged donations, while hospital staff developed plans aimed at improving efficiency through staffing changes, expanded appointment availability and updated billing practices.

Hospital leaders say keeping the center open does not eliminate its long-term financial challenges. Brattleboro Memorial plans to continue pursuing payment reforms and is examining models including Critical Access Hospital designation and closer integration with federally qualified health centers.
