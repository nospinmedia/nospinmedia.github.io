⚖️ Former Healey Aide Charged With Federal Food Assistance FraudProsecutors Say He Collected SNAP Benefits While Out on Bail for Drug and Gun Charges 👇

A former aide to Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey has been arrested on federal fraud charges after prosecutors accused him of obtaining government food assistance benefits by concealing financial resources while awaiting trial on separate drug and firearm charges.

LaMar Cook, 46, of Springfield, was arrested Wednesday, October 14, and charged with Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) fraud and wire fraud. He was released on personal recognizance following an appearance in federal court in Boston.

According to the U.S. Attorney's Office for Massachusetts, Cook applied for SNAP benefits on April 6, 2026, claiming he had no income, less than $100 in his bank account and a household consisting only of himself and his minor child.

Federal investigators allege those statements were false. Prosecutors say Cook had more than $8,800 in a bank account when he applied and failed to disclose that his fiancée and her mother also lived at the residence.

His fiancée allegedly earned more than $80,000 annually working for Springfield Public Schools.

Investigators also identified approximately $12,000 in transfers from Cook's mother, who had won about $1 million in the Massachusetts Lottery earlier in 2026.

Prosecutors say Cook received approximately $546 per month in SNAP benefits, which were used for purchases in Massachusetts and during a July vacation in Florida. Investigators allege his fiancée also used the benefits card.

Cook previously served as deputy director of Healey's Western Massachusetts office. He was fired in October 2025 following an investigation involving cocaine trafficking and firearm allegations.

At the time of his SNAP application, Cook was free on bail while facing state charges involving trafficking more than 200 grams of cocaine, conspiracy and firearm offenses. He has pleaded not guilty to those charges.

Separately, Massachusetts is pursuing recovery of approximately $22,374 in vacation payments that state officials say were mistakenly issued following his termination.

U.S. Attorney Leah Foley criticized the state's handling of the benefits, questioning how Cook allegedly obtained assistance despite the financial circumstances described in court filings.

The federal charges are allegations, and Cook is presumed innocent unless proven guilty. His former employment does not establish involvement by Governor Healey or her administration in the alleged fraud.
