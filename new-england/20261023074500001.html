⚖️ Massachusetts agrees to $10 million settlement over death of David Almond14-year-old died after DCF returned him to his father's care 👇

EducationOctober 23, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 23, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Massachusetts has agreed to a $10 million settlement over the death of David Almond, a 14-year-old autistic boy who died from starvation and neglect in Fall River after the state's child welfare agency returned him to his father's care. The proposed agreement still requires court approval.

David died on October 21, 2020, after emergency responders found him emaciated and unresponsive in his father's apartment. He was approximately 5 feet 10 inches tall and weighed about 80 pounds. The medical examiner determined that he died from malnutrition caused by starvation and neglect and ruled his death a homicide.

The Massachusetts Department of Children and Families had removed David and his triplet brothers from their father's care in 2017. The agency later returned David and one brother to their father, John Almond, despite earlier concerns about whether he could safely care for them. An investigation by the state's Office of the Child Advocate later described David's case as a multi-system failure involving DCF, Fall River Public Schools and the Juvenile Court.

The lawsuit brought by David's estate and his two surviving brothers alleged that DCF ignored warning signs, moved too quickly toward reunification and failed to adequately investigate reports of abuse and neglect. The Commonwealth disputed the allegations and argued, among other defenses, that state employees were not responsible for the criminal acts that ultimately caused David's death.

Under the proposed settlement, David's two surviving brothers, who are now adults and also have autism, would each receive about $2.9 million through special-needs trusts. Another $600,000 would establish a memorial fund supporting Massachusetts nonprofits and schools serving neurodivergent people, while $150,000 would establish an education and vocational scholarship prioritizing neurodivergent children in foster care. Attorneys' fees account for approximately $3.4 million of the settlement.

David's father and his girlfriend, Jaclyn Coleman, pleaded guilty in connection with his death and were sentenced to life in prison in 2023.
