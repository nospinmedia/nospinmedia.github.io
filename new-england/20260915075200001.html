🏥 Vermont orders hospital price cutsUVM Medical Center hit with 4.4% commercial rate reduction 👇

EducationSeptember 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 15, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Vermont health regulators have approved fiscal year 2027 budgets for all 14 hospitals in the state, imposing new limits on what most can collect from commercial insurers as part of an effort to slow rising health care costs. The steepest reduction went to the University of Vermont Medical Center, which was ordered to cut commercial reimbursement rates by 4.4% from fiscal year 2026 levels.

The Green Mountain Care Board approved the UVMMC budget 3-2 on September 14, going considerably further than its general guidance calling for a 1% reduction in commercial reimbursement rates. The board also limited the hospital's commercial revenue, with regulators arguing that Vermont's largest hospital has greater financial reserves and more ability to absorb reductions than smaller facilities.

Board member David Murman supported the deeper cut, citing UVMMC's reserves and financial position. Board Chair Owen Foster, however, voted against the decision and warned that imposing substantial reductions for a second consecutive year could eventually affect patient care. UVMMC President Dr. Stephen Leffler said the new order could contribute to a second consecutive annual loss of approximately $75 million, although the hospital said it would comply with the board's requirements.

The dispute reflects Vermont's broader struggle with health care affordability. Hospitals account for nearly half of health care spending in Vermont, compared with roughly one-third nationally. Regulators argue that restraining hospital prices is necessary to reduce pressure on insurance premiums paid by businesses and patients, while hospital leaders warn that aggressive cuts can threaten staffing, services and access to care.

Brattleboro Memorial Hospital also faces significant financial pressure. Regulators froze its budget at zero growth while allowing an exception involving prices intended to help preserve its birthing center, which hospital leaders recently committed to keeping open. Brattleboro projects a $7.1 million operating loss in fiscal 2027 as it continues a multi-year financial turnaround and must submit an updated budget this fall.
