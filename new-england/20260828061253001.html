🌲 USDA proposes ending Roadless Rule protections in White Mountains235,000 New Hampshire acres could face new forest management 👇

EnvironmentAugust 28, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 28, 2026 at 5:26 PM ET

The U.S. Department of Agriculture and the U.S. Forest Service have proposed repealing the 2001 Roadless Area Conservation Rule, a change that would remove nationwide restrictions on new road construction, road reconstruction and most commercial timber harvesting in designated backcountry areas. In New Hampshire, about 235,000 acres of the White Mountain National Forest could be affected, while another 25,000 acres in Vermont's Green Mountain National Forest would also lose the rule's protections if the proposal is finalized.

The Roadless Rule currently limits new development in inventoried roadless areas while allowing activities such as hiking, camping, hunting, fishing, wildfire response and certain forest management work. The USDA says repealing the rule would give local forest managers greater flexibility to address wildfire risks, improve forest health and make management decisions through individual forest plans rather than a single nationwide standard.

Conservation organizations strongly oppose the proposal, arguing it could open more remote forest areas to expanded logging and road construction. A coalition led by Earthjustice, Standing Trees and the Sierra Club has asked the Forest Service to extend the public comment period from 32 days to at least 120 days and hold additional public hearings. The groups argue the proposal could affect drinking water supplies, wildlife habitat and popular recreation areas, including portions of the Appalachian Trail, Mount Chocorua, Mount Moosilauke and the Kancamagus Highway corridor.

The proposed repeal is part of a broader nationwide review covering approximately 44.7 million acres of national forest land. The public comment period is currently scheduled to close on September 21, after which the Forest Service will review comments before deciding whether to finalize, modify or withdraw the proposal.
