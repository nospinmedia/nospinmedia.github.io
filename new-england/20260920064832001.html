🔥 New England heating oil nears $6 before winterMaine and Massachusetts prices are about 70% higher than last year 👇

EconomySeptember 20, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 20, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Heating oil prices are approaching $6 a gallon across parts of New England before the winter heating season has even begun, putting additional pressure on households that depend on oil for heat. Official state surveys show the average price reached $5.77 a gallon in Maine and $5.98 in Massachusetts as of September 14.

The year-over-year increases are particularly sharp. Maine officials say the statewide average is 74% higher than a year ago, while Massachusetts reports a 69.9% increase, from $3.52 to $5.98 a gallon. At Maine's current average, a 250-gallon delivery would cost about $1,443, compared with roughly $830 at last year's price — an increase of more than $600 for the same amount of fuel.

The pressure extends beyond those two states. Dealer price data show heating oil at roughly $5 or more per gallon throughout New England, including New Hampshire, Vermont, Connecticut and Rhode Island, although comparable September year-over-year official surveys are not yet available for every state. Federal weekly residential heating-oil surveys normally resume in October, making Maine and Massachusetts among the clearest current official measures of the regional increase.

Supply conditions add uncertainty heading into colder weather. U.S. Energy Information Administration data show New England held about 2.95 million barrels of distillate fuel oil in storage as of September 11. That's up from late-August levels but well below the roughly 4 million barrels the region held entering last October. Distillates include heating oil and diesel, meaning winter heating demand will compete with transportation and other uses for available supplies.

Maine is particularly exposed because a large share of households rely on heating oil, but prices near $6 are becoming a broader New England concern. Current prices are not a forecast of what residents will pay during the coldest months, and future costs will depend on crude oil markets, regional supplies, weather and global energy disruptions.
