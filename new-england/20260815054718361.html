🚆 Maine Wins $48 Million to Move Portland Amtrak StationNew mainline location aims to cut Downeaster travel times 👇

EnvironmentAugust 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 15, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Maine has secured a $48 million federal grant to relocate Portland's Amtrak Downeaster station, advancing a long-planned project designed to improve passenger rail service and eliminate a major operational bottleneck. The funding, awarded through the Federal Railroad Administration's Consolidated Rail Infrastructure and Safety Improvements (CRISI) Program, will allow the Northern New England Passenger Rail Authority (NNEPRA) and the Maine Department of Transportation to move the station from Thompson's Point to a new location along the CSX mainline east of Interstate 295.

The relocation will eliminate the current maneuver that requires northbound Downeaster trains to leave the mainline, travel to the existing station, and then backtrack before continuing toward Brunswick. Transportation officials estimate the change will reduce travel times by approximately 15 minutes, improve on-time performance, lower operating costs, and reduce conflicts with freight traffic. The operational improvements are also expected to create additional capacity for future passenger rail service along the Downeaster corridor.

NNEPRA Executive Director Patricia Quinn said the grant represents a major milestone after years of planning and positions Maine to modernize one of New England's busiest intercity passenger rail routes. Plans for the new station include high-level passenger platforms, improved accessibility, expanded parking, and stronger connections to local transit. The project is also intended to support continued ridership growth while making rail travel between Boston, Portland, and Brunswick faster and more reliable.

Although federal funding for construction has now been secured, additional engineering, environmental review, property acquisition, and local coordination remain before construction begins. Portland officials and NNEPRA continue discussing the preferred station location, balancing rail operations, neighborhood access, redevelopment opportunities, and nearby institutional concerns. State transportation officials expect design work to move forward as the project enters its next phase.
