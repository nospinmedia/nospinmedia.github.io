🏥 Massachusetts Legislature Approves Emergency Plan To Acquire Norwood Hospital SiteState Would Use Eminent Domain To Help Restore Hospital Services 👇

EnvironmentAugust 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 2, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The Massachusetts Legislature has approved emergency legislation authorizing the Commonwealth to acquire the former Norwood Hospital property through eminent domain, sending the measure to Governor Maura Healey for consideration. The proposal is intended to break years of legal and financial uncertainty surrounding the hospital following its closure after severe flooding in 2020 and the subsequent bankruptcy of Steward Health Care.

Under the legislation, the state would obtain the hospital property and related assets through the eminent domain process, allowing officials to resolve ownership complications that have delayed redevelopment. Lawmakers said the goal is to clear barriers created by bankruptcy proceedings and position the site for redevelopment as a full-service hospital serving residents of Norfolk County and surrounding communities.

If signed into law, the Executive Office of Health and Human Services would oversee the acquisition process and work to identify a qualified healthcare operator capable of completing the unfinished facility and restoring emergency, inpatient and other essential medical services. State officials said reopening a hospital in Norwood remains a priority because nearby healthcare systems have absorbed additional patient demand since the facility closed.

Norwood Hospital ceased operations after catastrophic flooding caused by a major storm in June 2020. Although reconstruction efforts began, financial difficulties involving Steward Health Care and related ownership issues left the project stalled. Community leaders, healthcare advocates and local officials have continued to press for a long-term solution, arguing that the loss of the hospital has reduced access to emergency care across the region.

The bill received final legislative approval during the closing days of the formal session but will not take effect unless Governor Healey signs it or otherwise allows it to become law. Supporters describe the measure as an extraordinary step to restore regional healthcare capacity after years of delays.
