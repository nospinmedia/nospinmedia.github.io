🏛️ Boston City Council subpoenas MBTA chief after officials skip hearingRare summons orders Phil Eng to answer questions about Blue Hill Avenue project 👇

EconomyOctober 22, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 22, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Boston City Council has voted to subpoena MBTA General Manager Phil Eng after transit officials failed to attend a council hearing about the proposed Blue Hill Avenue transportation project.

Councilors approved the unusual step on October 21, directing Eng to appear before the council and answer questions about the project. The action followed an October 15 hearing at which representatives from the MBTA had been expected to discuss the plan but did not attend.

The Blue Hill Avenue project would redesign a major transportation corridor running through Roxbury, Dorchester and Mattapan. Plans have included dedicated bus lanes and other changes intended to improve service for riders along one of the MBTA's busiest bus corridors. The proposal has also generated opposition and questions from residents and businesses concerned about traffic, parking, access and how the redesigned street would affect surrounding neighborhoods.

Councilors said the MBTA's absence prevented them from getting answers about those concerns. Because the MBTA is a state agency rather than a Boston city department, the council has limited direct authority over its operations, making the decision to use its subpoena power particularly unusual.

The council's action requires Eng to appear and provide testimony concerning the Blue Hill Avenue project. Councilors said they want information about the MBTA's plans, its coordination with the city and the agency's response to concerns raised by residents and businesses along the corridor.

The dispute comes as Boston and state transportation officials consider major changes intended to make bus travel faster and more reliable on Blue Hill Avenue. Supporters have argued that riders who depend on buses need faster and more dependable service, while critics have raised concerns about how proposed street changes could affect drivers, parking and neighborhood businesses.

The subpoena escalates what had been a policy disagreement into a formal confrontation between Boston's elected council and the state agency responsible for the region's public transportation system.
