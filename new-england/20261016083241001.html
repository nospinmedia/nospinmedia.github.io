🗳️ New Hampshire Takes Student ID Voting Dispute to Supreme CourtState Seeks Emergency Stay Before November 3 Midterm Election 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationOctober 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 16, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

New Hampshire has asked the U.S. Supreme Court to intervene in a dispute over whether election officials can consider student identification cards when verifying voters who lack one of the forms of photo ID specifically accepted under state law.

The emergency application was filed October 15, 2026, after the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals declined to stay a federal judge's order. The state wants the Supreme Court to pause that order before the November 3 midterm election.

The dispute stems from House Bill 323, signed by Gov. Kelly Ayotte in April and effective since June. The law removed student identification cards from the list of photo IDs that, by themselves, satisfy New Hampshire's identification requirement for obtaining a ballot. Previously, qualifying IDs issued by New Hampshire colleges and certain other educational institutions could be used for that purpose.

The federal court did not block HB 323 itself. Instead, U.S. District Judge Joseph Laplante partially blocked separate guidance issued by the Secretary of State's office that went further by telling election officials they could not consider student IDs at all.

Under Laplante's October 2 order, a student ID still cannot serve as one of the state's expressly accepted forms of photo identification. However, election officials may consider one along with other available evidence under existing procedures for voters who do not possess an accepted photo ID.

The Coalition for Open Democracy, which challenged the rules, argues that completely excluding student IDs places an unjustified burden on young voters. State officials argue they need clear and consistent identification standards and say the federal order changes procedures shortly before Election Day.

The Secretary of State's office has already updated its voter guidance to reflect the federal court order while the appeal continues. The Supreme Court had not ruled on New Hampshire's emergency request as of October 16.
