🚨 More than 130 evacuated after power failure at Lexington nursing homeMaintenance worker shocked as officials order entire building powered down 👇

HealthOctober 25, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 25, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

More than 130 residents were evacuated from a Lexington, Massachusetts, nursing home Saturday night after persistent electrical problems escalated into a major power failure, prompting fire officials to order the entire building shut down.

Emergency crews responded to CareOne at Lexington on Lowell Street after electrical problems that officials said had occurred intermittently for several days became severe enough to create unsafe conditions. Lexington Fire Chief Derek Sencabaugh said the decision was made to completely de-energize the building because electricity was needed to operate essential systems, including the facility's sewage system.

A maintenance worker who had been trying to address the electrical problem received an electrical shock and was taken to a hospital. Sencabaugh said the worker was expected to be OK. No injuries among the evacuated residents were reported.

The evacuation required a large, coordinated response because many of the residents needed assistance or specialized transportation. Ambulances and transport vehicles lined Lowell Street as residents were moved from the facility.

Most residents were transferred to other nursing and rehabilitation facilities through MassMAP, Massachusetts' long-term care mutual-aid system. A small number of residents who required acute medical care were transported to hospitals as a precaution, while some families picked up relatives directly.

Officials said the facility could not be safely occupied while the electrical system remained shut down. Engineers and other specialists were working to determine what caused the failure and what repairs would be necessary before residents could return.

The facility had previously been cited during a December 2025 fire-safety inspection for a deficiency involving its emergency power system. Records indicate that issue was subsequently corrected. Officials have not connected that earlier deficiency to Saturday's electrical failure, and the cause of the current problem remains under investigation.
