🗳️ Sununu and Pappas headline New Hampshire's November matchupsPrimary results set races for Senate, governor and U.S. House 👇

PoliticsSeptember 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 9, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

New Hampshire's September 8 primary settled the state's major November matchups, led by the open U.S. Senate race between Republican former Sen. John E. Sununu and Democratic Rep. Chris Pappas. The two won their respective primaries for the seat being vacated by retiring Democratic Sen. Jeanne Shaheen, setting up a Senate contest drawing national attention ahead of the midterm elections.

Sununu defeated a Republican field that included former Massachusetts Sen. Scott Brown, while Pappas prevailed over his Democratic challengers. Sununu previously represented New Hampshire in the Senate from 2003 to 2009 before losing to Shaheen in 2008. Pappas has represented New Hampshire's 1st Congressional District since 2019 and is leaving that House seat to run for the Senate.

The primary also set the November race for governor. Republican Gov. Kelly Ayotte won renomination and will face Democrat Cinde Warmington, a former Executive Council member. Ayotte is seeking a second term after winning the governor's office in 2024.

In the open 1st Congressional District, Democrat Stefany Shaheen, the daughter of Sen. Jeanne Shaheen, narrowly defeated Maura Sullivan, approximately 37% to 35%, in the Democratic primary. Shaheen will face Republican businessman Anthony DiLorenzo, who won his party's nomination. The seat is open because Pappas is running for the Senate.

In the 2nd Congressional District, Democratic Rep. Maggie Goodlander defeated primary challenger Paige Beauchemin and will face Republican Lily Tang Williams, who again secured the GOP nomination after also running for the seat in 2024.

The results mean New Hampshire voters now have their major-party nominees for the state's major federal races and the governor's office, shifting the focus from the primaries to the November 3 general election.
