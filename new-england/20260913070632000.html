🥬 Record lettuce-linked Cyclospora outbreak declared overNearly 13,000 sickened as FDA pushes new safeguards after outbreak 👇

File photoLogo of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA).Credit: U.S. Food and Drug Administration
New EnglandSeptember 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 13, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Federal health officials have declared the massive Cyclospora outbreak linked to iceberg lettuce over after 12,883 confirmed illnesses, 570 hospitalizations and two deaths. Cases were identified across 21 states, including Massachusetts, Maine and New Hampshire, making the final announcement particularly relevant to New England consumers who were among those affected. Both deaths associated with the outbreak occurred in Michigan.

The CDC declared the outbreak over on September 11, while the FDA said work to determine how the contamination occurred and prevent similar outbreaks is continuing. Epidemiological and traceback evidence linked the illnesses to iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico and supplied by Taylor Farms de Mexico. The company voluntarily removed its iceberg lettuce from the U.S. market on July 17, including lettuce supplied to Taco Bell and certain Marketside products sold through Walmart. The affected products have passed their expiration dates and should no longer be available to consumers.

The decline in illnesses following the recall was dramatic. At the outbreak's peak, more than 1,000 infections per day were occurring. By August, that figure had fallen to fewer than two infections per day, according to reporting based on federal outbreak data. New England cases were confirmed in Massachusetts, Maine and New Hampshire, while recalled lettuce was distributed more broadly throughout the region.

Cyclospora is a microscopic parasite that causes the intestinal illness cyclosporiasis, which can produce prolonged diarrhea, stomach cramps, nausea, fatigue and weight loss. Unlike many foodborne pathogens, Cyclospora is not effectively killed by standard chlorine-based sanitizers commonly used on fresh produce, making prevention at farms and packing facilities especially important.

Alongside the outbreak's conclusion, the FDA issued a letter to the produce industry calling for stronger prevention measures, including improved agricultural-water management, worker sanitation and controls intended to prevent human fecal contamination of fresh produce.
