📜 250-Year-Old Declaration Transcription Uncovered In MaineWells town clerk recorded the historic document in July 1776 👇

New EnglandAugust 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 17, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Officials in Wells, Maine, have uncovered a handwritten 1776 transcription of the Declaration of Independence preserved inside the town's historic records. Staff at the Wells Town Clerk's Office found the document while researching old municipal records, discovering that then-Town Clerk Nathaniel Wells had copied the Declaration into the official town record book in July 1776, the same month the Continental Congress adopted it.

At the time, Maine was still part of Massachusetts, which instructed communities to publicly read the Declaration and preserve it in local records. The Massachusetts Council distributed printed copies and directed town clerks to record the document. Wells' transcription, along with those original instructions, remained preserved in a town record book covering 1775 through 1810.

The discovery was not the result of a planned search for the Declaration. Town employees were examining historical materials when a question about whether Wells possessed such a record prompted them to look through the archives. The handwritten transcription was then identified among records that had been maintained by the town for roughly 250 years.

The document is a transcription rather than one of the original printed copies produced immediately after independence was declared. Those early printed versions were distributed throughout the colonies so the Declaration could be publicly announced and recorded. Maine did not become a separate state until 1820, explaining why the instructions preserved alongside the Wells copy came from Massachusetts authorities.

The discovery gives Wells a direct documentary connection to the moment news of American independence reached individual New England communities and was formally entered into their public records.
