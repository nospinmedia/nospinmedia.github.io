⚖️ Acton teen pleads not guilty to killing mother and brotherProsecutors detail alleged weapons as ChatGPT conversations draw scrutiny 👇

Arjun Aravind, a 17-year-old from Acton, Massachusetts, pleaded not guilty Tuesday to charges that he murdered his mother and younger brother inside their family home in August. A Middlesex County Superior Court judge ordered him held without bail by agreement of the prosecution and defense.

A grand jury indicted Aravind on October 1 on two counts of murder, along with assault charges and motor vehicle theft. The victims were his mother, Sudha Venkatesan, 45, and his brother, Siddharth Aravind, 14. Their bodies were discovered during a well-being check at the family's Martha Lane home on August 11.

The Office of the Chief Medical Examiner determined both died from blunt and sharp force injuries and mechanical asphyxiation consistent with strangulation. Prosecutors allege a heavily damaged and bloody laptop found near the scene was used during the attack. Court records also describe a bent, orange-handled kitchen utensil among the physical evidence investigators recovered.

The investigation began after a tutor scheduled to meet with Aravind's brother arrived at the house and found the doors locked and the home unusually quiet. The tutor contacted the boys' father, who was traveling outside Massachusetts, and he called 911 requesting a well-being check.

The case has drawn additional attention because of Aravind's alleged use of ChatGPT before the killings. Middlesex District Attorney Marian Ryan has said investigators found that Aravind used the internet and ChatGPT to develop theoretical or fantasy stories involving the deaths of family members. Authorities have not alleged that the AI system caused the killings, and fictional conversations alone do not establish criminal intent.

Investigators say Aravind showered, changed clothes and left the home in his mother's car after the deaths. Police located him early the following morning in Wayland and arrested him without incident. His next court appearance is scheduled for October 27.
