🎓 NH chair in 'segregated schools' disputeLeaked messages detailed 👇

EducationJanuary 18, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published January 18, 2026 at 5:58 AM ET

New Hampshire state Rep. Kristin Noble, a Republican who chairs the House Education Policy and Administration Committee, is at the center of a growing political firestorm after multiple news outlets reported on private messages attributed to her that appeared to endorse the idea of “segregated schools.” Reports describe messages in a private group chat in which a user labeled as Noble wrote that “when we have segregated schools we can add all the fun stuff lol,” and later added, “imagine the scores though if we had schools for them and some for us.” Critics say the language echoes deeply troubling ideas of separation, while Noble and her allies argue she meant political or ideological separation rather than race.

The leaked messages appear to come from an encrypted messaging group named “EdPolicy2026,” reportedly involving Republican members of the House education committee. Screenshots published by Granite Post News show another Republican lawmaker reacting with a laughing emoji to Noble’s suggestion. After the messages circulated, Noble’s office released a statement through the House Republican Office saying she did not intend to advocate racial segregation, framing her comments as reflecting political separation, criticizing what she characterized as “leftist indoctrination centers,” and suggesting that families should be able to choose their preferred schooling. Democrats in the legislature swiftly condemned the remarks and called on leadership to hold Noble accountable.

The controversy has drawn responses from educators and civil rights advocates as well as Democratic lawmakers who describe the leaked language as unacceptable coming from someone tasked with shaping education policy. A press statement from a state teachers’ union affiliate criticized the broader trend toward fragmentation in public education and warned against policies that could deepen inequities, describing the reported comments as alarming. The debate highlights wider tensions in New Hampshire over curriculum issues, ideological divisions, and the role of public education in a politically polarized environment.
