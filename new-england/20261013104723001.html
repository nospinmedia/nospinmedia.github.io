⚓ Pentagon Clears Way for Bath Iron Works to Build Second DestroyerFinal Construction Funding Still Requires Congressional Approval 👇

File photoU.S. Capitol building in Washington, D.C.Credit: Architect of the Capitol
EconomyOctober 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 13, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The U.S. Department of Defense has certified that Maine's Bath Iron Works has the production capacity to build a second Arleigh Burke-class guided-missile destroyer during the current fiscal year, clearing an important hurdle for additional Navy work at one of Maine's largest private employers.

The Pentagon's decision, announced October 12, 2026, confirms that the General Dynamics-owned shipyard has sufficient production capability to handle another destroyer. It does not, however, represent a final construction contract. Congress must still approve the necessary defense appropriations before funding for the additional ship can be finalized.

The certification follows efforts by members of Maine's congressional delegation to secure support for building two destroyers rather than one. Sen. Susan Collins, chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee, and Sen. Angus King were among those advocating for additional shipbuilding work at BIW.

The Pentagon has backed a plan that would provide funding for two destroyers, creating an opportunity for BIW to receive additional work once Congress completes the appropriations process. The certification establishes that the Maine shipyard is capable of taking on the additional vessel if lawmakers provide the money.

Bath Iron Works builds the Navy's DDG 51 Arleigh Burke-class destroyers, sophisticated surface warships designed for missions including air and missile defense, anti-submarine warfare and surface combat. The shipyard has been a major component of the Navy's destroyer construction program for decades.

The decision is also economically important for Maine. BIW employs more than 6,000 workers, making the shipyard one of the state's largest private employers. Its operations also support suppliers, contractors and other businesses throughout Maine and the broader New England maritime economy.

An additional destroyer would provide BIW with more long-term work as the Navy seeks to maintain and modernize its surface fleet. Ship construction stretches across several years, meaning procurement decisions can affect workforce planning and hiring well beyond the year in which Congress initially provides funding.

The next major step now rests with lawmakers. Until Congress passes and finalizes the necessary defense appropriations, BIW cannot treat the Pentagon's certification as a fully funded order for another ship.
