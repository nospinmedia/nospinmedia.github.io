🏥 4,000 Brigham nurses set date for open-ended strikeWalkout could begin Nov. 12 if contract talks fail 👇

HealthNovember 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 2, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

More than 4,000 nurses at Brigham and Women's Hospital in Boston have issued notice of an open-ended strike beginning November 12 at 7 a.m., escalating a contract dispute with Mass General Brigham that has continued for nearly a year.

The Massachusetts Nurses Association issued the required 10-day strike notice Sunday after members rejected the hospital's latest contract proposal on October 23. The two sides are scheduled to return to negotiations on November 9, leaving several days to reach an agreement before the planned walkout.

The dispute follows a one-day strike in July that became a five-day work stoppage after the hospital said contracts with replacement nurses required them to work for five days. Nurses later voted 2,986-215 in September to authorize an open-ended strike. A walkout scheduled for October was postponed while members considered what the hospital described as its last, best and final offer.

The union says its remaining priorities include wage increases, health insurance choices, limits on temporary nurses and compensation for nurses who are moved between assignments. The union argues those provisions would improve retention and continuity of patient care.

Brigham says the remaining unresolved issues involve compensation and benefits, including wages and health insurance, and says no patient or workplace safety proposals remain unresolved. The hospital says its latest offer provides competitive benefits and market-leading compensation. Management says it has contingency plans to continue providing care during a strike.

The hospital says newly hired nurses earn nearly $87,000 annually, with scheduled increases based on experience, while nurses at the top of the pay scale earn at least $220,000. The union argues an across-the-board cost-of-living increase remains necessary given inflation and Greater Boston's cost of living.

If it proceeds, the union says the action would be the largest open-ended nurses strike in Massachusetts history.
