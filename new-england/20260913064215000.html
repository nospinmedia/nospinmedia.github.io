🌧️ Flood watches expand as heavy rain targets southern New EnglandLocalized downpours could bring several inches and flash flooding 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 13, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Flood watches have expanded across parts of southern New England as forecasters warn that heavy Sunday downpours could overwhelm drainage systems and cause localized flash flooding. The National Weather Service says the greatest concern is across Rhode Island, southeastern Massachusetts and parts of Connecticut, with periods of torrential rain possible from early Sunday morning into the afternoon.

The NWS Boston/Norton office initially issued a Flood Watch for Rhode Island and southeastern Massachusetts before expanding the watch to additional areas, including southern Worcester County, Suffolk County and Windham County, Connecticut. A separate NWS office has Flood Watches in effect for portions of southern Connecticut. Forecasters say showers and embedded thunderstorms will be capable of producing intense rainfall over short periods, with urban areas, poor-drainage locations and low-lying roads particularly vulnerable.

Rainfall amounts will vary considerably depending on where the heaviest bands develop. WCVB meteorologists said a particularly heavy band could produce 2 to 3 inches or more in parts of Massachusetts. In southern Connecticut, the National Weather Service forecasts widespread totals around 1 to 2 inches, with localized amounts of 3 to 5 inches possible where repeated downpours occur. The precise location of the highest totals remains uncertain because narrow bands of heavy rain can affect relatively small areas.

The NWS says the flooding threat is more significant than the severe-thunderstorm threat with this system. Forecasters are monitoring a very low possibility of waterspouts or a weak tornado near the south coast, but excessive rainfall remains the primary concern. Motorists are advised not to drive through flooded roadways because water depth and damage beneath the surface can be difficult to judge.

Conditions are expected to improve as the system moves away, with lingering showers departing by early Monday and generally quieter weather returning to the region.
