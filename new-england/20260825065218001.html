⚖️ Transgender Defendant Challenges Illinois Jail TransferDefense says proposed placement threatens safety and medical recovery 👇

HealthAugust 25, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 25, 2026 at 2:37 PM ET

Attorneys for Blu Zeke Daly, a 26-year-old transgender woman from Manchester, New Hampshire, are opposing a proposal to transfer her to the Pulaski County Detention Center in southern Illinois while she awaits trial on federal charges stemming from the February shooting of a U.S. Border Patrol agent. In a court memorandum, defense attorneys Jeff Levin and Eric Wolpin argued that placing Daly in an all-male housing unit would put her safety at risk and jeopardize her ongoing recovery from a traumatic brain injury.

Daly is charged with attempted murder of a federal officer and assaulting a federal officer with a dangerous weapon. Prosecutors allege the encounter began during the early morning hours of February 23, 2026, after a Border Patrol agent encountered Daly near the Canadian border in northern New Hampshire. According to court documents, Daly fired at the agent, who returned fire, striking Daly in the head. Court records state she suffered catastrophic injuries, including the loss of a significant portion of her skull, and spent months receiving intensive medical treatment before being released under strict supervision for continued rehabilitation.

Federal prosecutors have asked that Daly be returned to detention pending trial, proposing placement at the Pulaski County facility, which houses federal detainees for the U.S. Marshals Service. Defense attorneys argue they identified court filings describing alleged failures to protect inmates from physical and sexual assault at the jail and contend the facility lacks the specialized resources needed for Daly's continued neurological, physical and speech rehabilitation. They also noted the jail's remote location would make access to advanced medical care more difficult.

The federal court will determine whether Daly should remain under supervised release while recovering in New Hampshire or be transferred into pretrial detention. The decision could affect both the conditions of her confinement and her continued medical treatment as the criminal case moves forward.
