🏠 Sen. Angus King proposes faster path through federal sourcing rules for affordable housingBipartisan bill would create database of pre-certified U.S.-made products as Maine projects face months-long delays 👇

EconomyOctober 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 2, 2026 at 6:31 PM ET

U.S. Sen. Angus King of Maine has introduced bipartisan legislation aimed at reducing delays and costs for affordable housing projects that must comply with federal requirements to use American-made construction materials.

King and Rep. John Curtis, R-Utah, introduced the Build American Efficiency Act on September 30. Rather than eliminating the federal Build America, Buy America Act requirements, the proposal would make it easier for housing developers to identify products that already meet those domestic sourcing standards.

The legislation would direct the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development to establish and maintain a centralized, publicly available database of construction products that have been verified as compliant with Build America, Buy America requirements. Developers could consult the database before purchasing materials instead of repeatedly determining whether individual products meet federal standards.

King's office says the current compliance process has become a significant obstacle for some affordable housing projects. Developers can seek waivers when compliant American-made products are unavailable or impractical, but King's office says some HUD waiver requests have taken as long as eight months to process.

The issue has had a direct impact in Maine. According to King's office, more than 400 affordable housing units in the state are currently stalled because of complications involving Build America, Buy America compliance and the availability of qualifying materials.

The underlying federal law is intended to ensure taxpayer-supported projects use domestically produced iron, steel, manufactured goods and construction materials. King's proposal would leave those requirements in place while attempting to make compliance faster and more predictable by identifying qualifying products in advance.

Supporters argue that a centralized database could prevent developers across the country from repeatedly performing the same product-verification work and reduce the need for lengthy waiver requests. The proposal would need approval from Congress before becoming law.
