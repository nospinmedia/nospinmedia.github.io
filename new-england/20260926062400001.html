🌊 Nor’easter raises coastal flood threat across MassachusettsWinds could gust to 60 mph as dangerous surf pounds shoreline 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 26, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 26, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A slow-moving nor’easter is bringing heavy rain, damaging winds and an extended coastal flooding threat to Massachusetts this weekend, with forecasters warning that persistent northeast winds could prevent water from fully draining between successive high tides.

The National Weather Service in Boston/Norton has issued a Coastal Flood Warning for parts of the eastern Massachusetts shoreline, including areas along the South Shore, Cape Cod and the Islands. Forecasters warn that multiple high-tide cycles could produce coastal flooding, particularly in vulnerable low-lying communities.

Strong northeast winds are also expected across eastern and southeastern Massachusetts. Gusts could approach 60 mph in some coastal areas, increasing the risk of downed trees, power lines and scattered power outages. Saturated ground and trees that still have much of their foliage could make them more vulnerable to the prolonged winds.

Along the immediate coast, large breaking waves and rough surf are expected to increase the potential for beach erosion and dangerous conditions near the water. Conditions farther offshore will be even more severe, with very rough seas expected south of Martha’s Vineyard and Nantucket.

The storm is expected to remain near southern New England through much of the weekend rather than quickly moving away. The prolonged combination of rain, rough seas, strong winds and repeated high tides is what makes this nor’easter particularly disruptive.

Residents near the shoreline should be prepared for flooded low-lying roads, beach erosion and difficult travel, while people throughout coastal Massachusetts should be ready for possible power outages.
