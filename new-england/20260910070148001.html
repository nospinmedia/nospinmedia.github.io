🍎 SNAP cuts threaten federal funding for free school meals across New EnglandMaine, Massachusetts and Vermont may face higher costs protecting universal programs 👇

EducationSeptember 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 10, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Federal SNAP eligibility changes are beginning to create a second potential consequence for families: reduced access to federal funding that helps schools provide free breakfast and lunch. The effect is especially relevant in Maine, Massachusetts and Vermont, three of nine states that currently provide universal free school meals to students.

More than 1 million U.S. children have lost access to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program since last year, according to Stateline. SNAP participation matters to schools because children receiving the benefit can be directly certified for free school meals without completing a separate household application. SNAP enrollment also helps determine how much federal reimbursement some schools receive through the Community Eligibility Provision, which allows schools serving large numbers of low-income students to provide meals to everyone at no charge.

Students in Maine, Massachusetts and Vermont are generally better protected from immediately losing meals because their states have committed funding for universal programs. But declining SNAP participation could mean those states receive less federal reimbursement and must contribute more money themselves to maintain the same benefits.

The financial pressure is particularly significant in Maine, which currently spends about $64 million annually supplementing federal school-meal reimbursements. More than 15,000 Mainers have lost SNAP coverage since last July, according to figures cited by Stateline. Maine education officials also confirm that SNAP and other assistance-program data are used for direct certification and for determining federal reimbursement under community eligibility.

Vermont similarly requires schools receiving state universal-meal funding to maximize available federal reimbursements through programs such as Community Eligibility or Provision 2. State law then supplements the remaining cost with state funds.

Losing SNAP does not automatically mean every affected child will lose free breakfast or lunch. Some children can still qualify through household income applications, while universal-meal states provide additional protection. The concern is that fewer SNAP-certified students could shift more of the cost from Washington to state budgets and potentially threaten universal programs elsewhere.
