⚖️ Judge orders release of 18-year-old after warrantless Maine immigration arrestCourt filings conflict over whether agents had a warrant during traffic stop 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalNovember 20, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 20, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

A federal judge ordered immigration authorities to release an 18-year-old arrested without a warrant during a traffic stop in Livermore Falls, Maine, after court filings raised questions about the government's justification for detaining him. U.S. District Judge Stacey Neumann issued the release order on Thursday, November 19.

The case began on Monday, November 16, when U.S. Border Patrol agents stopped a van carrying eight people in Livermore Falls and arrested six of them. According to an incident report from the arresting agent, the arrests were made without warrants. The 18-year-old was subsequently held in federal immigration custody while his attorneys challenged his detention.

Court records show the teenager has special immigrant status and no known criminal history. His attorneys argued that federal agents lacked the legal grounds necessary to arrest and continue detaining him without a warrant.

The case also produced a significant contradiction in the government's own records. Before Neumann ordered the teenager released, a government attorney asserted in a court filing that he was being detained "under the authority of a valid arrest warrant." The arresting Border Patrol agent's incident report, however, stated that the arrests during the traffic stop were warrantless.

Federal immigration law allows officers to make warrantless arrests in certain circumstances, including when statutory requirements concerning immigration status and the likelihood that a person could escape before a warrant can be obtained are satisfied. Neumann's order addressed the legality of this teenager's continued detention rather than broadly prohibiting warrantless immigration arrests.

The release also does not resolve the teenager's underlying immigration case. Federal authorities may continue immigration proceedings even though the court ordered him freed from custody.

The case is separate from the recent fatal Maine shooting involving an ICE agent that NSM previously covered. Here, the initial roadside arrests were conducted by U.S. Border Patrol, making the distinction between the agencies important even though the teenager later entered the broader federal immigration detention system.
