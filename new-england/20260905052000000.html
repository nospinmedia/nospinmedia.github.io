⚖️ Mistrial declared in Lindsay Clancy murder caseJury remains deadlocked after seven days of deliberations 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthSeptember 5, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 5, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A Massachusetts judge declared a mistrial Friday in the murder case against Lindsay Clancy after jurors failed to reach a unanimous decision on whether she was criminally responsible for killing her three young children in 2023. Plymouth Superior Court Judge William Sullivan ended the trial after the 12-person jury reported for a third time that it remained deadlocked following more than 36 hours of deliberations over seven days.

Clancy, a 36-year-old former labor and delivery nurse, was charged with first-degree murder in the deaths of her children — 5-year-old Cora, 3-year-old Dawson and 8-month-old Callan — at the family's home in Duxbury, Massachusetts, on January 24, 2023. Clancy did not dispute that she killed the children. Her attorneys argued that severe mental illness, including postpartum psychosis, left her unable to appreciate the wrongfulness of her actions and therefore not criminally responsible. Prosecutors argued that Clancy understood what she was doing and deliberately planned the killings.

The final day included an unsuccessful attempt by Clancy's defense to prevent the mistrial. Defense attorney Kevin Reddington had argued that one juror was refusing to follow Sullivan's instructions concerning reasonable doubt. After Sullivan declined to remove the juror, the defense sought emergency intervention from the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court. Justice Dalila Argaez Wendlandt declined to intervene, and Sullivan formally discharged the jury at approximately 2:22 p.m. Friday.

The mistrial does not resolve the charges against Clancy. Prosecutors have not yet announced whether they will seek another trial, and a hearing is scheduled for September 29 to address the case's next steps. Clancy remains hospitalized at a state psychiatric facility while the criminal case continues. The trial featured testimony from 85 witnesses and competing expert assessments of Clancy's mental condition at the time of the killings.
