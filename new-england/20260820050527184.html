⚖️ Federal judge pauses Vermont dairy plant shutdown80 St. Albans jobs at center of retaliation fight 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EconomyAugust 20, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 20, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A federal judge has temporarily blocked Dairy Farmers of America from completing the shutdown of its milk-processing plant in St. Albans, Vermont, preserving roughly 80 union jobs while a dispute over whether the closure was retaliation for a workers' strike moves through federal court. U.S. District Judge William K. Sessions III issued a five-day temporary restraining order on August 17, the same day DFA had planned to idle the facility, after Teamsters Local 597 filed a lawsuit challenging the shutdown.

The union alleges DFA decided to close the plant because employees participated in a nearly two-week strike in September 2025 over wages, hours and working conditions. Its court complaint says a janitor recently discovered what the union describes as an internal management memo listing "union decertification" as the leading reason for idling the facility and stating that the company needed to change course because of the strike. DFA strongly disputes that evidence, calling the document fabricated and saying it was neither legitimate nor written by the manager identified in it. The company maintains that the closure was unrelated to the strike and resulted from operational and financial concerns.

The dispute is especially significant for Franklin County, where dairy-related employers have already announced hundreds of job losses. The St. Albans facility processes milk into products including cream, condensed milk and powder, and has supplied Vermont companies including Ben & Jerry's and Cabot Creamery. DFA says Vermont milk will continue to be processed elsewhere in its network, while farmers and industry groups have warned that sending more milk out of state could increase transportation costs.

The fight intensified Wednesday when U.S. Sen. Bernie Sanders joined workers outside the plant and called on DFA to keep it operating. DFA President and CEO Dennis Rodenbaugh responded that continuing operations would impose significant financial losses on the cooperative's farmer-owners and said the plant is no longer economically viable. The federal court is scheduled to hold a hearing Thursday afternoon on whether the temporary halt should be extended while the union's grievances are resolved.
