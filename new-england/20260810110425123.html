🌾 Maine Awards $500,000 For Community GreenhousesTen projects aim to expand local food production and education 👇

EducationAugust 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 10, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Governor Janet Mills announced on August 7, 2026, that 10 Maine communities will receive a combined $500,000 in state grants to build or expand shared-use greenhouses designed to increase year-round food production, strengthen local food security, and provide agricultural education. The grants are part of the state's broader investment in expanding Maine's agricultural infrastructure and helping communities grow more food locally despite the state's short outdoor growing season.

The competitive grants will support projects across Maine, including the Alfond Youth and Community Center in Waterville, the Healthy Island Project in Stonington, Portland Community Squash, and Limestone Community School. Community organizations plan to use the greenhouses for student nutrition programs, educational workshops, year-round produce cultivation, and programs that improve access to fresh food for local residents.

State officials said the greenhouse initiative is part of a broader $60 million investment supporting Maine agriculture through infrastructure improvements, local food production, and workforce development. By extending the growing season, communities can produce fresh vegetables for a longer portion of the year while creating opportunities for students and volunteers to learn about farming, nutrition, and sustainable food systems.

Governor Mills said the grants are intended to strengthen local food resilience by helping communities produce more food closer to home and reducing dependence on long-distance supply chains. Officials expect the shared facilities to serve schools, nonprofit organizations, food pantries, and community groups while encouraging partnerships that expand access to locally grown produce throughout the year.
