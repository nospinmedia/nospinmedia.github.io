🗳️ Massachusetts voters renominate Ed Markey for SenateMoulton's challenge falls short 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 3, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Massachusetts voters have chosen Sen. Ed Markey as the Democratic nominee for the U.S. Senate, giving the longtime incumbent a decisive victory over Rep. Seth Moulton in one of New England's most closely watched primary races. With more than 99% of ballots counted, Markey received about 64.8% of the vote to 35.2% for Moulton, securing another statewide nomination ahead of November's general election.

Moulton, who represents Massachusetts' 6th Congressional District, built his campaign around the need for generational change within Democratic leadership. Markey, an 80-year-old senator first elected to the Senate in 2013 after decades in the House, emphasized his record on climate policy, consumer protection, and technology issues while receiving strong support from progressive organizations and national Democratic leaders.

The primary also reaffirmed support for other veteran members of Massachusetts' congressional delegation. Rep. Richard Neal won renomination in the 1st Congressional District, while Rep. Stephen Lynch secured another nomination in the 8th Congressional District. The results preserve the state's incumbent Democratic congressional lineup heading into the general election. Markey will now face Republican nominee John Deaton in November.

The race drew attention throughout New England as a test of whether Democratic voters would favor experienced incumbents or a younger generation of leadership. Instead, Massachusetts primary voters delivered a clear victory to the incumbent, reinforcing the state's long-standing Democratic political landscape as campaigns shift toward the general election.
