🚘 Six States Launch 3-Day Crackdown on Dangerous DrivingOperation Wicked Safe Targets Speeding, Distraction and Impaired Drivers 👇

PoliticsOctober 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 14, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Drivers across New England can expect an increased police presence this week as state law enforcement agencies launch a coordinated three-day crackdown on dangerous driving along major highways and cross-border routes.

The initiative, called Operation Wicked Safe, runs from Tuesday, October 13, through Thursday, October 15, 2026. State police agencies from Massachusetts, Connecticut, New Hampshire, Rhode Island, Vermont and New York are participating in the regional enforcement campaign.

Troopers will increase patrols and enforcement targeting behaviors linked to serious crashes, including excessive speeding, aggressive driving, distracted driving and driving under the influence. The operation covers passenger vehicles as well as commercial and other motor vehicles traveling through participating states.

The coordinated approach is intended to prevent dangerous drivers from simply crossing state lines and encountering different levels of enforcement. Police agencies will concentrate resources along interstate corridors and other heavily traveled routes connecting the participating states.

In Massachusetts, State Police identified several major highways as enforcement priorities, including Interstates 84, 90, 91, 93, 95 and 395, along with Route 146. Those roads carry significant commuter, commercial and interstate traffic and connect Massachusetts with neighboring states participating in the operation.

Authorities say motorists should expect to see more marked and unmarked police vehicles during the campaign. Enforcement will focus on observed violations rather than checkpoints, with troopers looking for speeding, unsafe lane changes, following too closely, cellphone use and signs of impaired driving.

Although the campaign is centered heavily in New England, New York State Police are also participating because of the state's major highway connections with Massachusetts, Connecticut and Vermont.

Officials described the operation as a high-visibility enforcement effort designed both to catch dangerous drivers and deter motorists from taking risks behind the wheel. Police are also reminding drivers to wear seat belts, put phones away and leave additional following distance as fall travel increases across the region.

The three-day campaign is scheduled to conclude Thursday, but participating agencies regularly conduct their own traffic enforcement outside the coordinated operation.

For drivers, the immediate message is straightforward: expect additional state police patrols throughout the region through Thursday, particularly on major interstate routes.
