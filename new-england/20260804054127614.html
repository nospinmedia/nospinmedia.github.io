🏘️ Stowe approves cap on short-term rentalsNew rules limit licenses and restrict transfers to protect housing 👇

EconomyAugust 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 4, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

After years of debate over housing affordability and tourism, Stowe, Vermont, has adopted a new ordinance placing limits on short-term rentals in an effort to preserve homes for full-time residents. The Stowe Selectboard voted to approve the measure during a late July 2026 meeting, establishing a cap of 850 residential short-term rental licenses while creating new rules governing future registrations and ownership transfers.

Town officials estimate roughly 1,450 short-term rental registrations currently exist, but about 600 are commercial lodging units or inactive registrations that are not counted toward the residential cap. Once the 850-license threshold is reached, new residential licenses generally will become available only as existing licenses expire or are relinquished. Under the ordinance, residential short-term rental licenses generally will not transfer automatically when a property is sold, requiring new owners to apply under the town's licensing process rather than inheriting the previous owner's authorization.

The ordinance includes exemptions for certain lodging properties and commercial resort developments, including Spruce Peak and the von Trapp Family Lodge, while allowing qualifying owner-occupied homesteads to continue operating under separate provisions. Existing non-resident operators must register eligible properties by September 15, 2026, to preserve their status under the new framework before the licensing cap is fully implemented.

Supporters said the ordinance is intended to slow the conversion of year-round housing into vacation rentals and help maintain Stowe's limited housing supply for residents and local workers. Opponents argued the restrictions could reduce property values, limit owners' ability to earn rental income and create uncertainty for future buyers. Selectboard member Beth Gadbois said the town sought to balance its tourism-based economy with the growing need for attainable housing.

The ordinance follows several years of public meetings, committee work and revisions as Stowe joined other resort communities across Vermont and the country in responding to the rapid growth of short-term rental platforms. Town officials said they will continue monitoring the program's effects and may consider future adjustments as the new licensing system is implemented.
