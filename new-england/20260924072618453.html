🎓 CCSU president resigns amid bullying allegationsToro to receive $124,000-plus leave payout and stay off campus 👇

EducationSeptember 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 24, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Central Connecticut State University President Zulma Toro has resigned following allegations that she bullied and intimidated employees, ending a nearly 10-year tenure leading the public university in New Britain.

Toro announced her resignation on September 23. Under a four-page separation agreement with the Connecticut State Colleges and Universities system, she will use accrued time off through October 1 and then formally retire. The agreement also states that Toro will not return to the CCSU campus.

CSCU spokesperson Samantha Norton said Toro will receive more than $124,000 for unused leave, including $83,887 for 436 hours of accrued vacation time and $40,653 for 842 hours of unused sick time. She will also receive a $4,346 healthcare lump-sum payment.

The resignation followed a complaint filed by Lisa Bigelow, president of the union representing CCSU administrative faculty. The complaint alleged a pattern of bullying, intimidation and verbal abuse, including an incident at an off-site leadership retreat in August where Toro was accused of yelling at an administrator who contradicted her and then berating other employees. Other allegations included requiring employees to perform personal tasks for Toro and her family. Toro has not publicly responded in detail to the allegations.

CSCU opened a formal investigation in August but said the investigation will not continue following Toro's resignation and retirement. Interim Chancellor Natalie Braswell said accepting the resignation avoids a "prolonged employment dispute" that would create "uncertainty and distraction for the university." No findings had been reached in the investigation before the agreement was signed.

Complaints about Toro's workplace behavior reportedly dated back to 2019. CSCU said an announcement about interim leadership will be made in the coming days.
