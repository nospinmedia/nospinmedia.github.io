🎣 NOAA Closes Gulf of Maine Trophy Bluefin FisheryRecreational harvest of tuna 73 inches or larger ends for 2026 👇

EducationAugust 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 6, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

NOAA Fisheries has closed the recreational trophy Atlantic bluefin tuna fishery in the Gulf of Maine for the remainder of 2026 after federal regulators determined that the area's trophy subquota had been reached and exceeded. The closure took effect at 11:30 p.m. on August 4 and applies through December 31, 2026.

Recreational fishermen aboard vessels holding an Atlantic Highly Migratory Species Angling permit or an HMS Charter/Headboat permit may no longer retain, possess or land large medium and giant Atlantic bluefin tuna measuring 73 inches curved fork length or greater while fishing in the Gulf of Maine trophy area. The regulated area covers Atlantic waters north of 42 degrees north latitude, including offshore fishing grounds extending from the Cape Cod region toward the Canadian border.

The action does not prohibit anglers from targeting smaller bluefin tuna. Recreational fishing for school, large school and small medium bluefin measuring 27 inches to under 73 inches remains open under NOAA's existing retention limits. Anglers may also continue to catch and release trophy-size bluefin, although vessels must report all retained fish and bluefin that are discarded dead within 24 hours of a landing or the end of a trip.

Commercial bluefin tuna fisheries are not affected by the recreational trophy closure and continue to operate under their separate quotas, permit categories and retention rules. The Gulf of Maine closure follows earlier 2026 shutdowns of trophy fisheries in the Southern, Gulf of America and Southern New England management areas. With the latest action, recreational retention of trophy-size Atlantic bluefin is now closed in every federally managed trophy area for the rest of the year.

NOAA said the closure is intended to prevent additional harvest beyond the Gulf of Maine trophy allocation while helping the United States remain within its overall Atlantic bluefin tuna quota.
