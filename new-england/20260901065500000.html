🦈 Swimmer injured in suspected shark bite off Martha's VineyardEdgartown closes South Beach as officials investigate 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 1, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Officials in Edgartown, Massachusetts, temporarily closed South Beach on August 31, 2026, after a swimmer suffered injuries consistent with a suspected shark bite near Norton Point. Emergency crews responded at approximately 1:40 p.m., where they treated a woman in her late 20s for serious injuries to her lower leg before transporting her to Martha's Vineyard Hospital. Authorities said her injuries were not believed to be life-threatening.

According to Edgartown officials, witnesses did not see the animal responsible for the injuries, but first responders and medical personnel determined the wounds were consistent with a possible shark bite. Following the incident, lifeguards, park rangers, and emergency personnel cleared swimmers from the water while officials assessed conditions and monitored the area for potential hazards.

Town Administrator James Hagerty said the beach closure was implemented out of an abundance of caution while safety officials evaluated the shoreline. Residents and visitors were urged to follow lifeguard instructions, pay attention to posted advisories, and remain alert while swimming in ocean waters. Authorities later continued monitoring the area as beach operations were reviewed.

Shark sightings and encounters are uncommon but not unprecedented around Martha's Vineyard and the surrounding waters of Cape Cod, particularly during late summer when seal populations and migrating fish attract larger marine predators. State and local officials routinely encourage swimmers to stay near lifeguards, avoid swimming alone, and leave the water immediately if marine wildlife is observed nearby.
