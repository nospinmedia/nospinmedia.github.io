🗳️ New Hampshire Legislative Primaries Hit 16-Year LowOnly 77 incumbents face intra-party challengers before Sept. 8 vote 👇

LegalAugust 5, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 5, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

New Hampshire is experiencing its lowest level of contested legislative primaries in at least 16 years, according to an analysis of official candidate filings released on August 4, 2026. Only 77 incumbent lawmakers seeking re-election will face primary challengers ahead of the September 8 state primary, well below the average of 111 contested incumbents recorded between 2010 and 2024.

The decline spans both chambers of the New Hampshire General Court, which includes the 400-member House of Representatives and the 24-member Senate. Of the contested incumbents, 61 are Republicans and 16 are Democrats. Election data also shows 113 open legislative seats where no incumbent filed for re-election, ensuring dozens of new lawmakers will join the Legislature regardless of the primary outcomes.

Political observers cite several possible factors behind the reduced number of primary challenges, including increasing party unity, political polarization, and the demands of serving in New Hampshire's citizen legislature, where lawmakers receive modest compensation while balancing outside careers and personal commitments. Despite fewer incumbent challenges, overall participation remains strong, with 791 Democratic and Republican candidates filing to run for legislative office statewide.

The September primary will determine each party's nominees for the November general election, when voters will decide control of the Legislature. While fewer contested primaries may reduce intra-party competition, open seats and closely divided districts are expected to remain key battlegrounds during the fall campaign.

Key facts: - 77 incumbents face primary challengers - 61 Republicans; 16 Democrats - 113 open legislative seats - 791 major-party candidates filed statewide - Primary election: September 8, 2026
