🛩️ Debris found from Boston-bound medical jet off NantucketCoast Guard continues search for six people aboard 👇

HealthOctober 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 4, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The U.S. Coast Guard has confirmed that debris found in the Atlantic Ocean south of Nantucket, Massachusetts, came from a medical transport jet that disappeared while carrying six people from Bermuda to Boston. Coast Guard crews are continuing an extensive air-and-sea search for the people who were aboard.

The Canadian-registered Gulfstream G100, registration C-GRJP, was operated by Ontario-based Latitude Air Ambulance and was headed to Boston Logan International Airport. The aircraft carried two pilots, two medical personnel, a patient and a family member. The four crew members were Canadian citizens, while the patient and family member were from Bermuda.

The aircraft lost contact early Saturday, October 3, after its crew reported an emergency while approaching New England. Air traffic recordings captured controllers attempting to communicate with the pilots, including asking whether the aircraft was "coming in blind." Flight-tracking information showed the jet rapidly losing altitude before contact was lost. Officials have not determined what caused the aircraft to go down.

Crews from Coast Guard Air Station Cape Cod located debris associated with the aircraft south of Nantucket after search teams covered more than 1,600 square miles. The response has included Coast Guard aircraft and surface vessels, including the cutter William Sparling, as crews continue searching the waters around the debris field.

The disappearance has a particularly strong New England connection because the medical flight was bringing a patient to Boston for treatment and vanished near the Massachusetts coast. Authorities in the United States, Canada and Bermuda are coordinating as the search continues and aviation investigators begin examining the circumstances surrounding the flight's final minutes.

As crews continue searching south of Nantucket, our thoughts are with the six people aboard and the families waiting for answers.
