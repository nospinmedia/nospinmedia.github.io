🏛️ South Portland Sends $88.6M Public Safety Bond To VotersNew Police And Fire Stations Head To November Ballot 👇

EnvironmentAugust 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 9, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

South Portland voters will decide on November 3, 2026, whether to authorize an $88.6 million bond for new police and central fire stations after the City Council voted 5-1 to place the proposal on the ballot. The project would replace facilities city officials say no longer meet modern building codes, public safety standards, or the operational needs of the city's police and fire departments.

The proposed police station would be built at 279 Main Street in Cash Corner, on the former Walgreens property purchased by the city, while a new Central Fire Station would replace the existing station at 684 Broadway. The current fire station dates to 1955, and the police station was built in 1967. City assessments identify recurring flooding, aging heating and ventilation systems, deteriorating exterior structures, limited space, accessibility problems, and inadequate facilities for modern policing and firefighting.

The project would be financed in phases, beginning with the police station and followed by the fire station. City financial estimates show an average single-family home valued at about $514,000 could see approximately $267 in additional annual property taxes during the first phase. Once financing for the second station is added, the estimated combined increase could reach roughly $534 per year beginning in fiscal 2030. Officials caution that the actual impact will depend on interest rates, construction costs, property values, and borrowing conditions when the bonds are issued.

Council members acknowledged concerns about the size of the investment but said voters should make the final decision. City officials argue continued repairs would not resolve the buildings' underlying structural and operational problems, while opponents and some residents have questioned whether taxpayers can absorb another large municipal expense. If voters reject the bond, the city would need to reconsider how to address the aging facilities and their mounting maintenance needs.
