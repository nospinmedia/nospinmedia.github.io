⚖️ Massachusetts cites addiction program $1.15 millionState alleges residents performed unpaid labor 👇

HealthSeptember 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 3, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell has issued $1.15 million in citations against Adult & Teen Challenge, Northeast, Inc. (TCNE), alleging the faith-based addiction recovery organization required program participants to perform unpaid work that violated state labor laws. The enforcement action includes more than $850,000 in restitution for affected workers and civil penalties tied to alleged wage and workplace violations.

According to the Attorney General's Office, investigators found that more than 200 participants at TCNE facilities in Brockton and Worcester were assigned to commercial jobs through the organization's Labor Source program between August 2017 and August 2025. Participants reportedly performed services including office cleaning, landscaping, furniture moving, snow removal, food preparation, fundraising, and other work for outside businesses without receiving minimum wage or other protections required under Massachusetts law.

State officials cited the organization for alleged violations of the Massachusetts Wage Act, minimum wage requirements, payroll recordkeeping rules, and the Earned Sick Time Law. The Attorney General's Office said vulnerable individuals seeking addiction treatment are entitled to the same workplace protections as other workers when performing commercial labor. The enforcement action follows a June 2026 determination by the Massachusetts Department of Labor Standards, which rejected TCNE's claim that participants were exempt from state wage laws because the work was part of a rehabilitation or religious program.

The citations represent one of the largest recent labor enforcement actions involving an addiction treatment provider in Massachusetts. The organization has the opportunity to contest the findings through the state's administrative process. If upheld, the restitution and penalties would compensate affected participants and reinforce state labor standards governing treatment programs that operate commercial work activities.
