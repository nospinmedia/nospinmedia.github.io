⚡ Winter electricity costs climbing across New EnglandConnecticut joins Maine as higher natural gas prices push 2027 supply rates up 👇

EconomyNovember 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 19, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Electricity supply costs are heading higher for customers across parts of New England this winter, with utilities in Connecticut and Maine setting or proposing higher 2027 rates as natural gas prices and regional fuel constraints put pressure on the power market.

In Connecticut, Eversource filed to raise its standard residential supply rate from 11.58 cents to 14.93 cents per kilowatt-hour, while United Illuminating proposed an increase from 11.94 cents to 15.69 cents per kWh. The rates would apply from January 1 through June 30, 2027, subject to regulatory review.

For a residential customer using about 700 kWh per month, the supply increase would add roughly $23 per month for Eversource and $26 for UI. Eversource has also separately requested a December adjustment to its public benefits charge that would add about another $6 per month for an average residential customer. That charge is separate from the January supply-rate increase.

Maine regulators have already approved higher 2027 Standard Offer rates. Central Maine Power's residential supply rate will increase from 12.72 to 13.63 cents per kWh for the 2027 calendar year. Versant Power's Maine Public District rate will rise from 14.88 to 15.34 cents per kWh; rates for Versant's Bangor Hydro District are established separately.

For an average CMP residential customer using about 550 kWh monthly, the supply increase works out to approximately $4.99 more per month, while an average Versant Maine Public District customer would pay about $2.30 more.

The increases are part of a broader regional pattern. Rhode Island Energy implemented a substantial winter supply-rate increase in October, while Massachusetts utilities have also entered their winter pricing periods with smaller changes.

New England electricity prices are closely tied to natural gas, which fuels much of the region's power generation. During cold weather, gas is also needed for heating, while limited pipeline capacity can tighten regional supplies and expose electricity customers to volatile fuel prices.

These changes primarily concern the electricity supply portion of bills and are separate from distribution, transmission and other charges.
