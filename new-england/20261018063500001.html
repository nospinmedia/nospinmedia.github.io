🗳️ 'No Kings' Events Spread Across New England Ahead of Midterms20 New Hampshire Gatherings Join Regional Push Focused on Voter Turnout 👇

PoliticsOctober 18, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 18, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Communities across New England took part in the latest nationwide No Kings day of action Saturday, with organizers shifting much of their focus from large demonstrations to smaller local events encouraging participation in the November 3 midterm elections.

New Hampshire alone had 20 scheduled events, according to a statewide event listing, including gatherings in Exeter, Dover, Concord, Nashua, Portsmouth, Keene, Littleton, Salem and Wolfeboro. In Exeter, participants marched from four locations before gathering outside the historic Exeter Town Hall. Concord demonstrators assembled outside the State House, while Dover's event was held at Weeks Crossing.

The day's national theme was "No Kings: Vote Early," although New Hampshire does not offer traditional early in-person voting. Granite State voters generally cast ballots on Election Day or qualify to vote absentee. Organizers there instead emphasized making voting plans and turning out on November 3.

Similar events took place throughout the rest of New England. Massachusetts organizers moved away from the large Boston Common rallies used during previous No Kings demonstrations and instead promoted hundreds of smaller community-led activities around the start of the state's early-voting period. Events were held in Boston-area communities and elsewhere across the state.

Connecticut also saw gatherings across the state, including approximately 800 people outside the State Capitol in Hartford, along with events in Stamford, Waterbury and other communities. Rhode Island organizers scheduled at least 10 events, including a voter-outreach effort at Providence's Hope Street Farmers Market. Vermont organizers listed about a dozen activities in communities including Burlington, Winooski, Montpelier and Brattleboro. Maine events included gatherings in Portland and Rockland.

Nationally, organizers held more than 2,500 events across all 50 states and Washington, D.C., according to reporting on the coordinated day of action. Organizers described the October 17 effort as focused on voter participation, while the broader No Kings movement has been associated with opposition to President Trump and his administration's policies.
