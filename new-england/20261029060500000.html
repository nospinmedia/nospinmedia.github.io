🚔 New Hampshire fugitive captured in Lowell after nearly a yearSearch had prompted shelter-in-place order in Salem 👇

EconomyOctober 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 29, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A New Hampshire fugitive who had been on escape status for nearly a year was captured Wednesday in Lowell, Massachusetts, ending a search that prompted authorities to issue a shelter-in-place order in Salem, New Hampshire, the previous night.

Daquhan Cromer, 29, was taken into custody around 12:15 p.m. October 28 on Bowden Street in Lowell after investigators developed information about his location. Massachusetts State Police and the U.S. Marshals Service participated in the arrest along with local and other law enforcement agencies.

Cromer had been on escape status since December 1, 2025, when the New Hampshire Department of Corrections says he left the Calumet Transitional Housing Unit in Manchester. He had been serving a sentence for simple assault.

The search intensified Tuesday after authorities tracked Cromer to Salem. Police said he fled when officers attempted to apprehend him, leading officials to warn residents in part of the community to shelter in place because Cromer was considered armed and dangerous. The order was later lifted after investigators concluded he had left the area.

Authorities said Cromer was also wanted in Massachusetts. Methuen police had a warrant accusing him of assault and battery on a child under 14, while Lowell police had warrants involving assault with a dangerous weapon and other alleged offenses. Those allegations are separate from his New Hampshire escape case.

After his arrest, Cromer appeared in Lowell District Court on Massachusetts charges and as a fugitive from justice. The U.S. Marshals Service said he was ordered held without bail pending a dangerousness hearing scheduled for November 2. New Hampshire authorities are seeking his return to the state.
