🔥 Mills asks Congress for more heating aid as Maine oil prices surgeAverage price is $5.96 a gallon, nearly 80% higher than last year 👇

File photoU.S. Capitol building in Washington, D.C.Credit: Architect of the Capitol
EconomyOctober 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 3, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Maine Gov. Janet Mills is asking Congress for additional federal heating assistance as the state heads toward winter with heating oil prices nearly 80% higher than a year ago. In an October 2 letter to Maine's congressional delegation, Mills called for supplemental funding for the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP), arguing that money already appropriated for the program does not reflect the sharp increase in fuel costs.

The latest Maine Department of Energy Resources survey puts the statewide average heating oil price at $5.96 per gallon, down slightly from the record $6.02 reached the previous week but far above the $3.33 average at the same time last year. Current prices range from $5.00 to $6.30 per gallon across the state. At the statewide average, filling a standard 275-gallon tank costs about $1,639, roughly $725 more than a year ago.

The impact is particularly significant in Maine because approximately half of the state's homes use heating oil as their primary source of heat, the highest share in the country. Mills told Maine's congressional delegation that existing LIHEAP funding is insufficient to address the increased need created by higher energy prices and urged lawmakers to approve additional money before winter.

Mills also urged eligible residents to apply early for Maine's Home Energy Assistance Program, which is supported by federal LIHEAP funding. Applications opened in August and will continue until available funds are exhausted. The state has also released its 2026-2027 Winter Heating Guide, which includes information about heating assistance, weatherization and ways households can reduce energy costs.

The request comes one day after Maine announced that eligible low-income households will begin receiving automatic electricity discounts through the state's new SupportLink initiative. Up to 67,000 households could receive assistance under that separate program.
