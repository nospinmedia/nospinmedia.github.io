📜 Vermont Prioritizes Local News For State AdvertisingExecutive order gives preference to Vermont media and expands spending transparency 👇

File photoThe White House in Washington, D.C.Credit: Public domain
EconomyAugust 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 11, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Vermont Governor Phil Scott has signed Executive Order 06-26, directing state agencies to give preference to Vermont-based newspapers, digital news outlets, radio stations, and television broadcasters when purchasing advertising and public information campaigns whenever feasible and in the state's best interests. State officials said the policy is intended to strengthen local journalism while helping agencies communicate more effectively with Vermonters through trusted community media.

The executive order also requires the state to improve transparency by tracking where advertising dollars are spent and reporting those expenditures. Vermont typically spends between $4 million and $5 million each year on advertising covered by the order, although officials said they have not previously maintained a centralized accounting of how much reaches local news organizations. Advertising intended to attract out-of-state tourists is exempt because those campaigns target audiences outside Vermont.

The initiative was developed with input from the Vermont Journalism Coalition and the Vermont Association of Broadcasters after legislation addressing state advertising preferences stalled in the Legislature earlier this year. Supporters say the policy redirects existing advertising budgets rather than creating a new subsidy and could help sustain community news organizations that have faced years of declining advertising revenue.

Governor Scott said the executive order reflects Vermont's longstanding "buy local" philosophy and recognizes the role local news organizations play in keeping residents informed, particularly in rural communities. Supporters also noted that stronger local journalism can improve public awareness of state programs, emergency information, and other government services while increasing transparency around public advertising spending.
