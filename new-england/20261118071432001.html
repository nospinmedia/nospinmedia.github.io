💻 Wave of major tech outages raises concerns in New HampshireOfficials say six major disruptions show no evidence of coordinated cyberattack 👇

IllustrationCybersecurity teams responding to a large-scale system breach.Credit: AI-generated illustration (No Spin Media)
HealthNovember 18, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 18, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A rapid series of technology failures affecting airlines, banks, government networks and online services has raised concerns in New Hampshire and across the country, but senior national security officials say there is no evidence the six major disruptions were part of a coordinated cyberattack.

The string began Thursday, November 12, when Microsoft 365 experienced an outage attributed to a network capacity upgrade. In New Hampshire, the disruption temporarily left some Lakes Region town halls without email.

On Friday, November 13, a network upgrade involving Juniper routers triggered a roughly 4.5-hour nationwide FAA ground stop, stranding travelers in New Hampshire and elsewhere. On Monday, November 16, an internal software problem at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects involving other banks and Zelle, while a separate internal systems outage at United Airlines delayed roughly 150 flights.

Two more disruptions followed on Tuesday, November 17. The National Security Agency's classified internal communications network went offline during routine maintenance, while an internal update disrupted several Apple services, including iCloud, Apple Pay and the App Store.

Secretary of State and national security adviser Marco Rubio, speaking to reporters in Manila, Philippines, said officials had "zero evidence" that the incidents were connected or caused by hackers or foreign governments. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton and Senate Intelligence Committee Chair Mark Warner also said there was no evidence of a coordinated cyberattack. Officials have pointed instead to organizations rapidly updating and patching complex systems, including legacy technology, as vulnerabilities are discovered more quickly.

New Hampshire has also experienced an unrelated cyber incident. Monadnock Community Hospital in Peterborough reported a weeklong network outage that forced some elective surgeries to be postponed through November 23.
