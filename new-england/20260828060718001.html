⚖️ ACLU sues NH State Police over delayed ICE recordsLawsuit challenges 14-month wait for public documents 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EconomyAugust 28, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 28, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The ACLU of New Hampshire has filed a lawsuit against the New Hampshire Division of State Police, alleging the agency violated the state's Right-to-Know Law (RSA 91-A) by delaying the release of public records related to its partnership with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). Filed in Merrimack County Superior Court, the lawsuit seeks a court order requiring State Police to promptly produce records concerning the agency's participation in the federal Section 287(g) program, which authorizes specially trained state and local officers to perform certain immigration enforcement functions under ICE supervision.

According to the complaint, the ACLU submitted its records request on September 12, 2025, seeking documents including officer training materials, enforcement policies, demographic stop data, arrest statistics, detention records and information about any federal compensation connected to the program. State Police responded that the records would not be available until November 24, 2026, a delay of roughly 14 months. The ACLU argues that such a lengthy timeline is inconsistent with New Hampshire's public records law, which generally requires agencies to respond to requests within five business days, while permitting additional time when reasonably necessary.

The lawsuit notes that several other New Hampshire law enforcement agencies participating in the 287(g) program have already produced similar records. ACLU of New Hampshire Legal Director Gilles Bissonnette said the organization is seeking greater public transparency about how the program operates, how often it is used and what policies guide state troopers exercising federal immigration authority. The complaint does not challenge the legality of the 287(g) program itself but instead focuses on public access to government records.

The New Hampshire State Police joined the 287(g) program in April 2025. The court will determine whether the agency's response complied with RSA 91-A and whether additional disclosure should be ordered. The State Police had not publicly responded to the lawsuit at the time it was filed.
