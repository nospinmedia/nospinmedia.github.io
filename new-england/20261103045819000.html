🛩️ Recorder recovered from jet that crashed off NantucketInvestigators will examine cockpit audio for clues 👇

HealthNovember 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 3, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Investigators have recovered the cockpit voice recorder and part of the cockpit from a medical transport jet that crashed into the Atlantic Ocean south of Nantucket, a significant step toward determining what happened before the aircraft went down with six people aboard.

The Latitude Air Ambulance Gulfstream G100 departed Bermuda on October 3 and was flying to Boston when its crew reported electrical difficulties. The aircraft subsequently descended rapidly and lost contact with air traffic control about 13 nautical miles south of Nantucket, according to the Transportation Safety Board of Canada.

The Transportation Safety Board of Canada, working with the Royal Canadian Mounted Police and Canadian Coast Guard, said crews recovered the upper third of the aircraft's cockpit along with the voice recorder. Both are being transported to the agency's engineering laboratory in Ottawa for examination.

Four Canadian air ambulance crew members and two people from Bermuda were aboard the aircraft. The crew members were Philip Gregory Childerhose, Thomas Henry Farquhar, Sarah Jane Paton and Jolene Beth Weir. The two Bermudians were Marilyn Lavonne Bean, 66, and her son Sergio Wayne Lottimore, 41. All six are presumed dead.

The U.S. Coast Guard searched more than 4,400 square miles over 66 hours after the aircraft disappeared before suspending its search for survivors. A debris field associated with the jet was located south of Nantucket.

Investigators have not determined what caused the crash. The crew's report of electrical difficulties is part of the investigation, but officials have not concluded that an electrical failure caused the aircraft to go down. The recovered recorder could provide investigators with cockpit conversations and sounds from the aircraft's final period of flight.
