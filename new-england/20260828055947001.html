⚖️ Massachusetts leads lawsuit over new Title X grant conditionsAll six New England states join challenge to HHS funding rules 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalAugust 28, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 28, 2026 at 11:40 AM ET

Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell joined a coalition of 23 states in filing a federal lawsuit challenging new U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) conditions attached to the Title X family planning program. The lawsuit, filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Maryland, argues the new funding requirements exceed the federal government's legal authority and threaten access to reproductive health services. Every New England state, including Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Connecticut, and Rhode Island, joined the legal challenge.

Title X is the federal government's family planning program, providing grants that support contraception, cancer screenings, preventive care and related health services for low-income and underserved patients. The coalition argues that HHS imposed new grant conditions that conflict with the program's statutory purpose and place funding at risk for states and healthcare providers that do not comply with the revised federal requirements. The complaint alleges the agency adopted the new conditions without following required administrative procedures and exceeded authority granted by Congress.

According to Massachusetts officials, the state receives more than $6.5 million annually through Title X. Approximately $5.5 million supports programs administered by the Massachusetts Department of Public Health, serving about 100,000 patients, while nearly $1 million is provided to Action for Boston Community Development, which serves roughly 27,000 clients each year. State officials argue that losing or restricting those funds could reduce access to preventive healthcare services across the Commonwealth.

The lawsuit alleges the revised funding conditions violate the Administrative Procedure Act and the Spending Clause of the U.S. Constitution by attaching unlawful conditions to congressionally authorized grants. HHS has not yet filed its formal response in court. The case is expected to become another closely watched legal challenge over the scope of federal authority to set conditions on public health funding.
