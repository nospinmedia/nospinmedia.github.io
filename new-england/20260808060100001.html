🗳️ Vermont Reports Strong Campaign Finance Compliance Before PrimaryState says 95% of candidates registered ahead of election 👇

EconomyAugust 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 8, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The Vermont Secretary of State's Office released its second Campaign Finance Transparency Report of the 2026 election cycle on August 7, showing that 95% of non-federal candidates had registered in the state's campaign finance reporting system ahead of the upcoming primary election. The report is intended to help voters evaluate campaign finance compliance before ballots are cast while identifying offices where additional filings are still required.

According to Secretary of State Sarah Copeland Hanzas, every statewide candidate successfully met the registration requirement, resulting in a 100% compliance rate for those races. Registration rates varied among other offices, however, with legislative and local contests generally showing high participation while county-level offices recorded a lower compliance rate of approximately 71%. Vermont law requires candidates seeking state and local office to register and file campaign finance disclosures under 17 V.S.A. Chapter 61 before appearing on the ballot.

The transparency report tracks candidate registration and disclosure activity throughout the election cycle, providing voters with information about campaign fundraising and spending before the primary. State election officials said the reports are designed to encourage timely compliance, improve public access to campaign finance information, and promote confidence in Vermont's election process. Candidates who have not yet completed required filings are encouraged to do so promptly to remain in compliance with state law.

The Secretary of State's Office plans to continue publishing campaign finance updates throughout the 2026 election cycle as additional disclosure deadlines approach. Officials said regular reporting allows the public to monitor compliance across all levels of state government while giving campaigns an opportunity to correct missing or incomplete filings before election deadlines.
