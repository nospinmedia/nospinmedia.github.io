✈️ New England airports recover after nationwide FAA ground stopFlights resume as travelers face lingering delays through the weekend 👇

New EnglandNovember 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 14, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Flights are moving again across New England after a nationwide Federal Aviation Administration ground stop halted departures for more than four hours Friday, disrupting travel at airports from Boston to Burlington and leaving airlines working through delays into the weekend.

The ground stop began at 3:54 p.m. EST Friday, November 13, and was lifted at 8:15 p.m., roughly 4 hours and 21 minutes later. The disruption affected airports throughout the region, including Boston Logan International Airport, Manchester-Boston Regional Airport, Bradley International Airport in Connecticut and Burlington International Airport in Vermont. Flights already airborne were allowed to continue while departures were held.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said outdated switches and routers in the air traffic control network became overloaded with data while more than 3,500 aircraft were airborne, producing inconsistent radar and communications information. Duffy said newer equipment being integrated into the system did not itself fail and said the FAA's AI-assisted SMART tool was not responsible. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said officials found no evidence of a cyberattack.

More than 9,000 flights were delayed or canceled nationwide during the disruption and its aftermath. After the FAA lifted the restriction, flights gradually resumed across New England, but travelers continued facing cancellations, rebooking and schedule changes as airlines repositioned aircraft and crews.

Because the disruption resulted from an FAA equipment problem rather than an airline-controlled failure, passengers whose flights were canceled are entitled to refunds or free rebooking, but airlines generally are not required to provide additional compensation for expenses caused by the disruption.

Travelers flying this weekend are being advised to check directly with their airlines before heading to the airport as schedules continue to recover.
