🍁 Peak foliage draws visitors to New Hampshire and Vermont despite soaring gas pricesNorthern New England businesses brace for one of fall's busiest weekends 👇

EnvironmentOctober 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 11, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Peak fall foliage is drawing visitors into New Hampshire and Vermont this weekend, with tourism businesses preparing for one of the busiest periods of the autumn season even as unusually high gas prices make road trips more expensive.

Brilliant fall colors are reaching their seasonal peak across parts of northern New England, traditionally bringing large numbers of visitors to the region's mountain communities, scenic highways, restaurants and lodging businesses.

Popular driving destinations include New Hampshire's White Mountains and Kancamagus Highway, along with Vermont's mountain communities and scenic routes. Hotels, inns and other tourism businesses are expecting heavy Columbus Day weekend traffic as visitors arrive to see the changing leaves.

This year's foliage rush comes with an added expense. Gasoline prices have risen sharply, increasing the cost of the driving trips that form a major part of New England's fall tourism economy.

The higher prices could particularly affect visitors traveling long distances or spending several days driving through the region. But tourism businesses are still expecting strong demand, with foliage season remaining one of the most important periods of the year for many communities in northern New Hampshire and Vermont.

The annual leaf-peeping season supports a wide range of businesses beyond hotels. Restaurants, shops, farm stands, apple orchards and other attractions depend on the surge of visitors who travel through smaller communities during the relatively short peak-color window.

Weather will also play a role in the weekend. The remnants of Hurricane Isaias are expected to bring rain into New England on Sunday, potentially giving visitors a narrower window for outdoor activities and scenic drives before conditions deteriorate.

For travelers who do make the trip, tourism officials generally recommend allowing extra time for congestion on popular routes and avoiding stopping along roadways where parking is prohibited or unsafe.

The weekend provides an early test of whether sharply higher fuel costs will significantly change travel behavior during one of New England's signature tourism seasons or whether visitors will absorb the additional expense to catch the colors while they last.
