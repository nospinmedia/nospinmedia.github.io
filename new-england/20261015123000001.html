🏛️ Burlington Limits Police Role in Federal Immigration ArrestsMayor's Order Restricts City Assistance and Seeks to Protect Access to Public Spaces 👇

EconomyOctober 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 15, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Burlington Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak has signed an executive order limiting how city employees and police can assist federal immigration authorities, expanding local protections as immigration enforcement generates growing concern in Vermont.

The order directs the Burlington Police Department and other municipal employees not to voluntarily assist federal agents with civil immigration enforcement except under specific circumstances required by law or involving public safety.

It also establishes procedures intended to prevent federal immigration arrests from disrupting access to city-owned property and public spaces.

Mulvaney-Stanak said the directive is intended to make clear that Burlington residents should be able to use parks, libraries and other municipal services without fearing that city employees will help federal authorities conduct civil immigration enforcement.

The order does not prevent U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement or other federal agencies from enforcing federal law in Burlington. Local governments generally cannot prohibit federal officers from carrying out lawful immigration operations within their jurisdictions.

Instead, the directive governs the actions of city employees and city resources, defining when Burlington personnel may cooperate with federal authorities and when they should decline voluntary assistance.

The distinction between a judicial warrant, signed by a judge, and an administrative immigration warrant issued by a federal immigration agency is particularly important. Burlington officials say city personnel should not treat an administrative immigration document as automatically requiring local participation in an arrest.

The policy follows concern about immigration enforcement activity in Vermont and elsewhere in New England. Local officials and immigrant-rights advocates have argued that fear of immigration arrests can discourage residents from reporting crimes, seeking emergency assistance or using government services.

Supporters of greater cooperation with federal immigration authorities argue that local governments should assist agencies charged with enforcing federal law and that restrictions can complicate enforcement operations.

Burlington's order therefore places the city within a broader national dispute over how much assistance states and municipalities should provide to federal immigration authorities.

The directive applies only to Burlington's government and does not change federal immigration law. Federal agents retain their existing legal authority, while Burlington employees must follow the city's new rules when deciding whether to provide voluntary assistance.

The policy could also become an important test of where local authority ends and federal enforcement power begins as immigration operations continue under President Trump.
