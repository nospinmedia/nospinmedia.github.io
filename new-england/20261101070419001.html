🚨 Texas teen wanted in fatal South Station stabbing turns himself in18-year-old accused in August rush-hour killing surrendered Friday 👇

HealthNovember 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 1, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

An 18-year-old Texas man wanted in connection with a fatal stabbing at Boston's South Station has turned himself in to authorities after more than two months. Marico Levy, of Aubrey, Texas, surrendered at a police station in Texas on Friday night, October 30, according to the Suffolk County District Attorney's Office.

Levy had been wanted by Boston police in connection with the August 12 killing of Latavius Owens, 29, of Memphis, Tennessee. Levy was 17 years old at the time of the stabbing.

Investigators said Owens and Levy were involved in an altercation at about 5:30 p.m. on an escalator leading from the MBTA Red Line station at South Station during the evening commute. Transit Police responding to a report of a fight found Owens suffering from at least one stab wound. He was taken to a Boston hospital, where he died.

Boston police subsequently identified Levy as the suspect and obtained a murder warrant. Authorities released his photograph and identifying information several days after the killing while asking the public for help locating him. Police said during the initial investigation that they did not believe the stabbing was a random attack.

The killing drew attention in part because it occurred during rush hour at South Station, one of New England's busiest transportation hubs. Investigators said there were numerous potential witnesses and reviewed surveillance footage while searching for the suspect.

The Suffolk County District Attorney's Office announced Levy's surrender Saturday. Authorities have not yet announced when he will be returned to Massachusetts or when he will appear in court. The district attorney's office also had not specified the charges he will face following his return as of the latest reporting.
