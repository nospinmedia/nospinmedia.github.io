⚡ Maine regulators reject CMP's temporary $7 monthly rate hikeUtility warns layoffs and delayed grid projects could follow 👇

EconomyOctober 7, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 7, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Maine utility regulators unanimously rejected Central Maine Power's request for a temporary rate increase that would have added about $7 per month to the bill of a typical residential customer while the company's much larger rate case remains under review.

The Maine Public Utilities Commission voted 3-0 Tuesday against CMP's request for an approximately $69 million temporary increase. CMP sought the interim increase while regulators consider its broader request for roughly $189 million in additional annual revenue. The larger case could eventually produce a substantially greater increase for customers, but regulators have not yet decided how much, if any, of that request should be approved.

CMP argued it needs additional revenue now to cover rising operating costs and investments needed to maintain and strengthen its electric grid. The utility said delaying the increase could force it to reduce spending before the full rate case is resolved. Company officials have raised the possibility of a hiring freeze, postponing some infrastructure work and potentially eliminating jobs.

Commissioners said Maine law did not allow them to approve the temporary increase under the circumstances. State law permits a temporary rate adjustment when the utility and other parties agree that at least some portion of a pending rate increase is undisputed. In CMP's case, regulators found there was no agreed-upon amount that could serve as the basis for an interim increase.

Consumer advocates had opposed the temporary hike. Maine's Office of the Public Advocate argued that customers should not be required to pay higher rates before regulators complete their examination of CMP's finances and determine what expenses are justified.

The decision does not end CMP's larger rate request. The PUC will continue reviewing that case, meaning customers could still face an increase after regulators examine the company's costs, proposed investments and requested return.
