⚖️ Cornell rape investigation reopened after former student files lawsuitBethel man among 7 former fraternity members named in allegations 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationSeptember 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 29, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A New York prosecutor has reopened a criminal investigation into allegations that a former Cornell University student was sexually assaulted by multiple fraternity members in October 2024, after a recently filed civil lawsuit raised allegations that differed from her original statement to authorities. One of the seven former Chi Phi fraternity members named in the lawsuit, Diego Sarabia, has ties to Bethel, Connecticut.

The woman, identified as Jane Doe, filed the lawsuit on September 16 against seven former fraternity members, Cornell, the fraternity and other defendants. She alleges that while she was a 20-year-old Cornell student, fraternity members pressured her to use what they said was ketamine after she had been drinking and that she was subsequently sexually assaulted over several hours inside the Chi Phi house. The allegations have not been proven in court, and no criminal charges have been filed against the seven men.

Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten said his office previously determined there was insufficient evidence to pursue criminal charges. He said the woman's sworn statement from November 2024 did not allege that she had been drugged against her will or gang-raped and described her participation in drug use and sexual activity differently from the allegations contained in the new lawsuit. Her attorney disputes the suggestion that the accounts materially conflict and argues investigators failed to adequately pursue evidence after her initial report.

Van Houten has now begun reopening the investigation and said he hopes to present the case to a Tompkins County grand jury within about 45 days. The grand jury could hear testimony from Jane Doe and consider new evidence raised by the civil complaint before deciding whether anyone should be indicted.

Cornell says it conducted its own disciplinary investigation after the woman reported the incident. The university says sanctions included expulsions and suspensions, and the Chi Phi chapter remains barred from campus. Cornell has also said federal privacy law prevents it from publicly detailing disciplinary actions against individual students.

Court records show a summons in the civil case was delivered on September 23 to Sarabia's Bethel home and received by his mother.
