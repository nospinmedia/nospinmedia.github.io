⚖️ Bridgton Man Released After Months In ICE DetentionImmigration court grants permanent resident status 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EconomyAugust 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 19, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Marcos Gaspar Da Silva, a 32-year-old Bridgton resident originally from Brazil, has returned to Maine after spending nearly seven months in federal immigration detention. His release followed proceedings before an immigration court in Arizona, where he was granted lawful permanent resident status, allowing him to return home to his wife after months of detention that drew attention from community members, immigration advocates and elected officials.

Gaspar Da Silva was detained by federal immigration authorities in January 2026 after being stopped while driving in Portland. His wife said he had been living and working in Maine while pursuing an asylum case and was transferred multiple times through detention facilities in Massachusetts, Louisiana and Arizona during his custody. Throughout the case, family members worked with attorneys, community organizations and the office of U.S. Rep. Chellie Pingree as his legal proceedings continued.

Following hearings in immigration court, Gaspar Da Silva was granted permanent resident status and released from the detention facility in Florence, Arizona before traveling back to Maine. His case became one of the highest-profile individual cases connected to the surge in immigration enforcement operations carried out in Maine earlier this year and was closely followed by local media and advocacy groups.

The outcome does not change broader federal immigration enforcement policies, but it concludes a months-long legal process for one Maine family. Supporters say the case highlights the complexity of immigration proceedings and the lengthy detention that can occur while legal cases remain pending.
