⚖️ Judge allows possible second Lindsay Clancy trial to move forwardDouble-jeopardy challenge rejected after September mistrial 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalNovember 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 10, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A Massachusetts judge has rejected Lindsay Clancy's attempt to have murder charges against her dismissed, removing a major legal obstacle to a possible second trial over the deaths of her three young children.

Plymouth Superior Court Judge William Sullivan ruled Monday that retrying Clancy would not violate constitutional protections against double jeopardy. Her first trial ended in a mistrial on September 4 after jurors reported they were deadlocked, with 11 jurors favoring acquittal based on lack of criminal responsibility and one favoring conviction.

Clancy's attorneys argued that Sullivan should have investigated further after the jury foreperson reported that the holdout juror was not properly applying the legal standard for reasonable doubt. Sullivan ruled that the deadlock created the "manifest necessity" required to declare a mistrial and that questioning jurors further risked improperly intruding on their deliberations or coercing a verdict.

Sullivan also rejected a defense request for additional records and an inquiry involving the holdout juror. Separately, he denied prosecutors' request for a gag order restricting defense attorney Kevin Reddington's public comments about the case.

Clancy is charged with three counts of first-degree murder in the January 2023 deaths of her children, Cora, 5; Dawson, 3; and Callan, 8 months, at the family's Duxbury home. Clancy has acknowledged killing the children but pleaded not guilty, arguing that severe mental illness left her not criminally responsible. Prosecutors maintain that she acted deliberately and understood that her actions were wrong.

The ruling does not mean a second trial has formally been ordered. Plymouth County District Attorney Timothy Cruz has not publicly announced whether prosecutors will again pursue first-degree murder charges, seek lesser charges or take another course. The next court hearing is scheduled for November 16, when the case's next steps are expected to be discussed.
