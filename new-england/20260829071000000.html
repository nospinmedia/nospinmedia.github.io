🚆 Rail Unions Push CSX for More Yard Staffing After DerailmentsRecent crashes in Maine and Massachusetts renew safety concerns 👇

EconomyAugust 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 29, 2026 at 11:49 AM ET

Rail labor leaders are calling on CSX Transportation to restore additional on-site staffing and safety oversight across its New England rail network following two freight train derailments this month in Maine and Massachusetts. Speaking at Rigby Yard in South Portland, representatives of the SMART Transportation Division (SMART-TD), joined by labor organizations and Democratic U.S. Senate candidate Troy Jackson, argued that staffing reductions at major freight yards have increased safety risks for workers, emergency responders and nearby communities.

The renewed push follows two derailments occurring within a week. On August 8, 13 cars of a 32-car CSX freight train derailed near Orono, Maine, prompting inspections of tracks close to the Penobscot River. On August 14, five freight cars left the tracks near Union Station in Worcester, Massachusetts. No injuries or hazardous material releases were reported in either incident, but union officials contend the back-to-back derailments highlight the need for stronger operational oversight and more personnel at key rail facilities.

Union leaders say Rigby Yard, New England's largest freight rail yard, no longer has dedicated union yardmasters responsible for coordinating switching operations and serving as direct contacts for local emergency responders. They argue restoring those positions would improve communication during emergencies and strengthen day-to-day operational safety. CSX has responded that its centralized operations management system and Public Safety Coordination Center provide continuous oversight and emergency support throughout its network following the company's 2022 acquisition of Pan Am Railways.

The derailments remain under investigation, and no official findings have linked either incident to staffing levels. However, the events have renewed debate over rail safety as freight volumes continue to move across New England. Union officials say they will continue pressing for additional staffing and operational changes, while CSX maintains its current safety procedures meet federal requirements and support safe railroad operations.
