🌿 EPA Proposes Removing New Hampshire From Ozone Transport RegionPlan could end federal emissions testing mandate if finalized 👇

EnvironmentAugust 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 8, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) announced on August 6, 2026 that it has proposed approving New Hampshire's request to withdraw from the Ozone Transport Region (OTR), a multi-state program established under the Clean Air Act to coordinate efforts to reduce ozone pollution. The agency also proposed conditionally approving changes to the state's air quality plan that would eliminate the federally required Vehicle Inspection and Maintenance (I/M) program if the proposal is finalized.

EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin said the proposal would recognize New Hampshire's current air quality while giving state officials greater flexibility to determine how best to meet federal clean air standards. State leaders have argued for years that advances in vehicle technology and improved regional air quality have made mandatory emissions testing unnecessary. Under the proposal, New Hampshire would remain responsible for meeting all applicable federal air quality standards despite leaving the regional program.

The EPA's second proposed action would conditionally approve revisions to New Hampshire's State Implementation Plan (SIP) that remove the inspection and maintenance program previously required under OTR membership. Before those changes can take effect, the rulemaking process must be completed, including a public comment period and issuance of a final EPA decision. Until then, existing federal requirements remain in place.

Governor Kelly Ayotte welcomed the proposal, saying it reflects years of work by state officials to reduce regulatory burdens while maintaining clean air protections. Supporters contend the changes would reduce costs and administrative requirements for motorists and the state, while critics are expected to closely examine whether removing emissions testing could affect long-term air quality. The proposal represents a significant step but does not immediately change New Hampshire law or federal environmental requirements.
