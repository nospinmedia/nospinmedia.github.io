🗳️ Massachusetts leads new challenge to USPS mail ballot ruleBoston court fight intensifies weeks before midterms 👇

LegalAugust 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 27, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell is co-leading a new federal lawsuit in Boston challenging a finalized U.S. Postal Service rule that would impose new requirements on mail voting before the November 3, 2026 midterm elections. The complaint was filed on August 26 by 23 states, the District of Columbia, and Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro after the U.S. Supreme Court stayed an earlier injunction that had blocked portions of President Trump's Executive Order 14399. The new lawsuit directly challenges the Postal Service's completed regulation rather than the executive order alone.

The USPS rule requires states to provide the Postal Service with information identifying voters receiving mail ballots and requires ballots to use envelopes meeting new federal standards, including unique barcodes. The states argue USPS lacks congressional authority to regulate state election procedures and contend the requirements violate the Privacy Act, federal voting protections and constitutional limits on federal control of elections. Massachusetts officials say the changes would force states to redesign ballot materials, build new data-sharing systems and retrain election workers on an extremely compressed timetable.

The dispute has become particularly significant in Massachusetts, where nearly 1.2 million voters, or 33.4% of those who participated in the 2024 general election, cast ballots by mail. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani lifted a previous nationwide injunction on August 26 after the Supreme Court ruled that the earlier state challenge lacked standing at that stage. The high court did not decide whether the underlying voting restrictions are lawful. Talwani has ordered the federal government to respond to the new challenge and scheduled a hearing for September 3 on the states' request for preliminary relief.

Voting rights groups, including the League of Women Voters of Massachusetts and the ACLU, are pursuing a parallel challenge to the finalized rule. The states argue that implementing major postal ballot requirements so close to the election could disrupt established voting systems, while the administration maintains the measures are intended to improve election security and standardize handling of mailed ballots.
