🚨 ICE Sweep Across Connecticut Draws Sharp Local ResponseDanbury-area arrests spark criticism from mayors and advocacy groups 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EducationAugust 26, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 26, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Federal immigration agents conducted a two-day enforcement operation across Danbury, Stamford, and Bridgeport, detaining at least 20 people, including at least 16 in Danbury, according to local officials and advocacy organizations. U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) carried out the operation on August 24 and 25, concentrating on day-labor gathering locations, commercial areas, and residential neighborhoods. Witnesses and immigrant advocacy groups said agents used unmarked vehicles and mobile technology during the arrests, while ICE has not publicly released a detailed account of the operation.

The largest number of arrests occurred in Danbury, where advocates said many of those detained were day laborers waiting for work, while others were taken into custody outside businesses or near their homes. CT Students for a Dream said approximately half of those arrested in Danbury were day laborers. The organization and other advocates raised concerns about enforcement tactics and their effect on immigrant communities throughout Fairfield County.

The operation prompted strong reactions from local leaders. Danbury Mayor Roberto Alves and Stamford Mayor Caroline Simmons criticized the enforcement actions, arguing that broad immigration sweeps can damage trust between residents and local government. Alves emphasized that Danbury Police do not participate in civil immigration enforcement under Connecticut law and encouraged residents to understand their constitutional rights during encounters with federal agents. U.S. Representative Jahana Hayes also criticized the operation, expressing concern about the detention of long-term community members without serious criminal histories.

The arrests have renewed debate over immigration enforcement in Connecticut, including the state's Trust Act, which limits how local law enforcement cooperates with federal civil immigration authorities. Community organizations continue assisting affected families while local officials seek additional information about the operation and whether further enforcement activity is planned in the region.
