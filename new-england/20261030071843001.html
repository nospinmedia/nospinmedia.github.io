🚗 Vermont gets $4.5 million in General Motors privacy settlementStates say driving data was sold without customers' consent 👇

LegalOctober 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 30, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Vermont will receive about $4.5 million as part of a $220 million multistate settlement with General Motors over allegations that the automaker collected and sold drivers' behavior and location data without adequately informing them or obtaining their consent.

Vermont Attorney General Charity Clark said the data involved tens of thousands of Vermonters enrolled in GM's Smart Driver program. The program collected information about driving behavior, including acceleration, braking and vehicle location. State investigators alleged GM sold that information to data brokers, which then provided it to insurance companies that could use the information when setting premiums.

The investigation involved a coalition of 36 attorneys general, with Vermont participating in the group that helped lead the case. Clark said Vermont's early involvement resulted in the state receiving more money than it otherwise would have based on its population. The approximately $4.54 million payment will go to Vermont's general fund.

Under the settlement, GM must obtain affirmative express consent before collecting or disclosing certain driving and location information. The company must also limit the driving data it collects, strengthen its privacy program and provide additional oversight and training at dealerships.

The states alleged that some dealership employees accepted enrollment terms on customers' behalf and that some drivers did not realize they had been enrolled in Smart Driver. GM discontinued the program in 2024.

GM did not admit that it violated the law as part of the settlement. The company said it has already strengthened its privacy practices and is committed to providing customers clearer information about how their data is used and what choices they have.
