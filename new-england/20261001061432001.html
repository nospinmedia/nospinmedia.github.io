⛪ Mass. probe finds 270+ clergy abused nearly 1,000 childrenState details decades of failures across three Catholic dioceses 👇

LegalOctober 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 1, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A Massachusetts Attorney General's Office investigation found evidence that more than 270 Catholic clerics abused nearly 1,000 children in the dioceses of Fall River, Springfield and Worcester, providing the state's first comparable public accounting of clergy sexual abuse and institutional failures across those three dioceses. The investigation began in 2019 and examined abuse and diocesan responses spanning decades.

Investigators reviewed more than 300,000 pages of records and interviewed more than 100 people, including survivors, advocates, attorneys, clergy and diocesan employees. The report found instances in which diocesan leaders failed to act on allegations and warning signs, transferred accused or offending priests without disclosing why, returned priests to ministry after treatment without adequate supervision, failed to enforce restrictions and did not consistently report allegations to law enforcement.

The investigation produced no new indictments. The Attorney General's Office said some conduct occurred outside applicable statutes of limitations, some occurred before Massachusetts strengthened criminal child-protection laws in 2002, and many people identified in the investigation are deceased. Three cases were referred to district attorneys during the investigation, and one referral to the Worcester County District Attorney's Office resulted in criminal charges in District Court.

The report also found that reforms adopted after the 2002 clergy sexual abuse crisis improved child-protection practices but were not always consistently followed. Worcester remains the only Massachusetts diocese without a publicly accessible list of credibly accused clergy, and Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell called for greater transparency across the dioceses.

Campbell is also urging lawmakers to eliminate Massachusetts' $20,000 charitable immunity cap for civil child sexual abuse claims, eliminate the civil statute of limitations for such cases and establish an indefinite lookback period allowing survivors with expired claims another opportunity to seek legal relief.

The report recommends trauma-informed responses to survivors, independent investigations of allegations, stronger enforcement of restrictions on accused clergy and greater public transparency.
