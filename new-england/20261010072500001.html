🔥 Maine Secures $38 Million in Early Federal Heating AssistanceFunding Arrives as Heating Oil Prices Climb Above $6 a Gallon 👇

File photoAn oil pumpjack operates at a petroleum production site.Credit: PickPik
EconomyOctober 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 10, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Maine will receive approximately $38.1 million in federal heating assistance, providing financial relief to low-income households and tribal communities as residents prepare for another expensive winter.

The funding, announced on Friday, October 9, 2026, includes $36,675,395 for Maine and an additional $1,408,729 for the Wabanaki Nations. The money comes through the federal Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP), which helps qualifying households pay heating expenses.

According to U.S. Senator Susan Collins, the allocation represents one of the earliest releases of LIHEAP funding in the program's history. Last year's funding was delayed until December because of the federal government shutdown.

The early distribution comes at a particularly important time for Maine, where approximately half of all households rely on heating oil as their primary heating source. The state has the highest dependence on heating oil in the country, making residents especially vulnerable to fluctuations in petroleum prices.

Heating oil prices recently reached approximately $6 per gallon, substantially higher than a year ago. At that price, filling a standard 275-gallon heating oil tank costs approximately $1,650, placing considerable pressure on families already managing rising household expenses.

Governor Janet Mills previously urged Congress to increase federal heating assistance, arguing that existing funding would not adequately address the sharp rise in energy costs. Maine's congressional delegation has also supported efforts to make additional assistance available.

The newly released funding will be administered through MaineHousing and local Community Action Agencies, which help determine household eligibility and coordinate assistance payments.

LIHEAP supports qualifying homeowners and renters who heat their homes with oil, propane, natural gas, electricity, and other approved energy sources. Payments generally help offset heating expenses rather than covering an entire winter's fuel costs.

Residents experiencing immediate heating emergencies may also qualify for additional assistance through the state's Energy Crisis Intervention Program.

The federal allocation is separate from Maine's recently announced $40 million electricity discount initiative, which will provide automatic bill reductions to thousands of eligible households beginning in November.

Applications for Maine's heating assistance program are already open, and residents are encouraged to apply before available funding is exhausted.
