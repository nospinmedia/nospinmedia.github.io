⚠️ OSHA Says Maine Paper Mill Ignored Broken Alarm Before Fatal Gas LeakFederal safety findings detail alleged hazards that killed two workers 👇

New EnglandAugust 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 30, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Federal workplace safety investigators allege that Woodland Pulp LLC in Baileyville, Maine, knowingly allowed employees to work without a functioning emergency alarm system before a January 27, 2026, hydrogen sulfide release killed 20-year-old engineering intern Kasie Malcolm and 26-year-old process engineer Allen Hornberger. Newly released details from the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) expand on citations issued in July, alleging the alarm system failed to activate during the toxic gas release and that workers were exposed to deadly hazards without adequate protections.

OSHA proposed $794,456 in penalties after issuing four willful and eight serious citations. The agency alleges the company failed to provide appropriate hydrogen sulfide alarms and respiratory protection, inadequately trained employees assigned to respond to hazardous releases, failed to include proper first-aid procedures for chemical exposures and neglected other required safety measures. OSHA also cited the mill over a March 7, 2026, chemical fire, alleging combustible wood pulp was allowed to accumulate near hydrogen peroxide storage tanks, creating a known fire hazard.

The findings build on an ongoing investigation by the U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board, which previously reported that the bleach plant lacked fixed hydrogen sulfide detectors and that the two workers who died had not been issued personal hydrogen sulfide monitors. The CSB said 10 additional employees were exposed to the toxic gas during the incident. Hydrogen sulfide is a highly toxic, flammable gas that can rapidly cause unconsciousness or death at high concentrations.

Woodland Pulp has formally contested OSHA's citations and the proposed penalties, meaning the allegations have not been finally adjudicated. The company declined to comment in detail because related investigations remain pending. The OSHA case will now proceed through the agency's administrative review process while the Chemical Safety Board continues its investigation into the fatal release.
