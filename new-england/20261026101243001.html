🗳️ Massachusetts voters will decide whether to keep sweeping 2024 gun lawQuestion 9 asks voters to uphold or repeal state's expanded firearm regulations 👇

EducationOctober 26, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 26, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Massachusetts voters will decide on November 3 whether to keep or repeal a major firearms law enacted by the state Legislature in 2024, putting regulations covering ghost guns, assault-style weapons, licensing and other firearm rules directly before voters.

Question 9 is a referendum on the existing law rather than a proposal for a new gun-control measure. A Yes vote keeps the law in place, while a No vote repeals it. Opponents of the law collected enough signatures to force the statewide referendum after the Legislature approved the measure in July 2024 and Gov. Maura Healey signed it.

The law requires firearms, including privately manufactured and 3D-printed guns, to have serial numbers and establishes penalties involving untraceable ghost guns. It also changes how Massachusetts defines and regulates assault-style weapons and machine guns, with the latter category expanded to include devices such as bump stocks, Glock switches and auto sears.

Other provisions change firearm licensing requirements, require training and testing for new license applicants and allow school administrators and licensed healthcare providers to seek Extreme Risk Protection Orders when they believe someone poses a danger to themselves or others. The law also generally prohibits firearms in locations including schools, polling places and government buildings, with specified exceptions.

The Legislature approved the measure by wide margins, 124-33 in the House and 35-5 in the Senate. Supporters argue the changes strengthen Massachusetts' existing firearm regulations and address newer technologies such as ghost guns. Opponents argue the law places excessive restrictions on lawful gun owners and have sought its repeal through the referendum process.

Massachusetts is one of nine statewide questions appearing on this year's ballot, the largest number the state has presented to voters at one election.
