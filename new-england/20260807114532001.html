🏥 Maine Nurse Released From ICE Custody After Union CampaignAsylum-seeking Maine Medical Center nurse returns home following detention 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
HealthAugust 7, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 7, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Debora Kapisha, a registered nurse at Maine Medical Center in Portland, was released from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) custody after several days of detention that prompted public demonstrations by fellow nurses, labor organizations, and Maine's congressional delegation. Rep. Chellie Pingree announced her release on August 6, 2026, confirming that Kapisha is returning to Maine while continuing her immigration case through the legal asylum process.

Kapisha, an asylum seeker employed as a medical-surgical nurse, was detained at Boston Logan International Airport while returning to New England. Her detention prompted rallies organized by the Maine State Nurses Association (MSNA) and National Nurses United (NNU), whose members argued that she had complied with immigration requirements and should be allowed to continue working while her case proceeds. MaineHealth, the parent organization of Maine Medical Center, said it supported efforts to resolve the matter and coordinated with her legal representatives during the detention.

Rep. Pingree said Kapisha remains in the immigration system and will continue through the asylum process, which can take years before a final decision is reached. Union leaders welcomed her release but said they remain concerned about the detention of healthcare workers with pending immigration cases. A joint press conference is scheduled outside Maine Medical Center on August 7 to discuss the case and broader immigration policies affecting essential healthcare employees.

The case has drawn statewide attention as hospitals across Maine continue working to recruit and retain healthcare professionals amid ongoing workforce shortages. Supporters say Kapisha's release allows her to reunite with her family and return to serving patients while her immigration case moves forward.
