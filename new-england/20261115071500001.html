💻 Boston residents rally after South End Technology Center closesNearly 30-year community program received eviction notice as Tent City tenants raise broader concerns 👇

EducationNovember 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 15, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Residents and community activists rallied Saturday outside Boston's South End Technology Center, which has closed after nearly three decades of providing free and low-cost technology education to children and adults.

The center, located at the Tent City housing complex at 359 Columbus Avenue, received an eviction notice, according to Heather Cook, president of the Tent City Tenant Association, who told the Boston Globe she had reviewed the notice. The exact reason for the eviction and precisely when the center stopped operating remain unclear. Residents said the closure occurred in recent months.

Founded in 1997 by Boston civil rights activist Mel King in partnership with MIT and Tent City, the center was created to expand access to computers and technology. Its programs included computer training, after-school education and hands-on technology projects. In 2003, the center and MIT's Center for Bits and Atoms launched what the center describes as the world's first Fab Lab, giving community members access to digital fabrication tools.

About three dozen people gathered Saturday at Columbus Avenue and Yarmouth Street to protest the closure and raise concerns about the future of Tent City. Speakers included former Massachusetts state senator Dianne Wilkerson and Boston City Councilor Miniard Culpepper.

The dispute extends beyond the technology center. Tent City contains about 270 mixed-income residential units and was built in the late 1980s after a decades-long community campaign for affordable housing. Its name traces back to 1968, when activists erected tents on the property to protest plans for development after homes in the area had been demolished.

The complex is owned by the nonprofit Tent City Corporation, while Peabody Properties handles day-to-day management. The Boston Planning and Development Agency owns the land. The Globe reported that Tent City Corporation President Danzel Samuels and Peabody Properties President and CEO Melissa Fish-Crane did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Residents say the technology center's eviction has increased fears about the direction of the housing complex. Tenant association members have raised concerns about possible evictions as well as previous complaints involving maintenance, mold and security. Cook described the technology center's eviction as a warning sign, while residents at the rally said they want to preserve both affordable housing and the community institutions created around it.

No evidence has been presented that Tent City itself is closing or that residents are facing a confirmed mass eviction. Those concerns currently come from tenants and activists, while the reason for the technology center's eviction has not been publicly explained.
