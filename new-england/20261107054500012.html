🔥 White Mountains wildfire reaches historic scale amid droughtWild River Fire nears 1,000 acres as containment improves to 20% 👇

File photoWildfire burning through forest.Credit: Salam2009
EnvironmentNovember 7, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 7, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The Wild River Fire burning in New Hampshire's White Mountain National Forest has grown to nearly 1,000 acres, reaching a scale rarely seen in the state as prolonged drought leaves forests unusually vulnerable to fire.

The wildfire was first reported on Sunday, November 1, approximately seven miles southeast of Gorham in Coos County near the Maine border. The fire initially covered several hundred acres before expanding rapidly through dry leaves, timber and other forest fuels. Estimates have placed the burned area at roughly 856 acres to nearly 1,000 acres as crews continue mapping the perimeter.

By Friday, November 6, firefighters had increased containment to approximately 20%, an improvement from the 11% containment previously reported. More than 50 federal and local firefighters have been involved in the response, with aerial resources assisting crews working in steep, rocky and difficult-to-reach terrain.

Officials say persistent drought across northern New Hampshire and western Maine has played a major role in the fire's behavior. Josh Sjostrom, district ranger for the Androscoggin Ranger District, said unusually dry conditions have left vegetation and forest-floor material highly susceptible to burning. Smoke and ash have also been reported in communities including Gorham and Shelburne.

Despite the fire's size, officials reported no immediate threat to homes or other residential structures. Portions of the forest surrounding the Wild River drainage have been closed, including the Kenduskeag Trail and other nearby areas. The Appalachian Trail remains open, although hikers have been warned about smoke and changing fire conditions.

The latest figures represent a meaningful update from earlier reports on the Wild River Fire, when the blaze was approaching 1,000 acres but containment remained at 11%. Firefighters continue working to strengthen containment lines while officials monitor weather and exceptionally dry forest conditions.
