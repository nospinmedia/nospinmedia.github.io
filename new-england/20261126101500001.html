🎟️ Massachusetts lottery winner arrested by ICE days after claiming $1 millionEast Boston man chose $650,000 cash payout before being taken into custody 👇

EconomyNovember 26, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 26, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A Massachusetts man was arrested by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement days after claiming a $1 million lottery prize, drawing attention to both his immigration case and the status of his winnings while he remains in federal custody.

ICE's Enforcement and Removal Operations division in Boston said officers arrested Ariel Antonio Velasquez-Morales, a citizen of El Salvador, in East Boston on Tuesday, November 24. According to federal officials, Velasquez-Morales fled on foot when officers approached him early Tuesday morning, leading to a brief foot chase before he was taken into custody.

Days earlier, the Massachusetts State Lottery announced that Velasquez-Morales had won $1 million on a $10 "$2,000,000 50X Cashword" scratch ticket purchased at Sumner Market in East Boston. He selected the one-time cash option of $650,000 before taxes and posed with an oversized check at lottery headquarters in Dorchester.

According to ICE, Velasquez-Morales crossed the U.S.-Mexico border near Roma, Texas, in the Rio Grande Valley on May 15, 2013. Federal authorities said he was issued a notice to appear before an immigration judge and later received a final removal order on April 24, 2014, after failing to appear for his immigration hearing.

ICE Boston publicized the arrest on social media using the same photograph of Velasquez-Morales holding the oversized lottery check. The agency wrote, "Welcome to the ERO Boston lottery!" while criticizing undocumented immigration and sanctuary policies.

The lottery win itself does not appear to be invalid because of his immigration status. Massachusetts Lottery rules do not require a player to be a U.S. citizen or legal resident to purchase a ticket. Prizes above $601 require additional identification when claimed.

Velasquez-Morales had already claimed the winning ticket and selected the cash option before his arrest. Reporting as of Wednesday did not establish whether the $650,000 payout had already been disbursed or whether his detention would affect access to the money.

ICE has not publicly said that the lottery win triggered the arrest or explained whether the publicity surrounding the prize played any role in locating him.
