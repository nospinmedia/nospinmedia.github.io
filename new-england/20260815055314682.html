⚡ Powerful Storm Slams Greater Portland With Damaging WindsJetport loses power as thousands face outages across southern Maine 👇

EnvironmentAugust 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 15, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A powerful thunderstorm swept across Greater Portland on August 13, bringing destructive straight-line winds, heavy rain and hail that damaged homes, toppled trees and knocked out electricity to thousands of customers across Cumberland County. The storm forced the Portland International Jetport onto emergency generator power for several hours after losing commercial electrical service, while emergency crews responded to widespread reports of fallen trees blocking roads and damaging property.

The National Weather Service confirmed that a microburst produced wind gusts approaching 80 mph near Cape Elizabeth, making it one of the strongest localized wind events recorded in the area in recent years. Central Maine Power reported more than 13,600 customers without electricity at the height of the outages as crews worked through the night and into Friday restoring service. Municipal public works departments and first responders in Portland, South Portland, and Cape Elizabeth cleared debris from streets and responded to trees that had fallen onto homes, vehicles and power lines.

Despite the outage, the Portland International Jetport remained operational using backup generators while utility crews restored commercial power. Airport officials reported flight disruptions during the outage but continued serving passengers as safely as possible until normal electrical service returned later in the evening. Officials urged travelers to check with their airlines for updated schedules as operations gradually returned to normal.

Cleanup efforts continued Friday as utility crews restored remaining outages and local officials assessed storm damage. Forecasters noted that microbursts can produce wind damage similar to weak tornadoes but occur when powerful downdrafts spread outward after striking the ground. Emergency management officials reminded residents to remain cautious around damaged trees, downed power lines and debris while recovery operations continue.
