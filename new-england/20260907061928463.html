🏥 Nearly 300 NH residents may lose Medicaid Oct. 1Federal eligibility changes affect refugees and humanitarian parolees 👇

HealthSeptember 7, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 7, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Nearly 300 New Hampshire residents may lose full-benefit Medicaid coverage beginning October 1 because of new federal eligibility restrictions affecting some non-U.S. citizen adults. The New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services says it has identified 296 people who may lose coverage and has contacted them to explain the change and provide an opportunity to verify or update their immigration status.

Beginning October 1, non-pregnant adults generally must be a U.S. citizen, U.S. national, lawful permanent resident, Cuban-Haitian Entrant or Compact of Free Association migrant to remain eligible for full-benefit Medicaid under the new federal rules. The changes stem from H.R. 1, signed into law in 2025, and affect groups that previously qualified under other immigration categories, including some refugees, asylees and humanitarian parolees. Eligibility rules for children under 19 and pregnant or one-year-postpartum women are not changing under this provision.

Among those facing the change is 86-year-old Nataliia Otbliesk, a Ukrainian woman who moved to New Hampshire in 2023 through the Uniting for Ukraine humanitarian parole program. She now lives at Pleasant View Center in Concord, where Medicaid covers her medical and nursing care. Her son, Dmitry, said the facility costs approximately $12,000 per month and that his mother requires extensive assistance with basic daily activities.

Pleasant View Center executive director Richard Rogers said nursing facilities cannot simply discharge patients who are unable to pay if no safe discharge plan exists. DHHS says emergency Medicaid may remain available in certain circumstances and affected residents can explore health coverage through the federal Marketplace. Otbliesk's family is trying to determine what coverage and care options will be available when the new eligibility rules take effect.
