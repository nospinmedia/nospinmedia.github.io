⛽ Diesel tops $6 in five New England statesMaine reaches $6.15 as record U.S. fuel costs squeeze freight 👇

EconomySeptember 12, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 12, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Diesel prices have climbed above $6 a gallon in five of New England's six states, extending a nationwide fuel surge that is raising transportation costs for businesses and consumers. AAA data for September 12 shows diesel averaging $6.1508 in Maine, $6.1112 in Connecticut, $6.0432 in New Hampshire, $6.0366 in Vermont and $6.0248 in Massachusetts. Rhode Island, at $5.9853, remains just below the threshold.

The increases have been rapid. A day earlier, Maine diesel averaged $6.0171, while New Hampshire and Massachusetts were still below $6 at $5.9200 and $5.9315, respectively. Connecticut had just crossed the mark at $6.0028. In Maine, diesel is now more than $2.26 per gallon higher than a year ago, while New Hampshire's average is more than $2.23 above its year-ago level.

The regional increases follow a national surge that pushed the U.S. diesel average to a record of roughly $6.05 per gallon on September 11, according to AAA data cited by the Associated Press. Diesel is heavily used by trucking companies, delivery networks, farms and construction equipment, meaning sustained increases can raise the cost of moving food and other goods. Businesses including package and online-delivery companies have already introduced or increased fuel-related surcharges as transportation expenses rise.

Higher diesel costs can be particularly important for New England because many consumer goods and food products are transported into the region by truck. Perishable products such as meat and produce can face additional pressure because they require frequent deliveries and refrigerated transportation, although changes in fuel costs can take time to appear in retail prices.

The latest fuel spike has accompanied renewed turmoil in global energy markets. Brent crude settled at $104.61 a barrel Friday, while U.S. crude finished at $100.05, as disruptions involving Middle East oil production and shipping continued to constrain supplies.
