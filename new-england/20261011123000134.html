⚖️ Trial begins for former Stoughton officer accused of killing Sandra BirchmoreProsecutors say Matthew Farwell strangled pregnant 23-year-old and staged suicide 👇

LegalOctober 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 11, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Opening statements began Friday, October 9, 2026, in the federal trial of former Stoughton police officer Matthew Farwell, who is accused of killing 23-year-old Sandra Birchmore and staging her death to look like a suicide.

Federal prosecutors allege Farwell strangled Birchmore inside her Canton, Massachusetts, apartment in February 2021 after learning she was pregnant and after his relationship with her had come under scrutiny. Farwell has pleaded not guilty.

Prosecutors say Farwell first met Birchmore when she was 13 years old and participating in the Stoughton Police Explorers program. They allege he later began an illegal sexual relationship with her when she was 15 and continued the relationship for years while serving as a police officer.

Birchmore was found dead in her apartment on February 4, 2021. Her death was initially ruled a suicide, but questions about that conclusion persisted as details emerged about her relationships with Stoughton police officers.

Federal authorities charged Farwell in 2024 after a forensic pathologist retained by Birchmore's family concluded that her injuries were inconsistent with suicide and indicated strangulation.

Prosecutors allege Farwell killed Birchmore because she was pregnant and because disclosure of their relationship threatened his career and freedom. They contend he then arranged the scene to make it appear that Birchmore had taken her own life.

Farwell's attorneys dispute that account. The defense maintains that Birchmore died by suicide after Farwell ended their relationship and denied that he was the father of her unborn child.

Farwell faces federal charges including killing a victim or witness and causing the death of an unborn child. Prosecutors previously announced that they would not seek the death penalty. A conviction could result in life imprisonment.

A 16-member jury was seated for the case before U.S. District Judge Denise Casper in Boston. Testimony is expected to begin Tuesday, and the trial could last approximately five weeks.

The proceedings are expected to revisit years of questions surrounding Birchmore's death, her involvement with Stoughton police officers and how allegations involving the department were handled.
