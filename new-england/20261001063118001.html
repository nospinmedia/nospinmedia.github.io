⚖️ Massachusetts abortion shield law challenged at Supreme CourtThree states ask justices to strike down protections for providers 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthOctober 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 1, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Three states with abortion bans are asking the U.S. Supreme Court to strike down abortion shield laws in Massachusetts, New York and California, opening a potentially consequential interstate fight over whether doctors can legally prescribe and mail abortion pills to patients living in states where abortion is prohibited.

The attorneys general of Louisiana, Alabama and Arkansas filed the action directly with the Supreme Court, arguing that the three shield laws interfere with their ability to enforce their own abortion restrictions. The Supreme Court has not agreed to hear the case. Because it is a dispute between states, the plaintiffs are asking the justices to exercise the Court's original jurisdiction rather than beginning the case in a lower court.

Massachusetts' shield law is designed to protect health care providers from investigations, prosecutions and other legal actions initiated by states where abortion is restricted when the care provided is legal under Massachusetts law. The protections have become increasingly important as telehealth providers prescribe mifepristone and misoprostol to patients across state lines.

The three plaintiff states argue that the shield laws violate constitutional principles governing relations between states and prevent them from enforcing their own laws against out-of-state providers. Massachusetts and the other defendant states maintain that they have authority to protect providers operating legally within their borders.

Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell said her office will defend the state's law and its authority to protect Massachusetts residents. Similar resistance has occurred elsewhere: New York officials have previously declined to cooperate with Louisiana efforts targeting a New York doctor accused of prescribing abortion medication to a Louisiana patient.

The same three attorneys general also filed a separate lawsuit in federal court in Louisiana against 30 providers and organizations involved in prescribing or distributing abortion medication. Among those named is the Massachusetts Medication Abortion Access Project and its founder, Angel Foster.

The Supreme Court challenge could eventually determine how far one state's abortion laws can reach into another state following the Court's 2022 Dobbs decision, which ended the federal constitutional right to abortion and returned abortion policy largely to the states.
