🚇 Teen Dies After Third-Rail Accident at MBTA StationTransit Police Say Rescue Attempt Ended in Tragedy 👇

EducationAugust 31, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 31, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A 19-year-old man identified by his family as Frankey Wright Jr. died after falling onto the electrified third rail at MBTA Forest Hills Station in Boston's Jamaica Plain neighborhood on August 29. According to MBTA Transit Police, the incident occurred around 1:09 p.m. as Wright and his 19-year-old girlfriend were heading to Boston's annual Caribbean Carnival celebrations carrying flags. Investigators said the woman lost her balance near the platform edge, and Wright fell onto the tracks while trying to prevent her from falling.

Police said surveillance video showed the woman briefly regained her footing before stumbling backward into the track area. Wright attempted to catch her but also fell, landing on the energized third rail. Emergency crews responded immediately, but Wright was pronounced dead at the scene. The woman was taken to a local hospital with serious injuries. MBTA Transit Police Superintendent Richard Sullivan said investigators found no evidence of foul play and determined the incident was a tragic accident.

The incident temporarily disrupted Orange Line service as the MBTA Operations Control Center shut off traction power to allow first responders to safely reach the victims. Shuttle buses replaced train service between Forest Hills and Stony Brook stations until power was restored and rail operations resumed later that afternoon. Officials reminded riders that the system's third rail carries approximately 600 volts of electricity and should never be entered except by trained emergency personnel after power has been disconnected.

The fatal accident occurred during a busy weekend for Boston transit, with thousands of visitors traveling to the city's Caribbean Carnival festivities. MBTA officials extended condolences to Wright's family and said the agency continues cooperating with investigators while encouraging passengers to remain behind platform safety lines and seek assistance from transit personnel if someone appears to be in danger.
