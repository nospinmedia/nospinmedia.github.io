🦈 Shark Sightings Prompt New Maine Beach WarningsSouthern Coast Expands Patrols and Swimming Restrictions 👇

EducationAugust 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 21, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Police and beach officials along Maine's southern coast issued renewed shark safety advisories after multiple confirmed near-shore sightings on August 20, adding to an unusually active summer for white shark encounters. The Kennebunk Police Department and Kennebunkport Police Department urged visitors at Gooch's Beach and Goose Rocks Beach to remain alert after credible sightings were reported offshore. The advisories follow a series of temporary swimming closures this month at beaches including Wells Beach, Biddeford, Pine Point, and Reid State Park, where lifeguards and marine officials have repeatedly cleared the water following shark reports.

At Scarborough Beach State Park, officials strengthened safety protocols after park manager Greg Wilfret was pulled underwater when a juvenile white shark grabbed his rescue buoy during a training swim earlier this month. Although Wilfret was uninjured, the incident prompted the park to limit swimmers to approximately waist-deep water while lifeguards conduct continuous surveillance using Jet Ski patrols, drone flights, and shoreline binocular observations between 9:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m. each day. Officials emphasized that no unprovoked shark attacks on swimmers have been reported despite the increase in sightings.

At the same time, the Maine Department of Marine Resources (DMR), working with researchers from the University of Maine and the University of Massachusetts Dartmouth, expanded efforts to better understand juvenile great white sharks in the Gulf of Maine. On August 12, researchers successfully tagged an 8-foot juvenile female white shark nicknamed "Bug", marking the state's first vessel-based white shark tagging mission. DMR now maintains an acoustic monitoring network of more than 35 receiver stations stretching from Ogunquit to the Canadian border to track shark movements and improve public safety.

Marine scientists say the increase in sightings does not necessarily mean Maine has more sharks than in previous years. Instead, they point to expanded research, improved tracking technology, abundant seal populations, warming Gulf of Maine waters, and greater public reporting as reasons more sharks are being documented. Officials continue encouraging swimmers to follow beach advisories, avoid swimming near seals or schools of bait fish, and immediately report suspected shark sightings to local authorities.
