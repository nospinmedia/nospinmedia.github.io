🗳️ Susan Collins Rejects ICE at Maine PollsSenator Breaks with GOP Governor Nominee 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
PoliticsSeptember 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 2, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

U.S. Sen. Susan Collins publicly rejected a proposal by Maine Republican gubernatorial nominee Bobby Charles to seek the deployment of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents at polling places during the November 2026 general election, saying such a federal presence could discourage lawful voters from casting ballots. Speaking during a visit to manufacturing facilities in Biddeford, Collins said she saw no justification for stationing immigration agents at voting locations and warned the idea would intimidate legitimate voters rather than strengthen confidence in elections.

Charles' proposal became public after leaked audio from an Aug. 20 meeting with Maine Young Republicans in Arundel captured him discussing plans to ask border czar Tom Homan to send ICE agents and deputy U.S. marshals to polling places. Charles argued the presence would help prevent illegal voting by noncitizens. His comments quickly drew criticism from political opponents and voting-rights advocates, who argued the proposal could discourage eligible voters from participating in the election.

Collins' remarks marked a notable public disagreement within Maine's Republican Party as the campaign for governor enters its final months. While Collins has supported measures aimed at strengthening election integrity, including the federal Safeguard American Voter Eligibility (SAVE) Act, she said deploying federal immigration officers at polling locations is not appropriate. Her comments also distinguish her position from Charles on how election security should be handled in Maine.

The exchange adds another point of contrast in an already competitive gubernatorial race. Democratic nominee Hannah Pingree and independent candidate Rick Bennett have also criticized Charles' proposal, while Charles has defended it as a way to protect election integrity. The issue is expected to remain part of the broader debate over voting access, election administration and public confidence ahead of the Nov. 3 general election.
