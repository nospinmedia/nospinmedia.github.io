🎓 NH campuses step up voter outreach after student ID changeOrganizers work to clear up confusion before Tuesday's election 👇

EducationOctober 31, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 31, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

College organizers across New Hampshire are increasing voter outreach before Tuesday's election as students adjust to new identification requirements that prevent them from using school-issued IDs to prove their identity at the polls.

The change follows an October 22 U.S. Supreme Court order that restored enforcement of House Bill 323, which removed high school, college and university identification cards from New Hampshire's expressly accepted forms of voter identification. The underlying lawsuit challenging the law is continuing in federal court.

At Dartmouth College, the nonpartisan Dartmouth Civics organization says some students mistakenly believe the new rule means they cannot vote in New Hampshire. The group is emphasizing that eligible students can still vote but must meet the state's updated identification requirements. Organizers have held information events and registration drives and plan to lead groups of students to the polling location on Election Day. Similar outreach is occurring on other campuses.

Under current state guidance, accepted identification includes a driver's license from any state, a state-issued non-driver ID, a U.S. passport or passport card, a military ID, or a New Hampshire voting-only photo ID. The Secretary of State says voters without one of those IDs will be referred to election supervisors to determine whether an authorized local election official can verify their identity through personal knowledge.

Students registering for the first time face additional requirements to establish identity, age, U.S. citizenship and domicile. New Hampshire allows Election Day registration, and there is no minimum period someone must have lived in a community before registering.

The Supreme Court ruling was already covered by NSM; the new development is how campuses are responding as November 3 approaches. Dartmouth organizers say their immediate challenge is making sure students understand what changed and arrive with the documentation they need.
