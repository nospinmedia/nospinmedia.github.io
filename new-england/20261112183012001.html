🇺🇸 Rutland unveils new World War II memorialGranite monument honors 164 local service members who died in the war 👇

PoliticsNovember 12, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 12, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

More than 300 people gathered in Rutland, Vermont, on Veterans Day for the unveiling of a new World War II memorial honoring 164 service members from Rutland County who died during the war.

The approximately 10-foot-tall granite monument was dedicated Wednesday at Main Street Park, where it stands near the city's existing Vietnam Veterans Memorial. Across the top are the words "In memory of the greatest generation," followed by the names of the 164 local service members.

The Veterans Day ceremony brought together veterans, active-duty military personnel, families, residents and local officials. Rutland Mayor Tom Donahue and representatives of local veterans organizations spoke during the dedication about the people represented by the names carved into the memorial and the importance of preserving their stories.

Among those remembered was Jack Hawkins of Bomoseen, who enlisted in the Army and was killed during World War II. Organizers used individual stories like his to connect the monument's list of names with the families and communities affected by the war.

The new monument also fills a long-standing gap in Main Street Park, which already contained memorials recognizing those who served in World War I and the Vietnam War but lacked a dedicated memorial honoring Rutland County's World War II dead. Its placement alongside the other monuments creates a shared area commemorating local residents who served and died across different generations of American military service.

World War II ended more than 81 years ago, and the number of surviving veterans of the conflict continues to decline. Local memorial projects increasingly serve as permanent records of communities' wartime losses as firsthand connections to the generation disappear.

The Rutland monument was formally unveiled on Nov. 11, 2026, tying its dedication to Veterans Day and the annual recognition of Americans who have served in the armed forces.
