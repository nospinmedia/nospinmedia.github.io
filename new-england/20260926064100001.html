🏛️ Vermont Supreme Court clears return-to-office mandateState employees can again be required in person three days a week 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EconomySeptember 26, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 26, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Vermont Supreme Court unanimously sided with Gov. Phil Scott's administration on Friday, overturning a labor board decision and clearing the way for the state to require employees to work in person at least three days per week. The ruling ends a months-long legal fight over how much authority the administration has to change telework arrangements for state workers.

Scott's administration announced the return-to-office requirement in 2025, arguing that more in-person work would improve collaboration among employees and make state government more accessible to the public. The policy required many executive branch employees who had been working remotely to report to their worksites at least three days each week beginning in December.

The Vermont State Employees' Association challenged the mandate, arguing that the administration should have negotiated the change with the union. In April, the Vermont Labor Relations Board agreed with the union and ordered the state to rescind the requirement. The board also directed the state to reimburse affected workers for certain monetary losses associated with the policy and offer reinstatement to employees who had left because of it.

The state's highest court reversed that decision. The justices concluded that Vermont's existing negotiated telework policy already gives the state broad discretion over remote-work arrangements. Under the policy, telework is voluntary, requires approval from the employee's appointing authority and can be terminated by the employer. The court said the administration did not have to demonstrate a specific operational need before changing or ending a telework arrangement.

Union leaders criticized the decision, saying the mandate creates significant commuting, housing and childcare challenges for some employees. The union represents about 6,000 state workers, and a 2023 survey found more than 2,500 state employees were working remotely more than two days per week.
