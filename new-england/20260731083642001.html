📜 Massachusetts Legislature Approves Major Child Welfare And Foster Care Reform BillMeasure Expands Oversight, Creates Foster Youth Protections And Heads To Governor Healey 👇

EducationAugust 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 1, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The Massachusetts Legislature has approved a wide-ranging child welfare reform bill that strengthens oversight of the Department of Children and Families (DCF), expands the authority of the Office of the Child Advocate (OCA), and establishes new legal protections for children and teenagers in foster care. Lawmakers passed the legislation during the final hours of the 2025–2026 formal legislative session, sending it to Governor Maura Healey for consideration.

A central feature of the bill creates a statewide Foster Child Bill of Rights outlining protections for children in state custody, including educational stability, access to health care, communication with supportive adults, protection of personal belongings and participation in decisions affecting their care. The legislation also creates a dedicated DCF Education Unit to help reduce school disruptions and improve educational outcomes for foster youth.

The measure expands the investigative and oversight responsibilities of the Office of the Child Advocate, requiring additional reporting and accountability within the child welfare system. It also includes reforms to the Children Requiring Assistance (CRA) process by encouraging greater use of community-based Family Resource Centers to assist families before court involvement becomes necessary.

Additional provisions strengthen legal protections for minors seeking harassment prevention orders, improve data collection and reporting requirements, and require greater attention to accessibility and services for children with disabilities. Supporters said the legislation is intended to improve transparency, accountability and long-term outcomes for vulnerable children while providing additional resources to families facing behavioral and educational challenges.

The legislation now awaits action from Governor Healey, who may sign the bill, return it with amendments or allow it to become law without her signature under Massachusetts legislative procedures.
