⚖️ Bail Revoked for Former Healey Aide LaMar Cook After New Federal ChargesCook Returned to Jail While Facing Separate Drug and Gun Case 👇

EconomyOctober 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 16, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

A Massachusetts judge has revoked bail for former Gov. Maura Healey aide LaMar Cook after federal prosecutors charged him with fraudulently obtaining SNAP benefits while he was already awaiting trial on separate drug and gun charges.

Cook, 46, was ordered back into custody by a Hampden Superior Court judge after prosecutors argued that the new federal case violated the conditions of his release in the state criminal case. He had previously posted bail while facing charges stemming from an investigation that authorities say uncovered cocaine and firearms.

The federal case alleges Cook fraudulently obtained food assistance by providing false information about his income and household finances. Prosecutors say he received more than $500 per month in SNAP benefits while failing to disclose income and other payments that could have affected his eligibility.

Cook previously served as deputy director of western Massachusetts in Healey's administration. He was fired after his arrest in 2025 in the separate state case. His position paid approximately $130,000 annually.

In that case, prosecutors have accused Cook of trafficking cocaine and illegally possessing firearms. Those allegations have not been proven, and Cook has pleaded not guilty. The new federal charges are separate from the state prosecution.

Federal prosecutors allege that Cook continued receiving SNAP assistance while collecting money that included payments connected to his previous employment. He now faces federal counts involving wire fraud and SNAP fraud. Cook is presumed innocent unless proven guilty.

The bail revocation does not determine Cook's guilt in either case. Instead, it means he will remain in custody while the state prosecution proceeds because the judge found the new federal charges violated conditions imposed when he was released.

The development adds another legal complication to a case that has received attention because of Cook's former position in the Healey administration. He was no longer employed by the state when the latest federal charges were filed.
