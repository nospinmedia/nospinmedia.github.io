🔒 Vermont police dispatch and records system remains offlinePotential security incident forces agencies onto backup procedures across state 👇

PoliticsNovember 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 19, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A computer system used by nearly all Vermont law enforcement agencies remains offline after a potential security incident, forcing police and other first responders to use backup procedures while state officials and the system's vendor investigate what happened.

The affected platform, Valcour, handles computer-aided dispatch and records management for nearly all Vermont law enforcement agencies and supports many fire and EMS operations. It tracks incidents from the initial call through the final resolution and serves as a central repository for reports and other documentation.

Vendor CrossWind Technologies notified Vermont officials late Monday, November 16, of a potential security incident. The Vermont Department of Public Safety and Agency of Digital Services then helped take Valcour offline as a precaution and contained state systems connected to the platform.

Officials stressed that 911 and emergency responses are not affected. Vermonters should continue calling 911 normally during emergencies. Non-emergency department phone numbers also remain operational.

Police and other agencies have switched to backup procedures, including manual methods in some departments. Records and administrative work may take longer while Valcour is unavailable, and several police departments have reported disruptions to online reporting services. Other major criminal justice resources, including state criminal-history records and national crime-information databases, remain accessible to law enforcement.

State officials said Wednesday that Valcour will remain offline until the investigation and any necessary remediation are completed, and they did not expect the system to return that day. The nature and scope of the security incident remain under investigation, and officials have not publicly said whether data was accessed, stolen or altered.

The outage comes amid several unrelated technology and cybersecurity disruptions around New England. Officials said they have seen no evidence that the Valcour incident is connected to those events.

Because Valcour shares information among participating agencies to improve situational awareness and officer safety, the shutdown highlights the importance of maintaining workable backup procedures when centralized public-safety technology becomes unavailable.
