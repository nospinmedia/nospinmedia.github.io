⛈️ Multi-Day Rain Pattern Targets Southern New EnglandForecasters Warn of Localized Flooding Through Midweek 👇

EnvironmentAugust 31, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 31, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Forecasters are warning that a slow-moving weather system is expected to bring several rounds of showers and thunderstorms across southern New England beginning late August 31 and continuing through at least September 3. The National Weather Service Weather Prediction Center has highlighted parts of the region for a risk of excessive rainfall as a stalled frontal boundary combines with warm, humid air to produce repeated downpours. While Monday is expected to remain mostly dry, rain chances increase significantly Monday night before becoming more widespread on Tuesday and Wednesday.

Meteorologists expect many communities across Massachusetts, Connecticut, and Rhode Island to receive 1 to 2 inches of rain, with isolated locations potentially seeing higher totals where thunderstorms repeatedly move over the same areas. Localized flash flooding is possible, particularly in urban neighborhoods, poor-drainage locations, and along heavily traveled roadways, including portions of the Interstate 95 corridor. Frequent lightning, gusty winds, and brief periods of heavy rainfall are also possible with stronger storms.

Regional weather offices and local broadcasters have advised residents to monitor forecasts closely as the timing and placement of the heaviest rainfall could shift over the coming days. Some Connecticut forecasters have designated Tuesday and Wednesday as heightened weather alert days because of the potential for flooding and isolated severe thunderstorms. Officials recommend allowing extra travel time during the morning and evening commutes, reducing speed on wet roads, and avoiding driving through flooded streets.

Temperatures are expected to vary across New England as the stalled front separates cooler air to the north from warmer, more humid conditions farther south. That contrast is expected to help sustain the unsettled pattern until the front begins moving offshore later in the week, allowing drier weather to gradually return.
