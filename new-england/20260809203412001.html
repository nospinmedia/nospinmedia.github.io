⚖️ Former NH Trooper Pleads Guilty To False ReportGPS Tracker Case Ends With Permanent Law Enforcement Ban 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalAugust 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 9, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Former New Hampshire State Police Trooper James Lamoureux, 43, of Keene, pleaded guilty to one count of making a false report to law enforcement after a state investigation found he placed GPS tracking devices on vehicles and then lied to police about who installed them. Attorney General John Formella announced the plea on August 6, 2026, saying the case was resolved through a negotiated agreement in Keene District Court.

Investigators determined that Lamoureux placed GPS trackers on a vehicle regularly used by his then-girlfriend and on his own vehicle in September 2025 and downloaded a tracking application to his phone. After his girlfriend discovered a device and confronted him, Lamoureux told the Keene Police Department that he believed other people had placed the trackers on the vehicles, despite investigators finding that he had installed them himself. Authorities said he later asked an acquaintance to provide false information to Keene police about the devices.

Under the plea agreement, Lamoureux received a six-month House of Corrections sentence, fully suspended for two years. He also resigned from the New Hampshire State Police and surrendered his state law enforcement certification. The agreement places him on the national registry of decertified officers and prohibits him from seeking future employment as a law enforcement officer.

The investigation was conducted by the New Hampshire Department of Justice with assistance from the Keene Police Department. The case was prosecuted by Senior Assistant Attorney General Alexander J. Kellermann of the state's Public Integrity & White-Collar Crime Unit. The resolution ends Lamoureux's law enforcement career without requiring him to serve the suspended jail term unless he violates the conditions imposed during the next two years.
