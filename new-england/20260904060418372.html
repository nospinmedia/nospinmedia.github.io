📷 Revere officer suspended after misusing Flock camera systemRecords show more than a dozen searches involving former girlfriend 👇

New EnglandSeptember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 4, 2026 at 5:23 PM ET

A Revere, Massachusetts, police officer served a three-day unpaid suspension after an internal investigation found that he improperly used the department's Flock Safety automated license-plate reader system in searches involving a former girlfriend and members of her family. Officer Michael Tiso accessed the surveillance network more than a dozen times across four separate occasions, according to records detailing the department's investigation and disciplinary action.

The investigation began after Tiso's former girlfriend, Marissa Todisco, complained to authorities about possible misuse of police databases. Revere investigators examined Tiso's searches of the Flock system, which allows authorized users to search images and location records generated when cameras capture vehicle license plates. The department sustained findings that Tiso violated policies governing use of law-enforcement databases and surveillance tools. He served his unpaid suspension from August 24 through August 26 and subsequently returned to duty. The misconduct finding was also reported to the Massachusetts Peace Officer Standards and Training Commission, which oversees certification and discipline of police officers statewide.

Tiso disputed that he used the system to stalk or harass Todisco. He told investigators that his searches were connected to concerns that she had been following or harassing him. Records reviewed by the Boston Globe describe a complicated history of complaints and restraining-order proceedings involving the former couple. The department's disciplinary finding establishes that Tiso's searches violated its rules, but it does not establish every allegation concerning his motive for conducting them.

The case is the latest Massachusetts controversy involving police access to automated license-plate readers. In New Bedford, officials suspended use of the city's Flock network after Officer Emily Pacheco was accused of using the system to monitor a former girlfriend. Revere has chosen to continue using its cameras. Police officials said the department has added automated auditing designed to identify unusual search patterns and strengthen oversight of officers accessing the system.

Flock cameras can provide investigators with vehicle locations across networks of cameras, making access controls and audit trails particularly important when officers use the technology for searches involving people they know personally.
