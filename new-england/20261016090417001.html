✉️ Suspicious Letter Sent to New Hampshire Secretary of State InterceptedMailing Is Part of Nationwide Investigation Targeting Election and Congressional Offices 👇

File photoU.S. Capitol building in Washington, D.C.Credit: Architect of the Capitol
PoliticsOctober 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 16, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A suspicious letter containing a white substance and addressed to the New Hampshire Secretary of State's Office was intercepted by the U.S. Postal Service before reaching the office as federal authorities investigate similar mailings sent to election officials and members of Congress across the country.

The letter was discovered before delivery to the Secretary of State's Office in Concord, meaning employees there were not exposed to its contents. Secretary of State David Scanlan's office confirmed it had been notified about the mailing as authorities investigated its contents and origin.

The FBI and U.S. Postal Inspection Service are investigating suspicious letters sent to government offices in multiple states. Some have contained white powder or threatening messages, prompting hazardous-material responses and additional security precautions. Substances tested from several of the mailings elsewhere have not been found to be hazardous, although authorities continue examining individual letters.

The New Hampshire mailing comes less than three weeks before the November 3 midterm election, when Scanlan's office will oversee statewide election procedures and reporting. Authorities have not announced evidence that the letter disrupted election operations in New Hampshire.

Similar mailings have targeted election officials in other states as well as congressional offices. In neighboring Connecticut, a suspicious letter was sent to Democratic Sen. Chris Murphy's office as part of the broader investigation. Federal authorities are working to determine whether the letters are connected and who sent them.

The investigation follows earlier reports of suspicious white-powder mailings to election offices in Michigan, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin. The addition of New Hampshire and congressional offices indicates that the scope of the investigation has expanded beyond the states initially identified.

No injuries associated with the New Hampshire letter were reported, and authorities had not publicly identified a suspect or motive. The investigation remains active.
