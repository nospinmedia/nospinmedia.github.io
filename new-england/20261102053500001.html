🏥 Connecticut Sen. Chris Murphy hospitalized after strokeNew England lawmaker expected to make full recovery 👇

HealthNovember 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 2, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Connecticut Sen. Chris Murphy remains hospitalized after suffering a stroke while attending his son's hockey game Saturday, temporarily taking one of New England's most prominent lawmakers away from his Senate and campaign activities just before Election Day.

Murphy, 53, experienced numbness in his arm during the game and was taken to a local hospital, where doctors determined that he had suffered a stroke. He was later transferred to Johns Hopkins for additional monitoring and treatment.

His chief of staff, Allison Herwitt, said Murphy is awake and alert and responding well to treatment. His doctors are encouraged by his progress, and his office said they expect him to make a full recovery. No timetable has been announced for his release from the hospital or return to Senate duties.

Murphy has represented Connecticut in the U.S. Senate since 2013 after previously serving three terms in the U.S. House. He currently serves on the Senate Appropriations Committee and has been particularly active on gun policy and foreign affairs.

Although Murphy is not on the ballot in the 2026 midterm elections, he had been campaigning for Democratic candidates ahead of Tuesday's voting. His hospitalization interrupted those activities during the final weekend of the campaign.

The news drew responses across Connecticut and New England. Connecticut Sen. Richard Blumenthal offered support to Murphy and his family, while regional news organizations in Connecticut, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Maine and Rhode Island closely followed updates on his condition.

Murphy's office has not indicated that his condition will require an extended absence from the Senate, and its latest public statement remains optimistic about his recovery.
