🏘️ New Hampshire opens commercial zones to multifamily housingState law preserves local review for roads, water and sewage 👇

EconomyAugust 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 3, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

New Hampshire municipalities must now allow multifamily dwelling units on commercially zoned land under a statewide zoning law intended to create more housing near jobs and existing infrastructure. The requirement took effect on July 1, 2026, changing how cities and towns review residential proposals in districts previously reserved primarily for offices, stores and other commercial uses.

The mandate originated with House Bill 631, enacted in 2025, and is codified in RSA 674:79 and RSA 674:80. Lawmakers revised the framework during the 2026 session through House Bill 1010 and House Bill 1588, clarifying the authority municipalities retain when reviewing projects. Governor Kelly Ayotte approved HB 1010 on July 15, 2026, with its provisions applying retroactively from the law’s July 1 effective date.

Municipalities must permit multifamily units in commercial zones when adequate roads, water supplies and sewage systems are available or can be provided by the applicant. Local planning boards may require traffic-impact studies, confirmation that a project can connect to public water or develop an approved private supply, and evidence that wastewater can be handled legally. Boards may deny projects when road design cannot support the expected traffic, pedestrian safety is not adequately addressed, a reliable water source cannot be secured or sewage cannot be properly disposed of.

The statute does not eliminate site-plan review or ordinary height, setback and frontage rules. Municipalities may also restrict housing in commercial districts that permit industrial or manufacturing activities producing incompatible conditions such as noise, dust, vibration, odors or heavy transportation impacts. They may require some or all ground-floor space to remain dedicated to retail or similar uses.

Existing buildings converted through adaptive reuse receive additional protection. Municipalities must waive certain dimensional requirements when a building is converted to multifamily or mixed use without expanding its floor area, height or setbacks.

Supporters describe the policy as a way to place more homes near employment centers, while municipal officials have emphasized the need to protect infrastructure capacity and local public services.
