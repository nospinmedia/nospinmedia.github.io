⚖️ Kennedy family joins legal fight over Trump's proposed archNine relatives say 250-foot monument would harm Arlington gravesites and historic views 👇

LegalNovember 12, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 12, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Nine members of the Kennedy family are seeking to join federal litigation challenging President Trump's proposed 250-foot memorial arch near Arlington National Cemetery, arguing the project would permanently affect their relatives' gravesites and the surrounding memorial landscape.

The relatives filed their motion Tuesday in federal court in Washington. They include children and grandchildren of Robert F. Kennedy and Edward M. Kennedy, among them Kerry Kennedy, Rory Kennedy, Kathleen Kennedy Townsend, Joseph P. Kennedy II, Joseph P. Kennedy III and Edward M. Kennedy Jr. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. did not join the filing.

The family members argue that the arch, planned for Memorial Circle across the Potomac River from the Lincoln Memorial, would cast shadows over graves near President John F. Kennedy's Eternal Flame and disrupt longstanding sightlines between Arlington and the Lincoln Memorial.

They are asking to intervene in existing litigation brought by Washington, D.C., Virginia and Vietnam War veterans challenging the project. Opponents argue the administration lacks required congressional authorization and failed to follow legal procedures governing construction at the site.

The National Capital Planning Commission approved the project 8-4 on Nov. 5. Hours later, U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan temporarily prohibited construction-related activity for 14 days while the legal challenges proceed. Chutkan wrote that the plaintiffs were likely to succeed in showing that required procedures had not been followed.

The Trump administration disputes those arguments and says the challengers lack legal standing. The White House says the monument would commemorate 250 years of American independence and honor U.S. military service. President Trump said on Veterans Day that the arch could be dedicated to veterans and service members.

Plans call for an arch standing about 166 feet tall, topped by gilded figures that would bring the structure's total height to approximately 250 feet. The project would also include an observation deck and other interior spaces. Its final cost and funding arrangement have not been publicly established.

The Kennedy relatives are represented by the Democracy Defenders Fund. Their request to join the case does not itself determine whether construction can ultimately proceed; the broader legal challenge remains before the federal court.
