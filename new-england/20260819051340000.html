🚑 York Weighs $350K Emergency Ambulance FundingFinancial review recommends referendum to stabilize EMS operations 👇

HealthAugust 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 19, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The York Select Board is considering asking voters to approve up to $350,000 in emergency funding to help stabilize the York Ambulance Association (YAA) after a financial review concluded the nonprofit requires substantially more support than originally requested. The recommendation follows an analysis by the town's finance department, which found the ambulance service faces a projected funding shortfall through June 30, 2027, raising concerns about the long-term sustainability of emergency medical services in the community.

Earlier this summer, the association requested $180,000 in stabilization funding. After reviewing YAA's financial records, Finance Director Wendy Anderson reported that approximately $350,000 would be needed to maintain operations through the end of the current fiscal year. The analysis cited a projected $264,000 cash deficit along with a defaulted Small Business Administration (SBA) loan among the financial challenges affecting the organization.

Town Manager Peter Joseph advised the Select Board that the approved municipal budget does not contain funding for the request. Following the financial review, town officials recommended referring a special budget question to the Budget Committee that could allow voters to authorize up to $350,000 from the town's unassigned general fund balance. The proposal would require additional public review before any funding is approved.

York officials said emergency medical services will continue while discussions move forward, but the funding review highlights the financial pressures facing many independent ambulance providers as operating costs continue to increase. The forthcoming budget discussions will help determine how York plans to support emergency medical response while balancing long-term fiscal responsibilities.
