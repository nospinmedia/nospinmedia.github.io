🚒 Six-alarm fire tears through historic Roxbury buildingSeveral businesses displaced as crews battle blaze for hours 👇

EconomySeptember 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 29, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A massive six-alarm fire tore through the historic Edison Building at 72 Warren Street in Boston's Nubian Square on Monday, heavily damaging the 103-year-old commercial building and displacing several businesses. Firefighters spent hours surrounding the structure and pouring water onto the blaze as concerns grew that portions of the building could collapse.

The Boston Fire Department received the first report of smoke at about 7:30 a.m. September 28, and crews arrived within about 30 seconds. Firefighters initially entered the two-story building but were ordered outside after confirming that nobody remained inside and conditions became too dangerous. Ladder pipes and other large exterior streams were then used to attack the fire.

The response grew to six alarms, with approximately 200 firefighters involved, according to reporting on the emergency response. Crews had to draw water from as far as two blocks away, leaving thousands of feet of feeder hose across streets surrounding Nubian Square. The operation caused significant traffic disruptions around Warren, Harrison and Dudley streets while firefighters worked to contain the blaze.

Officials said the fire was largely knocked down to the basement by around noon, although crews continued working as smoke escaped from the building. Fire officials expressed significant concern about structural stability because of the amount of water poured into the property and a large billboard located on its roof. Several firefighters sustained minor injuries, according to the Boston Fire Department.

The Edison Building housed several small businesses, including a sandwich shop and barbershop. Roxbury Main Streets described the fire as devastating to the heart of Nubian Square, saying the businesses were part of the neighborhood's history and economic strength. Mayor Michelle Wu said the city would work to support businesses affected by the fire.

The cause of the blaze had not been publicly determined as firefighters continued operations Monday.
