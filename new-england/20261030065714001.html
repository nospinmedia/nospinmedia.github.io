🔫 14-year-old arrested with loaded ghost gun at Boston schoolPolice say weapon contained 10 rounds of live ammunition 👇

Illustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationOctober 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 30, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A 14-year-old Boston student was arrested after police say they found a loaded ghost gun in his waistband at Snowden International High School in Boston's Back Bay on Wednesday morning.

According to the Boston Police Department, officers with the School Engagement Unit were patrolling near the school at about 8:30 a.m. on October 28 when someone told them the school had learned that a student might have a firearm. An officer later saw the student walking between school campuses near Newbury and Exeter streets.

Police said a school resource officer also reported believing that a student had a firearm. Additional officers went to an office where the teenager was located and conducted a frisk, finding the weapon in his waistband. He was taken into custody without police reporting any injuries.

Police identified the firearm as a Smith & Wesson ghost gun loaded with 10 rounds of live ammunition. Ghost guns generally lack conventional serial numbers, making them more difficult for law enforcement to trace.

The teenager, who police said is from Roxbury, is expected to be arraigned in Boston Juvenile Court. Police said he faces juvenile delinquency charges for unlawful possession of a firearm, carrying a loaded firearm on school grounds, possession of a firearm without a license and possession of ammunition without an FID card.

Police have not publicly said how the student obtained the firearm or why he allegedly brought it to school. The Boston Globe reported that police had no indication the gun was used on school property.

The incident is not the first involving a firearm at Snowden. In January 2024, police arrested a 15-year-old student after authorities said he brought a loaded handgun to the same school.
