🏥 UVM Health and Dartmouth Health announce major workforce cutsMore than 600 filled and vacant positions affected across two networks 👇

EducationSeptember 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 16, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Two of the largest health systems serving Vermont patients announced major workforce reductions on September 15, with UVM Health eliminating approximately 199 positions and Dartmouth Health terminating 124 employees while canceling another 303 vacant positions. Together, the changes affect more than 600 filled and vacant positions across the two regional health networks as both confront financial pressure ahead of the new fiscal year.

At UVM Health, the reductions span its six hospitals in Vermont and New York, along with other network operations. Approximately 118 positions are being affected at University of Vermont Medical Center in Burlington. The medical center also plans to close a general medicine unit and consolidate an alternative care unit. UVM Health said the latest reductions are expected to save approximately $16.8 million annually. About 55 of the 199 positions will eventually be replaced by newly structured jobs, meaning the net reduction is approximately 144 positions.

Dartmouth Health said 124 employees are losing their jobs and another 303 open positions at Dartmouth Hitchcock Medical Center in Lebanon, New Hampshire, and regional clinics are being eliminated. The network is also ending several programs, including its Tele-ICU and Tele-ED services, and plans to eliminate the New England Alliance for Health, a collaborative involving hospitals, behavioral health centers and home health agencies across northern New England. Dartmouth Health reported a $63.5 million operating loss during the six months ending in March.

The cuts are particularly significant for Vermont because approximately 40% of Dartmouth Hitchcock's patients are Vermonters, while UVM Health is the state's largest health care provider. UVM Health has also been dealing with substantial financial losses and has warned of a financial gap exceeding $300 million over three years.

The announcements came one day after Vermont's Green Mountain Care Board ordered UVM Medical Center to reduce commercial reimbursement rates by 4.4% for the coming fiscal year. UVM Health said its latest workforce reductions were already being planned before that decision. Union representatives said approximately 90 union positions are affected by the UVM cuts and raised concerns about maintaining patient care as staffing is reduced.
