⚡ Five New England States Challenge Utility MergerOfficials warn $66.8B deal could raise regional energy costs 👇

IllustrationExecutives review a major corporate merger agreement.Credit: AI-generated illustration (No Spin Media)
EnvironmentAugust 20, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 20, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Officials from Maine, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Vermont have jointly urged federal regulators to closely scrutinize NextEra Energy's proposed $66.8 billion acquisition of Dominion Energy, arguing the merger could reduce competition across New England's electricity markets and ultimately increase costs for consumers. The multistate filing represents one of the strongest coordinated regional challenges to the proposed transaction since it was announced in May 2026.

The coalition argues that combining two of the nation's largest energy companies would give a single corporation significant control over electric generation, transmission assets, and wholesale power markets that influence electricity prices throughout New England. Officials warned that greater market concentration could make it more difficult for states to maintain competitive electricity markets while pursuing clean energy goals and protecting ratepayers from higher bills. The filing asks the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) to carefully examine the merger's impact before making a decision.

The proposed acquisition would create one of the world's largest regulated electric utilities and combine extensive energy infrastructure across multiple regions. Critics argue the deal could reduce competition at a time when electricity demand is rising because of data centers, electrification, and other growing power needs. Supporters of the merger, including the companies, contend the larger organization would have greater financial resources, improved operational efficiency, and increased ability to invest in electric infrastructure while maintaining reliable service.

The latest filing adds to growing regulatory scrutiny surrounding the merger. Maine Gov. Janet Mills has warned the transaction could make it harder to lower electricity costs in New England, while other public officials have sought additional commitments on affordability, competition, and clean energy investments before offering support. The acquisition remains subject to multiple federal and state regulatory reviews before it can move forward.
