🌡️ 86-year-old woman found dead in 99-degree assisted living roomHopkinton officials investigate heating problem as cause of death remains unknown 👇

HealthOctober 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 8, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Authorities are investigating after an 86-year-old woman was found dead inside an apartment at Golden Pond Assisted Living in Hopkinton, Massachusetts, where first responders reported an indoor temperature of approximately 99 degrees. Officials have not determined that the excessive heat caused her death, and the woman's cause and manner of death remain under investigation.

Hopkinton police and firefighters responded to the facility on West Main Street on September 30 after the resident was found unresponsive. According to police records obtained by local news organizations, responders immediately noticed that her room was unusually hot. A thermostat in the apartment displayed 99 degrees, and firefighters used thermal imaging equipment while examining conditions inside the unit.

Facility staff told investigators that Golden Pond's building-wide heating and cooling system had recently been switched from air conditioning to heat as temperatures outside became cooler. According to the police report, staff explained that once the central system is switched to heating, residents who leave their individual thermostats set to cooling can instead receive hot air. Police also documented concerns about whether the woman's thermostat had been left in that configuration.

The Hopkinton Health Department subsequently inspected Golden Pond and found additional apartments with elevated temperatures, according to local reporting. Officials worked with facility management to address the heating conditions and check on residents. Golden Pond said it was cooperating with authorities and expressed condolences to the woman's family.

The Massachusetts Office of the Chief Medical Examiner is responsible for determining the cause and manner of the woman's death. Until that examination is completed, authorities have not established whether room temperature, an underlying medical condition or another factor caused or contributed to her death.
