⚖️ Massachusetts judge publicly reprimanded over conduct in 2018 ICE caseSJC finds no evidence Shelley Joseph knew of courthouse escape plan 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
LegalSeptember 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 11, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court has issued a public reprimand to Judge Shelley Joseph, ending a years-long disciplinary case stemming from a 2018 Newton District Court incident involving a defendant sought by federal immigration authorities.

Joseph had been accused of helping the man avoid arrest by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement after a court hearing. But the disciplinary proceedings did not establish that she knew about or authorized the plan by the defendant's lawyer and a court officer to take him through a rear courthouse exit. The court instead disciplined Joseph for her own conduct during the hearing.

The SJC found that Joseph created an appearance of impropriety when she suggested the defendant could potentially be held overnight in state custody despite lacking a lawful basis to do so and when she directed a clerk to switch off the courtroom's audio recording system during part of the discussion. The recording was off for approximately 52 seconds.

A hearing officer appointed during the judicial-conduct proceedings concluded that Joseph did not know about the escape plan and recommended a public reprimand rather than suspension or removal. The Commission on Judicial Conduct subsequently sought the same sanction, and the state's highest court adopted that recommendation.

The controversy began after an ICE officer waited outside the Newton courthouse on April 2, 2018, intending to arrest the defendant. Federal prosecutors charged Joseph and a court officer with obstruction-related offenses in 2019. Prosecutors dropped the case against Joseph in 2022 after she agreed to have the allegations reviewed through Massachusetts' judicial disciplinary system.

Joseph's attorney has argued that the case became distorted publicly and said the disciplinary record demonstrated that Joseph did not participate in the rear-door plan. The SJC nevertheless concluded that a public reprimand was appropriate because judges must avoid conduct that could reasonably call their impartiality or adherence to the law into question.

Joseph has already spent more than three years suspended from judicial duties during the controversy and has since returned to administrative responsibilities within the Boston Municipal Court system.
