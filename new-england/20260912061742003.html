⚖️ Clancy jurors reveal 11–1 split favoring insanity acquittalDefense asks judge to prevent a second murder trial 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthSeptember 12, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 12, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Jurors from the Lindsay Clancy murder trial say deliberations ended 11–1 in favor of finding her not guilty by reason of lack of criminal responsibility, revealing how close the Massachusetts case came to an acquittal before a mistrial was declared. Plymouth Superior Court Judge William Sullivan ended the trial on September 4 after seven days of deliberations and repeated notes from jurors saying they could not reach a unanimous verdict.

Clancy, 36, admitted killing her three children — Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months — in their Duxbury home on January 24, 2023. Her attorneys argued that she was experiencing postpartum psychosis and was unable to appreciate the wrongfulness of her actions or conform her behavior to the law. Prosecutors argued that she remained criminally responsible and had deliberately planned the killings.

Several jurors have now publicly described the deliberations. Juror Paula Devlin told the Boston Globe that the panel eventually reached an 11–1 split for acquittal after reviewing psychiatric evidence and a videotaped forensic interview with Clancy. Jury foreperson Roni Carlson separately said the lone holdout acknowledged having reasonable doubt but would not agree to a verdict finding Clancy not criminally responsible. Other jurors have criticized the holdout's participation in deliberations, although the juror has not publicly responded.

The mistrial leaves prosecutors with the option of trying Clancy again, but her attorney, Kevin Reddington, is now asking Sullivan to acquit her rather than allow a second trial. Reddington argues that the prosecution failed to produce sufficient evidence that Clancy was criminally responsible at the time of the killings. The Plymouth County District Attorney's Office has not announced whether it intends to retry the case.

Clancy remains in a state psychiatric hospital. Sullivan is expected to consider the defense motion at a court hearing scheduled for September 29.
