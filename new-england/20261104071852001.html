🗳️ Troy Jackson ends Susan Collins' nearly 30-year Senate runMaine elects its first Democratic U.S. senator since 1988 👇

EconomyNovember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 4, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Democrat Troy Jackson defeated Republican Sen. Susan Collins in Maine, ending her nearly 30-year career in the U.S. Senate and giving the state its first Democratic Senate election victory since 1988.

Collins conceded early Wednesday after seeking a sixth term. First elected in 1996, she went on to win five consecutive Senate elections and became one of Maine's longest-serving members of Congress. Her defeat closes a political career in which she built a national reputation as a Republican willing at times to break with her party while accumulating substantial seniority in the Senate.

Jackson enters the Senate from a markedly different background. A fifth-generation logger from Aroostook County, he previously served as president of the Maine Senate and built his campaign around working-class and economic issues. His victory makes him the first Democrat elected to represent Maine in the U.S. Senate since George Mitchell won reelection in 1988.

The result also represents a significant change in Maine's congressional representation. Collins had remained electorally durable through repeated shifts in the national political environment, winning reelection in 2002, 2008, 2014 and 2020 after her initial 1996 victory.

This year's contest drew national attention because Collins was one of the longest-serving Republican senators seeking reelection and because control of the Senate was at stake. Jackson's victory gave Democrats a Republican-held seat as the party accumulated enough victories nationally to take control of the chamber.

For Maine, however, the result is also the end of an unusually long period of continuity. Collins has represented the state in Washington since January 1997. When the new Congress begins in January 2027, that seat will have a new senator for the first time in three decades.
