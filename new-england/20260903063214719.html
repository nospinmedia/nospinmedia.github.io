⚖️ Clancy jury deadlocks again in Massachusetts trialJudge orders deliberations to continue 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthSeptember 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 3, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The jury in the Lindsay Clancy murder trial has reported for a second time that it cannot reach a unanimous verdict, prompting a Plymouth Superior Court judge to instruct jurors to continue deliberating. The panel has now spent nearly 30 hours over five days considering whether Clancy is criminally responsible for the January 24, 2023, deaths of her three children: Cora, Dawson, and Callan Clancy.

On September 2, Judge William F. Sullivan delivered Massachusetts' Tuey-Rodriguez instruction, a standard judicial instruction used when jurors report they are deadlocked. The instruction encourages jurors to re-examine the evidence, listen carefully to one another's views, and make every reasonable effort to reach a unanimous verdict without abandoning honestly held convictions. Deliberations are scheduled to continue into a sixth day.

Earlier in the day, the judge individually questioned all deliberating jurors and alternate jurors after a spectator was arrested on allegations of photographing members of the jury outside the courthouse. Court officials said the inquiry was conducted to ensure no juror had been improperly influenced or intimidated before deliberations resumed. The incident did not result in any juror being removed from the panel.

During the trial, prosecutors argued that Clancy intentionally killed her children and understood the nature of her actions. Defense attorneys argued that she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis and the effects of multiple prescribed medications, leaving her unable to appreciate the wrongfulness of her conduct. If the jury remains unable to reach a unanimous verdict after further deliberations, the court will determine the appropriate next steps under Massachusetts law.
