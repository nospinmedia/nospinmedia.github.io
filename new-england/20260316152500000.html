🚨 Maine Courts Order ICE ReleasesDozens detained in January immigration sweep return home 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
LegalMarch 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published March 16, 2026 at 7:44 AM ET

Federal court rulings are leading to the release of dozens of migrants detained during a major January immigration enforcement operation in Maine, after judges determined many of the individuals posed neither a public safety threat nor a significant risk of fleeing while their asylum cases proceed.

The arrests were part of a large immigration enforcement action known as "Operation Catch of the Day," conducted by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) in January. The sweep resulted in 206 arrests across Maine, according to reporting from regional outlets and immigration attorneys involved in the cases.

Many of the individuals detained during the operation were asylum-seekers from countries including Angola and the Democratic Republic of Congo, according to lawyers representing the detainees. Attorneys say a significant number of those arrested had pending asylum applications, valid work permits, and Social Security numbers, allowing them to legally work while awaiting immigration court proceedings.

Following the arrests, immigration lawyers filed a wave of legal challenges arguing that the detainees should not remain in custody. Federal judges reviewing the cases have ordered dozens of migrants released, concluding in multiple hearings that the individuals did not pose a danger to the community and were unlikely to evade immigration court appearances.

Many of the legal challenges were filed as habeas corpus petitions, a legal mechanism used to challenge detention in federal court. Immigration attorneys say more than 18,000 habeas petitions related to immigration detention have been filed nationally during the first year of the current administration, exceeding the combined total filed during the previous three presidential administrations.

The January arrests also created logistical complications for detainees and their legal teams because many migrants were transferred out of Maine shortly after their arrest. Due to limited detention capacity in the state, ICE moved some detainees to facilities in Massachusetts and New Hampshire, requiring attorneys and families to navigate multiple jurisdictions while pursuing release hearings.

By early March, reporting from the Portland Press Herald indicated that dozens of detainees had already returned to their homes and communities following court rulings. Advocates for the migrants say the decisions highlight the role of the federal courts in reviewing immigration detention practices and ensuring that detention decisions comply with legal standards.

ICE officials have defended the enforcement operation as part of broader efforts to strengthen immigration enforcement and ensure compliance with federal immigration law. The agency has said its operations focus on individuals who are subject to removal from the United States.

The Maine cases illustrate the tension between immigration enforcement actions and the legal protections available to asylum-seekers who are allowed to remain in the United States while their claims are being processed in immigration courts.
