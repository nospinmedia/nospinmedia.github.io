☀️ Maine fines Bristol solar project nearly $94,000Failed erosion controls sent sediment into nearby wetlands 👇

EnvironmentNovember 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 9, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Maine environmental regulators have fined Overlook Solar Partners nearly $94,000 after erosion controls failed at its solar development in Bristol and sediment-laden runoff polluted nearby wetlands.

The Maine Board of Environmental Protection approved a consent agreement between the company and the state last week. According to the settlement, erosion and sediment-control measures at the project's 5-megawatt solar site failed in 2022, allowing polluted stormwater runoff to leave the construction area and enter wetlands.

The company later corrected the violations and stabilized the site, according to the settlement. Overlook Solar Partners is an affiliate of Luminace Holdings, which is owned by global investment company Brookfield. Maine Public reported that a Luminace spokesperson did not respond to a request for comment about the enforcement action.

The Bristol case is part of a broader pattern of environmental enforcement involving Maine's rapid expansion of community solar. A review of Maine Department of Environmental Protection records by Maine Public found the state has finalized settlements with at least six solar developers since 2022, with nearly all involving erosion controls or stormwater runoff. Another settlement involving Fryeburg Solar was proposed in October.

Overlook is not the only Luminace-related project to face a penalty. Another subsidiary, Tower Solar Partners, was fined $236,000 last year for violations at a project in Embden that resulted in sediment pollution reaching the Kennebec River.

Altogether, Maine has recovered nearly $660,000 in penalties from the solar-related settlements reviewed by Maine Public. The violations occurred during a period of rapid solar construction encouraged by subsidized electric rates for community solar projects of 5 megawatts or less. Maine scaled back those incentives last year.

The Bristol solar facility entered commercial operation in December 2023, after the violations occurred during construction.
