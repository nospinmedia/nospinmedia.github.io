🗳️ New Hampshire polls point to split-ticket electionPappas leads Senate race while Ayotte leads governor contest 👇

EducationNovember 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 1, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

New Hampshire voters could produce different partisan outcomes in two of the state's biggest races on November 3, with recent polling consistently showing Democrat Chris Pappas ahead in the U.S. Senate contest while Republican Gov. Kelly Ayotte leads her reelection race.

A UMass Lowell/YouGov poll released October 28 found Pappas receiving 48% support among likely voters compared with 41% for Republican John E. Sununu. Constitution Party candidate Edmond LaPlante received 4%, while 7% were undecided. In the governor's race, the same voters gave Ayotte 48% to Democrat Cinde Warmington's 42%, with 7% undecided. The survey included 600 likely voters, was conducted October 2-19 and had an adjusted margin of error of ±5 percentage points.

Other recent polling shows the same cross-party pattern. An October Saint Anselm College survey found Pappas at 50% and Sununu at 43%, while Ayotte led Warmington 50% to 42%. An earlier Saint Anselm poll conducted for AARP New Hampshire found Pappas ahead 51% to 44% and Ayotte ahead 52% to 45%.

The AARP survey also provided evidence of voters considering candidates from different parties. Among unaffiliated voters, Pappas held a substantially larger advantage in the Senate contest, while Ayotte held a narrower advantage in the governor's race. AARP found that roughly two-thirds of voters identified as ticket-splitters were age 50 or older.

New Hampshire has elected candidates from different parties to major statewide offices in recent elections. Democratic Sen. Jeanne Shaheen, whose retirement created this year's open Senate contest, won reelection in 2020 while Republican Chris Sununu won the governor's race on the same ballot.

Polls measure voter preferences during their survey periods and do not predict the election result, particularly with undecided voters remaining in both races.
