✉️ Suspicious Letter Forces Evacuation of Sen. Susan Collins' Portland OfficeUnknown Substance Is Investigated as Similar Mailings Target Political Offices 👇

PoliticsOctober 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 17, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

U.S. Sen. Susan Collins' office in Portland, Maine, was evacuated after receiving a suspicious letter containing an unknown substance, adding another New England political office to a growing series of suspicious mailings under investigation.

The letter arrived at Collins' constituent services office on October 16. Staff contacted authorities after discovering the substance, prompting a response from Portland police, firefighters and hazardous-materials personnel. The office was evacuated while emergency crews secured the area and examined the mailing.

The FBI took possession of the envelope for additional testing and investigation. Authorities had not publicly identified the substance or announced that anyone had been injured or exposed.

Another suspicious envelope addressed to Collins was intercepted before reaching her Washington office, according to reports. The incidents come amid heightened concern over suspicious letters being sent to government and political offices around the country.

New Hampshire officials recently reported that a suspicious letter addressed to the New Hampshire Secretary of State's Office was intercepted before reaching the office. That mailing was linked to a broader federal investigation involving suspicious letters sent to election officials and members of Congress in several states.

Collins' office has also dealt with other security incidents during the current election season. Law enforcement agencies have not publicly established that all of those incidents are connected.

Federal authorities, including the FBI and U.S. Postal Inspection Service, have been investigating suspicious mailings sent to political and election offices. Investigators are working to determine who sent the letters, whether the substances inside them are dangerous and whether the mailings are part of a coordinated campaign.
