🦠 Healey blames Trump administration after U.S. loses measles elimination statusMassachusetts governor says state will maintain science-based vaccine guidance 👇

EducationNovember 18, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 18, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey is criticizing the Trump administration after the United States lost its measles elimination status for the first time since 2000, while emphasizing that Massachusetts will continue its own vaccination recommendations and access programs.

The Pan American Health Organization announced November 17 that the United States no longer qualifies as having eliminated measles after related strains of the virus circulated continuously for at least 12 months. CDC data through November 12 show 5,412 confirmed U.S. cases, including 591 hospitalizations, or 11% of cases. The CDC has confirmed 6 deaths, while state health departments have reported as many as 10 measles-associated deaths during 2026.

Healey called the loss of elimination status "unacceptable" and accused President Donald Trump and Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. of ignoring public-health experts and spreading misinformation. Kennedy, responding Tuesday, attributed declining vaccination rates partly to disruptions during the COVID-19 pandemic and pledged federal support for outbreak response and treatment guidance.

Massachusetts has reported 2 measles cases in 2026. About 96.6% of Massachusetts kindergartners received recommended MMR vaccinations, above both the approximately 95% level generally considered necessary to prevent sustained measles transmission and the 92.4% national kindergarten average for the 2025–2026 school year. Coverage is lower in some individual communities and schools.

Across New England, Maine has reported 6 cases, Connecticut 3, Massachusetts and Rhode Island 2 each, while Vermont has also reported cases, including a November cluster in Orleans County. New Hampshire has reported none.

CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz said the agency has invested $100 million in state and local health departments and continues to recommend the MMR vaccine as the most effective way to prevent measles. CDC figures show 96% of confirmed cases involved people who were unvaccinated or whose vaccination status was unknown.

Massachusetts has maintained state vaccine guidance aligned with the American Academy of Pediatrics when it differs from federal recommendations.
