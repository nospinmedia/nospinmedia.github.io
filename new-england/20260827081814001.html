⚖️ Boston boater indicted in fatal Logan Harbor crashGrand jury charges man with manslaughter and drunken boating death 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalAugust 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 27, 2026 at 11:47 AM ET

A Suffolk County grand jury has indicted Lawrence Shieh, 40, of Boston, on multiple felony charges stemming from a fatal boating crash in Boston Harbor that killed Elizabeth "Lizzie" Dankert, 24, and seriously injured two other passengers. Announcing the indictment on August 25, Suffolk County District Attorney Kevin Hayden said Shieh faces charges including involuntary manslaughter, operating a vessel while under the influence causing death, operating a vessel while under the influence causing serious bodily injury, and several additional boating offenses. Shieh was arraigned in Suffolk Superior Court and entered not guilty pleas to all charges.

According to prosecutors, the crash occurred shortly before 11:30 p.m. on May 13 when a powerboat struck Pier 4R, a structure supporting Runway 4R at Boston Logan International Airport. Investigators allege the vessel was traveling about 36 mph when it entered a restricted area and collided with the pier. All four occupants were thrown from the boat. Dankert, an Andover native and recent Union College graduate, later died from her injuries at Massachusetts General Hospital, while two other passengers suffered serious injuries.

Prosecutors allege Shieh's blood alcohol concentration measured approximately 0.20%, more than twice Massachusetts' legal limit for operating a boat. Court filings also allege the vessel, owned by Freedom Boat Club, had been taken after normal operating hours without authorization. Defense attorneys disputed portions of the prosecution's account during the arraignment and said they intend to challenge the evidence as the case moves forward.

The indictment follows a months-long investigation by the Massachusetts Environmental Police, the Massachusetts State Police, the U.S. Coast Guard, and other agencies. If convicted on the most serious charges, Shieh could face substantial prison time. The case will now proceed through pretrial hearings in Suffolk Superior Court.
