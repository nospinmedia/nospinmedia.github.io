🦃 Massachusetts turkey farm shut down days before ThanksgivingHealth officials cite 'imminent health hazard' as customers scramble for alternatives 👇

EconomyNovember 25, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 25, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Customers of a longtime Massachusetts turkey farm are being told to make other Thanksgiving plans after local health officials ordered the business closed over conditions they described as an "imminent health hazard."

The Seekonk Board of Health notified Belwing Turkey Farm on Monday, November 23, that its food establishment permit was being immediately suspended. The town publicly announced the action Tuesday, just two days before Thanksgiving. The farm, now in its 82nd year, said it could not sell turkeys Tuesday or Wednesday and that employees were contacting customers with existing orders to tell them to find alternatives.

An official Board of Health memorandum provides more detail about the conditions behind the suspension. Officials cited unpermitted processing of poultry, attempted resealing of packaging, tables unsuitable for food contact, an excessive number of flies in a processing shed, and a lack of demonstrated food-safety knowledge. A follow-up inspection on Monday, November 23, found no live birds at the farm.

The farm had also drawn scrutiny earlier in the year. At a January 2026 Board of Health meeting, Assistant Health Agent Ryan Daw reported concerns that included turkeys being kept at room temperature, pests and a foul odor. Daw reportedly described the conditions he encountered as among the worst he had seen during 25 years in the industry.

The November findings prompted an immediate permit suspension rather than a routine inspection notice. The Town of Seekonk said the Board of Health determined that conditions constituted an imminent health hazard under state food-safety requirements.

Belwing Turkey Farm apologized for the last-minute disruption. The business draws customers from southeastern Massachusetts and neighboring Rhode Island, leaving people who had reserved birds with little time to find replacements before Thanksgiving.

Officials have not announced when the farm will be permitted to reopen. The closure does not by itself establish that anyone became ill from food purchased there, and authorities have not reported an outbreak associated with the farm's turkeys.
