🚧 New Hampshire Lawmakers Prepare High-Stakes Vote On Highway PlanAug. 19 veto session will decide fate of state's 10-year transportation program 👇

EconomyAugust 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 17, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

New Hampshire lawmakers are preparing for a pivotal August 19 veto session that will determine whether the Legislature can override Gov. Kelly Ayotte's veto of the state's 2027–2036 Ten-Year Transportation Improvement Plan. The proposal, approved earlier this year as House Bill 2026, outlines highway, bridge and turnpike projects across the state while authorizing future toll adjustments intended to fund long-term transportation improvements.

Gov. Ayotte vetoed the measure after objecting to provisions allowing higher turnpike tolls, saying she would not support additional costs for motorists. Lawmakers from both parties argue the funding mechanism is necessary to keep major infrastructure projects on schedule and preserve the state's long-term transportation planning. Republican and Democratic legislators have indicated there may be enough bipartisan support to attempt an override when the House and Senate reconvene.

Supporters of the transportation plan say delaying it could postpone improvements to heavily traveled corridors, including projects involving Interstates 89 and 93 near Concord and upgrades to Interstate 293 in Manchester. Construction groups and transportation advocates warn that delaying approved projects could increase future costs because of inflation and create uncertainty for municipalities planning local infrastructure work. Opponents of raising tolls argue the state should identify alternative funding sources rather than increase costs for drivers.

Because overriding a gubernatorial veto requires a two-thirds vote in both legislative chambers, the outcome remains uncertain. If lawmakers fail to override the veto, New Hampshire will continue operating without the updated 10-year transportation plan until a revised proposal is approved in a future legislative session. The Aug. 19 vote is expected to be one of the Legislature's most significant decisions before lawmakers adjourn for the summer.
