🎤 Macklemore dropped from Ed Sheeran tour after venue backlashGillette owner Robert Kraft confirms decision to bar rapper from Foxborough shows 👇

New EnglandSeptember 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 15, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Rapper Macklemore has been removed from the remaining U.S. dates of Ed Sheeran's Loop Tour after venues objected to his participation following pro-Palestinian comments he made during a September 4 performance at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey. The decision includes Sheeran's September 25 and 26 concerts at Gillette Stadium in Foxborough, Massachusetts, where Macklemore had been scheduled to open.

Macklemore said Sheeran told him that New England Patriots and Gillette Stadium owner Robert Kraft had called to say the rapper would not be allowed to perform at Gillette. Macklemore further alleged that Kraft contacted other stadium owners and that multiple venues threatened not to host Sheeran's concerts if Macklemore remained on the lineup. Reporting confirms that several venues objected and that tour promoter Messina Touring Group removed Macklemore rather than risk cancellations, but the full extent of Kraft's role with other venues has not been independently established.

Kraft confirmed his decision to exclude Macklemore from the Foxborough concerts. He said Gillette Stadium should not provide a platform for what he considers hate speech and cited Macklemore's recent stage material along with what Kraft described as a broader history of antisemitic rhetoric and imagery. Kraft said the decision was not intended to minimize Palestinian suffering or prevent people from advocating for Palestinians, but argued that such advocacy should not come at the expense of the Jewish community.

The controversy followed Macklemore's MetLife performance, where he called for a "Free Palestine" and criticized Israel while addressing the war in Gaza. Macklemore has rejected accusations that his advocacy is antisemitic, arguing that criticism of Israel and Zionism should not be treated as hostility toward Jewish people. He said losing the tour dates would not change his support for Palestinians.

The dispute has drawn competing reactions from advocacy groups and turned what began as a concert controversy into a broader debate over political expression, private venues and where owners should draw the line between advocacy and speech they consider discriminatory.
