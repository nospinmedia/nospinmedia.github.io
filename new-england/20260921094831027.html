🪲 Another spotted lanternfly found in New HampshireOfficials set traps near Derry vineyard 👇

New EnglandSeptember 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 21, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Another spotted lanternfly has been found in Salem, New Hampshire, weeks after state officials confirmed the invasive insect's first field detection in the Granite State. Mike Appolo, owner and winemaker of Appolo Vineyards, told WMUR that he spotted the distinctive insect flying outside his wine bar in Salem, captured it in a beer glass, photographed it and killed it.

The sighting is drawing particular attention because Appolo operates a vineyard in nearby Derry. Following the discovery, the New Hampshire Department of Agriculture placed traps among the vineyard's rows to watch for additional insects. State officials said there is currently no evidence of an established spotted lanternfly population in Derry.

New Hampshire agriculture officials confirmed the state's first field detection of spotted lanternflies in Salem in August 2026, when a small number of adults were observed and specimens were confirmed by federal identifiers. State and federal partners began surveys to determine whether the pest had become established. New Hampshire's invasive-species reporting program continues to say there is no evidence of an established population in the state.

The spotted lanternfly is native to Asia and has spread through parts of the northeastern United States since being detected in Pennsylvania in 2014. The insect feeds on sap from numerous plants, but grapevines are particularly vulnerable. Heavy feeding can weaken vines, reduce fruit sugar accumulation and delay ripening. New Hampshire officials also warn that the insects can hitchhike on vehicles, landscaping materials, plants and other goods, allowing them to spread into new areas.

Residents who encounter a suspected spotted lanternfly are being asked to photograph it, record the location and report the sighting through New Hampshire's invasive-species reporting system.
