🏛️ Connecticut Officials Debunk False Voter Claims After Fatal CrashMistaken identity led to threats against an innocent resident 👇

LegalAugust 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 6, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Connecticut election officials and law enforcement leaders are condemning online misinformation after a case of mistaken identity falsely linked an innocent voter to the fatal July 13, 2026 crash that killed off-duty Bridgeport Police Officer Cooper Whiteside and his longtime partner, Brittany Islami. Officials say inaccurate claims spread widely online after the suspect, Melissa Esperanza Ramirez of Stamford, was confused with a different Connecticut woman who shares the same name and birth date.

Ramirez, 27, is charged with two counts of second-degree manslaughter, driving under the influence, traveling unreasonably fast and other offenses in connection with the crash on Sugar Hollow Road near the Danbury-Ridgefield line. Investigators allege she crossed into oncoming traffic while impaired, striking the victims' motorcycle. She has pleaded not guilty, and the criminal case remains pending. Authorities later acknowledged that an address associated with the unrelated New Britain woman appeared in court paperwork, fueling the confusion.

Secretary of the State Stephanie Thomas said her office confirmed the Stamford defendant has no Connecticut voter registration record, while the New Britain resident whose information circulated online is a U.S. citizen with no connection to the crash. Officials said the misinformation resulted in harassment and threats directed at the innocent woman and urged the public to verify information before sharing it. Danbury police also warned that multiple people with similar names may have been incorrectly identified online.

State officials said the incident demonstrates how errors in identifying individuals can rapidly spread through social media and public discourse, causing harm to uninvolved people while undermining confidence in public institutions. They urged residents to rely on verified information from official sources as the criminal case proceeds.
