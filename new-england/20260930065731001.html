🪵 New England families turn to firewood as heating oil prices surgeConnecticut prices approach $6 while Maine hits record high 👇

File photoAn oil pumpjack operates at a petroleum production site.Credit: PickPik
EconomySeptember 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 30, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

With winter approaching, some New England homeowners are turning to firewood to reduce their dependence on increasingly expensive heating oil. Connecticut firewood sellers are reporting increased demand as households look for ways to control heating costs, while oil prices across the region remain sharply elevated.

In Connecticut, heating oil prices are now commonly above $5.50 per gallon, with some suppliers approaching or exceeding $6 per gallon. Recent supplier data show prices varying substantially by location and dealer, adding hundreds of dollars to the cost of filling a typical home heating-oil tank compared with lower prices earlier in the year.

The situation is even more pronounced in Maine, where the statewide average reached a record $6.02 per gallon in late September, according to the Maine Department of Energy Resources. Maine is particularly vulnerable to heating-oil increases because a larger share of its homes rely on the fuel than in any other state. Maine Public reported that prices were roughly 81% higher than a year earlier, meaning a 275-gallon tank could cost nearly $750 more to fill than last fall.

Global energy disruptions are a major factor behind the increases. The war involving Iran, disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the continuing Russia-Ukraine war and limited global refining capacity have pushed up prices for distillate fuels such as diesel and heating oil. The increases have prompted lawmakers from both parties to call for additional federal assistance and possible use of the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve.

For households with wood stoves or fireplaces capable of safely providing supplemental heat, firewood can reduce the amount of heating oil consumed. But increased demand can also push up wood prices and tighten supplies as colder weather approaches, making early preparation increasingly important for families that rely on either fuel.
