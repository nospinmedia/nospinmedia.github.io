📱 New Hampshire Launches School Screen Time Task ForceState Expands Review Beyond Cellphones To Classroom Technology 👇

EducationAugust 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 14, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The New Hampshire Department of Education (NHED) has launched a statewide School Screen Time Task Force to examine how laptops, tablets, interactive classroom technology, and instructional software affect student learning, health, and classroom engagement. Announced by Education Commissioner Caitlin D. Davis with support from Governor Kelly Ayotte, the initiative expands the state's focus on student technology use following the implementation of New Hampshire's statewide bell-to-bell cellphone policy during the 2025–2026 school year. While that law addressed personal mobile devices, the new task force will evaluate the broader role of educational screens used throughout the school day.

The multidisciplinary panel includes educators, school administrators, parents, healthcare professionals, and technology specialists. Members will examine research on both the benefits and potential drawbacks of extended classroom screen use, including its effects on academic performance, attention, social interaction, sleep, eye strain, and student well-being. State officials said early feedback from schools indicated the cellphone policy reduced classroom distractions and increased face-to-face engagement, prompting a broader review of digital learning practices.

The task force officially convened on August 12, 2026, and is expected to meet through the fall before delivering recommendations to NHED by the end of 2026. Areas under review include developing age-appropriate guidance for elementary and secondary students, identifying technology that provides measurable educational value, and creating model best practices that local school districts may voluntarily adopt. The panel does not have authority to impose statewide classroom technology mandates, and decisions regarding instructional devices will remain with local school boards.

New Hampshire Education Association President Megan Tuttle said the review is a logical next step as educators continue balancing digital learning tools with student wellness. State officials say the goal is to ensure technology supports learning while minimizing unnecessary screen exposure and giving districts evidence-based guidance for future classroom policies.
