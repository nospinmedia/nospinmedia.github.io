⚖️ Justice Department Investigates Gun Permit Delays in Two Connecticut CitiesFederal Probe Examines Practices in Middletown and Waterbury Police Departments 👇

Illustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalOctober 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 24, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The U.S. Department of Justice has opened a civil rights investigation into the firearm-permitting systems operated by police departments in Middletown and Waterbury, Connecticut, examining whether residents have faced unnecessary delays or denials when applying for permits to carry firearms.

The Justice Department's Civil Rights Division announced the investigation on October 22. Federal officials said the inquiry will examine the policies and practices used by both departments and whether otherwise qualified applicants have been forced into lengthy appeals before receiving permits.

The department has not concluded that either city violated federal law. In letters to local officials, investigators specifically said they had not reached any conclusions and would close the investigation if they determine there is no pattern or practice of violating constitutional or statutory rights.

The federal inquiry cites the Supreme Court's 2022 New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen decision. The Justice Department noted that the ruling recognized a constitutional right to carry firearms outside the home and warned that excessively long licensing delays could effectively prevent people from exercising that right.

Connecticut's permitting process begins locally. Someone seeking a state pistol permit must first apply for a temporary permit through a local police department or other local issuing authority. After completing that step, the applicant can seek a state permit.

The Justice Department has not publicly explained why Middletown and Waterbury were selected or released data showing how their processing times compare with other Connecticut municipalities. It also has not said whether similar investigations are planned elsewhere.

Officials in both cities have said they will cooperate. Waterbury Mayor Paul Pernerewski said the city takes its responsibilities seriously and expects to cooperate fully, while Middletown police said the department is committed to transparency and compliance with state and federal law.

The investigation could ultimately end without a finding of wrongdoing or lead to federal action if investigators conclude that the cities' practices violate protected rights.
