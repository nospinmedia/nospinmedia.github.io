✉️ Suspicious Letters Target Massachusetts Elections Office and CongressmanGalvin Staff Intercepts Envelope as Hazmat Team Responds to Seth Moulton's Office 👇

IllustrationIllustration of hazmat crews responding to a refinery chemical leak.Credit: AI-generated illustration (No Spin Media)
PoliticsOctober 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 17, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Federal authorities are investigating suspicious letters sent to the Massachusetts Elections Division and U.S. Rep. Seth Moulton's district office as similar mailings continue to target election officials and government offices around the country.

Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth William Galvin said his staff intercepted an unopened suspicious envelope on October 16 before it reached the Elections Division in Boston. Galvin said advance warning and cooperation with the FBI and U.S. Postal Inspection Service helped employees identify the mailing before anyone opened it.

A separate suspicious letter was delivered the same day to Moulton's district office in Salem, Massachusetts. That envelope contained a white, powdery substance and prompted a response from Salem police, hazardous-materials personnel, U.S. Capitol Police and the FBI.

Preliminary testing of the substance found at Moulton's office did not identify anything of immediate concern. No injuries were reported in connection with either Massachusetts incident.

The FBI and U.S. Postal Inspection Service are investigating a broader series of suspicious mailings sent to election officials and members of Congress in multiple states ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. Investigators have not publicly identified a suspect or announced a motive.

The Massachusetts incidents add to similar cases elsewhere in New England. A suspicious letter addressed to the New Hampshire Secretary of State's Office was recently intercepted, while U.S. Sen. Susan Collins' Portland, Maine, office was evacuated after receiving a letter containing an unknown substance.

Authorities are examining whether the mailings are connected and are urging government offices to follow established procedures for isolating suspicious packages and contacting law enforcement.
