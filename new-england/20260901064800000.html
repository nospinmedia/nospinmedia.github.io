🦟 Massachusetts confirms two more West Nile virus casesState's human total rises to five as mosquito activity increases 👇

HealthSeptember 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 1, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health (DPH) confirmed two additional human cases of West Nile virus (WNV) on August 31, 2026, bringing the state's total to five confirmed infections this year. The newly identified patients are a man in his 80s who was likely exposed in Middlesex County and a man in his 70s with likely exposure in Suffolk County. Health officials said the cases reflect continued mosquito-borne virus activity as the summer season enters its peak transmission period.

The latest diagnoses increase Middlesex County's total to three human cases this year and Suffolk County's total to two. Public Health Commissioner Robbie Goldstein urged residents to continue taking precautions against mosquito bites until the first hard frost, noting that older adults and people with weakened immune systems face the greatest risk of severe illness from West Nile virus infection.

State surveillance has identified 232 West Nile virus-positive mosquito pools across 13 Massachusetts counties since the first positive mosquito sample was detected on June 16. Several Greater Boston communities, including Boston, Brookline, Cambridge, Everett, Newton, Somerville, and Watertown, remain classified as having a high risk of virus transmission. Officials also reported Eastern equine encephalitis (EEE) has been detected in mosquito samples from Plymouth County, although no human EEE cases have been confirmed in Massachusetts this year.

Health officials recommend using EPA-registered insect repellents containing DEET or picaridin, wearing long sleeves and pants outdoors when practical, eliminating standing water around homes, and limiting outdoor activities during the peak mosquito hours around dusk and dawn. Residents experiencing symptoms such as fever, severe headache, confusion, or weakness after mosquito exposure are encouraged to seek medical attention.
