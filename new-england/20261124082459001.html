🚨 Salem, N.H. man charged over threat to shoot at Massachusetts high schoolPolice say Snapchat video showed Winchester High School as suspect used racial slurs 👇

EducationNovember 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 24, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A Salem, New Hampshire, man has been arrested and charged after allegedly posting a Snapchat video threatening a shooting at Winchester High School in Massachusetts while using racial slurs, authorities said.

Michael Doucette, 25, faces two counts of making threats and one civil rights violation, according to Middlesex District Attorney Marian Ryan and Winchester Police Chief Daniel O'Connell.

The investigation began around 7 p.m. Sunday, November 22, when Boston Police alerted Winchester officers to a Crime Stoppers tip concerning the Snapchat video. Authorities said the video appeared to show Winchester High School being filmed from inside a vehicle while a man made threats to shoot at the school and used racial slurs.

Investigators obtained emergency account information from Snapchat that authorities said linked Doucette to the account behind the video. Winchester Police and the Middlesex District Attorney's Office then obtained an arrest warrant Sunday night.

Doucette was arrested at his home in Salem, New Hampshire, overnight into Monday morning. Authorities said no weapons were found at his residence. He is not a student or employee of Winchester Public Schools, and investigators have not announced a motive.

Doucette was arraigned Monday, November 23, in Woburn District Court, where bail was set at $100,000. If released, he would be required to remain under house arrest with GPS monitoring, stay away from Winchester and not possess firearms. His next court appearance is scheduled for December 21.

Winchester Superintendent Dr. Frank Hackett notified families Monday morning that an arrest had been made. The district also increased the police presence at Winchester High School and other schools in town as a precaution.

Ryan said threats against schools can traumatize communities, particularly when directed at a specific group. The case remains under investigation.

The investigation crossed state lines from the initial Crime Stoppers tip in Massachusetts to Doucette's arrest at his New Hampshire residence within hours.
