⚖️ Federal Judge Blocks HUD Limits On Permanent Homeless Housing FundsRhode Island-led lawsuit preserves nationwide support programs 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EconomyAugust 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 8, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A U.S. District Court judge in Rhode Island granted summary judgment in favor of a coalition led by Rhode Island Attorney General Peter Neronha, ruling that the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) cannot enforce funding restrictions that would have limited federal support for permanent supportive housing programs serving people experiencing homelessness. The decision preserves billions of dollars in Continuum of Care (CoC) funding while the challenged conditions are set aside.

Judge Mary S. McElroy concluded that HUD violated the Administrative Procedure Act by attempting to impose major policy changes through its 2025 Notice of Funding Opportunity without following the required federal rulemaking process. The challenged policy placed a cap on funding available for permanent supportive housing, effectively shifting federal priorities toward transitional housing. State officials argued the change conflicted with the McKinney-Vento Homeless Assistance Act, which has long emphasized permanent housing solutions for people experiencing homelessness.

According to the coalition, the funding changes could have reduced permanent supportive housing resources nationwide and threatened housing stability for tens of thousands of vulnerable residents, including veterans, individuals with disabilities, and families. The states argued that local Continuum of Care organizations rely on predictable federal funding to operate permanent housing programs and that sudden changes could force providers to reduce services or close existing housing units.

Attorney General Neronha said the ruling protects both Rhode Island and communities across the country from what the coalition viewed as unlawful funding restrictions. The court's decision prevents HUD from implementing the challenged conditions while reaffirming that significant federal policy changes must follow the procedures established by Congress. The ruling leaves existing Continuum of Care funding rules in place unless future changes are adopted through lawful rulemaking.
