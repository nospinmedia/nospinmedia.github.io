🗳️ Maine Senate race remains tight days before Election DayMarist poll finds Troy Jackson at 49% and Susan Collins at 47% 👇

U.S. Capitol building in Washington, D.C.Credit: Architect of the Capitol
PoliticsNovember 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 1, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A new Marist Poll shows a close U.S. Senate contest in Maine just days before the November 3 election, with Democrat Troy Jackson at 49% and Republican Sen. Susan Collins at 47% among likely voters.

The difference is well within the poll's ±6.6 percentage-point margin of error, meaning the survey does not establish a statistically clear leader. Marist interviewed Maine voters from October 22 through October 25.

Among independent likely voters, Jackson received 50% support compared with 41% for Collins. The broader registered-voter sample showed Jackson with a larger advantage, while the likely-voter results indicated a considerably closer contest.

The poll also found an unusual difference between voters' preferences and their expectations about the outcome. Although Jackson received slightly more support in the likely-voter matchup, 58% of likely voters said they expected Collins to be reelected.

Collins has represented Maine in the U.S. Senate since 1997 and is seeking another six-year term. Jackson, a former president of the Maine Senate, is challenging her in a race that could affect the balance of power in the U.S. Senate.

Marist also asked likely voters which party they supported in their congressional district without naming candidates. 52% chose the Democratic candidate and 47% the Republican, with Democrats holding a larger advantage among independents in that question.

The Maine results stand in contrast with polling elsewhere in New England, where recent surveys have shown substantially wider margins in Senate contests in New Hampshire and Massachusetts. As with all polling, the Maine survey represents voter preferences during its October 22-25 interviewing period rather than a prediction of the final result.
