🏥 Brigham nurses reach tentative deal, averting strikeAgreement covering more than 4,000 nurses still requires ratification 👇

HealthNovember 12, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 12, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

More than 4,000 registered nurses at Brigham and Women's Hospital in Boston reached a tentative contract agreement with Mass General Brigham late Wednesday, averting an open-ended strike that had been scheduled to begin at 7 a.m. Thursday.

The nurses, represented by the Massachusetts Nurses Association, had been preparing to walk out after months of negotiations. Hospital officials had also begun preparations for the strike, including arranging temporary replacement staff. The agreement came only hours before the planned walkout.

The tentative deal follows a one-day strike in July, which the union described as the largest nurses strike in Massachusetts history. The new agreement would run through September 30, 2028, but must still be approved in a ratification vote by the nurses. Union leadership is recommending approval.

The nurses had previously rejected the hospital's Oct. 10 last, best and final offer in a Nov. 1 vote before setting the Nov. 12 strike date. Mass General Brigham said the tentative agreement reached Wednesday remains within the financial limits of that earlier offer, while the final negotiations produced additional contract language addressing issues including temporary staffing and floating between units.

Under the agreement, current nurses working 40 hours a week would retain premium-free health insurance. Newly hired 40-hour nurses would contribute 2.5% toward their premiums.

The contract would also establish new top wage scale steps of 2.5% in November 2026 and another 2.5% in September 2027. Nurses below the top step would receive a prorated $1,000 ratification bonus.

Staffing provisions were another major part of the negotiations. The tentative agreement includes language limiting the hospital's ability to replace permanent nurses with temporary workers and restricting when nurses can be moved, or floated, between units. The hospital would also have to provide justification when temporary nurses account for more than 15% of a unit's full-time-equivalent positions.

The agreement means Brigham and Women's can continue normal operations without the disruption that an indefinite strike by thousands of nurses could have caused. The dispute is not formally over, however, until the bargaining unit completes its ratification vote.
