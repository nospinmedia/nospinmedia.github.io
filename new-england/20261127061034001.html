💨 Thanksgiving storm knocks out power across New EnglandMore than 26,000 remain without electricity as crews work on restoration 👇

EnvironmentNovember 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 27, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A powerful Thanksgiving storm left tens of thousands of customers across New England without electricity as heavy rain and strong winds swept through the region Thursday night into Friday, turning earlier forecasts of holiday disruptions into widespread outages.

Regional outage tracking showed approximately 26,700 customers across the six New England states remained without power around 3:30 a.m. Friday, as utilities continued restoration work. The figure represents electric customers, such as homes and businesses, rather than individual people.

Massachusetts was among the hardest-hit states. The Massachusetts Emergency Management Agency reported about 8,800 customers without power by 9:30 p.m. Thursday as the storm intensified. Separate utility-feed tracking showed outages later climbed above 12,000 simultaneously shortly after midnight before beginning to decline. Worcester, Berkshire and Barnstable counties were among the areas with significant outages.

In New Hampshire, outages also increased overnight as the storm moved north. Utility tracking showed several thousand customers without electricity Friday, with disruptions spread across communities including areas of Carroll, Rockingham, Cheshire and Merrimack counties.

Vermont and Connecticut also experienced thousands of outages, while smaller numbers were reported in Maine and Rhode Island early Friday. Because outage totals change rapidly as new failures occur and crews restore service, statewide figures varied depending on the reporting time and utilities included by individual trackers.

The storm brought heavy rain and strong winds throughout the Northeast. National Grid had positioned additional crews in Massachusetts ahead of the storm, warning that high winds could temporarily prevent crews from safely using elevated bucket trucks during restoration work.

The impacts mark a significant update from forecasts earlier in the week that warned all six New England states could face strong coastal gusts, heavy rain, mountain snow and scattered outages during Thanksgiving travel.

Across the broader East Coast, the storm left more than 900,000 customers without power Thursday and caused flooding and travel disruptions. It also forced New York City's 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday, the first weather postponement in the event's history.
