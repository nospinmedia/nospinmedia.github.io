📸 ACLU Urges Cumberland To End Flock Camera ContractWrongful Arrest Lawsuit Fuels Rhode Island Surveillance Debate 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalAugust 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 9, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The ACLU of Rhode Island is urging the Cumberland Town Council to cancel its contract with Flock Safety after a federal lawsuit alleged that a local woman was wrongfully arrested following a mistaken identification involving images from the company's automated license plate reader system. The civil liberties group sent its request on August 7, 2026, arguing that the case highlights broader privacy and oversight concerns surrounding the growing use of automated vehicle surveillance in Rhode Island.

The lawsuit was filed in U.S. District Court for the District of Rhode Island by Eva Pizzarelli, who alleges Cumberland police incorrectly identified her from a Flock camera image after a March 26, 2025 traffic pursuit. According to the complaint, investigators compared the image with photographs from social media and Pizzarelli's driver's license before obtaining criminal charges and a warrant. She was later detained for more than six hours after an unrelated traffic stop, and the charges against her were dismissed in October 2025. Cumberland Police Chief Matthew Benson has declined to comment on the allegations while the civil lawsuit remains pending.

The ACLU says the case also raises questions about whether the cameras capture more than license plates, including images of drivers and passengers. The organization argues that Cumberland residents were not fully informed about those capabilities when the town approved the system in 2024. Flock Safety says its technology does not use facial recognition and that searches rely on vehicle characteristics rather than identifying individuals.

Cumberland's debate comes as several other Rhode Island communities reconsider the technology. South Kingstown voted unanimously on July 27 to cancel its Flock contract, Narragansett followed on August 3, and Glocester voted 4-1 on August 6 to end its program. The ACLU estimates at least 295 Flock cameras are operating across Rhode Island, while the state currently has no law specifically regulating how automated license plate reader data is collected, shared, or retained.
