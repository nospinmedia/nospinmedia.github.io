🚔 14-year-old charged with murder in great-aunt's Lowell deathMedical examiner found multiple fractures before ruling August death a homicide 👇

EducationNovember 20, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 20, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A 14-year-old boy has been arrested and charged with murder in the death of his 69-year-old great-aunt at the Lowell, Massachusetts, home where they lived together, authorities announced Thursday, November 19. Brayden Lee is accused of killing Sheila Ingebritson, who was found dead in her kitchen on August 11.

Lee faces charges of murder and assault and battery on a person over age 60 causing serious bodily injury and is scheduled to be arraigned in Lowell District Court on Friday, November 20. Although Lee is 14, prosecutors said he is being charged as an adult because Massachusetts juvenile court does not have jurisdiction over murder charges.

Police originally responded to the home on Puffer Street after receiving a report that a woman in her 60s had fallen and was bleeding. Ingebritson was found dead inside. According to authorities, the state medical examiner later identified numerous injuries, including a broken jaw, a broken bone in her neck and a skull fracture.

The Massachusetts Office of the Chief Medical Examiner ruled Ingebritson's death a homicide on October 28, leading investigators to continue building the case before Lee's arrest Thursday.

Authorities said Ingebritson had custody of Lee and that the two lived together. Investigators also said she had previously expressed fear of the teenager and described him as volatile.

According to Middlesex District Attorney Marian Ryan and Lowell Police Superintendent Gregory Hudon, phone records and surveillance video from the area allegedly indicate that Lee and Ingebritson argued on the day she died. Investigators allege the evidence also showed that the two were the only people inside the home at the time of the fatal assault. Ryan and Hudon jointly announced the arrest and charges on Thursday.

The charges are allegations, and Lee has not been convicted. Because the defendant is a minor despite facing an adult murder charge, the case also raises unusual questions about how Massachusetts handles extremely serious criminal accusations involving young defendants.
