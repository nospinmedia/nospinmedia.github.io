⚖️ Supreme Court lets New Hampshire fully restrict student IDs for votingRuling changes what students can use at polls before November 3 👇

Illustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationOctober 23, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 23, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The U.S. Supreme Court has cleared the way for New Hampshire to fully enforce restrictions on the use of college and university student identification cards in the November 3, 2026 midterm election, reversing a lower court order that had preserved a limited role for student IDs when voters lacked an accepted form of photo identification.

The dispute centers on House Bill 323, enacted this year, which removed student IDs from the forms of identification expressly accepted when a voter requests a ballot. A separate provision of New Hampshire law allows election officials to verify a person's identity through other reasonably reliable evidence when the voter lacks qualifying photo identification. After the law passed, Secretary of State David Scanlan's office directed election officials that student IDs could not be used for identification purposes at all.

The Coalition for Open Democracy challenged the policy. On October 2, U.S. District Judge Joseph Laplante declined to block HB 323 itself but ruled that Scanlan's directive went further than the statute. His order allowed election officials to consider a student ID alongside other evidence when determining a voter's identity. The 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals declined to put Laplante's order on hold, leading New Hampshire to seek emergency relief from the Supreme Court.

In an unsigned order Thursday, the Supreme Court granted the state's request and cited the Purcell principle, under which federal courts ordinarily should avoid changing election rules shortly before an election. The Court said the lower court had not identified a reason to depart from that principle. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only justice to publicly dissent, questioning whether Purcell applied because the lower court had not blocked HB 323 itself but instead limited the secretary of state's interpretation of it.

The decision means student IDs cannot serve as accepted voter identification or as backup evidence of identity under the challenged state guidance for the upcoming election. New Hampshire voters who lack an accepted photo ID will need to use other methods permitted under state election law to establish their identity.
