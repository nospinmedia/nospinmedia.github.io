☕ Mass. cites Dunkin’ operator, staffing firms for $1.46 million100 workers owed restitution over wage and classification violations 👇

EconomySeptember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 4, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Massachusetts Attorney General's Office has issued approximately $1.46 million in citations against an Eastern Massachusetts Dunkin' franchise operator, two staffing agencies and several managers over alleged wage and employment-law violations affecting 100 workers. Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell announced the enforcement action on September 3 against Northern Management Group LLC, which operates more than 20 Dunkin' locations in Eastern Massachusetts, along with D and J Services Group LLC and Prime Management Services Corp.

State investigators found that workers supplied to the Dunkin' locations were improperly classified as independent contractors even though they performed ordinary restaurant jobs, including work as store managers, shift leaders, bakers and crew members. Massachusetts officials determined that Northern Management and the staffing agencies functioned as joint employers and were responsible for complying with state wage laws. The citations include restitution owed to workers as well as civil penalties payable to the Commonwealth.

Investigators also found repeated late wage payments between August 2023 and August 2025. Massachusetts law generally requires employees to receive their wages within specified periods after the end of a pay period. Some employees were also allegedly required to travel between Dunkin' locations without being paid for that travel time. The Attorney General's Office said those practices violated state wage-and-hour requirements.

The enforcement action additionally cites alleged violations of Massachusetts' Temporary Workers Right to Know Law. State officials said the staffing businesses failed to provide required written information about job assignments and could not produce some records requested during the investigation. Individual managers associated with the businesses were also cited, including Jigar Patel, Nikul Patel, Nilesh Patel and Daylon Oliveira.

Campbell's office said misclassifying employees as independent contractors can deprive workers of protections including minimum wage, overtime and unemployment benefits while allowing employers to avoid obligations such as payroll taxes and workers' compensation. The Attorney General emphasized that businesses using staffing companies can still be responsible for violations when they qualify as joint employers under Massachusetts law.
