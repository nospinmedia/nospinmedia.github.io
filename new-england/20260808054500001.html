📜 Massachusetts Creates First Statewide Home Care Licensing SystemNew law requires oversight, background checks, and caregiver standards 👇

HealthAugust 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 8, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey signed legislation on August 7, 2026, creating the Commonwealth's first statewide licensing system for non-medical home care agencies, establishing new oversight intended to improve safety for older adults and strengthen standards across an industry that previously operated without statewide licensure.

The law directs the Executive Office of Health and Human Services (EOHHS) to develop licensing regulations within one year. Those regulations will require fingerprint-based criminal background checks for home care workers, establish minimum training and operational standards, require consumer service plans and contracts, and create a public licensing framework for agencies providing services such as bathing, dressing, meal preparation, transportation, and other assistance that helps older adults remain in their homes. The legislation also aligns oversight with existing state registries and aging services programs.

The measure establishes a Home Care Oversight Advisory Council to help guide implementation and recommend additional standards as the licensing system is developed. It also includes workforce protections intended to improve transparency, strengthen labor standards, and help ensure caregivers receive appropriate training while giving families greater confidence when selecting home care providers. Massachusetts had been one of only a handful of states without a comprehensive statewide licensing system for non-medical home care agencies.

State officials said the law is designed to support the growing number of residents choosing to age at home while creating consistent expectations for agencies operating across the Commonwealth. Once regulations are finalized, agencies will be required to meet the new licensing requirements before providing covered home care services, giving Massachusetts its first uniform regulatory framework for this sector.
