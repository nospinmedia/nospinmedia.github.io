⚖️ Federal Judge Dismisses Harvard Antisemitism LawsuitCourt Says Civil Rights Claims Were Too Isolated To Proceed 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationAugust 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 14, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A federal judge in Boston has dismissed the Department of Justice's civil lawsuit accusing Harvard University of violating Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 by failing to adequately protect Jewish and Israeli students from antisemitic harassment. The lawsuit, filed by the Trump administration, alleged that Harvard showed deliberate indifference to campus incidents following the October 2023 Hamas attack and subsequent pro-Palestinian demonstrations. The case formed part of the administration's broader dispute with Harvard over more than $2 billion in federal research funding.

In a four-page ruling, U.S. District Judge Richard G. Stearns concluded that the Justice Department failed to allege sufficient facts showing an ongoing pattern of institutional noncompliance. The opinion stated that the incidents described in the government's complaint were "too isolated and episodic" to support a claim that Harvard remained in continuing violation of federal civil rights law. The judge also noted that the complaint did not identify new acts of discrimination occurring after the federal government formally notified Harvard of its concerns.

Judge Stearns further ruled that Title VI requires federal officials to provide notice of alleged violations and an opportunity for voluntary compliance before pursuing enforcement through litigation. The court found the complaint did not adequately establish those statutory prerequisites. The dismissal applies only to this specific lawsuit and does not resolve separate litigation challenging the administration's efforts to terminate more than $2 billion in federal grants and research funding awarded to Harvard.

The Justice Department said it is reviewing the decision and considering its legal options. Harvard welcomed the ruling, maintaining that it has taken substantial steps to combat antisemitism while protecting free expression on campus. The broader dispute over federal funding and university governance remains active in separate court proceedings.
