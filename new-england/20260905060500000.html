🚲 MIT professor dies after Memorial Drive bicycle crashFatal collision renews calls for Cambridge safety improvements 👇

EducationSeptember 5, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 5, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

MIT assistant professor Thomas Rose, 37, died on September 2 from injuries suffered when he was struck while bicycling on Memorial Drive in Cambridge nearly two weeks earlier. Rose, a professor in MIT's Department of Materials Science and Engineering, was riding near the intersection of Memorial Drive and Massachusetts Avenue early on August 21 when the collision occurred. He was taken to Massachusetts General Hospital, where he remained hospitalized until his death.

The crash remains under investigation by the Massachusetts State Police and Middlesex District Attorney's Office. Authorities said the driver remained at the scene, and no charges had been announced as of Friday. Memorial Drive is a state-controlled roadway managed by the Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation, placing responsibility for the investigation and potential roadway changes across several state agencies rather than primarily with the city of Cambridge.

Rose joined MIT's faculty in January 2026 as the POSCO Professor of Materials Science and Engineering. His research focused on archaeometallurgy, using materials science to study how metals were produced and used in ancient societies. Before coming to MIT, he conducted research and taught at institutions including University College London. MIT colleagues remembered him as a dedicated researcher and teacher whose career at the university had only recently begun.

Students, colleagues and bicycle-safety advocates gathered at MIT's Killian Court on September 4 for a vigil honoring Rose. The gathering also renewed calls for safety improvements along Memorial Drive and greater transparency about the circumstances of the collision. State Representative Mike Connolly called for a thorough investigation and additional information about what happened.

Cycling advocates have long raised concerns about the Memorial Drive corridor, including its intersections, vehicle speeds and accommodations for people traveling by bicycle. Rose's death has added urgency to requests that state officials examine possible infrastructure changes designed to reduce the risk of similar crashes.
