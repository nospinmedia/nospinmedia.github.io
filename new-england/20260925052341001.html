🌧️ Utilities prepare for outages as nor’easter closes inWind and saturated ground raise tree-damage risk across southern New England 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 25, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 25, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Utilities and communities across Massachusetts and southern New England are preparing for possible power outages as a rare September nor’easter approaches the region, bringing heavy rain, strong winds and coastal flooding. Eversource said it was preparing to shift crews toward southeastern Massachusetts, Cape Cod and Martha’s Vineyard, where some of the strongest winds are expected as the storm intensifies this weekend.

The combination of wind and rain is a particular concern for the electric grid. Eversource spokesperson Olessa Stepanova told The Boston Globe that more than 90% of the utility’s storm-related outages are caused by trees. Heavy rain can saturate the ground before the strongest winds arrive, increasing the chance that trees and limbs will fall onto power lines. The company said crews from western Massachusetts could be moved east, while crews in Connecticut and New Hampshire could also be repositioned if needed. The storm’s path remained uncertain enough Thursday that Eversource was not estimating how many customers could lose power.

Communities are also making preparations. Boston Mayor Michelle Wu issued a coastal storm warning covering September 25 through September 27, with the city expecting heavy rain, damaging winds and possible coastal flooding and erosion. Boston said additional personnel and resources would be available through the weekend, while parks crews were placed on standby for fallen trees. Residents were urged to secure loose outdoor objects, prepare emergency supplies and limit travel during the worst conditions.

Elsewhere in southern New England, officials in Rhode Island warned that gusts could reach 50 to 60 mph or higher in some areas. Crews were clearing storm drains and trimming trees ahead of the storm, while residents were advised to charge devices and have several days of food, water and medication available. Coastal communities are also preparing for rough seas, erosion and flooding as the storm moves through the region.
