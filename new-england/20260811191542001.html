⚡ Connecticut Requires Higher Reliability For State-Funded EV ChargersPURA adopts 97% uptime standard under statewide charging program 👇

New EnglandAugust 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 11, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Connecticut's Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA) has adopted new reliability requirements for electric vehicle charging stations participating in the state's Nine-Year Statewide EV Charging Program. Under the updated standards, publicly funded charging stations must maintain at least 97% operational uptime, consistent with federal National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) reliability requirements, or risk losing eligibility for certain state incentive payments.

The new requirements are intended to improve charger reliability after EV drivers across the country reported frequent outages, payment failures, and equipment malfunctions at public charging stations. Participating charging network operators must monitor equipment performance, report availability data, and repair outages within established timeframes to remain in compliance with program requirements.

PURA said the standards are designed to ensure public investments result in dependable charging infrastructure as Connecticut expands its electric vehicle network. State officials continue working toward long-term goals of significantly increasing public and private charging availability while supporting broader adoption of zero-emission vehicles.

The requirements apply to chargers participating in Connecticut's state-funded charging program and align the state's reliability standards with federal NEVI guidance for publicly funded fast-charging infrastructure. Officials said the program emphasizes both expanding charger availability and ensuring stations remain operational after installation.
