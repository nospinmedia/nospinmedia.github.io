🪧 Connecticut lawmaker charged after opponent's campaign signs disappearAirTags allegedly led Republican challenger to signs at Anne Hughes' property 👇

EconomyNovember 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 1, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Connecticut state Rep. Anne Hughes, a Democrat from Easton, has been issued a misdemeanor larceny summons after police said she took campaign signs belonging to her Republican opponent, Darrell Harris. The signs were found after Harris' campaign used hidden Apple AirTags to track them, according to reporting on the case.

Harris is challenging Hughes for her seat in Connecticut's 135th House District. His campaign had placed signs criticizing Hughes using comments attributed to her from a previously reported hot-mic incident involving taxes. After some of those signs disappeared, the campaign tracked their location electronically.

The tracking devices led Harris to property associated with Hughes, where the missing signs were located. Easton police investigated and subsequently issued Hughes a summons on a sixth-degree larceny charge, a misdemeanor under Connecticut law.

Hughes acknowledged taking the signs. She said she believed they had been improperly placed on public property and maintained that her intention was to remove signs she thought violated local rules rather than steal campaign material. Hughes also acknowledged that she should have contacted police, town officials or Harris' campaign instead of removing the signs herself and apologized for her actions.

The dispute comes in the closing days of the 2026 election campaign, with Hughes and Harris competing for the state House seat representing Easton, Redding and Weston. The criminal summons requires Hughes to respond to the charge in court but does not constitute a finding of guilt.

Campaign signs are frequently the subject of local disputes during election season, but removing another candidate's signs can result in criminal charges when authorities determine that the property was taken unlawfully.
