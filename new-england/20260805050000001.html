⚖️ Rhode Island Joins Lawsuit Over Federal TANF Data PolicyStates seek to block sharing of recipient records before Aug. 11 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalAugust 5, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 5, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Rhode Island Attorney General Peter Neronha joined a coalition of 23 attorneys general and two governors in suing the Trump administration over a federal policy allowing broader access to personal information collected through Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) programs. The lawsuit was filed on August 3, 2026, in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, ahead of the policy’s planned August 11 implementation.

The policy was announced in June by the Administration for Children and Families, a division of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. It would allow TANF records—including Social Security numbers, home addresses, immigration information, and other personal details—to be shared with federal agencies such as the Department of Homeland Security and potentially with outside organizations performing government functions.

Rhode Island administers TANF cash assistance through the Rhode Island Works program, which supports parents and families with little or no income who have dependent children. Neronha and the other plaintiffs argue that Congress placed responsibility for determining TANF eligibility with states and that the federal government cannot impose broader monitoring and data-sharing requirements without following federal law. The complaint alleges violations of the Administrative Procedure Act, the U.S. Constitution, and existing privacy restrictions.

Federal officials have said expanded access is needed to assess whether states properly verify recipients’ citizenship or immigration status. The states counter that the policy could discourage legally eligible families from seeking assistance, undermine trust in state benefit programs, and expose sensitive records to uses unrelated to administering TANF. The Administration for Children and Families declined to comment on the litigation when contacted by reporters.

The dispute is part of a broader series of lawsuits over federal efforts to obtain benefit-program data for immigration and fraud investigations. Earlier this year, a federal judge blocked an attempt to withhold TANF and other assistance funding from several Democratic-led states.
