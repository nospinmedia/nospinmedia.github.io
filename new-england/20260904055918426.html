🏫 New IRS tax rule could reach private colleges across New EnglandDartmouth already faces separate federal race-admissions investigation 👇

EducationSeptember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 4, 2026 at 11:49 AM ET

Private colleges and schools across New England could be forced to review scholarships, admissions policies and other student programs under a sweeping new Treasury Department and IRS proposal that would deny federal tax-exempt status to private educational institutions using race-based preferences. The regulation, announced September 3, would apply to private K-12 schools, colleges, universities, professional schools and trade schools and could affect as many as 18,000 institutions nationwide if finalized.

The proposal would bar tax-exempt schools from making decisions or providing benefits based on race, color, or national or ethnic origin in admissions, scholarships and loans, athletics, facilities and other school-supported programs. Race-neutral programs based on factors such as financial need, geography, first-generation status, hardship or academic achievement would remain permitted. That distinction is important for New England schools: simply maintaining a diversity or inclusion office would not automatically violate the proposed rule, but programs that explicitly confer benefits based on race could face scrutiny.

Several regional institutions illustrate the kinds of policies schools may need to examine. Maine College of Art & Design in Portland states in its strategic planning materials that it intends to develop a menu of "diversity scholarships," although its currently published financial-aid information emphasizes scholarships and grants without establishing that current awards are being made on an impermissible racial basis. Bates College in Lewiston operates an all-expenses-paid admissions fly-in program called Prologue to Bates, aimed at prospective students interested in joining an inclusive and pluralistic community, but its public description does not restrict participation to a specific race.

In New Hampshire, Dartmouth College's Geisel School of Medicine is already under a separate U.S. Department of Education civil-rights investigation opened on July 22 over alleged racial discrimination in admissions. Dartmouth has said the investigation is not a finding of wrongdoing and that it remains confident its admissions process complies with applicable law. The new Treasury rule is a separate regulatory action, but it raises the financial stakes for private institutions if federal agencies later determine that covered race-based practices violate the proposed tax standard.

If finalized, the rule would apply to taxable years beginning on or after May 31, 2027, giving schools time to review and revise policies before enforcement begins.
