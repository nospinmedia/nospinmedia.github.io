⚖️ Former Stoughton officer convicted of killing Sandra BirchmoreMatthew Farwell faces mandatory life sentence after federal jury finds him guilty 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalNovember 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 11, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A federal jury in Boston has convicted former Stoughton police detective Matthew Farwell of killing Sandra Birchmore, a 23-year-old woman who was pregnant with his child, and causing the death of her unborn son. The jury returned guilty verdicts on both federal counts Tuesday, November 10, after previously telling the judge it was deadlocked.

Federal prosecutors said Farwell began sexually exploiting Birchmore when she was 15 years old and participating in the Stoughton Police Department's youth explorers program. Prosecutors argued that years later, when Birchmore was pregnant and could expose the relationship, Farwell killed her in her Canton apartment in February 2021 and staged her death to appear to be a suicide. More than 50 witnesses testified during the roughly four-week trial.

Farwell, 39, was convicted of killing a witness or victim to prevent communication about a federal offense and causing the death of an unborn child. Federal prosecutors did not seek the death penalty, leaving mandatory life in federal prison without the possibility of parole as the sentence Farwell faces. Chief U.S. District Judge Denise J. Casper scheduled sentencing for February 3, 2027.

Farwell's defense maintained that he went to Birchmore's apartment to end their relationship and that she died by suicide. Jurors rejected that account by finding him guilty on both counts.

The verdict brought reactions from Birchmore's family and law enforcement officials. Her cousin Barbara Wright expressed relief that the family had finally received justice. Cousin Ray Cosgrove, speaking alongside Wright, emphasized that Birchmore mattered to her family long before the case became widely known. U.S. Attorney Leah Foley said the verdict held Farwell accountable for abusing his position of trust and killing Birchmore to prevent her from exposing his conduct.

Birchmore's death was initially ruled a suicide, but questions about that conclusion persisted as details about her relationships with Stoughton officers emerged. A subsequent federal investigation ultimately led to Farwell's prosecution and conviction.
