⚖️ NH fathers win 'XX' wristband free-speech caseBow district settlement ends more than two years of litigation 👇

EducationNovember 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 6, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Two New Hampshire fathers who were barred from school grounds after wearing pink "XX" wristbands to protest transgender athletes participating in girls' sports have won their First Amendment case against the Bow School District, ending more than two years of litigation.

U.S. District Court Judge Steven McAuliffe issued an order Thursday declaring that Kyle Fellers and Anthony Foote have a First Amendment right to silently wear the wristbands at Bow school sporting events and that school officials violated that right. The wristbands display "XX," representing female chromosomes.

The dispute began during a September 2024 girls' soccer game involving a transgender player on the opposing team. Fellers and Foote wore the wristbands as a protest and were subsequently barred from school property. School officials argued they had an obligation to protect students from harassment by adults and raised concerns that the protest could target the transgender athlete.

Both men testified that they did not intend to harass or otherwise target the player. A federal court initially prevented them from wearing the wristbands while the lawsuit continued, but an appeals court later sided with the parents, finding that school officials could not favor one viewpoint in a public debate involving parental speech.

Under the settlement approved Thursday, the district's risk pool will pay $275,000 in attorneys' fees and costs — $225,000 to the Institute for Free Speech and $50,000 to the men's attorney. The plaintiffs will each receive $17.91, a symbolic amount representing 1791, the year the First Amendment was ratified.

The ruling specifically protects the silent wearing of the wristbands at Bow District sporting events. It does not establish an unrestricted right to harass students or disrupt school activities, and it does not decide whether transgender students may participate in girls' sports.
