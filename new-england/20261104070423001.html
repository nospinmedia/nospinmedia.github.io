🔥 Wild River Fire grows beyond 375 acres in White MountainsMore than 40 firefighters battle rugged terrain near Maine border 👇

EnvironmentNovember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 4, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The Wild River Fire burning in New Hampshire's White Mountain National Forest has grown to more than 375 acres, up from about 300 acres previously reported, as crews continue working in steep and difficult terrain near the Maine border.

The wildfire was first reported Sunday, November 1, in Coos County, southwest of Shelburne and near Gorham. It has been burning through dry leaves, timber and other forest fuels as crews attempt to establish containment lines around the fire.

More than 40 firefighters and forestry personnel have been assigned to the response, according to officials. The location presents additional challenges because crews must work across rugged slopes where access for equipment is limited.

Despite the fire's growth, officials reported no immediate threat to homes or other structures as of Tuesday. Smoke from the fire has been visible from surrounding communities, however, and firefighting operations are continuing inside the national forest.

Dry conditions have contributed to elevated wildfire concerns across parts of northern New England. The lack of moisture can allow fallen leaves, branches and other vegetation on the forest floor to ignite and spread more easily, particularly in areas where firefighters have limited access.

The cause of the Wild River Fire remains under investigation. Officials have not announced when they expect the fire to be fully contained, and its size could change as crews obtain more accurate measurements of the perimeter.

The latest estimate represents a meaningful increase from the approximately 300 acres reported earlier, making the expanding fire an ongoing incident rather than a repeat of the previous update.
