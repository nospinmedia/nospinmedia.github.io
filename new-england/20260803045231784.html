🏘️ Maine dissolves manufactured housing boardNew state office takes over oversight of 480 mobile home parks 👇

EconomyAugust 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 3, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Maine has dissolved its nearly 50-year-old Manufactured Housing Board and transferred its responsibilities to the Maine Office of Community Affairs, beginning a major restructuring of how the state regulates manufactured homes and its 480 registered mobile home parks.

The board held its final meeting in early June 2026 and was formally dissolved on July 29, 2026. Its four employees — an executive director, two housing inspectors and a secretary — were moved from the Department of Professional and Financial Regulation to the Office of Community Affairs, where they will continue investigating potential violations of state rules.

The former board was responsible for construction and installation standards, manufacturer and park licensing, warranty regulation, complaint investigations and inspections involving electrical systems, sanitation and lot conditions. Under the developing structure, those matters will be handled administratively by the Office of Community Affairs rather than decided by a nine-member adjudicatory board that included manufactured housing dealers, a park owner, a builder, an engineer, a code enforcement officer and public representatives.

The change follows a report from the Governor’s Office of Policy Innovation and the Future that identified gaps in state oversight and described residents being repeatedly referred among agencies when seeking help with unsafe conditions, disputed fees, community rules and other park-related problems. The issue has become more urgent as out-of-state investors purchase more Maine parks and some residents face rising lot rents while owning their homes but not the land beneath them.

Office Director Samantha Horn said the agency is adding a training and certification coordinator and a technical outreach assistant. The Maine Attorney General’s Office is also adding an attorney focused on manufactured housing matters, funded through a temporary increase in park registration fees.

Industry representatives have warned that eliminating the board could remove specialized knowledge from the regulatory process. State officials must consult residents, former board members and other stakeholders before submitting recommendations to the Legislature by January 1, 2027.
