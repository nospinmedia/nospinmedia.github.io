⚖️ First Responders Give Emotional Testimony In Lindsay Clancy Triple-Murder TrialJurors Hear Detailed Accounts Of Rescue Efforts At Duxbury Home 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthJuly 31, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published July 31, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Jurors in the murder trial of former Duxbury nurse Lindsay Clancy heard emotional testimony Thursday from police officers, firefighters and paramedics who responded to the family's home after her three children were found unresponsive in January 2023. The testimony focused on the emergency response and efforts to save the children following a 911 call from Clancy's husband, Patrick Clancy.

Officer Stephen Hall and other first responders described arriving at the home to find a chaotic scene as emergency crews attempted lifesaving measures on 5-year-old Cora, 3-year-old Dawson and 8-month-old Callan. Witnesses recounted finding the children in the basement and described their attempts to resuscitate them before they were transported to area hospitals. Cora and Dawson were pronounced dead that day, while Callan died several days later from his injuries.

Lindsay Clancy has pleaded not guilty by reason of lack of criminal responsibility. Her defense argues she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis and other mental health conditions that prevented her from understanding the nature or wrongfulness of her actions. Prosecutors contend the killings were intentional and have argued that the evidence will demonstrate planning before the children's deaths.

Clancy survived after jumping from a second-story window following the deaths and now uses a wheelchair because of injuries sustained in the fall. The trial, being held in Plymouth Superior Court before Judge William Sullivan, is expected to continue for several weeks as jurors hear testimony from investigators, medical experts and additional witnesses.

Clancy faces three counts of first-degree murder. The central issue before the jury is whether she was legally criminally responsible at the time of the children's deaths.
