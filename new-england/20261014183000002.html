🗳️ Collins and Jackson Clash Over ICE, Heating Costs and TariffsThird Maine Senate Debate Highlights Sharp Differences Ahead of Election 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
HealthOctober 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 14, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Republican U.S. Senator Susan Collins and Democratic challenger Troy Jackson faced off Tuesday evening, October 13, 2026, in their third debate as Maine voters prepare for the November 3 election.

The debate, hosted by WABI and WAGM, focused on immigration enforcement, rising living costs, federal spending and trade policies. Both candidates defended their records while questioning whether their opponent could effectively represent Maine in Washington.

One of the sharpest exchanges concerned Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and the fatal July shooting of Johan Sebastián Durán Guerrero by a federal immigration agent in Biddeford.

Jackson criticized Collins for supporting legislation that increased immigration enforcement funding. He argued that ICE should be abolished, with its responsibilities transferred to other federal agencies, including Customs and Border Protection.

Collins defended continued immigration enforcement but acknowledged problems with the agency's expansion. She said too many officers had been hired and deployed without adequate screening and training.

Collins pointed to funding she secured for body cameras, additional oversight and de-escalation training, arguing that reforming the agency would be more effective than eliminating it.

The candidates also disagreed over heating costs and federal assistance as Maine households prepare for winter. Collins highlighted her efforts to secure the early release of approximately $37 million in federal heating assistance.

Jackson questioned whether those measures adequately addressed the economic pressures facing residents, including higher energy prices and healthcare expenses.

Trade policy produced another disagreement. Collins emphasized her work obtaining exemptions from federal tariffs affecting Canadian products important to Maine businesses, including pulp, concrete and road salt.

Jackson argued that securing individual exemptions did not address the broader economic damage caused by the tariffs. Collins responded that her experience and relationships in Washington allowed her to obtain results for Maine.

Both candidates also addressed the growing demand for data centers, expressing support for some restrictions on their locations and consumption of electricity and water.

The debate included disagreements over healthcare programs and campaign finance. Collins questioned Jackson's campaign disclosure filings, while Jackson raised previous reporting concerning a federal investigation involving a defense contractor and political contributions associated with Collins.

The two candidates are scheduled to meet for their fourth and final debate on Thursday, October 15, hosted by Maine Public, NEWS CENTER Maine and the Portland Press Herald.

With the election approaching, the debate illustrated competing arguments about congressional experience, government accountability and the cost of living.
