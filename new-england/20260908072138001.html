⚖️ Mass. AG assesses nearly $12.7M in workplace violationsMore than 16,400 workers aided in statewide enforcement 👇

EducationSeptember 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 8, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The Massachusetts Attorney General's Office says it assessed nearly $12.7 million in unpaid-wage restitution and penalties during the past fiscal year as state investigators pursued violations involving more than 1,100 employers across the Commonwealth.

Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell's annual Labor Day Report says the Fair Labor Division took 1,292 enforcement actions against 1,183 employers between July 1, 2025, and June 30, 2026, helping more than 16,410 workers. The cases involved violations of laws governing wages, overtime, prevailing wages, earned sick leave, employee classification and other workplace protections.

Construction accounted for the largest share of enforcement actions at 20%, followed closely by restaurants and hotels at 19.5%. Healthcare represented 8%, landscaping and snow removal 6.3%, and cleaning and janitorial businesses 5.6%. The most frequently cited violation was failure to make timely wage payments, resulting in 474 citations.

Construction investigations alone produced more than $2.5 million in restitution and penalties benefiting over 1,300 employees. Restaurant and hospitality cases resulted in nearly 400 citations and almost $2.5 million in assessments affecting more than 3,400 workers. Among those cases, Boston restaurants Carrie Nation and The Dubliner paid $422,000 in restitution and penalties after an investigation into service charges that were not fully distributed to employees.

The Fair Labor Division also conducted 141 worksite visits and participated in 98 community events during the fiscal year. Campbell's office says enforcement is paired with education and outreach intended to help both workers and employers understand Massachusetts labor laws.

Workers who believe their workplace rights have been violated can submit complaints to the Attorney General's Fair Labor Division, which investigates cases regardless of a worker's immigration status.
