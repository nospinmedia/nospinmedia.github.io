⚖️ Maine challenges federal offshore wind buyoutsTwo Gulf of Maine leases are among projects targeted 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 23, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 23, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Maine has joined seven other states in lawsuits challenging Trump administration agreements that use $1.4 billion in federal funds to cancel four planned offshore wind projects and direct the developers toward other energy investments. The legal challenges were filed September 22 and include a case in U.S. District Court in Maine involving offshore wind leases held by affiliates of Invenergy.

The Maine case challenges an agreement involving three offshore wind leases, including two in the Gulf of Maine. Invenergy affiliates had paid nearly $11 million for the two Gulf leases. Under the challenged federal agreement, the company would relinquish offshore wind leases while directing investment toward other domestic energy projects, including natural gas-fired power plants in the Midwest and geothermal projects in the western United States.

A second lawsuit, filed in the Eastern District of New York, challenges a separate agreement involving Bluepoint Wind. Together, the two agreements provide approximately $1.4 billion from the federal Judgment Fund, a permanent federal appropriation generally used to pay judgments and settlements against the government. The states contend the administration improperly used that fund and did not follow federal requirements governing cancellation of offshore wind leases.

The coalition includes Maine, Connecticut, Delaware, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Rhode Island and Vermont. The states argue the canceled projects would have supplied electricity needed to meet growing regional demand. They are asking federal courts to invalidate the agreements and restore the offshore wind leases.

The Department of the Interior has defended its agreements as part of President Trump's energy policy. In announcing its settlement with Invenergy in June, the department said redirecting investment toward natural gas and geothermal generation would strengthen domestic energy security and provide reliable, affordable power. The states dispute both the legality of the transactions and the administration's authority to cancel the leases through these agreements.
