🏛️ South Portland limits city cooperation with immigration enforcementEmergency ordinance takes effect immediately after unanimous vote 👇

EducationOctober 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 29, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The South Portland City Council unanimously approved an emergency ordinance restricting city employees from voluntarily assisting federal immigration enforcement, adding the city to a growing list of Maine communities adopting similar policies. The measure took effect immediately following the October 27 vote.

The ordinance applies to municipal employees, including police and school staff. Employees may not voluntarily participate in immigration-enforcement stops, arrests or detentions or use municipal funds and resources to facilitate federal immigration enforcement. City employees also cannot provide immigration agents special access to nonpublic areas of city property or allow city property to be used for staging or processing operations.

Employees are restricted from sharing addresses, contact information or immigration status with ICE or Border Patrol for immigration-enforcement purposes beyond what is required by law. Police also cannot transfer someone into federal immigration custody without a court order or judicial warrant.

The ordinance does not prevent ICE from operating in South Portland and does not give the city authority over federal agents. Instead, it establishes rules governing how city employees and resources may be used when federal immigration enforcement occurs locally.

Councilors used an emergency ordinance to bypass the city's usual process of two readings followed by a waiting period. The Portland Press Herald reported that councilors wanted the restrictions in effect before Election Day. South Portland had already formally opposed municipal cooperation with federal immigration enforcement since 2017 and adopted another resolution addressing increased federal immigration activity earlier this year.

Similar restrictions have been adopted by several other Maine municipalities, including Portland, Lewiston, Rockland, Waterville and Biddeford. Maine also enacted statewide limits on cooperation with federal immigration authorities earlier this year.
