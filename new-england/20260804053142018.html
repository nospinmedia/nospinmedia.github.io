📱 New Hampshire educators report fewer classroom distractionsState survey finds strong support for bell-to-bell cell phone policy 👇

EducationAugust 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 4, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Nearly one year after New Hampshire's statewide bell-to-bell cell phone policy took effect, a new survey by the New Hampshire Department of Education found that school leaders and teachers overwhelmingly believe the restrictions have improved classroom focus and reduced student distractions. According to the survey, 88% of secondary school administrators and nearly 80% of secondary teachers said their local policies have been either "very effective" or "somewhat effective" at limiting non-instructional phone use during the school day.

The survey included responses from 1,399 secondary teachers and 158 secondary administrators across the state. Before local policies were implemented, 68% of teachers reported seeing students use phones for non-instructional purposes sometimes, often or very often during class. Following implementation, 75% said they now rarely or never observe that behavior. Educators also reported improved student engagement, stronger face-to-face interaction during breaks and lunch, and fewer classroom interruptions.

The statewide requirement was enacted through House Bill 2, which added RSA 189:1-a and requires local school boards and chartered public school boards to adopt policies limiting student use of personal communication devices during the instructional day. The law also requires annual policy review and permits exceptions for documented medical needs, disability-related accommodations and language-proficiency needs when appropriate.

Governor Kelly Ayotte said the survey results demonstrate that limiting classroom phone use is helping students stay focused while strengthening learning environments. State education officials said local districts continue to implement their own policies within the framework established by state law, allowing flexibility in enforcement while meeting the statute's minimum requirements.

Education officials emphasized that the survey reflects educators' experiences during the policy's first school year rather than measuring long-term academic outcomes. The department said it expects districts to continue reviewing and refining their policies as implementation continues.
