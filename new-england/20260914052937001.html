🦟 West Nile risk rises across most of MassachusettsSeventh human case extends concern into Worcester County 👇

HealthSeptember 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 14, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Massachusetts health officials have raised the West Nile virus risk level to moderate across most of the state after confirming the seventh human case of the mosquito-borne illness this year. The latest patient is a man in his 60s who was exposed in Worcester County, the first human case reported there this season after the state's previous six cases were associated with Suffolk and Middlesex counties.

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health announced the expanded risk designation on September 11, 2026, citing the new case and increased West Nile activity. Only Dukes County, Nantucket County, and the Cape Cod communities of Provincetown and Truro remain at low risk. Eleven Greater Boston communities remain classified as high risk: Boston, Brookline, Cambridge, Chelsea, Everett, Malden, Newton, Revere, Somerville, Watertown and Winthrop.

State surveillance has found West Nile virus in 307 mosquito samples this season across 13 counties. The first positive mosquitoes were announced in June, and no animal cases have been reported. Massachusetts has also detected 14 mosquito samples positive for Eastern equine encephalitis, or EEE, although no human or animal EEE cases have been reported in the state this year.

Public Health Commissioner Robbie Goldstein warned that mosquitoes remain active and that West Nile risk will continue until the first hard frost. Officials recommend using EPA-registered insect repellent, wearing long sleeves and pants when practical, limiting exposure during peak mosquito activity from dusk to dawn and eliminating standing water around homes where mosquitoes can breed.

The expanded moderate-risk designation means West Nile precautions now apply broadly across Massachusetts rather than primarily to communities around Greater Boston.
