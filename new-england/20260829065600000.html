🏥 Easterseals NH Reverses Planned Senior Care ClosuresManchester and Rochester programs will remain open 👇

HealthAugust 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 29, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Easterseals New Hampshire has reversed its decision to close adult day care centers in Manchester and Rochester, allowing hundreds of seniors and their families to continue receiving services after state officials and community leaders pressed the nonprofit to reconsider. The organization's board voted on August 27 to keep both facilities open and continue providing in-home personal care services while working with state leaders on a longer-term financial solution.

The nonprofit had announced on August 19 that the programs would close by October 17, citing declining enrollment since the COVID-19 pandemic, persistent staffing shortages and reimbursement rates that no longer covered operating costs. The announcement prompted immediate concern from families, caregivers and state officials, who warned that eliminating the services would leave many older adults without daytime supervision, memory care and respite programs while increasing pressure on nursing homes and hospitals.

Under the revised plan, Easterseals will continue operating its adult day programs serving approximately 130 seniors and maintain homemaker and personal care services for 73 clients in Hillsborough and Strafford counties through at least June 30, 2027, under existing contracts with the New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services. Easterseals NH & VT President and CEO Maureen Beauregard credited discussions with Gov. Kelly Ayotte, Executive Councilor John Stephen and DHHS Commissioner Lori Weaver for helping identify a path that keeps services available while broader funding issues are addressed.

Although the immediate closures have been avoided, Easterseals officials said long-term challenges remain. The organization continues to face workforce shortages and financial pressures tied to Medicaid reimbursement rates, and leaders say additional work will be needed to ensure the programs remain financially sustainable beyond the current funding period.
