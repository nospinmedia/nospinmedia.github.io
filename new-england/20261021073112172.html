⛽ Massachusetts House leaders reject Healey's gas tax suspensionTwo-month, 24-cent-per-gallon tax holiday removed from spending bill 👇

EconomyOctober 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 21, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Massachusetts House leaders have rejected Gov. Maura Healey's proposal to temporarily suspend the state's 24-cent-per-gallon gasoline and diesel tax, removing the plan from a nearly $2.4 billion spending bill moving through the Legislature.

Healey proposed suspending the tax for two months as gasoline prices remained elevated across Massachusetts. Her administration estimated the plan would provide about $120 million in relief. The lost gas-tax revenue would have been replaced with projected surplus collections from the state's Fair Share surtax, preserving money dedicated to roads, bridges and public transportation.

House Ways and Means Chairman Aaron Michlewitz, a Democrat, questioned whether suspending the tax would produce meaningful savings for consumers. He called the proposal a "gimmick policy" and said the governor's plan lacked an adequate mechanism to prevent wholesalers or distributors from raising prices while the tax was suspended.

Healey's original proposal would have required fuel retailers to reflect the suspended tax in prices charged to customers. The governor said Tuesday night that she was disappointed by the House decision and would continue seeking passage of the tax suspension, either by restoring it to the spending bill or pursuing it separately.

The proposal has also become an issue in the 2026 Massachusetts governor's race. Republican candidate Michael Minogue has proposed suspending the gas tax whenever gasoline exceeds $4 per gallon and has criticized Healey's two-month plan as too limited. Healey had opposed a gas-tax suspension during her 2022 campaign but said current fuel prices warranted temporary relief.

AAA reported that regular gasoline averaged about $4.26 per gallon in Massachusetts on Tuesday, more than a dollar higher than a year earlier.
