🔥 Heating Bills Spike Across New EnglandCold snap drives costs higher 👇

EconomyDecember 26, 2025By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published December 26, 2025 at 7:29 AM ET

New England households are seeing noticeable increases in home heating bills as colder temperatures combine with elevated natural gas and electricity prices, according to utility data released in December 2025. Some customers report double-digit percentage increases compared to last winter.

### What happened Utilities including Eversource and National Grid reported higher per-unit costs tied to constrained natural gas supply and increased regional demand. Cold weather across Massachusetts, New Hampshire, and Rhode Island has driven heavier furnace and electric heater use.

### Who is affected and why it matters Renters and fixed-income households are most exposed, especially in older housing stock with limited insulation. Even modest rate increases can translate into hundreds of dollars in added seasonal costs, placing pressure on household budgets during peak winter months.

### Broader context New England’s reliance on natural gas, combined with limited pipeline capacity, has long made the region vulnerable to winter price spikes. While renewable generation has expanded, it has not fully offset heating demand during prolonged cold periods.

### What comes next State regulators are reviewing consumer assistance programs and monitoring rate adjustments as winter progresses.
