⚖️ Appeals court weighs nearly $3 billion in Harvard funding cutsJudges question whether lower court had authority to restore research grants 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationOctober 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 6, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A federal appeals court is weighing whether a judge had the authority to restore nearly $3 billion in federal research funding that the Trump administration cut from Harvard University, in a case that could determine both the fate of the money and which courts can hear disputes over federal grants.

A three-judge panel of the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals in Boston heard arguments October 5 over a lower-court ruling that found the administration acted unlawfully when it froze or terminated billions of dollars in Harvard research grants. The cuts were imposed amid the administration's broader confrontation with the university over its handling of antisemitism and other issues on campus.

In September, U.S. District Judge Allison Burroughs ruled that the government had violated federal law by using research funding as leverage to force changes at Harvard. Her decision restored more than $2.6 billion in grants and contracts and blocked additional funding cuts. Burroughs said combating antisemitism was a legitimate goal but concluded that the administration's actions were not sufficiently connected to that objective and violated Harvard's First Amendment rights.

The administration appealed, arguing that Burroughs lacked jurisdiction because disputes involving federal contracts and grants belong in the U.S. Court of Federal Claims. Government lawyers told the appeals court that Harvard's case is fundamentally a dispute over federal funding rather than a constitutional case that should be decided by a federal district court.

Harvard's attorneys countered that the university is challenging the legality of the government's actions, not simply seeking payment under contracts. They argued that forcing Harvard to pursue individual grant disputes in the Court of Federal Claims would leave the government free to use funding terminations to pressure the university while litigation continued.

The appeals court did not immediately rule. Its decision could determine whether Harvard keeps billions in restored research funding while also shaping how universities and other federal grant recipients can challenge future funding cancellations.
