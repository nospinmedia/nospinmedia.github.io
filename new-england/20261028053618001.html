⚖️ Barre officer’s credibility concerns affect dozens of criminal casesProsecutors dismiss about 50 cases following misconduct investigation 👇

LegalOctober 28, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 28, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Prosecutors in Washington County, Vermont, have dismissed approximately 50 criminal cases after an investigation raised concerns about the credibility of a Barre City Police Department officer whose work was central to the prosecutions.

The cases were dropped after prosecutors reviewed allegations involving the officer's conduct and determined that questions about the officer's credibility could affect their ability to rely on the officer's testimony or investigative work in court. The affected cases included matters in which the officer was an investigating officer or an important witness.

The dismissals followed an internal investigation by the Barre City Police Department. The officer has been placed on administrative leave while the allegations and their consequences continue to be reviewed. Officials have not publicly released all details of the underlying misconduct allegations, limiting what can currently be established about exactly what prompted the credibility concerns.

The issue has consequences beyond the officer's employment because prosecutors are required to disclose information that could undermine the credibility of law enforcement witnesses. Under longstanding constitutional rules, potentially exculpatory or impeachment evidence must be provided to defendants when it could affect the fairness of a criminal proceeding.

That can make cases difficult or impossible to prosecute when an officer whose credibility has been questioned provided essential evidence, conducted important interviews or would need to testify at trial. Prosecutors therefore reviewed pending cases connected to the Barre officer individually before determining which could continue without relying on that officer.

WCAX reported that roughly 50 cases have been dismissed, while VTDigger described dozens of criminal cases as being affected by the investigation. The cases involve defendants who had already been moving through Vermont's criminal justice system, making the consequences of the credibility review immediate rather than limited to future prosecutions.

The situation could also require authorities to examine whether previously resolved cases involving the officer need additional review. Officials have not announced how many past convictions or closed cases, if any, could ultimately be affected.

The investigation remains ongoing, and additional information about the officer's alleged conduct and employment status could be released as the review progresses.
