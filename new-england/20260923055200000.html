⚖️ NH chief justice resigns after admitting ethics violationsNearly $50,000 payout to longtime ally was at center of probe 👇

LegalSeptember 23, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 23, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Gordon MacDonald has resigned as chief justice and a justice of the New Hampshire Supreme Court after admitting to three violations of the state's Code of Judicial Conduct involving the removal and rehiring of a longtime professional ally. His resignation became effective September 15 after the court approved an agreement resolving proceedings brought by the state's Judicial Conduct Committee.

The investigation centered on Dianne Martin, a senior Judicial Branch employee who previously served as MacDonald's chief of staff when he was New Hampshire attorney general. Martin was removed from her position as director of the Administrative Office of the Courts in April 2025 and rehired days later in another Judicial Branch position. The layoff allowed her to receive nearly $50,000 for unused sick and vacation time and other benefits.

The Judicial Conduct Committee concluded that MacDonald failed to promote public confidence in the judiciary, failed to perform administrative duties competently and diligently in connection with Martin's employment, and failed to avoid favoritism. MacDonald admitted those three violations rather than contesting the findings through a public hearing. In his resignation letter to Gov. Kelly Ayotte, he said he accepted "responsibility and accountability" for his conduct.

The committee also found that an October 2025 public statement issued by the Supreme Court inaccurately portrayed Martin's removal and rehiring as part of a routine administrative reorganization. Records released with the investigation included hundreds of pages of emails, text messages and other documents detailing discussions among court officials about Martin's employment.

MacDonald became chief justice in 2021 after being nominated by Republican Gov. Chris Sununu. Before joining the court, MacDonald served four years as New Hampshire attorney general and had been active in Republican politics. Supreme Court justices serve in nonpartisan judicial positions, and the misconduct findings concerned MacDonald's administration of the court, not his political affiliation.
