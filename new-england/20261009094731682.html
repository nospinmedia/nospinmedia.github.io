📁 Maine Attorney General's Office accidentally leaks 45 pages of confidential case detailsReport sent to news organizations reveals information about active investigations and uncharged suspects 👇

HealthOctober 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 9, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Maine Attorney General's Office accidentally distributed a 45-page confidential report containing sensitive information about ongoing criminal investigations to multiple news organizations on Thursday, October 8, 2026. The disclosure included previously unreleased details about homicide investigations, missing persons cases and individuals who have not been charged with crimes.

The document, identified as a Criminal Division Report, was mistakenly attached to an email sent to a distribution list that included journalists and state employees. Its opening page carried a warning that it could contain confidential information, including attorney work product.

According to the Portland Press Herald and Bangor Daily News, the report contained updates on murder and manslaughter investigations, financial crimes, drug cases, healthcare-related offenses and other pending legal matters. Some entries described evidence gathered by investigators, injuries suffered by homicide victims and possible suspects who had not been formally charged.

Other information included financial amounts under investigation, details from interviews in missing persons cases and anticipated developments in criminal proceedings. Some of the information had previously appeared in public records, but significant portions had not been publicly disclosed.

The email was sent at approximately 2:50 p.m. Thursday by Kristy Winslow, a senior paralegal and supervisor in the Attorney General's Criminal Division. It appeared intended for attorneys and investigators who were being asked to update information for an internal report.

Deputy Attorney General Lisa Bogue subsequently replied to the email recipients with additional instructions. Both officials later attempted to recall their messages.

Approximately 32 minutes after the original email, the office sent a follow-up instructing recipients to delete the confidential document and destroy any printed copies.

Danna Hayes, spokesperson for Attorney General Aaron Frey, acknowledged the mistake and said the office accepted responsibility. She warned that further disclosure could threaten the integrity of criminal investigations and the safety of individuals connected to those cases.

Frayla Tarpinian, executive director of the Maine Commission on Public Defense Services, expressed concern about the potential consequences. She explained that releasing information before criminal charges are filed could complicate investigations, make evidence gathering more difficult and unfairly expose people who may ultimately be cleared of wrongdoing.

The incident raises questions about how confidential legal information is handled and why an internal criminal investigation report was distributed to an external media mailing list.

Officials have acknowledged the disclosure, but the full impact on the affected investigations remains unclear. There has been no confirmed finding that any prosecution has been compromised as a result of the mistake.

Should government agencies handling sensitive criminal investigations be required to publicly explain how accidental disclosures occurred and what safeguards will prevent them from happening again?
