⚖️ New Hampshire Sets High-Stakes Veto DayLawmakers will test whether Ayotte's vetoes stand 👇

EconomyAugust 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 13, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

New Hampshire lawmakers will return to Concord on August 19 for the Legislature's annual Veto Day, when the House and Senate will decide whether to uphold or override a record number of bills vetoed by Gov. Kelly Ayotte. The session is expected to determine the fate of legislation affecting energy policy, housing, state spending and several other major issues before the 2026 legislative cycle moves into its next phase.

Ayotte issued 29 vetoes during this year's legislative session, the highest total for a New Hampshire governor with unified party control of the Legislature in decades. Under the state constitution, overriding a veto requires a two-thirds vote of lawmakers present in both the House and Senate, making attendance and party unity critical to the outcome of each vote.

Among the measures expected to receive override votes are bills involving state energy requirements, housing and zoning policies, budget provisions and other legislation that cleared both chambers earlier in the year but was rejected by the governor. Legislative leaders have scheduled the votes so lawmakers can determine which proposals, if any, will become law despite the governor's objections.

Because Republicans hold majorities in both chambers but not enough seats to guarantee two-thirds support on every issue, several override attempts are expected to be closely watched. Individual absences or defections could determine whether specific bills survive or permanently fail for the remainder of the legislative session.

The outcome of Veto Day will shape New Hampshire's policy agenda heading into the fall and establish which measures become law before lawmakers turn their attention to the next legislative cycle.
