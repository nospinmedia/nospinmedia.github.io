⚖️ Judge dismisses federal lawsuit against HarvardGovernment can try again after fixing procedural problems 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationNovember 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 3, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A federal judge in Massachusetts dismissed the Trump administration's lawsuit seeking detailed admissions records from Harvard University, ruling that federal agencies had not completed required procedural steps before asking a court to force the university to provide the information.

U.S. District Judge Myong J. Joun dismissed the case without prejudice Monday, meaning the federal government can file another lawsuit after addressing the deficiencies identified by the court. The ruling did not determine whether Harvard discriminated in admissions or whether the university may ultimately be required to provide the requested records.

The Justice Department began a compliance review in 2025 examining whether Harvard continued considering race in admissions after the U.S. Supreme Court's 2023 decision barring race-conscious admissions programs at Harvard and the University of North Carolina. The Department of Education later joined the effort.

Federal officials sought five years of applicant-level information from Harvard's undergraduate, medical and law schools. Requested records included grades, test scores, application essays, extracurricular activities, admissions decisions, and applicants' race and ethnicity. The government said the information was needed to determine whether Harvard was complying with federal civil rights law.

Joun found that the government had not adequately demonstrated that it followed procedures requiring agencies to identify compliance problems and attempt to obtain voluntary cooperation before seeking judicial enforcement. He also found procedural shortcomings involving the Education Department's participation in the case.

The decision leaves the underlying dispute unresolved. It does not determine whether the government's records request is lawful, whether Harvard has violated Title VI of the Civil Rights Act, or whether federal officials can ultimately obtain applicant-level data. The Justice Department has indicated that it intends to pursue the matter again.
