🏙️ Burlington Businesses Press Mayor Over Downtown SafetyCity seeks more state help for homelessness and addiction 👇

HealthAugust 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 29, 2026 at 5:23 PM ET

Business owners packed Burlington City Hall on August 28 to urge Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak to take stronger action on public safety, homelessness and open drug use in the city's downtown. During the meeting, merchants described frequent encounters with discarded needles, public drug use and other conditions they said have discouraged customers from visiting downtown businesses. Several owners also reported declining foot traffic and warned that continued public safety concerns are affecting the local economy.

Mayor Mulvaney-Stanak told attendees that the city is expanding efforts to recruit police officers, increase outreach services for people experiencing homelessness and addiction, and install additional public restroom facilities downtown. She said Burlington remains committed to improving public safety but argued that the city cannot solve the broader behavioral health and housing challenges without greater assistance from the state.

The meeting followed a unanimous Burlington City Council resolution approved on August 24 requesting additional support from Gov. Phil Scott and state agencies. Among the requests are funding for emergency shelter capacity, support for a proposed 100-bed low-barrier shelter, expanded resources for the Chittenden County Accountability Docket, and increased patrol assistance from the Vermont State Police. City officials contend that coordinated state and local action is needed to address the intertwined issues of homelessness, addiction and public safety.

Governor Scott has expressed reservations about some of Burlington's requests while emphasizing the need to balance resources across Vermont. Business owners at the meeting said they hope city and state officials can reach common ground before the busy fall tourism season, warning that prolonged inaction could further affect downtown businesses and public confidence.
