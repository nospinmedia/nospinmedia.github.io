💰 Former Burlington school administrator investigated over $1.8 millionFBI alleges fake company was used to bill district for more than a decade 👇

EducationOctober 28, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 28, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Federal authorities are investigating a former longtime Burlington School District administrator for allegedly using a company she controlled to obtain nearly $1.8 million from the Vermont school system over more than a decade.

Federal court documents allege that Stephanie Phillips, 57, created a company called Leading and Learning Consultants of VT and used it to submit fraudulent invoices to the district. Investigators allege the company received at least $1,769,200 in district funds dating back to March 2013.

According to an FBI affidavit reported by Seven Days, some invoices allegedly charged the district for work Phillips was already responsible for performing as part of her regular job, effectively allowing her to be paid twice. Investigators also allege that other invoices were submitted for work that was never performed. An FBI agent described the business in court documents as appearing to be a shell company created and controlled by Phillips.

Phillips spent 28 years working for the Burlington School District and most recently served as its executive director of teaching and learning. She also served as interim superintendent in 2014. Phillips retired from the district this spring.

The FBI searched Phillips' Colchester home last week as part of the investigation, according to the district. Federal court documents state that investigators believe there is probable cause involving possible mail fraud, wire fraud and theft concerning programs receiving federal funds. Phillips has not been charged with a crime, and the allegations have not been proven in court.

Superintendent Tom Flanagan told the school community that the district discovered "concerning financial discrepancies" shortly after Phillips left and immediately reported them to authorities. The district says it is cooperating with local and federal investigators.

School officials have since reviewed and strengthened procurement and invoicing procedures, including placing tighter limits on individual spending. The district also plans to bring in an outside expert to review its financial safeguards.

Flanagan said that if the allegations are substantiated, the district will seek to recover the money and return it to programs serving students.
