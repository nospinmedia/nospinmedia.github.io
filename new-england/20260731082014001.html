📜 Massachusetts Legislature Approves PROTECT Act Limiting State Role In Civil Immigration EnforcementBill Heads To Governor Healey After Final Passage 👇

EducationAugust 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 1, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The Massachusetts Legislature has given final approval to the PROTECT Act, a sweeping immigration measure that limits how state and local agencies may participate in federal civil immigration enforcement. The House and Senate approved the compromise legislation on July 30, sending the bill to Governor Maura Healey for consideration after months of negotiations between lawmakers, the governor's office, law enforcement representatives and immigrant advocacy organizations.

The legislation prohibits state and local law enforcement officers from asking about a person's immigration status during routine encounters unless it is directly relevant to a criminal investigation. It also bars civil immigration arrests without a judicial warrant at sensitive locations including public schools, child care centers, hospitals, doctors' offices and state courthouses. The bill further requires multilingual guidance for responding to immigration enforcement actions, establishes deadlines for processing U and T visa certifications for crime victims, protects pre-arranged guardianship plans if parents are detained or deported, and requires employers to notify workers before certain federal immigration-related workplace inspections.

Supporters say the legislation strengthens due process protections, promotes trust between immigrant communities and local law enforcement, and clarifies that Massachusetts resources should not be used for routine federal civil immigration enforcement. Several Democratic lawmakers described the measure as a response to increased federal immigration activity and an effort to ensure residents continue accessing schools, hospitals and courts without fear.

Some immigration advocates praised the bill while expressing concern over language that would allow future 287(g) agreements with federal immigration authorities under limited circumstances if approved by the governor and focused on criminal investigations. Even if enacted, the legislation would not prevent federal immigration officers from enforcing federal immigration law within Massachusetts.

Governor Healey has not yet announced whether she will sign the legislation. If approved, the PROTECT Act would establish some of the most comprehensive state-level limits on cooperation with federal civil immigration enforcement in New England.
