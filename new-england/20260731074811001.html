🌊 Heavy Rain Triggers Sewage Overflow Into Alewife BrookHealth Advisory Issued For Waterway Through Arlington, Cambridge And Somerville 👇

EnvironmentJuly 31, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published July 31, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Heavy rainfall on July 30 overwhelmed portions of the combined sewer system serving Cambridge and Somerville, triggering a combined sewer overflow that discharged a mixture of stormwater and untreated wastewater into Alewife Brook. Public notifications were issued warning residents to avoid contact with the brook until water quality improves following the discharge.

The overflow occurred when intense rainfall exceeded the capacity of the region's combined sewer system, which carries both stormwater and wastewater through the same network of pipes. Officials said the controlled overflow is designed to prevent sewage from backing up into homes, businesses and streets during major storm events. Cambridge reported activation of its Alewife Brook combined sewer overflow outfall, while Arlington directed residents to combined sewer overflow notifications maintained by Cambridge, Somerville and the Massachusetts Water Resources Authority (MWRA).

Health officials advised residents to avoid swimming, wading, fishing or allowing pets to enter Alewife Brook until bacteria levels return to normal. People who come into contact with the water are encouraged to wash thoroughly, as combined sewer overflows can contain elevated levels of bacteria and other contaminants following heavy storms.

Alewife Brook has long experienced combined sewer overflows during periods of intense rainfall because portions of Greater Boston's sewer infrastructure date back more than a century. The MWRA and local communities have reduced overflow events significantly over the past several decades through infrastructure improvements, but combined sewer overflows remain part of the region's emergency stormwater management system while additional upgrades are evaluated.

Environmental groups have continued urging additional investment to further reduce combined sewer overflows in the Alewife Brook watershed, while the MWRA is evaluating future infrastructure projects to improve water quality and reduce the frequency of emergency discharges.
