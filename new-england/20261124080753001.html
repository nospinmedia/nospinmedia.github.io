🗳️ Maine sets new midterm turnout record with 731,486 ballotsNew Hampshire and Vermont also break records as New England turnout surges 👇

PoliticsNovember 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 24, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Maine has officially set a new record for voter participation in a midterm election, with 731,486 ballots cast in the November 3 election, surpassing the state's previous record by more than 50,000 votes.

Secretary of State Shenna Bellows transmitted Maine's certified election results to Gov. Janet Mills on Monday, November 23, confirming turnout equal to approximately 63.8% of the state's estimated citizen voting-age population.

The previous Maine midterm record was 680,909 ballots in 2022, when approximately 61.8% of the citizen voting-age population participated. Maine led the nation in midterm turnout that year, and Bellows said the state is expected to again rank among the country's leaders when the U.S. Election Assistance Commission releases nationwide 2026 data in early 2027.

Maine was not alone. New Hampshire and Vermont also established new midterm turnout records in 2026.

More than 641,625 ballots were cast in New Hampshire, surpassing the state's previous midterm high from 2022 by nearly 15,000 votes. The total also exceeded Secretary of State David Scanlan's pre-election projection of approximately 635,000 voters.

Vermont recorded 311,510 ballots, also establishing a new state midterm record.

Massachusetts came remarkably close to joining them. Secretary of the Commonwealth William Galvin reported 2,749,609 ballots cast, representing about 52.6% of registered voters. That was approximately 3,056 votes short of the state's midterm record of 2,752,665 set in 2018.

Participation increased in 343 of Massachusetts' 351 municipalities, with turnout jumping by more than 25% in Gateway Cities including Lawrence, Springfield, Chelsea and Brockton. In-person early voting also accounted for 12.6% of Massachusetts ballots, up from 7.5% in 2022.

The figures show Maine's record was part of a broader pattern of unusually high participation across northern New England, although states use different measures when calculating and reporting turnout percentages.
