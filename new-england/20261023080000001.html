⚖️ New Haven agrees to $16.5 million wrongful conviction settlementAdam Carmon spent nearly 29 years imprisoned before his conviction was overturned 👇

Illustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalOctober 23, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 23, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The city of New Haven, Connecticut, has agreed to pay $16.5 million to settle a federal civil rights lawsuit brought by Adam Carmon, who spent nearly 29 years in prison for a murder conviction that was later overturned. The settlement still requires approval from the city's Board of Alders.

Carmon was convicted in 1995 in connection with a February 1994 shooting in which more than a dozen bullets were fired through the window of a New Haven apartment. Seven-month-old Danielle Taft was killed and her grandmother was paralyzed.

A Connecticut Superior Court judge overturned Carmon's conviction in November 2022, concluding that suppressed information and newly discovered forensic evidence likely would have changed the outcome of his trial. Prosecutors subsequently declined to retry the case and dismissed the charges.

Carmon sued New Haven and six former police officers in 2023. His lawsuit alleged that officers suppressed favorable evidence, fabricated evidence, included false information in a warrant affidavit and coerced a self-incriminating statement. The case also raised questions about firearms evidence and eyewitness identification methods used during the original investigation.

New Haven Mayor Justin Elicker apologized to Carmon, saying the city has an obligation to respond when there has been a miscarriage of justice. Elicker also said some investigative techniques involved in the case are no longer used by the New Haven Police Department.

The $16.5 million payment comes in addition to $7.9 million Carmon previously received from Connecticut as compensation for his wrongful conviction. Because the city did not have insurance covering wrongful-incarceration settlements when the case originated, New Haven expects to borrow the full amount to pay the settlement.

The Carmon case is one of several costly wrongful-conviction cases confronting New Haven. A federal jury awarded $38 million to Stefon Morant in May, while the city recently agreed to pay a combined $30.5 million to Vernon Horn and Marquis Jackson in two other cases. The city is appealing the Morant verdict.
