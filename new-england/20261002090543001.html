💡 Up to 67,000 Maine households could get automatic electric bill discountsCMP customers can save up to 81% and Versant customers up to 83% 👇

EnvironmentOctober 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 2, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Up to 67,000 low-income Maine households could receive discounts on their electric bills this year after the state changed its assistance program so eligible residents already receiving certain public benefits can be enrolled automatically.

The change affects Maine's Low Income Assistance Program, or LIAP, which provides percentage-based electric bill discounts to eligible customers of Central Maine Power and Versant Power. Previously, qualifying households generally had to apply for LIAP separately through their electric utility. Under the new system, the Maine Department of Health and Human Services can use information from existing public-benefit programs to automatically connect eligible households with the electric assistance program.

The potential savings are substantial and vary based on income and utility. Eligible CMP customers can receive discounts of up to 81% on their electric bills, while qualifying Versant Power customers can save up to 83%, according to the Department of Health and Human Services. State officials estimate that tens of thousands of households that were eligible but had not previously enrolled could now receive assistance because they no longer need to complete a separate application.

People already receiving qualifying public health insurance, food assistance or heating benefits are among those who can be automatically enrolled. Gov. Janet Mills said the change is intended to remove paperwork and other administrative barriers that have prevented eligible Mainers from receiving electric-bill assistance.

Funding for the program has nearly doubled this year to approximately $40.5 million. Maine Public reports that the assistance is financed through the state budget, a surcharge collected on utility bills and money from a fund established by developers of the New England Clean Energy Connect transmission project in western Maine.

The automatic enrollment comes as Maine households head toward another winter facing elevated energy costs, making participation in existing assistance programs increasingly important for lower-income residents.
