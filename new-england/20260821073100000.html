🎗️ Hundreds Rally Outside Clancy TrialSupporters Spotlight Postpartum Mental Health Before Closing Arguments 👇

HealthAugust 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 21, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

More than 300 people dressed in pink gathered outside Plymouth Superior Court on August 20 in a silent demonstration supporting maternal mental health awareness as the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy entered its final stages. Participants wore shirts bearing messages including "Peace for Lindsay" and "Believe", while organizers said the event was intended to highlight gaps in care for mothers experiencing severe perinatal mental illness rather than protest the judicial process. The gathering marked the largest public demonstration held during the nearly month-long trial.

Inside the courthouse, 36-year-old Lindsay Clancy faces three counts of first-degree murder for the January 24, 2023, deaths of her children — Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months. Defense attorney Kevin Reddington argues Clancy was suffering from postpartum psychosis and bipolar disorder and lacked criminal responsibility at the time of the killings. Prosecutors contend she acted with intent and have challenged the defense's psychiatric conclusions throughout the trial.

Organizer Renee Kimball said the demonstration sought to draw attention to what supporters describe as shortcomings in diagnosing and treating severe postpartum psychiatric disorders. Demonstrators called for expanded maternal mental health resources, earlier intervention, and greater awareness among healthcare providers and families. The case has generated nationwide attention and renewed debate over how the legal system addresses defendants with serious mental illnesses following childbirth.

Judge William Sullivan indicated that the defense is expected to conclude its case after testimony from psychiatrist Dr. Phillip Resnick, with prosecutors scheduled to present rebuttal evidence before closing arguments begin. Jurors will ultimately determine whether Clancy was criminally responsible under Massachusetts law or whether a mental disease or defect prevented her from appreciating the wrongfulness of her actions.
