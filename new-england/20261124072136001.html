⚖️ Boston sues Trump administration over new immigrant housing ruleMore than 500 local families could lose subsidies under new eligibility requirements 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EconomyNovember 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 24, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The City of Boston and Boston Housing Authority are suing the Trump administration over a new federal rule restricting housing assistance for mixed-status immigrant families, a change city officials say could cost more than 500 Boston families their housing subsidies.

Boston and the BHA joined a coalition of 17 cities and public housing authorities in filing a lawsuit Monday in the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts. The case is one of several legal challenges seeking to stop the rule, which takes effect December 14.

The new Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) policy changes longstanding rules allowing mixed-status families to live together in federally assisted housing when at least one household member is a U.S. citizen or otherwise eligible noncitizen. Assistance currently is prorated so ineligible family members do not receive a subsidy.

Under the new rule, every household member would generally have to establish eligible citizenship or immigration status for the family to continue receiving assistance. Mixed-status households will have until March 14, 2027, to submit proof of eligibility before losing assistance.

The rule also tightens how citizenship is verified. Rather than relying on a signed declaration alone, tenants claiming U.S. citizenship will face a two-step verification process requiring certified physical documentation, such as a U.S. passport or birth certificate. HUD is also extending immigration and citizenship verification requirements to all 8.5 million HUD-assisted tenants, eliminating a previous exemption for people 62 and older.

Boston officials estimate more than 500 families in the city could be affected, while Massachusetts officials put the statewide impact at roughly 900 households.

HUD's regulatory analysis estimates approximately 20,000 mixed-status families, totaling about 78,100 to 79,300 people nationwide, could be affected. That includes roughly 55,000 U.S. citizens and other eligible noncitizens living with household members who do not qualify for assistance.

Boston's case is separate from another lawsuit brought by Massachusetts and other states and jurisdictions, while tenant and immigrant-rights organizations have filed an additional challenge in Massachusetts federal court.

HUD Secretary Scott Turner has defended the policy as enforcement of federal eligibility requirements, arguing that housing assistance should be directed toward qualified applicants currently on waiting lists.
