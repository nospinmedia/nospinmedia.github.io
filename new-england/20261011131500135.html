🏥 Brigham nurses postpone planned strike after hospital makes final offerNearly 4,000 union nurses will vote on proposed contract 👇

HealthOctober 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 11, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Nearly 4,000 union nurses at Brigham and Women's Hospital in Boston have postponed a planned strike after hospital management presented what it called its “best and final offer” during contract negotiations.

The nurses, represented by the Massachusetts Nurses Association, had been preparing for a 24-hour strike as negotiations continued over a new labor agreement. The union's elected bargaining committee agreed to postpone the walkout so nurses can review and vote on the hospital's proposal.

The decision avoids an immediate labor disruption at one of Boston's largest hospitals, but it does not necessarily end the contract dispute. The proposed agreement must still be approved by the union's membership.

Contract negotiations have focused on issues including wages, staffing, workplace safety and health benefits. Nurses have raised concerns about staffing levels and security, while the hospital has emphasized its compensation proposals and efforts to maintain patient care during the labor dispute.

The union plans to present the hospital's final proposal to its nearly 4,000 members for a ratification vote. A date for that vote had not yet been announced as of Saturday.

The postponed strike would have required Brigham and Women's to rely on replacement nurses and contingency staffing. Hospital officials had previously said they were prepared to maintain operations if the walkout occurred.

With the strike postponed, hospital services are expected to continue normally while nurses consider the proposed contract.

The dispute comes amid broader labor pressure in Massachusetts health care, where nurses at several major hospitals have pushed for higher wages, stronger staffing protections and improved workplace safety. Nurses at Boston Medical Center recently reached a new three-year contract, avoiding a separate strike.

For Brigham nurses, the next major decision now moves from the bargaining table to the membership. Approval would settle the dispute under the proposed agreement, while rejection could reopen the possibility of further labor action.
