🌧️ Nor’easter keeps soaking Massachusetts as flood threat lingersTaunton River rises after weekend storm brought damaging winds and outages 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 28, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 28, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A slow-moving nor’easter continues to affect Eastern Massachusetts Monday after a weekend of heavy rain, damaging winds, coastal flooding and widespread power outages across New England. Although the strongest winds have eased, additional showers and runoff are keeping flooding concerns active as the storm gradually moves away from the region.

The National Weather Service issued a Flood Warning for the Taunton River near Bridgewater, where water levels are expected to remain elevated into Tuesday. Parts of Eastern Massachusetts received several inches of rain during the storm, increasing runoff into rivers and streams and leaving some low-lying and poorly drained areas vulnerable to additional flooding.

The storm's strongest winds occurred over the weekend, with gusts reaching roughly 50 to 70 mph in some coastal and elevated locations. Trees and power lines came down across portions of New England, contributing to thousands of outages. New Hampshire was among the states reporting thousands of customers without electricity during the storm, while coastal communities from Massachusetts through Maine dealt with rough surf and strong winds.

Conditions along the coast also remain hazardous. Gale Warnings and Small Craft Advisories have remained in effect for portions of the region's coastal waters as rough seas gradually subside. Residents near the shoreline are being advised to continue watching conditions even as the storm weakens.

Rain is expected to become less widespread through Monday before the nor’easter finally pulls away Monday night into Tuesday. Forecasts call for substantially improved conditions Tuesday, followed by warmer and brighter weather later in the week.

The latest flooding concerns represent the final phase of a storm that has affected much of New England for several days, shifting the primary threat from damaging winds and coastal impacts toward river flooding and lingering runoff.
