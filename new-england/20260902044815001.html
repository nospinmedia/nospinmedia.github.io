🗳️ Massachusetts Voters Set Major November MatchupsMarkey and Minogue Advance After Primary Wins 👇

HealthSeptember 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 2, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Massachusetts voters settled several major races Tuesday, setting up November contests for U.S. Senate, governor and an open congressional seat. Democratic U.S. Sen. Ed Markey defeated U.S. Rep. Seth Moulton in the Democratic Senate primary, while Republican businessman Mike Minogue defeated former MBTA chief Brian Shortsleeve for the GOP nomination for governor.

Markey, 80, is seeking a third full Senate term and will face Republican attorney John Deaton in November. Moulton, 47, had made generational change a central theme of his campaign, challenging a senator whose congressional career began with his election to the U.S. House in 1976 before Markey moved to the Senate in 2013. Moulton conceded Tuesday night and congratulated Markey, who entered the race with strong support from progressive organizations, labor groups and prominent Democratic officials.

The governor's race is also now set. Minogue, a former chief executive of medical-device company Abiomed and a political newcomer, will challenge Democratic Gov. Maura Healey, who faced no opponent in her party's gubernatorial primary. Minogue had secured the Massachusetts Republican Party's convention endorsement earlier this year and entered primary day as the favorite over Shortsleeve. His victory gives Republicans their nominee for a race in which Healey is seeking a second term.

Another closely watched result came in Massachusetts' 6th Congressional District, where Dan Koh won the six-candidate Democratic primary for the seat Moulton is leaving behind. Koh defeated a field that included Tram Nguyen, John Beccia, Jamie Belsito, Bethany Andres-Beck and Mariah Lancaster. The district covers much of the North Shore and portions of the Merrimack Valley. Koh will advance to the November general election as Democrats seek to retain the seat.

With the primary complete, Massachusetts voters now have their major-party choices in several of the state's most prominent federal and statewide races.
