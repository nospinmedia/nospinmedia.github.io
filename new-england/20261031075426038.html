🗳️ New Hampshire predicts record midterm turnoutState expects about 635,000 voters to cast ballots Tuesday 👇

PoliticsOctober 31, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 31, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

New Hampshire election officials are predicting record turnout for a midterm election, with approximately 635,000 people expected to cast ballots when voters head to the polls Tuesday.

Secretary of State David Scanlan announced the projection ahead of the November 3 general election, saying participation could surpass New Hampshire's previous midterm record. The estimate represents roughly 60% of the state's voting-age population and reflects strong interest in several competitive federal and state races.

New Hampshire's previous midterm turnout record was set in 2018, when more than 580,000 ballots were cast. Turnout remained high in 2022, when more than 570,000 voters participated. Reaching Scanlan's new projection would therefore represent a substantial increase over both elections.

Election officials are also monitoring absentee voting as an early indicator of participation. Requests and returned absentee ballots have been running at levels that reinforce expectations of heavy Election Day voting, although most New Hampshire voters traditionally cast their ballots in person.

The state does not offer general early in-person voting. Absentee voting is available to people who meet qualifying conditions under state law, leaving the majority of ballots to be processed at polling places Tuesday. Local election officials are preparing for potentially heavy traffic throughout the day.

The turnout projection comes during a consequential New Hampshire election featuring contests for federal offices, governor, the state Legislature and other state and local positions. The 635,000 figure is a forecast rather than an actual count, and the final turnout will not be known until ballots are cast and counted.

Scanlan's office is also preparing election officials for implementation of New Hampshire's current voter-identification requirements, including changes affecting which identification documents can be accepted at polling places.
