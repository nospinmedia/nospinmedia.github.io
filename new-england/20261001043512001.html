⚖️ Family sues after Springfield teen drowns on school tripLawsuit says 150 children were in pool limited to 105 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationOctober 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 1, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The family of 14-year-old E’Mari Marshall-Woodard, a Springfield eighth grader who drowned during a school field trip in Connecticut, has filed a wrongful death lawsuit alleging overcrowding and safety failures at the pool where he died. E’Mari, a student at Kiley Prep Middle School, drowned on June 10, 2026, at High Meadow Resort in Granby, Connecticut, while hundreds of students from several school districts were visiting the facility.

According to the lawsuit, the pool normally had a maximum capacity of 142 people, but the closure of its diving area reduced the permitted capacity that day to 105. The complaint alleges that High Meadow nevertheless allowed more than 150 students into the pool. It also alleges that only three lifeguards were assigned to supervise and control access for the more than 800 students who were at the resort and seeking access to the pool during the field trips.

The lawsuit says the crowding made it difficult for swimmers to move safely and prevented lifeguards and other staff from adequately monitoring people in the water. It also alleges poor water clarity contributed to visibility problems. E’Mari disappeared beneath the surface in shallow water and was later pronounced dead. The allegations have not been proven in court.

The complaint also alleges that High Meadow had not received the required annual authorization from the Farmington Valley Health District to open the pool for the season. Two days after E’Mari's death, health district inspectors examined the pool, identified multiple deficiencies and ordered it to remain closed pending another inspection, according to the lawsuit. The defendants named in the case are High Meadow Management Company and 311 LLC. High Meadow did not provide a comment to news organizations reporting on the lawsuit.
