🏢 200 Franklin Towers Residents Must Relocate During Major RenovationHazardous Materials Force Portland Housing Authority to Abandon In-Building Moving Plan 👇

IllustrationIllustration of hazmat crews responding to a refinery chemical leak.Credit: AI-generated illustration (No Spin Media)
EnvironmentOctober 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 17, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

About 200 low-income residents of Franklin Towers in Portland, Maine, will have to temporarily leave the 16-story apartment building while it undergoes a major renovation, after hazardous materials complicated plans to keep residents inside during construction.

The Portland Housing Authority had originally planned to renovate Franklin Towers in phases, moving residents between floors as work progressed. Environmental testing and the scope of hazardous-material removal made that approach impractical, and residents have now been told they will need to relocate elsewhere during the project.

The building has documented problems including asbestos, PCBs, mold, bedbugs and cockroaches. Renovation work involving walls, flooring and other building materials could disturb hazardous substances, requiring residents to be moved away from the construction areas.

Franklin Towers contains 200 subsidized apartments and primarily houses elderly and disabled residents. The Portland Housing Authority plans extensive improvements to the aging building, including renovated apartments, new windows, air conditioning, upgraded building systems and improvements to common areas.

Housing officials are working to find temporary accommodations for residents and provide relocation assistance. Residents who move during construction will continue receiving housing assistance, and the project is intended to preserve Franklin Towers as affordable housing rather than permanently displace its tenants.

The relocation presents a significant challenge in Portland, where affordable housing is already limited. Some residents have lived at Franklin Towers for many years and rely on its central location for medical care, transportation and other services.

Officials expect relocating the building's residents to take several months before major renovation work can proceed. Residents have been meeting with housing officials to learn more about where they may be placed and how the moving process will work.
