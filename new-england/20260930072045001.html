🚔 Burlington police lieutenant resigns amid five misconduct investigationsProsecutors had raised concerns about his credibility 👇

EconomySeptember 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 30, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A longtime Burlington Police Department lieutenant resigned while facing five internal investigations involving his conduct, work hours, supervision and nearly $50,000 in excess pay. Former Lt. Michael Henry resigned in August before the department completed all of its investigations.

One investigation began in January after another officer reported that Henry was spending hours in a police break room while on duty. Investigators found that on one day he spent nearly nine hours there and on numerous other days about six hours. Another investigation began after Henry approached the officer who had reported him. Investigators concluded that he abused his authority as a lieutenant and undermined the integrity of the internal review.

A separate investigation found that Henry had been overpaid nearly $50,000 during the previous year, including some paychecks that were approximately $5,000 higher than they should have been. Henry said he did not routinely examine his pay stubs. He has repaid about $41,000, which he maintains is the amount he owes, while the city says the total overpayment was nearly $9,000 more.

Henry's credibility had also become an issue for prosecutors. Chittenden County State's Attorney Sarah George raised concerns about his conduct during the investigation of the 2023 killing of Kelly Cusson. An internal investigation did not substantiate an allegation involving comments Henry reportedly made, but investigators found that he failed to activate his body camera.

On July 15, George issued a Brady letter, meaning information that could affect Henry's credibility would have to be disclosed to defendants in criminal cases. George also said her office would no longer call Henry as a witness or accept criminal cases from him, potentially limiting his ability to perform important parts of his job.

The city's investigations ended when Henry resigned rather than reaching a final disciplinary process. Burlington Mayor Emma Mulvaney-Stanak said questions about an officer's credibility and competency can undermine confidence in the criminal justice system.
