⛽ Connecticut Republicans seek three-month fuel tax holidayProposal would suspend gas and diesel taxes through December as prices surge 👇

EconomyOctober 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 2, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

Connecticut Republican lawmakers are calling for a special legislative session to suspend state gasoline and diesel taxes through December 31, proposing a three-month tax holiday as sharply higher fuel prices increase costs for drivers and businesses.

The proposal from Senate Minority Leader Stephen Harding and House Minority Leader Vincent Candelora would temporarily suspend Connecticut's 25-cent-per-gallon gasoline tax and 49.9-cent-per-gallon diesel tax. The lawmakers are asking Gov. Ned Lamont and Democratic legislative leaders to bring the Connecticut General Assembly back into session to consider the plan.

The proposal could provide substantial savings at the pump but would also reduce state revenue. According to estimates reported by the CT Mirror, suspending the fuel taxes would cost approximately $54 million per month, or about $128 million through the end of December. Republicans argue Connecticut can absorb the cost using state reserves while providing immediate relief to residents and businesses.

The push comes as Connecticut motorists face unusually high fuel costs. Regular gasoline was averaging about $4.48 per gallon, while diesel had reached roughly $6.48 to $6.50 per gallon when Republican leaders announced the proposal. Diesel prices are particularly significant for trucking, construction, agriculture and other businesses whose transportation costs can eventually affect prices paid by consumers.

Republicans proposed the temporary suspension on September 30. It has not been enacted and would require legislative approval. Democrats control both chambers of the General Assembly, making their support necessary for the proposal to advance.

Connecticut used a similar approach during the fuel-price surge in 2022, temporarily suspending its gasoline tax before gradually restoring it. The current proposal would again trade several months of state fuel-tax revenue for immediate savings at the pump.
