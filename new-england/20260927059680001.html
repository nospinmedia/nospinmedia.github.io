☕ Man arrested in racist vandalism at Manchester coffee shopNH Civil Rights Unit takes over prosecution after swastikas and 'White Power' graffiti 👇

EconomySeptember 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 27, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

New Hampshire authorities have arrested a 20-year-old Allenstown man in connection with the racist vandalism of The Moka Pot coffee shop in downtown Manchester, a major development in a case that drew widespread community condemnation earlier this month.

Tyler Inman was arrested Sept. 24 and charged with criminal mischief and falsifying physical evidence. Police allege that during the early morning hours of Sept. 16, windows at The Moka Pot on Hanover Street were smashed and swastikas and the words 'White Power' were spray-painted on the building. The shop remained open after employees and supporters cleaned up the damage.

Manchester police said investigators established probable cause for Inman's arrest after reviewing evidence from the area. According to court records reported by Manchester Ink Link, investigators used footage gathered through the city's Axon Fusus camera system to trace a person approaching the coffee shop shortly before the vandalism. The records allege that surveillance captured the person carrying a hammer and spray paint before returning toward the business.

Court records also allege that investigators later found a Ku Klux Klan patch in Inman's bedroom while executing a search warrant. Police further accuse him of altering parts of his vehicle after the vandalism, allegations underlying the falsifying physical evidence charge. Inman has not been convicted, and the charges remain allegations as the case proceeds through court.

The New Hampshire Department of Justice Civil Rights Unit announced Friday that it would take over prosecution because of what it called the serious nature of the allegations. The unit had already been notified when the vandalism was initially investigated as a potential hate crime.

Police records describe The Moka Pot as a business that identifies itself as a safe space for LGBTQ+ people and minority communities. Authorities have not publicly established why the shop was selected, but police said the graffiti appeared intended to intimidate.

The arrest follows an outpouring of support for the coffee shop after the vandalism. Manchester Police Chief Peter Marr said investigators pursued every lead in the case, while Mayor Jay Ruais said the incident did not represent the city and thanked police for making an arrest.
