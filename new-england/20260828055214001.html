⚖️ First Circuit revives Bow parents' free-speech challengeCourt says pink 'XX' protest likely received unequal treatment 👇

EducationAugust 28, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 28, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A federal appeals court has revived a First Amendment challenge brought by New Hampshire parents and a grandparent who were sanctioned after wearing pink "XX" wristbands during a girls' soccer game at Bow High School. The 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled on August 26 that the plaintiffs had shown a reasonable likelihood of succeeding on their claim that the Bow School District engaged in viewpoint discrimination by restricting their passive protest because of the message it expressed.

The dispute began during a September 17, 2024 game between Bow High School and Plymouth Regional High School. Plaintiffs Kyle Fellers, Anthony Foote, Nicole Foote, and Eldon Rash opposed the participation of transgender girls in girls' sports and used the wristbands as a silent protest. School officials instructed participants to remove the wristbands, and additional sanctions followed, including no-trespass restrictions affecting access to school athletic events.

In its ruling, the appeals court emphasized that the protest was passive and did not involve chanting, heckling or other conduct that disrupted the game. The court said public-school administrators have authority to respond to genuine disruption and harassment, but concluded that the record indicated officials restricted the wristbands because they disagreed with the viewpoint being expressed. The panel said schools cannot favor one side of a public debate involving parental speech while suppressing the opposing viewpoint solely because administrators consider that message objectionable or demeaning.

The decision reverses the lower court's analysis but does not finally decide that the district violated the Constitution. The appeal arose from the parents' request for preliminary relief, and the First Circuit sent the case back to the U.S. District Court for the District of New Hampshire to determine whether an injunction, declaratory judgment or other interim remedy is appropriate. The appellate court also did not decide the underlying policy question of transgender participation in school sports; its ruling focused on government restrictions on spectator speech.
