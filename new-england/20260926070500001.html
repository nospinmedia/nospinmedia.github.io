🏛️ Lawrence mayor indicted on 15 new state chargesProsecutors allege secret recording and witness intimidation 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EconomySeptember 26, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 26, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Lawrence Mayor Brian DePeña has been indicted on 15 state charges involving an alleged secret recording inside City Hall, adding a second criminal case against the mayor within weeks.

The Massachusetts Attorney General's Office announced Thursday that a statewide grand jury indicted DePeña on 14 counts of distributing a wiretapped communication and one count of witness intimidation. His arraignment on the new charges has not yet been scheduled.

Prosecutors allege DePeña had a private security system installed for his office beginning in 2023, separate from Lawrence City Hall's existing security cameras. In March, one of those cameras allegedly captured both audio and video of a hallway conversation between two city employees without the knowledge of at least one participant. City-operated security cameras do not record audio, and prosecutors say there was no public notice that DePeña's private system was doing so.

The Attorney General's Office alleges DePeña knew the conversation had been recorded with audio and then distributed copies of it to 14 people. Prosecutors separately allege that he intimidated a witness connected to the investigation in August. The charges are allegations, and DePeña is presumed innocent unless proven guilty.

The new state case comes after a federal grand jury indicted DePeña in August on four counts of wire fraud and seven counts of money laundering. Federal prosecutors allege he fraudulently obtained more than $1.5 million in COVID-era small-business loans and used money for personal expenses and his political campaign. DePeña has pleaded not guilty in that case and remains mayor of Lawrence.

The two prosecutions are separate: the federal case concerns pandemic business loans, while the new state case concerns the alleged City Hall recording and its distribution.
