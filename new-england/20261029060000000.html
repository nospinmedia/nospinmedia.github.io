⚖️ Legal groups sue over ICE policy concealing detainee locationsMore than 16,000 people were removed from online locator system 👇

ICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EconomyOctober 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 29, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Four immigrant legal-services organizations have sued the Trump administration in federal court in Boston, challenging a new ICE policy that withholds the locations of detainees who have final deportation orders from the agency's online locator system.

The policy was adopted in September as the administration sought to accelerate deportations. According to the lawsuit, it affected more than 16,000 of the nearly 69,000 people in ICE custody at the time. ICE's online system has been used since 2010 by attorneys and families to determine where detainees are being held as they are transferred among facilities.

The plaintiffs include Massachusetts-based Greater Boston Legal Services and the Rian Immigrant Center, along with the Florence Immigrant and Refugee Rights Project and Amica Center for Immigrant Rights. They argue that removing detainees from the locator and directing ICE personnel not to disclose their locations has made it difficult or sometimes impossible for attorneys to find clients.

The organizations say the change has resulted in attorneys spending significantly more time locating people, contributed to delayed or missed legal filings and reduced the number of cases some organizations can accept. Having a final removal order does not necessarily end every legal proceeding. Detainees may still seek to reopen cases, challenge detention conditions or pursue relief involving removal to another country.

The lawsuit argues that ICE adopted the policy without adequate explanation in violation of the Administrative Procedure Act and that it interferes with the organizations' First Amendment rights to provide legal advice and seek clients. The groups are asking a federal judge to block the policy.

DHS disputes the characterization that detainees are being disappeared, saying people in custody have ways to communicate with their families and attorneys. The department says people with final deportation orders are being prioritized for removal.

No court has yet ruled that the ICE policy is unlawful.
