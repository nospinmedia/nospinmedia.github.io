🤖 Trumbull's data center ban won't stop existing site from adding AIResidents sought tougher restrictions this week — zoning officials said no 👇

EconomyOctober 25, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 25, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Residents of Trumbull, Connecticut, have lost a bid to impose tougher restrictions on data centers as the town's existing facility undergoes a major modernization designed to support artificial intelligence computing.

The Trumbull Planning and Zoning Commission rejected a proposed one-year moratorium Wednesday that would have restricted new data centers and prevented existing facilities from increasing their capacity or intensity. Commissioners said the proposal's language was vague and could be difficult to enforce, while also noting that Trumbull currently has no applications for new data centers.

The decision does not undo a separate two-year moratorium approved by the Town Council on September 9. That measure prevents the establishment and development of new data centers through September 2028. However, it specifically does not apply to data centers already operating in town.

That distinction matters because Trumbull has a 228,000-square-foot data center at 80 Merritt Boulevard that has operated for more than 50 years. The property is undergoing a modernization expected to make it capable of handling higher-density computing equipment used for AI workloads. The project is associated with property owner Aphorio Carter and prospective long-term tenant 365 Data Centers.

Residents seeking the additional moratorium argue that existing facilities should also face restrictions on future increases in power consumption, water use, noise or computing capacity. A petition supporting additional protections collected more than 1,460 signatures.

Town officials have emphasized that the current project is a renovation, not construction of a new hyperscale AI data center. First Selectman Vicki Tesoro has said the modernization will remain within the property's existing electrical allocation, will not increase noise or water use, and will reduce the building footprint and wastewater discharge. The existing data center is also Trumbull's largest taxpayer.

The debate comes as data-center development faces increasing scrutiny across Connecticut. Several municipalities have adopted temporary moratoriums or bans while considering how rapidly expanding AI infrastructure could affect electricity demand, water use, noise and local development. State lawmakers are also discussing possible statewide restrictions.
