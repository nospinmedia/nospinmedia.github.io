🔥 New England heating oil bills could jump $900 this winterMaine households may face even steeper increases 👇

EconomyOctober 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 4, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Households across New England that rely on heating oil are facing substantially higher costs heading into the 2026–2027 winter, with regional estimates indicating a typical household could spend roughly $900 more than last winter. The increase could be even steeper in Maine, where a recent analysis estimates some families may spend as much as $1,260 more over the heating season.

Heating oil prices are already near historic highs across Northern New England. The Maine Governor's Energy Office reported a statewide average near $5.96 per gallon, with regional prices reaching approximately $6.30 per gallon. In New Hampshire, cash heating oil prices have been averaging around $5.50 per gallon in early October, although prices vary by supplier and location.

The increase represents a significant financial burden in a region where heating oil remains a major source of home heat. A household using hundreds of gallons during a typical New England winter can see relatively small changes in the per-gallon price translate into hundreds of dollars in additional seasonal expenses. Maine's higher projected increase illustrates how the impact can vary considerably across the region depending on local fuel prices and household consumption.

Residents concerned about paying their winter heating bills can seek assistance through programs including the federally funded Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) and state-administered fuel assistance programs. Energy officials encourage households that may qualify to apply early rather than waiting until the coldest part of winter, when demand for assistance typically increases.
