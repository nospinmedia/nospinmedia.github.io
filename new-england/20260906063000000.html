🇺🇸 More than 1,000 rally in Danbury after wave of ICE arrestsMore than 60 businesses close as immigration enforcement shakes community 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EducationSeptember 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 6, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

More than 1,000 people rallied in Danbury, Connecticut, on September 2 following a surge in federal immigration arrests that advocates and local officials say resulted in more than 100 detentions statewide, including more than half in Danbury. The demonstration capped a local "Day Without an Immigrant", during which more than 60 businesses closed in solidarity with immigrant families affected by the enforcement operation.

Most of the reported arrests occurred between August 24 and August 28, with Danbury experiencing the largest concentration. Local officials and advocates said federal immigration officers made arrests at businesses and in public areas. Reports that arrests also occurred near school-bus stops drew particular concern from families and elected officials. White House border czar Tom Homan said he was not aware of a specific bus-stop arrest and would seek additional information from the Department of Homeland Security.

One man identified publicly as Luis told rally attendees that immigration officers detained him while he was shopping with his young son at a Danbury grocery store. He said he was moved from Connecticut to detention facilities in Massachusetts and Colorado before being released about a week later and returned to his family.

Danbury Mayor Roberto Alves said uncertainty surrounding the federal operation had created widespread fear in the community. The enforcement activity also affected local schools, with officials reporting substantial student absences as some families kept children home.

Connecticut's U.S. senators have since become directly involved. Sen. Chris Murphy spoke at the September 2 rally and called reported ICE detentions near school-bus stops an "insidious tactic." On September 3, Sen. Richard Blumenthal convened a public town hall in Danbury to hear testimony from residents, local officials and advocacy groups about the impact of the federal enforcement surge.

The demonstration, widespread business closures and subsequent congressional involvement show how the enforcement operation has developed into a broader dispute over federal immigration tactics and their effects on the Danbury community.
