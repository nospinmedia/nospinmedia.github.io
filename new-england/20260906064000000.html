🏛️ Maine lawmakers say ICE plans new facility in NewportFormer district courthouse would expand agency's presence into central Maine 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EconomySeptember 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 6, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement is reportedly preparing to open a facility in Newport, Maine, at the former district courthouse at 12 Water St., according to state lawmakers who learned about the plans following a recent visit to ICE's existing facility in Scarborough. ICE has declined to publicly confirm the Newport plans, citing security concerns involving its personnel.

State Rep. Mike Soboleski, R-Phillips, announced the proposed facility after joining four other Republican lawmakers on a tour of the Scarborough ICE site. Soboleski said a new facility would soon be under construction at the former Newport courthouse and would help speed up the deportation process. He later said he did not have additional details about the project's timeline or planned operations and referred questions to ICE.

Rep. Mathew McIntyre, R-Lowell, who participated in the Scarborough tour, said he overheard discussion about a potential Newport office but was not involved in those conversations. Rep. Ken Fredette, R-Newport, said he had heard that a federal agency rented the former courthouse building but did not have specific information about ICE's plans.

The Newport District Courthouse closed abruptly on January 27, 2025, after mold was discovered in the leased building. Court cases previously handled there were shifted to the Penobscot County Judicial Center in Bangor, while state officials considered options for restoring a courthouse serving western Penobscot County.

The reported ICE expansion has drawn differing reactions. Supporters say another federal facility could increase the efficiency of immigration enforcement and provide local economic activity. Immigrant-rights advocates have opposed additional ICE infrastructure in Maine, while Rep. Chellie Pingree, D-Maine, described reports of the Newport facility as "deeply alarming" and said she would oppose an expansion of the agency's footprint in the state.

For now, important details remain unconfirmed, including when the facility would open, how many employees would work there and whether it would primarily function as an office, processing center or holding facility.
