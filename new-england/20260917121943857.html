⚖️ Jury hears recording from man accused of shooting Palestinian studentsDefense psychiatrist says he was psychotic and believed voices ordered attack 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationSeptember 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 17, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Jurors in Burlington, Vermont, heard a recording Wednesday in which Jason Eaton, the man accused of shooting three Palestinian college students in 2023, described believing that intelligence agencies and other voices were directing his actions before the attack. The recording was played during the third day of Eaton's trial as his attorneys presented their argument that he was legally insane at the time of the shootings.

Eaton is charged with three counts of attempted second-degree murder in the November 25, 2023 shootings of Hisham Awartani, Kinnan Abdalhamid and Tahseen Ali Ahmad. The three longtime friends were walking near Eaton's Burlington residence when they were shot. All survived, but Awartani was left paralyzed and uses a wheelchair. Eaton has pleaded not guilty and does not dispute that he was the shooter; the central issue at trial is his mental state and whether he was criminally responsible for his actions.

In portions of an interview recorded days after the shooting and played for jurors Wednesday, Eaton described believing he was receiving communications through FM radio and a Bluetooth headset from the CIA, Israeli intelligence and other sources. Eaton said he received what he perceived as an urgent command to act and subsequently believed the three students were the people he was supposed to confront.

Dr. Fabian Saleh, a forensic psychiatrist retained by the defense, testified that Eaton was suffering from a psychotic disorder involving hallucinations and delusions at the time. Saleh told jurors that, in his professional opinion, Eaton's mental illness prevented him from adequately appreciating the criminality of his actions. That assessment is the defense expert's opinion and is disputed by prosecutors. The state is expected to present expert testimony arguing that Eaton was not legally insane when the shootings occurred.

Eaton's sister also testified Wednesday about unusual behavior she had observed before the shootings, including an earlier conversation in which he spoke about receiving messages and described himself as a 'lightbearer.' Prosecutors questioned whether other explanations could account for some of Eaton's past behavior and challenged elements of the defense expert's conclusions.

The prosecution rested its initial case Tuesday after presenting testimony and forensic evidence that investigators say connects Eaton to the shootings, including a handgun recovered from his apartment that authorities say matched cartridge casings found at the scene.

The trial is expected to continue this week as jurors hear competing evidence about Eaton's mental condition and ultimately determine whether he is criminally responsible for the shootings.
