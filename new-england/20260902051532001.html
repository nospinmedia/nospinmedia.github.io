⚖️ Lawsuit Seeks to Remove NH School Tax Cap QuestionEmergency Filing Challenges Ballot Language 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationSeptember 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 2, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Two New Hampshire parents filed an emergency lawsuit in Merrimack County Superior Court seeking to prevent a statewide school property tax cap question from appearing on the November 2026 general election ballot. The complaint, filed against Secretary of State David Scanlan, argues that the ballot language required under House Bill 1300 is misleading and does not accurately explain how the proposed measure could affect local school district budgets.

The plaintiffs, Kelly Wieser, a member of the Campton School Board, and former Warner Budget Committee member David Bates, filed the lawsuit on Sept. 1 with representation from the law firm Hinckley Allen. They contend the ballot wording incorrectly suggests the spending limits would apply only to administrative costs and would not affect classroom instruction or other school services. According to the complaint, districts operating under the proposed limits could instead face broader budget decisions if revenue growth is restricted.

Under House Bill 1300, municipalities would vote on whether to adopt a local school district property tax cap that limits future spending growth. The measure requires approval by at least 60% of voters in each municipality before taking effect. The lawsuit asks the court to issue a preliminary injunction before ballots are finalized following the Sept. 8 state primary, arguing that voters should not be asked to decide the question using language the plaintiffs believe is legally defective.

The case places another major education funding issue before New Hampshire courts as election preparations continue across the state. If the court grants the requested injunction, election officials could be required to revise or remove the ballot question before November. If the request is denied, the measure is expected to remain on ballots while any additional legal challenges continue through the judicial process.
