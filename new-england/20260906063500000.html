⚖️ Boston judge blocks new USPS mail-ballot requirementsPreliminary injunction preserves existing procedures through midterms 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalSeptember 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 6, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A federal judge in Boston has blocked the U.S. Postal Service from enforcing new requirements for mail ballots during the November 3 midterm elections, extending an earlier temporary halt as states begin sending ballots to voters. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani issued the 49-page preliminary injunction on September 4, concluding that states and voting-rights organizations challenging the rule are likely to succeed on key legal claims.

The Postal Service finalized the rule in August following an executive order issued by President Trump in March. It would require state and local election officials using the mail for federal ballots to comply with new envelope-design standards, register with a federal ballot-mail portal and upload information about voters approved to receive mail ballots. Ballot mail failing specified requirements could be rejected and returned to election officials.

Talwani found that the plaintiffs are likely to establish that the Postal Service exceeded authority granted by Congress by effectively creating a new category of mail that could be refused based on election-specific requirements. Her decision also cited the Constitution's Elections Clause, which assigns regulation of congressional elections to states while giving Congress authority to alter those rules.

Timing was another major factor. Election administrators told the court that changing ballot envelopes and election systems can require weeks of preparation, while the Postal Service's voter-information portal was not yet operational. Talwani wrote that attempting to implement the requirements with voting already beginning made disenfranchisement of some eligible voters "practically inevitable."

The injunction applies to elections occurring through November 3 and prevents USPS from implementing the mandatory portions of the rule or taking additional preparatory steps to enforce them. It replaces a temporary restraining order Talwani issued on August 27.

The ruling does not permanently resolve the dispute. The administration has continued seeking appellate relief as litigation over President Trump's executive order and the subsequent Postal Service rule proceeds.
