💵 Maine Adds 2% Income Tax Surcharge On High EarnersNew rate applies above $1M for individuals and $1.5M for joint filers 👇

EducationAugust 11, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 11, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Maine's newly enacted supplemental budget includes a 2% income tax surcharge on high levels of taxable income, applying retroactively to tax years beginning January 1, 2026. The additional tax applies only to income above $1 million for single filers and above $1.5 million for heads of household and married couples filing jointly. Combined with Maine's existing top income tax rate of 7.15%, the surcharge creates a top marginal rate of 9.15% for income above the applicable threshold.

The surcharge applies only to income exceeding those limits rather than to a taxpayer's entire income. For example, a single filer with $1.1 million in Maine taxable income would owe the additional 2% tax on the final $100,000, resulting in an additional $2,000 in state income tax. The income thresholds will be adjusted for inflation beginning with the 2026 tax year.

State fiscal estimates cited by supporters project the surcharge will generate approximately $96 million during fiscal year 2027 and about $150 million combined during fiscal years 2028 and 2029. The revenue will flow into Maine's General Fund rather than being legally dedicated to a single program, helping support the broader state budget, including spending on education, health care, child care, housing and other services.

The change is expected to affect roughly 2,600 Maine tax filers, representing a small share of taxpayers statewide. Supporters argue the surcharge provides additional revenue from households with the highest taxable incomes, while opponents have warned that raising Maine's top marginal rate could affect business investment, competitiveness and decisions by high-income residents about where to live or invest.
