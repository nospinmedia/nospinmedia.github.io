💧 Drought grips Maine, New Hampshire and MassachusettsNearly half of Maine now in severe drought as water shortages persist across New England 👇

EnvironmentOctober 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 9, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Drought conditions continue to affect Maine, New Hampshire, Massachusetts and Vermont, with the latest federal monitoring showing worsening conditions across northern New England. Nearly half of Maine is now experiencing severe drought or worse, while parts of New Hampshire and Vermont have also seen conditions deteriorate.

According to the U.S. Drought Monitor, released October 8 using data through October 6, 2026, approximately 46% of Maine is experiencing at least severe drought (D2). That represents a substantial increase from approximately 19% the previous week.

About 5% of Maine remains in extreme drought (D3), particularly along portions of the southern and Midcoast coastline. The entire state is experiencing at least moderate drought conditions.

The most significant deterioration occurred across northern Maine, including much of Aroostook County and portions of Penobscot, Piscataquis and Somerset counties. Federal monitors expanded severe drought classifications after continued precipitation shortages and declining soil moisture.

New Hampshire is also experiencing persistent drought, although conditions vary considerably across the state. The latest federal assessment expanded moderate drought (D1) across northern New Hampshire as streamflows declined. Adjacent portions of Vermont experienced similar deterioration.

Southern New Hampshire saw some improvement following recent rainfall. Federal monitors removed moderate drought from southeastern New Hampshire and eliminated abnormally dry classifications from portions of south-central New Hampshire. However, other areas continue to face precipitation deficits and reduced water availability.

In Massachusetts, drought remains a concern despite improvements in several regions. Federal monitors maintained moderate drought in northeastern and southeastern Massachusetts because groundwater levels remain unusually low and streamflows decline quickly after rainfall.

Massachusetts' state drought classifications also identify Cape Cod and Nantucket County as experiencing significant drought, while the Connecticut River Valley, Northeast and Southeast regions remain under mild drought conditions. These state classifications use a different system from the federal Drought Monitor.

The continuing dry conditions raise concerns about private wells, agriculture, streamflows and wildfire risks. Low groundwater levels can persist even after substantial rainfall because replenishing underground water supplies generally takes longer than restoring surface moisture.

Maine illustrates that challenge. A late-September nor'easter delivered as much as 4 inches of rain in some locations, but the precipitation was insufficient to reverse the broader drought. Federal estimates cited by the Portland Press Herald indicate that Maine would need approximately 9 to 11 inches of rain within a month to eliminate its drought conditions.

The National Weather Service in Caribou reported that some water levels are approaching record lows, while groundwater remains below normal.

For residents across New England, especially those relying on private wells, the continuing drought underscores the importance of monitoring water supplies and following any local conservation restrictions.
