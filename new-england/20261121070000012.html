⚖️ Federal Judge Blocks Connecticut Restrictions On ICE AgentsRuling Halts Mask And Badge Rules While Other State Protections Remain 👇

EducationNovember 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 21, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A federal judge has temporarily blocked several parts of a Connecticut law regulating federal immigration agents, including provisions restricting masks, requiring visible name badges and expanding state oversight of federal officers' use of force.

U.S. District Judge Vernon Oliver issued a preliminary injunction Friday in United States v. State of Connecticut, blocking four sections of S.B. 397, enacted as Public Act 26-14. Oliver concluded that the federal government is likely to succeed in arguing that the challenged provisions violate the Supremacy Clause of the U.S. Constitution. The ruling is not a final decision in the lawsuit.

One blocked provision generally prohibited federal officers from wearing face coverings while interacting with the public during official duties. Another required visible identification. The injunction also affects provisions involving use-of-force standards and the authority of Connecticut's Inspector General to investigate and potentially pursue cases involving federal agents.

Oliver concluded that Connecticut likely cannot impose those requirements on federal officers performing federal duties. He also emphasized that federal officers do not have blanket immunity from state prosecution; protections depend on whether they were acting within their federal authority and whether their conduct was necessary and proper.

Other portions of Connecticut's law remain in effect. Those include provisions allowing civil lawsuits over alleged constitutional violations and protections involving immigration enforcement at sensitive locations such as schools, hospitals and places of worship.

Gov. Ned Lamont's office criticized the ruling, while Connecticut Attorney General William Tong's office said it was reviewing the decision and considering its next steps. Republican lawmakers welcomed the injunction, while supporters of the law maintain that states have legitimate authority to protect residents and oversee conduct within their borders.

Connecticut is not alone in facing this constitutional dispute. Federal judges have also blocked restrictions involving masks or identification for immigration agents in California, New York and Pennsylvania, creating similar conflicts over how far states can regulate federal enforcement operations.
