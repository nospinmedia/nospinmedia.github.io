⚖️ Lindsay Clancy Jury Reports DeadlockJudge Orders Deliberations to Continue 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthSeptember 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 2, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Jurors in the Lindsay Clancy murder trial told a Massachusetts judge they were unable to reach a unanimous verdict after more than 23 hours of deliberations spanning four days. In response, Plymouth Superior Court Judge William Sullivan instructed the 12-member jury to continue deliberating rather than ending the case without a verdict. Jurors are scheduled to resume discussions on Sept. 2 as they continue weighing one of the state's most closely watched criminal cases.

Clancy, a former labor and delivery nurse from Duxbury, is charged in the Jan. 24, 2023, deaths of her three children: 5-year-old Cora, 3-year-old Dawson, and 8-month-old Callan. Prosecutors contend the killings were deliberate, while defense attorneys argue Clancy was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis and lacked criminal responsibility under Massachusetts law at the time of the deaths. The jury has heard testimony from more than 80 witnesses and reviewed over 300 exhibits during the trial.

Judge Sullivan reminded jurors of the importance of continuing their deliberations given the length and complexity of the case before dismissing them for the day. The jury's note did not specify how members were divided or identify the issues preventing a unanimous decision. Massachusetts law requires a unanimous verdict in criminal cases, meaning deliberations may continue until jurors either reach agreement or the court determines they cannot do so.

The trial has drawn widespread public attention because of its focus on postpartum mental illness and the legal standards governing criminal responsibility. A verdict would determine whether Clancy is found criminally responsible for the deaths or whether the jury accepts the defense's mental illness argument. Until a unanimous decision is reached or the court declares otherwise, deliberations will continue.
