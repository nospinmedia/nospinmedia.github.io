🌧️ Nor’easter knocks out power and pounds New England coastGusts top 70 mph as dangerous surf reaches Maine 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 27, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A slow-moving September nor’easter battered New England with damaging winds, heavy rain, coastal flooding and power outages Saturday as the storm pushed its effects north toward Maine. More than 30,000 customers in Massachusetts, Rhode Island and Connecticut were without electricity at one point, while wind gusts reached 70 mph on Cape Cod and 71 mph on Block Island.

Connecticut recorded some of the region’s largest outages, while thousands also lost power in Massachusetts and Rhode Island. Utilities deployed hundreds of workers, but strong winds complicated restoration work because bucket trucks generally cannot be raised safely when gusts become too strong. In Rhode Island, crews had already restored service to nearly 30,000 customers by Saturday afternoon after wind and falling trees damaged electrical equipment.

Along the coast, the storm produced significant flooding and dangerous surf. Parts of Cape Cod and Martha’s Vineyard received more than 3 inches of rain, while a flash flood warning covered communities from Newport, Rhode Island, to Marion, Massachusetts. Flooding also affected coastal roads in Westerly and Misquamicut, Rhode Island, as high tides pushed water into low-lying areas.

The storm’s northern edge also began affecting Maine, particularly the southern coastline. Waves reached roughly 12 feet in some areas, sending water and debris across roads in Wells and pounding beaches in Kennebunk. Central Maine Power said it had restored service to more than 500 customers by Saturday afternoon. For Sunday, a Coastal Flood Advisory remains in effect for coastal York County, where a 1-to-2-foot storm surge combined with high astronomical tides could produce additional flooding, splashover and beach erosion. Breaking waves of 7 to 12 feet are also expected along portions of the southern Maine coast.

The storm is expected to remain close enough to New England for periods of rain, gusty winds and rough coastal conditions to continue through Sunday, with conditions gradually improving as the system weakens.
