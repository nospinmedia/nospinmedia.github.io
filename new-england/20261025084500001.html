⚖️ Judge blocks DHS door-to-door voter investigations in Connecticut and 8 statesAgents had been questioning residents about citizenship and whether they voted 👇

LegalOctober 25, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 25, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A federal judge has temporarily restricted Department of Homeland Security agents from conducting certain door-to-door election investigations in Connecticut and eight other states, after agents questioned residents about their citizenship and whether they had voted.

The investigations were part of a Homeland Security Investigations initiative examining possible voting by noncitizens ahead of the November midterm elections. Court records described a federal “surge initiative” that began September 1 and involved field interviews at people's homes.

An internal HSI document identified California, Georgia, Missouri, Pennsylvania, Washington and Wisconsin among states targeted for field interviews, while additional locations were redacted. The resulting court order applies in California, Connecticut, Georgia, Missouri, Nevada, New York, Pennsylvania, Washington and Wisconsin through the close of polls on Election Day.

The ruling does not prohibit the federal government from investigating suspected illegal voting by noncitizens. Instead, U.S. District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín restricted election-related field operations involving people whom agents know, suspect, or reasonably should know or suspect are U.S. citizens.

The distinction became important because voting-rights groups argued that federal databases being used to identify potential noncitizens could include American citizens. The plaintiffs said sending federal agents to people's homes to question them about citizenship and previous voting could intimidate eligible voters shortly before an election.

The judge concluded that the plaintiffs were likely to succeed on claims that the challenged operations violated protections against voter intimidation under the Voting Rights Act and issued a preliminary injunction while the case continues.

The federal government defended its authority to investigate possible violations of laws prohibiting noncitizens from voting in federal elections. Justice Department attorneys argued that restricting legitimate investigations could harm the public interest. DHS has also said it has no plans to question or investigate people it believes are U.S. citizens.

The case has particular regional significance because Connecticut is directly covered by the injunction, as is neighboring New York.
