⚖️ Sandra Birchmore's Friend Says She Urged Police to Investigate Matthew FarwellTrial Testimony Also Examines Early Police Investigation Into Birchmore's Death 👇

IllustrationPolice station exteriorCredit: AI-generated illustration (No Spin Media)
EducationOctober 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 16, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A longtime friend of Sandra Birchmore testified that she went to police after Birchmore's death and told them to investigate former Stoughton police officer Matthew Farwell, as testimony continued in Farwell's federal murder trial in Massachusetts.

Lesley Davis, who had known Birchmore since middle school, testified Thursday that Birchmore began telling her about Farwell when she was about 15 years old. Davis said Birchmore later discussed their sexual relationship and had told her she was pregnant and wanted Davis to be the baby's godmother.

Davis testified that she spoke with Birchmore for about 25 minutes on the night of her death in February 2021 and did not hear her express an intention to kill herself. After learning Birchmore had died, Davis said she went with her mother and brother to the Canton Police Department and told officers about Farwell. She testified that the officers did not take notes during that conversation. Davis later told another law enforcement officer that investigators should look into Farwell.

The defense questioned Davis about periods when her friendship with Birchmore was strained and pointed to evidence that Birchmore had increased her antidepressant dosage and had written a suicide note in 2017.

Earlier testimony also focused on the initial investigation. Massachusetts State Police Trooper Matthew Dunne testified that he did not record or take notes during two interviews with Farwell and did not attend Birchmore's autopsy. Dunne said investigators initially treated the death as a suicide and found no signs of a struggle inside the apartment.

Farwell is accused of killing the 23-year-old Birchmore and her unborn child and staging her death as a suicide. Prosecutors allege he began sexually abusing Birchmore when she was 15 and he was 27. Farwell has pleaded not guilty. His defense maintains that Birchmore died by suicide and says DNA testing established that Farwell was not the father of her unborn child.

The trial is expected to continue for several weeks in federal court in Boston.
