🔥 Wild River Fire grows to nearly 1,000 acres in White MountainsMore than 50 firefighters battle blaze as containment reaches 11% 👇

EnvironmentNovember 5, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 5, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The Wild River Fire burning in New Hampshire's White Mountain National Forest has grown to nearly 1,000 acres, more than doubling in size as crews continue working across steep and difficult terrain near the Maine border.

The fire was estimated at approximately 982 acres Wednesday, up from about 378 acres a day earlier. It was first reported Sunday in Coos County, roughly seven miles southeast of Gorham, and has been spreading primarily along its northern and western edges.

More than 50 firefighters are working the fire with support from two helicopters and reconnaissance drones. Crews have been conducting water drops and constructing containment lines, but steep slopes, rocky terrain, heavy timber and fallen trees have complicated suppression efforts. Officials reported the fire at approximately 11% contained.

The rapid growth marks a significant update from earlier this week, when the fire had expanded beyond 375 acres and more than 40 personnel were assigned to the response. Dry vegetation and prolonged dry conditions have contributed to the fire's spread.

The U.S. Forest Service said there were no immediate threats to homes or other residential structures. Smoke from the fire has been visible in communities across northern New Hampshire and parts of western Maine.

Officials are preparing a closure order for portions of the Wild River drainage area to protect firefighters and the public. Visitors are being asked to avoid the affected area and not to operate unauthorized drones near the fire because they can interfere with firefighting aircraft.

The cause of the Wild River Fire remains under investigation as crews continue containment efforts.
