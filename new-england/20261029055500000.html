🏛️ Vermont state senator pleads not guilty to domestic assault chargeTerry Williams was arrested less than a week before Election Day 👇

U.S. Capitol building in Washington, D.C.Credit: Architect of the Capitol
LegalOctober 29, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 29, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Vermont state Sen. Terry Williams, a Republican from Rutland County, pleaded not guilty Wednesday to a misdemeanor domestic assault charge after police alleged that he struck a family member in the face during an argument at his Poultney home. Williams, 74, is seeking reelection on November 3.

According to a police affidavit released after the arraignment, Williams' adult daughter told investigators that he struck her with an open hand Monday evening. A state trooper who interviewed her the following day reported seeing a reddish abrasion extending from her left eyelid toward her cheek. Williams told police that his daughter moved toward him during their argument and that he put up his hand and pushed outward, making contact with her face. He denied intentionally assaulting her.

Vermont State Police arrested Williams Tuesday after investigators said they developed probable cause that he had caused pain and injury to a household member. He was processed and initially jailed at Marble Valley Regional Correctional Facility on $500 bail.

Williams was released following Wednesday's court appearance. Judge Alexander Burke ordered him not to contact the alleged victim or witnesses in the case. Williams' attorney, Toni Dutil, said the senator is not guilty and intends to contest the charge.

Senate Minority Leader Scott Beck said domestic violence allegations should be treated seriously while emphasizing that everyone involved is entitled to due process. Williams has served in the Vermont Senate since 2022 and represents the three-seat Rutland County district.

The charge remains an allegation, and Williams has not been convicted of a crime.
