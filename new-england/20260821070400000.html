🦟 Massachusetts Detects First EEE Mosquitoes of 2026Four Plymouth County Towns Move to Moderate Risk 👇

HealthAugust 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 21, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health (DPH) confirmed the state's first Eastern equine encephalitis (EEE)-positive mosquito samples of 2026, prompting officials to raise the mosquito-borne disease risk level to moderate in Carver, Halifax, Kingston, and Plympton. The infected mosquitoes were collected in Kingston, marking the first confirmed detection of the virus this season. State officials said no human or animal cases of EEE have been identified in Massachusetts this year, but residents are being urged to take precautions as mosquito activity reaches its seasonal peak.

Public Health Commissioner Robbie Goldstein said the late-season detection provides a shorter period for widespread transmission than in some previous years but still warrants heightened vigilance because EEE is one of the most serious mosquito-borne diseases in the United States. Health officials also noted that West Nile virus has already been detected in mosquitoes from several Massachusetts communities this summer, creating overlapping mosquito-borne disease concerns across parts of the state. Residents are advised to use EPA-approved insect repellents containing DEET, picaridin, oil of lemon eucalyptus, or IR3535, wear long sleeves and pants outdoors during peak mosquito hours, and eliminate standing water where mosquitoes can breed.

State Epidemiologist Dr. Catherine M. Brown said Massachusetts continues to monitor mosquito populations through an extensive surveillance program that tracks virus activity throughout the season. Historically, EEE activity has followed cyclical patterns in the Commonwealth, with significant outbreaks occurring in 2019, 2020, and 2024. Human infection is rare but can be severe, with approximately 30% of symptomatic cases proving fatal and many survivors experiencing long-term neurological complications.

Mosquito control districts in southeastern Massachusetts are expanding surveillance and targeted mosquito control efforts in response to the positive samples. Health officials said additional mosquito testing will continue throughout the season and urged residents to stay informed about local risk levels as conditions change before colder autumn weather naturally reduces mosquito populations.
