🛩️ Coast Guard suspends search for 6 off NantucketElectrical trouble reported before medical jet rapidly descended 👇

HealthOctober 5, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 5, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The U.S. Coast Guard has suspended its search for six people aboard a medical transport jet that disappeared off Nantucket, Massachusetts, while flying from Bermuda to Boston, ending a 66-hour search-and-rescue operation after crews covered approximately 4,445 square miles without finding survivors.

The Canadian-registered Gulfstream G100, registration C-GRJP, operated by Latitude Air Ambulance, was carrying four Canadian crew members and two Bermudian nationals when it disappeared early Saturday, October 3. The aircraft had departed Bermuda's L.F. Wade International Airport and was headed to Boston Logan International Airport when air traffic controllers lost contact.

Air traffic control recordings and emergency dispatch communications indicate the aircraft was experiencing an electrical problem shortly before it disappeared. A controller asked the pilot whether the jet was "coming in blind," while emergency crews in Boston were initially told to prepare for an arriving aircraft experiencing an electrical issue. Flight-tracking data also showed the jet descending rapidly from approximately 24,000 feet to 10,000 feet before radar contact was lost. Investigators have not determined what caused the aircraft to go down.

Search crews located a debris field about 20 miles south of Nantucket and confirmed wreckage belonged to the missing aircraft. The debris was found in water approximately 80 to 90 feet deep. Coast Guard aircraft, helicopters, boats and cutters searched the surrounding Atlantic before officials suspended active search-and-rescue operations at sunset Sunday.

Attention is now shifting toward recovery and investigation. Authorities are coordinating sonar capabilities that could help locate the aircraft's main wreckage on the seafloor, while the National Transportation Safety Board is gathering information about the crash.

The identities of the six people aboard had not been publicly released as of the latest reporting.
