👮 Danbury opens police jobs to green card holders and DACA recipients18–2 vote expands eligibility while keeping existing hiring standards 👇

EconomySeptember 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 10, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The Danbury City Council has voted 18–2 to allow lawful permanent residents and recipients of Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals, or DACA, to apply to become city police officers. The change removes Danbury's previous U.S. citizenship requirement but does not open police jobs to all noncitizens or eliminate the department's existing hiring standards.

The council approved the ordinance change on September 1. Mayor Roberto Alves said the measure removes an unnecessary barrier for qualified residents who already live in the community and are legally authorized to work in the United States. Police Chief Patrick Ridenhour supported the change and emphasized that applicants will still face the department's normal screening process, including background reviews, testing and other requirements.

Danbury's action follows earlier changes at the state level. The Connecticut Police Officer Standards and Training Council partially waived its citizenship requirement in 2020, making lawful permanent residents eligible for appointment as probationary police candidates. In March 2025, POST expanded that waiver to include DACA recipients. State guidance says those applicants remain subject to the same training and certification requirements as other candidates.

Local officials said Danbury is joining the Connecticut State Police and more than 30 other municipalities that already allow lawful permanent residents or DACA recipients to apply for law-enforcement positions. Supporters said the change could broaden the applicant pool and help the department recruit from the city's diverse population.

The decision also drew opposition. Council member Candace Fay, one of the two members who voted against the change, questioned the policy's justification and raised concerns about whether expanding eligibility would improve public safety. The vote came shortly after federal immigration enforcement activity in Danbury, which had generated debate among local officials and residents.
