⚖️ Keene Reviews Limits On Local Response To ICE OperationsPacked council meeting follows immigration enforcement concerns 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
LegalAugust 18, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 18, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A special Keene City Council workshop drew a standing-room-only crowd on August 17, 2026, after recent U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) activity in the city prompted protests, community concern, and questions about the role local officials can play. The meeting was called by Mayor Jay Kahn following a federal enforcement operation conducted in Keene the previous week.

During the workshop, Police Chief Steven Russo told residents that the Keene Police Department did not assist ICE agents during the operation. City officials also explained that New Hampshire law places significant limits on municipalities attempting to adopt local policies restricting or interfering with federal immigration enforcement, leaving cities with little authority to direct or limit federal actions.

Mayor Kahn said he has contacted members of New Hampshire's congressional delegation seeking information about the enforcement activity and asking them to monitor potential civil rights concerns raised by residents. Officials emphasized that while the city can communicate with federal representatives and ensure local police follow state and local law, it cannot prohibit federal immigration authorities from carrying out their duties within the community. The workshop focused largely on clarifying those legal boundaries and responding to questions from residents about transparency and public safety.

The meeting followed several days of demonstrations and public discussion after reports of ICE activity in Keene sparked concerns among immigrant advocates and some residents. City leaders said they expect to continue monitoring the situation and remain in contact with state and federal officials as additional information becomes available.
