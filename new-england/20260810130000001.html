⚖️ NH AG Finds Amherst School Staff Violated Electioneering LawCease-and-desist follows campaign for $52 million school project 👇

EducationAugust 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 10, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The New Hampshire Attorney General's Office found that employees of the Amherst School District violated state election law while promoting a proposed $52 million renovation and expansion of Wilkins School ahead of the 2025 district election. Assistant Attorney General Matthew Broadhead issued Superintendent Mike Berry a cease-and-desist letter directing him to prevent similar conduct in the future.

The investigation centered partly on a promotional video produced at Wilkins School during the school day. Before filming, Berry and Principal Kathleen Murphy told parents that a film crew would gather footage highlighting areas of the building that could benefit from improvements as part of efforts supporting the Wilkins building initiative. Berry supported the $52 million warrant article, which ultimately failed to receive enough votes.

According to reporting based on the Attorney General's findings, investigators also determined that teachers and other Wilkins staff used school resources, including photocopiers, to produce flyers supporting the warrant article. Teachers then placed the materials in students' backpacks to take home. The Attorney General's Office concluded that the conduct violated RSA 659:44-a, New Hampshire's prohibition on electioneering by public employees. The law bars public employees from electioneering while performing their official duties or using government property or equipment for electioneering.

The $52 million proposal was the latest in a series of attempts to fund major work at the aging Wilkins School. A $49 million proposal failed in 2024, followed by the $52 million proposal in 2025. A separate $37 million citizen-petition proposal also failed in 2026.

The Attorney General's action was a cease-and-desist order, not an announcement of criminal charges against Berry or other district employees. The case establishes that school employees cannot use their official positions, public resources or students to promote a ballot question.
