🌬️ Two major New England offshore wind farms fully operationalVineyard Wind and Revolution Wind add 1,504 megawatts to regional grid 👇

EnvironmentNovember 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 10, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Two of New England's largest offshore wind projects are now fully operational, adding a combined 1,504 megawatts of generating capacity after years of construction, legal challenges and federal policy disputes.

Vineyard Wind 1, located about 15 miles south of Martha's Vineyard, has completed commissioning of all 62 turbines. The 800-megawatt project is expected to generate enough electricity annually to match the consumption of roughly 400,000 Massachusetts homes.

Revolution Wind, located about 15 miles south of Rhode Island, has also reached full commercial operation. Its 65 turbines provide up to 704 megawatts, with 400 megawatts contracted to Rhode Island and 304 megawatts to Connecticut. Developers estimate its annual output will be equivalent to the electricity used by more than 350,000 homes.

Together, the projects represent a major expansion of New England's offshore wind capacity, but their completion followed substantial setbacks. Vineyard Wind experienced a turbine blade failure in July 2024 that scattered debris into the ocean and onto Nantucket beaches, temporarily halting construction and power production. Revolution Wind faced its own interruption when the Trump administration ordered construction stopped in August 2025. A federal judge later allowed work to resume while the dispute continued.

Both projects also moved forward amid broader federal opposition to offshore wind development. Their completion does not settle ongoing debates over project costs, environmental effects, fishing interests or the future of additional offshore wind farms proposed for the region.

The electricity generated by the projects enters the broader New England power system rather than being delivered directly to individual homes. Estimates describing how many homes they can power are comparisons based on expected annual electricity generation and household consumption.
