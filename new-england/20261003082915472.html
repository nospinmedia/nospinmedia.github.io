⚡ Healey asks Trump administration to speed Massachusetts gas projectExpansion expected to save 600,000 customers $40 million a year 👇

EnvironmentOctober 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 3, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey is asking the Trump administration to accelerate its federal review of an expansion of the Algonquin natural gas pipeline, saying the project could lower energy costs for hundreds of thousands of customers. In an October 1 letter to U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright, Healey asked federal agencies to move with urgency while still completing required environmental reviews and community engagement.

The project, called Reliable Affordable Resilient Enhancement (RARE), is being developed by Canadian energy company Enbridge. It would increase capacity on the existing Algonquin pipeline by about 73,500 dekatherms of natural gas per day, primarily by replacing sections of existing pipe with larger-diameter pipe and upgrading compressor stations. The system carries natural gas from New Jersey through New York, Connecticut and Rhode Island into Massachusetts.

Healey's administration says the additional supply would serve approximately 600,000 Massachusetts gas customers and reduce their combined bills by an estimated $40 million annually. The project would be financed by Enbridge rather than directly through Massachusetts ratepayers. State regulators have already approved contracts allowing Eversource to purchase additional gas from the expansion, and the project is targeting a 2028 start of service.

The request reflects the state's effort to address high energy costs while continuing its transition toward cleaner sources. Healey said Massachusetts needs an “all-of-the-above” energy strategy incorporating solar, wind, storage, nuclear, geothermal, hydropower and natural gas. New England's limited pipeline capacity can become particularly consequential during periods of extreme cold, when demand for natural gas for both heating and electricity generation rises sharply.

Environmental groups oppose the expansion. The Conservation Law Foundation argues that additional fossil-fuel infrastructure would deepen the region's dependence on natural gas and disputes claims that the project will produce lasting savings. Healey has said a faster federal process should not mean bypassing environmental or community reviews.
