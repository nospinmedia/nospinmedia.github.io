⚖️ Lindsay Clancy defense challenges evidence she killed her childrenJudge weighs bid to avoid second murder trial 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalSeptember 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 30, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The defense for Lindsay Clancy is challenging whether prosecutors presented enough evidence to prove she killed her three children, adding a new argument in an effort to prevent a second murder trial after a Massachusetts jury deadlocked earlier this month. Attorney Kevin Reddington raised the issue during a September 29 hearing in Plymouth Superior Court, arguing that the prosecution's case relied on speculation rather than sufficient evidence establishing Clancy caused the deaths.

The argument marks a significant change in emphasis from Clancy's first trial. The defense previously did not dispute that she caused the deaths of Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months, at the family's Duxbury home in January 2023. Instead, Reddington argued that Clancy was not criminally responsible because she was suffering from postpartum psychosis. The trial ended in a mistrial on September 4 after jurors were unable to reach a unanimous verdict, reportedly splitting 11-1 in favor of finding her not guilty by reason of lack of criminal responsibility.

Reddington is now asking Judge William Sullivan for a required finding of not guilty, arguing that prosecutors failed to present evidence sufficient for a rational jury to find Clancy guilty beyond a reasonable doubt. He questioned parts of the original investigation and emphasized that Clancy has said she has no memory of what happened. Prosecutor Shanan Buckingham rejected the argument, pointing to what the state says is extensive evidence presented during the trial.

The defense is also pursuing separate efforts to prevent another trial, including a motion arguing that retrying Clancy would violate protections against double jeopardy because of how the mistrial was declared. Reddington has sought an inquiry into the holdout juror, while prosecutors have raised concerns about public comments and juror privacy following the mistrial.

Sullivan did not rule on the defense's request Tuesday. He scheduled another hearing for November 2, when the parties are expected to address unresolved motions and potentially discuss a date for a second trial. Plymouth County District Attorney Timothy Cruz said his office is still reviewing the case and has not formally announced whether it will retry Clancy.
