📱 Meta settlement sends major payouts to all six New England statesRegional awards accompany sweeping new protections for teen users 👇

EducationAugust 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 27, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

All six New England states will receive substantial payments under Meta Platforms' landmark $17.1 billion multistate settlement over allegations that Instagram and Facebook were designed in ways that harmed young users. Announced on August 26, the agreement combines financial penalties with mandatory changes to Meta's platforms, including limits on how long minors can use the apps, overnight access restrictions, school-hour notification blocks and stronger age-assurance requirements. The settlement remains subject to court approval.

The regional payouts vary significantly by state. Massachusetts can receive up to $516 million, including approximately $366 million guaranteed over the next decade. Connecticut is eligible for up to $265.4 million, while New Hampshire can receive up to $224.3 million in 10 installments. Vermont is guaranteed approximately $92.7 million, with additional money possible if other major social media companies agree to comparable settlements. Rhode Island will receive at least $49.4 million and up to $64.5 million, while Maine is guaranteed no less than $56 million.

The settlement requires a combined two-hour daily limit on Instagram and Facebook for young users, along with mandatory "Productive Pauses" after 15, 60 and 90 minutes of continuous use. Meta must block access for minors between midnight and 6 a.m., eliminate push notifications on weekdays from 8 a.m. to 3 p.m., strengthen parental controls and age verification, and place tighter limits on visible like counts, beauty filters and content involving self-harm, eating disorders and bullying. An independent auditor and the settling states will evaluate both implementation and effectiveness.

State officials say much of the money will be directed toward programs addressing youth harms associated with social media. Massachusetts identified mental health services, crisis intervention, after-school programs, digital literacy and phone-free school initiatives as potential uses, while Connecticut similarly plans to direct most of its allocation toward youth mental health, crisis services and school programs. The agreement resolves claims involving 47 states, the District of Columbia and several U.S. territories.
