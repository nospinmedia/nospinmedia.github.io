⚖️ Maine High Court Restores Mother's Parental Rights CaseJustices say lack of affordable housing alone is not parental unfitness 👇

EducationAugust 25, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 25, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Maine Supreme Judicial Court has unanimously overturned a lower court decision that terminated a mother's parental rights after concluding her inability to secure affordable housing in Maine did not, by itself, demonstrate parental unfitness. In In re Child of Karen J. (2026 ME 91), the state's highest court ruled that economic hardship and the practical inability to relocate cannot substitute for the clear and convincing evidence required under Maine law to permanently sever the parent-child relationship.

The case began after the Maine Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) took custody of the woman's autistic child while the family was traveling through Maine from New Mexico. Court records showed the mother completed required services, including substance-use treatment, parenting education and other reunification requirements. However, the trial court concluded she had failed to make sufficient progress because she had not relocated to Maine, where suitable housing remained financially out of reach.

Writing for a unanimous court, Justice Andrew Mead said the record demonstrated the mother's inability to move reflected the realities of Maine's limited affordable housing market rather than a refusal to comply with reunification efforts. The Law Court concluded the evidence did not support terminating parental rights on that basis and vacated the judgment, sending the case back to the trial court for further proceedings consistent with its opinion.

The decision clarifies that courts must distinguish between poverty-related barriers and parental unfitness when applying 22 M.R.S. § 4055, which governs termination of parental rights. Child welfare advocates said the ruling reinforces that financial hardship alone cannot justify permanently separating families without additional evidence that a parent is unwilling or unable to safely care for a child.
