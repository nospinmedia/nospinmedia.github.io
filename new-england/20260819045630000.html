🖥️ Rochester Adopts Tight Restrictions On Data CentersNew zoning rules limit facilities to one industrial district 👇

EducationAugust 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 19, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The Rochester City Council has adopted new zoning restrictions governing where data centers can be built, following a public hearing on August 18, 2026. The ordinance adds data centers to Chapter 275 of the city's zoning rules and prohibits them in every zoning district except Rochester's Recycling Industrial District, where any future proposal would still require discretionary approval from the Planning Board. The rules take effect before Rochester has approved any specific data center project.

The ordinance establishes a formal definition covering facilities primarily used to house and operate servers and related digital infrastructure, including large-scale cloud computing, artificial intelligence and enterprise computing operations. Supporting systems such as cooling equipment, backup generation, energy storage and electrical infrastructure are included. A server room serving another primary use, such as a school, hospital, office or factory, does not fall under the same definition.

Even within the Recycling Industrial District, a data center is not permitted automatically. A developer would need a Conditional Use Permit and would generally undergo site-plan review before the Planning Board. The city can consider compatibility with surrounding properties, natural resources, traffic, lighting and other impacts, and the board can impose additional conditions when justified. The district includes industrial land primarily around the Rochester Neck Road area.

City records do not identify a company, developer, property address or formal application for a data center. Officials began discussing regulations after interest was expressed in developing a data center and acquiring land in Rochester. The ordinance therefore establishes the city's ground rules before any named project reaches the formal approval process rather than authorizing construction of a facility.
