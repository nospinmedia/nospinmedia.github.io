🥶 Connecticut Adds $12.7 Million in Winter Heating AssistanceGovernor Lamont Targets Rising Oil Costs as Nearly 94,300 Households Seek Help 👇

EconomyOctober 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 14, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Connecticut Governor Ned Lamont announced plans on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, to provide an additional $12.7 million in state funding to help low-income households afford home heating as fuel prices climb ahead of winter.

The money will come from Connecticut's Federal Cuts Response Fund and supplement the federally funded Connecticut Energy Assistance Program (CEAP). The additional assistance is particularly important for households relying on heating oil and propane, which must purchase fuel deliveries to keep their homes warm.

According to the CT Mirror, Connecticut heating oil prices have climbed approximately 80% compared with a year ago, approaching $6 per gallon. State officials have linked the increases to disruptions in global oil supplies associated with the ongoing war involving Iran.

The state's heating assistance program expects to serve approximately 94,291 households during the 2026–2027 heating season, including roughly 32,893 households that rely on delivered heating fuels.

The program prioritizes vulnerable residents, including older adults, families with young children, and people with disabilities. Assistance is administered by the Connecticut Department of Social Services through nine community action agencies.

Eligible households can receive a base heating benefit ranging from $355 to $705, depending on their circumstances. Additional crisis benefits of up to $430 each may also be available. The new state funding is intended to provide another layer of assistance when existing federal benefits are exhausted.

Households generally qualify when their annual gross income is at or below 60% of Connecticut's state median income. For the current heating season, that equals $93,681 for a family of four. Residents receiving certain benefits, including SNAP, Supplemental Security Income or Temporary Family Assistance, may qualify through categorical eligibility rules.

Applications for the 2026–2027 heating season are already open. The first program-funded heating fuel deliveries are scheduled for November 2, 2026, and the application deadline is May 31, 2027.

Residents can apply through Connecticut's heating assistance program online, contact their local community action agency, or call 2-1-1 for help finding application assistance.

The proposed allocation is the sixth use of Connecticut's Federal Cuts Response Fund, established in 2025 to address disruptions in federal funding and policy. Approximately $250.6 million would remain available after the proposed allocation.

Under state law, bipartisan legislative leaders have 24 hours to review the governor's proposal and may vote to reject it. Previous allocations from the fund have received approval.

The additional assistance could help thousands of Connecticut families manage winter heating expenses, particularly those facing higher costs for oil deliveries.
