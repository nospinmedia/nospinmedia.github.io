⚖️ Two Maine residents sue ICE over property kept after detentionLawsuit says cash, IDs, phones and other belongings remain unreturned 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EconomyNovember 26, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 26, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Two Maine residents are among seven plaintiffs nationwide suing federal immigration authorities over allegations that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement continues to hold their cash, identification documents and personal belongings long after they left federal custody.

Domingo Tot Quip of Portland and Joel Vigil Rodriguez were both arrested during ICE operations in Maine in January 2026. They are represented by the ACLU of the District of Columbia in the proposed federal class-action lawsuit Gonzalez et al. v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, Case No. 1:26-cv-03564, filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia. The plaintiffs also filed a motion for a preliminary injunction on October 30 seeking the return of property and changes to ICE practices.

Tot Quip, a Guatemalan national who worked as a dishwasher in Portland, was arrested at a bus stop on January 21. He was deported to Guatemala on January 28 after ICE confused him with another person, despite a federal court order barring his removal. According to court filings, ICE realized the mistake on February 6, but Tot Quip did not return to the United States until August 13.

The lawsuit says that delay was prolonged because ICE had retained Tot Quip's passport, requiring him to obtain a new passport in Guatemala before he could return. It alleges ICE still has not returned most of the cash he was carrying, his backpack, phone charger, headphones and clothing.

Vigil Rodriguez, originally from El Salvador, was arrested at his workplace in Windham on January 20 and released on bond on February 13 while his immigration case remained pending. The complaint says ICE has not returned his cellphone, car keys, AirPods, coat or wallet containing two Salvadoran driver's licenses, despite inquiries from his attorneys and the office of U.S. Rep. Chellie Pingree.

The lawsuit alleges that retaining non-contraband property without justification violates the Fourth Amendment and federal administrative law. Plaintiffs are seeking class-action status, an injunction requiring property to be returned and broader safeguards governing how ICE stores and returns belongings.

ICE and the Department of Homeland Security had not responded to media requests for comment on the lawsuit.
