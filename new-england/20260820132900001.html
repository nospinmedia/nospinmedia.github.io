🏛️ NH Lawmakers Override Seven Ayotte VetoesToll plan, new-trial rights and six other measures become law 👇

EducationAugust 20, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 20, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

New Hampshire lawmakers overrode seven vetoes by Gov. Kelly Ayotte on August 19, putting a series of measures into law despite opposition from the Republican governor. The successful overrides covered transportation funding, criminal appeals, parental involvement in student questioning, municipal notices, public-property trespass orders, research tax credits and state park fees, and medical cannabis cultivation. The Legislature considered 31 vetoes during the session after two others had been addressed earlier.

The most prominent override involved House Bill 2026, the state's $4.5 billion 10-Year Transportation Improvement Plan. The House voted 292-25 to override Ayotte and the Senate followed 24-0. The plan doubles turnpike tolls for out-of-state motorists and New Hampshire residents who pay cash rather than using a New Hampshire E-ZPass transponder. Additional toll revenue supports projects including a $286 million widening of Interstate 93 north of its junction with Interstate 89 through Concord. Ayotte opposed the increase, arguing it would raise costs for Granite Staters who pay cash.

Lawmakers also overrode the veto of House Bill 1422, creating an exception to New Hampshire's three-year deadline for seeking a new trial when qualifying defendants discover new evidence. That override passed 314-7 in the House and 18-6 in the Senate. House Bill 1267 requires parental or guardian presence when a minor student is questioned by an attorney or other non-school person, with an exception allowing child-protection caseworkers to conduct investigations. House Bill 1184 establishes procedures for emergency no-trespass orders on public property, while House Bill 1369 expands how municipalities and school districts may notify residents about meeting warrants, including through government websites.

The remaining overrides were House Bill 1102, which expands New Hampshire's research-and-development tax credit while changing state park fee rules, and Senate Bill 468, allowing alternative treatment centers to operate greenhouse cultivation locations for medical cannabis. The Senate overrode Ayotte's SB 468 veto 16-8, followed by a 257-44 House vote. Despite the seven overrides, lawmakers sustained most of Ayotte's vetoes, including measures involving single-sex bathrooms and locker rooms.
