🐾 Dozen animals rescued from South Berwick house fireNo people hurt as three-story Maine home is left uninhabitable 👇

EnvironmentNovember 23, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 23, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Firefighters rescued 12 animals, mostly cats, from a house fire in South Berwick, Maine, on November 21, while mutual-aid crews from Maine and New Hampshire helped bring the second-alarm blaze under control. No people were reported injured.

The South Berwick Fire Department was dispatched at about 2:45 p.m. to a three-story home on Great Works Drive. Firefighters arrived within approximately five minutes and found flames that had started behind the house spreading up the rear of all three floors.

The size of the fire prompted a second alarm and brought assistance from surrounding departments. Among the responding agencies was the Newington, New Hampshire, Fire Department, which was called to assist at approximately 2:53 p.m. Crews brought the fire under control in about 45 minutes.

First responders removed a dozen pets from the residence, with officials describing most of them as cats. Several of the rescued animals were taken by bystanders to local emergency veterinarians for evaluation and treatment. Officials did not immediately provide details on the animals' conditions.

Although everyone escaped without reported human injuries, fire damage left the residence uninhabitable. Authorities had not announced an estimate of the financial damage or how long the occupants might be displaced.

The cause of the fire remained under investigation, and officials had not publicly identified what started the fire behind the home or whether any specific equipment or activity contributed to the blaze.

The response also demonstrated the role of cross-border mutual aid in the Seacoast region, with New Hampshire firefighters assisting their counterparts in southern Maine during the emergency.
