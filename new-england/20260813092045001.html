🥬 New Hampshire Reports 79 Cyclosporiasis Cases38 infections tied to recalled produce as outbreak grows 👇

HealthAugust 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 13, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

New Hampshire health officials are reporting 79 cases of cyclosporiasis as the state continues investigating illnesses associated with a wider outbreak involving recalled iceberg lettuce. The new total, reported by WMUR from an August 11, 2026 state surveillance update, is up by 10 cases in one week and represents a substantial increase from earlier in the outbreak.

According to the state figures reported by WMUR, 38 cases are associated with recalled produce, 35 were domestically acquired from sources that remain unknown, and 6 are associated with international travel. Three people have been hospitalized. One confirmed New Hampshire cluster involves Cheshire Medical Center in Keene, where 9 cases were confirmed among people who ate lettuce served in the hospital cafeteria. Cheshire Medical Center said the illnesses were likely contracted through consumption of recalled Taylor Farms lettuce served before the national recall, and the hospital removed the affected products and temporarily closed its salad bar.

The FDA says Taylor Farms de Mexico voluntarily removed all iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico from the U.S. market on July 17, 2026 after federal traceback identified the company as the supplier of shredded iceberg lettuce involved in part of the multistate investigation. Recalled food-service lettuce was distributed in 27 states, including New Hampshire and other New England states. The FDA has continued investigating the outbreak even after laboratory officials determined that an initially reported positive lettuce sample was a false positive; federal officials have said epidemiological and traceback evidence continues to implicate recalled lettuce in the outbreak.

Cyclosporiasis is caused by the parasite Cyclospora cayetanensis and commonly causes prolonged or recurring watery diarrhea, along with symptoms including stomach cramps, nausea, fatigue and loss of appetite. Health officials advise people with severe or persistent symptoms after possible exposure to contact a healthcare provider and specifically ask about testing for Cyclospora.
