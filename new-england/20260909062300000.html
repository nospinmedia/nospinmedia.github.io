🗳️ Rhode Island voters cast primary ballots in major statewide racesGovernor and Providence mayor contests headline Wednesday voting 👇

EconomySeptember 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 9, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Rhode Island voters are casting primary ballots Wednesday as the state chooses party nominees for federal, state and local offices, with a closely watched Democratic gubernatorial rematch and the Providence mayoral contest among the day's most prominent races.

Democratic Gov. Dan McKee is seeking another term against former CVS executive Helena Foulkes, whom he narrowly defeated in a crowded Democratic primary in 2022. McKee became governor in 2021 after Gina Raimondo left to become U.S. commerce secretary and then won his first full term the following year.

The prolonged replacement of the Washington Bridge has become a major issue in their rematch. Rhode Island closed the westbound bridge in December 2023 after critical structural problems were discovered. The span, which connects Providence and East Providence and normally carries about 100,000 vehicles per day, was later determined to require demolition and replacement. A project once expected to restore traffic much sooner is now projected for completion in November 2028. Foulkes has criticized McKee's handling of the project, while McKee says his administration acted to protect public safety and is moving the replacement forward.

Republicans are also choosing a gubernatorial nominee between Aaron Guckian, the party's 2022 nominee for lieutenant governor, and first-time candidate Elaine Pelino. Guckian has received the endorsement of the Rhode Island Republican Party.

In Providence, Democratic Mayor Brett Smiley faces state Rep. David Morales in a primary contest that has included debates over housing affordability, city services and public safety.

Rhode Island's primary is being held on a Wednesday this year after lawmakers changed the election date because the traditional Tuesday schedule fell too close to Labor Day. The state is one of four holding primary elections in September this year.

The last Rhode Island governor to lose a party primary was Democrat Bruce Sundlun in 1994, when state Sen. Myrth York ended his bid for another term.
