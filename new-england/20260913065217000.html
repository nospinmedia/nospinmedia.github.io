🚌 Boston pitches revised Blue Hill Avenue bus plan to packed meetingCenter-running lanes remain despite changes and community opposition 👇

EconomySeptember 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 13, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Hundreds of residents packed Morning Star Baptist Church in Mattapan as Boston and MBTA officials presented a revised $163 million Blue Hill Avenue improvement plan, with debate again centering on dedicated bus lanes running through the middle of the roadway. Mayor Michelle Wu and MBTA General Manager Phillip Eng defended the project at the September 10 meeting, while opponents rallied outside and challenged the proposal inside the church.

The project would redesign the heavily traveled corridor with center-running lanes intended to separate buses from regular traffic. Officials say the changes could reduce some bus trips by 10 to 15 minutes and improve reliability for roughly 40,000 daily bus riders. The center-running design is also tied directly to the project's financing: an $80 million federal grant earmarked by Congress calls for a continuous, bi-directional, center-running bus priority facility, meaning abandoning that central feature could jeopardize a major portion of the funding.

The latest design includes several changes following community feedback. Two general traffic lanes have been restored across about 75% of the corridor, and the plan would provide 42 more parking spaces than currently exist. Instead of separate bicycle lanes on both sides of Blue Hill Avenue, the revised design consolidates bicycle infrastructure into a two-way, sidewalk-level path along the west side. Plans also include about 200 new trees, six miles of rebuilt sidewalks and additional signalized pedestrian crossings.

Before the meeting, about 100 opponents joined a demonstration led by Boston City Councilors Miniard Culpepper and Brian Worrell. Critics raised concerns about pedestrian access to center platforms, effects on businesses and whether residents' objections have been adequately incorporated. Culpepper and Worrell have also advocated studying an MBTA Orange Line extension along the corridor as an alternative. Supporters argue that unreliable bus service and existing safety problems require substantial changes.

Additional public meetings are scheduled for September 14, September 22 and September 30, with construction currently expected to begin in 2028.
