🏥 Vermont Medicaid changes begin as bigger rules loom for 49,000Immigration limits start today, work requirements follow in 2027 👇

EducationOctober 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 1, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

New federal Medicaid rules begin taking effect in Vermont on October 1, starting with tighter eligibility restrictions for some immigrants. A much broader set of changes arriving in 2027 is expected to affect about 49,000 Vermonters, adding work or community-engagement requirements and requiring some recipients to renew their eligibility twice a year.

The October 1 change narrows which non-citizens can receive full Medicaid coverage. Certain lawful permanent residents, Cuban and Haitian entrants, and migrants covered by the Compact of Free Association can remain eligible, while separate protections continue for qualifying people who are pregnant or under age 21. Some immigrants who previously qualified will lose full Medicaid coverage because of their immigration status.

The larger changes begin January 1, 2027 and primarily affect some Medicaid recipients ages 19 to 64. People subject to the new requirements can qualify by earning at least $580 in a month, being enrolled as a student at least half-time, or completing at least 80 hours of qualifying work, education, community service or other approved activities. Numerous groups are excluded from the requirements, including certain caregivers, people with qualifying health conditions and other protected populations.

New applicants will have to demonstrate that they met the requirement during the month before applying. For current Medicaid members, Vermont plans to begin checking the requirement at renewals starting in March 2027. The state says existing members generally need to meet the requirement during at least one month of the applicable lookback period rather than continuously meeting an 80-hour threshold every month.

Affected adults will also move from annual Medicaid eligibility reviews to reviews every six months. Retroactive Medicaid coverage will be shortened to one month for most affected adults, while pregnant people and children under 19 will have a two-month retroactive period.

Vermont officials stress that being among the roughly 49,000 affected residents does not mean someone will necessarily lose Medicaid. State officials and health advocates are instead concerned that additional paperwork and eligibility requirements could cause otherwise eligible people to lose coverage or create more uncompensated care for hospitals and clinics.

The Department of Vermont Health Access has begun contacting potentially affected residents and created online tools explaining the changes and who may qualify for exemptions.
