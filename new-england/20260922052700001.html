✈️ Logan flight disruptions expected to continue TuesdayNearly 500 flights were delayed Monday after Northeast air traffic outage 👇

PoliticsSeptember 22, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 22, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Travel disruptions at Boston Logan International Airport are expected to continue into Tuesday, September 22, after an air traffic control communications failure delayed nearly 500 flights at Logan and disrupted airports across the Northeast. Just after 10 p.m. Monday, 487 Logan flights had been delayed and 76 canceled, according to flight-tracking data cited by the Boston Globe.

The problems began after a primary communications circuit failed Monday morning at a Federal Aviation Administration facility in Philadelphia. When the system attempted to switch to its backup connection, officials discovered that a fiber-optic line in New Jersey had been cut. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy initially said an Amtrak construction crew had severed the line, but Amtrak denied responsibility. NJ Transit said a crew working at one of its sites had been digging about 10 feet from utility markings and that it was investigating whether the markings had been made by Verizon or the contractor. The telecommunications lines were repaired Monday evening and regional operations began returning to normal, but officials warned that clearing the backlog would take time.

At Logan, the FAA problems were compounded by ongoing runway construction, according to the Massachusetts Port Authority. Massport said the effects were expected to continue Tuesday and urged passengers to check directly with their airlines before heading to the airport.

The disruption came as about 130 world leaders and dozens of ministers were arriving in New York for the annual high-level meeting of the United Nations General Assembly. The FAA halted arrivals for hours at major airports including Boston, JFK, LaGuardia, Newark and Philadelphia, and more than 100 flights were diverted. Across the country, about 7,000 flights were delayed or canceled during the disruption.
