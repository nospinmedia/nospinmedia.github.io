🎙️ Markey, Moulton Spar in Final Senate Primary DebateCandidates Make Closing Appeals Before Early Voting Begins 👇

EducationAugust 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 21, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Democratic U.S. Senate candidates Sen. Ed Markey and Rep. Seth Moulton met on August 20 for their third and final televised debate before Massachusetts voters begin casting ballots in the September 1 primary. Hosted by WCVB-TV, WBUR, and The Boston Globe, the debate featured sharp exchanges over the economy, housing affordability, clean energy, foreign policy, campaign finance, and the future direction of the Democratic Party. With early voting beginning on August 22, the debate marked the candidates' final opportunity to appear together before voting gets underway.

Moulton, 47, argued that Massachusetts and the Democratic Party need a new generation of leadership, pointing to rising housing costs, utility bills, and broader affordability concerns. He repeatedly highlighted Markey's age, noting the incumbent turned 80 in July and has served in Congress since 1976. Markey rejected the argument that experience is a liability, instead emphasizing his legislative record on climate policy, consumer protection, and clean energy while defending his continued effectiveness in the Senate. The two candidates also clashed over LGBTQ+ rights, campaign strategy, and the party's response to recent national elections.

Recent polling has continued to show Markey holding a significant advantage heading into the primary. A UMass Amherst/WCVB survey conducted in early August found him leading 60% to 32% among likely Democratic primary voters, while a later Suffolk University/Boston Globe poll showed a 52% to 38% margin. Both campaigns have continued statewide outreach in the final days before voting begins, with each arguing they are best positioned to represent Massachusetts in Washington.

The winner of the Democratic primary will advance to the November 3 general election to face Republican John Deaton. Because Massachusetts has not elected a Republican to the U.S. Senate since 1972, political observers continue to view the Democratic primary as the contest most likely to determine the state's next senator.
