🛒 New Hampshire grocery distributor strike disrupts deliveriesMore than 255 workers remain off job as Thanksgiving approaches 👇

EconomyNovember 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 8, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A strike by more than 255 workers at a major grocery distribution center in Pembroke, New Hampshire, is disrupting deliveries to independent stores across New England, with some retailers reporting delayed shipments and reduced inventory as the Thanksgiving shopping period approaches.

Members of Teamsters Local 633 at Associated Grocers Northeast have been on strike since Monday, November 2. The workers include truck drivers, warehouse employees and maintenance personnel. Their previous five-year contract expired October 17 after negotiations failed to produce a new agreement.

The central dispute involves retirement benefits. Union representatives say the company wants to remove workers from the New England Teamsters Pension Fund and replace the pension with a 401(k) plan without an employer match. The union says 98% of members voted to authorize a strike. Other contract issues include wages, health insurance and working conditions.

Associated Grocers President and CEO Mike Violette said negotiations have stalled but company leaders remain willing to reach an agreement. He said the distributor has a contingency plan intended to maintain service through the Thanksgiving season.

The effects are already reaching stores beyond New Hampshire. Vermont grocers have reported missed or delayed deliveries, including Cambridge Village Market, which did not receive one of its regularly scheduled shipments. The Vermont Retail and Grocers Association said stores that depend heavily on the distributor are seeing varying levels of disruption.

In New Hampshire, union representatives told WMUR that some stores have reported empty shelves. Associated Grocers supplies independent supermarkets and convenience stores throughout the region, making prolonged disruption potentially significant for smaller retailers that lack the distribution networks of large supermarket chains.

Union representatives say members are prepared to remain on strike until an agreement is reached, while the company says it intends to continue serving stores during the dispute.
