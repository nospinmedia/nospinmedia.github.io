⛪ Massachusetts Catholic bishops criticize Healey over abortion lawEmergency action blocked effort to suspend new rules 👇

HealthNovember 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 8, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Catholic leaders in Massachusetts are criticizing Gov. Maura Healey’s use of an emergency measure to immediately implement a new abortion law, preventing opponents from temporarily suspending the law while they pursue a statewide referendum.

In a joint statement released Saturday, November 7, Boston Archbishop Richard Henning and Bishops Robert Joseph McManus of Worcester, Edgar M. da Cunha of Fall River and William Byrne of Springfield objected to Healey’s emergency preamble for An Act Prioritizing Patient Access to Care, also known as Chapter 188.

The dispute centers partly on changes governing abortions later in pregnancy, including care at 24 weeks or later. Healey has said previous exceptions were too narrow and vague and could delay medically necessary care. According to the governor, at least nine patients had to travel outside Massachusetts for late-stage medical care since August because of the previous rules.

Opponents organized by the Compassionate Care Coalition had gathered nearly 100,000 signatures as part of an effort to suspend the law while seeking its repeal through the state referendum process. Healey’s emergency preamble allowed the law to take effect immediately, preventing the signature campaign from temporarily freezing it.

The referendum effort itself is not over. Opponents say they will continue pursuing a statewide vote to repeal the law on the November 2028 ballot, meaning Massachusetts voters could ultimately decide whether the legislation remains in place.

The bishops criticized both the abortion provisions and Healey’s use of emergency authority. Supporters of the law argue the changes are necessary to prevent delays in care during serious or complicated pregnancies.

The dispute therefore involves both the substance of Massachusetts abortion policy and the governor’s authority to prevent a new law from being suspended while a referendum challenge proceeds.
