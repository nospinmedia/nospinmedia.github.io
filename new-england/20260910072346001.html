⚖️ Vermont Lt. Gov. reaches resolution in cannabis licensing caseTerms remain secret after regulators proposed $5,000 penalty over unapproved grow site 👇

EconomySeptember 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 10, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Vermont Lt. Gov. John Rodgers has reached a resolution with state cannabis regulators after they accused him of growing cannabis at a location that was not authorized under his cultivation license. The agreement followed a closed-door proceeding Wednesday, September 9, but its terms have not yet been released publicly.

The Vermont Cannabis Control Board issued Rodgers a formal notice of violation on August 13 after an inspector reported finding approximately 200 cannabis seedlings inside an unlocked greenhouse on Daniels Pond Road in Glover. Rodgers has a state license allowing cannabis cultivation at a separate property on Rodgers Road, about a mile away.

According to enforcement records obtained by VTDigger, Rodgers told an inspector in October 2025 that he intended to use the Daniels Pond Road greenhouse during the 2026 growing season. State rules allow licensed cultivators to operate at qualifying non-adjoining locations, but regulators said Rodgers never submitted the required documentation to have the second site approved.

During a June inspection, a state agent visited the Daniels Pond Road property and reported finding vegetable starts and roughly 200 cannabis seedlings. Regulators subsequently proposed a $5,000 administrative penalty. Rodgers told the board that the starter plants were moved to permitted fields several days later.

Rodgers characterized the dispute as a routine administrative matter and said he was working through the Cannabis Control Board's normal process. He had initially indicated that he planned to contest the violation.

The case advanced Wednesday when Rodgers appeared before the board in a proceeding that was closed to the public. WCAX reported afterward that the two sides had reached a resolution. The Cannabis Control Board has not publicly disclosed whether Rodgers will pay the proposed $5,000 penalty or whether the settlement changes any other terms of the enforcement action.

Rodgers has held a Vermont cannabis cultivation license since 2023 and is currently serving as lieutenant governor while seeking reelection in November.
