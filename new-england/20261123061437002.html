🚢 Swatting threat shuts down Woods Hole ferry serviceGun and bomb threats trigger two-hour security response before ferries resume 👇

EducationNovember 23, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 23, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A swatting hoax involving threats of firearms and explosives triggered a major law-enforcement response at the Steamship Authority terminal in Woods Hole, Massachusetts, temporarily halting ferry service on Saturday night.

Falmouth Police Chief Jeffrey Lourie said authorities received threats involving both guns and explosives at the Woods Hole terminal. Police treated the report as a potential emergency while investigators worked to determine whether a genuine threat existed, bringing multiple local, state and federal agencies into the response.

The operation included Falmouth Police, Massachusetts State Police, Mashpee Police, the Barnstable County Sheriff's Office and the U.S. Coast Guard. Coast Guard patrol boats and drones were used during the response. The nearby Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution also issued a campus safety alert while authorities investigated.

Steamship Authority operations were halted during the security response, affecting vessels serving Martha's Vineyard. The ferry Martha's Vineyard was among the vessels affected, while ferry movements in Vineyard Haven were also stopped as authorities secured the area.

The disruption lasted roughly two hours before investigators determined that the threats were a hoax. Regular Steamship Authority operations resumed at approximately 8:15 to 8:16 p.m. after authorities cleared the situation.

Chief Lourie characterized the incident as swatting, in which a false report of a serious threat is made to provoke a large armed emergency response. Available reporting did not identify who made the threat or indicate that an arrest had been made.

The incident disrupted a major transportation link between Cape Cod and Martha's Vineyard during the weekend before Thanksgiving, when holiday travel was increasing across the region.
