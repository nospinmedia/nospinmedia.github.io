⚖️ Connecticut ends in-cell shackling under legal settlementDOC agrees to new mental health safeguards and independent oversight 👇

HealthAugust 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 4, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

The Connecticut Department of Correction has agreed to permanently end the use of in-cell restraints as part of a legal settlement resolving a federal lawsuit brought by the American Civil Liberties Union of Connecticut and Disability Rights Connecticut. The agreement prohibits correctional staff from restraining incarcerated individuals inside their cells with wrist and ankle shackles for extended periods, a practice advocates argued caused unnecessary psychological harm, particularly for people experiencing mental health crises.

The settlement establishes new standards governing the use of restraints throughout Connecticut's prison system. While in-cell shackling will no longer be permitted, correctional staff may still use stationary therapeutic restraints in limited circumstances when an incarcerated person presents an immediate risk of serious self-harm or violence and less restrictive interventions have failed. The agreement also requires additional staff training, expanded documentation, supervisory review and ongoing compliance monitoring to ensure the new policies are consistently followed.

The lawsuit challenged restraint practices used within the Connecticut Department of Correction, with advocates arguing the procedures disproportionately affected individuals with serious mental illnesses and violated their constitutional rights. State officials denied wrongdoing but agreed to resolve the litigation through negotiated reforms rather than continue court proceedings. The settlement also provides mechanisms for independent oversight and reporting as the department implements the new requirements.

Connecticut Correction Ombuds DeVaughn Ward welcomed the agreement, saying the changes represent an important step toward improving the treatment of incarcerated people with behavioral health needs while maintaining institutional safety. The settlement comes as Connecticut continues broader correctional health reforms, including implementation of Public Act 26-40, which eliminated inmate medical copayments and created the Correction Medical and Health Commission to strengthen oversight of correctional healthcare.

Advocates said ending in-cell shackling reflects a growing emphasis on using mental health interventions instead of prolonged physical restraints during behavioral crises. Department officials are expected to begin implementing the policy changes and required training under the terms of the court-approved agreement.
