🏛️ New Haven Officer Reinstated After Arbitration In Randy Cox Transport CaseState Panel Finds City Lacked Just Cause For Termination 👇

HealthAugust 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 1, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Former New Haven police officer Luis Rivera has been reinstated after the Connecticut State Board of Mediation and Arbitration ruled that the city lacked just cause to terminate his employment following the 2022 police transport incident that left Richard "Randy" Cox permanently paralyzed. Rivera was one of five officers fired after Cox suffered a severe spinal injury while riding unrestrained in a police transport van.

The arbitration panel unanimously concluded that Rivera's dismissal was not justified based on the evidence presented during the administrative proceedings. The decision noted that Rivera had limited medical training and did not act with malice or intentional misconduct during the incident. Earlier this year, criminal misdemeanor charges filed against Rivera were dismissed in state court.

The City of New Haven has reinstated Rivera in accordance with the arbitration award. Officials said he must complete required departmental training and recertification before returning to full duty, consistent with department policies.

The Randy Cox case drew national attention after surveillance video showed Cox repeatedly telling officers he could not move following a sudden stop in the transport van. The incident resulted in criminal charges against several officers, the termination of multiple employees, and a civil lawsuit that was later settled by the City of New Haven for $45 million.

Rivera is the second officer involved in the incident to be reinstated through the arbitration process. The status of the remaining former officers continues to be addressed through separate legal and administrative proceedings.

An arbitration decision addresses employment status and does not determine civil or criminal liability.
