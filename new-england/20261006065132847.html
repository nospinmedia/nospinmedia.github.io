⚖️ Babson student's deportation reaches federal appeals courtJudges weigh whether ICE's rapid transfer cost her a chance to challenge detention 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EducationOctober 6, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 6, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Lawyers for Any Lucia Lopez Belloza, a 20-year-old Babson College student deported to Honduras last year, asked a federal appeals court in Boston on October 5 to revive her case and allow a Massachusetts judge to consider whether her detention and removal were unlawful.

Immigration agents arrested Lopez Belloza at Logan International Airport on November 20, 2025, as she prepared to fly to Texas to surprise her family for Thanksgiving. She had entered the United States as a child and was subject to a 2017 removal order issued when she was 11. Her attorneys say she did not know about that order.

The dispute now before the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals centers on what happened immediately after her arrest. Lopez Belloza was initially held at an ICE facility in Burlington, Massachusetts, but was rapidly transferred to Texas. Her family and attorneys said they struggled to determine where she had been taken. They filed a habeas corpus petition in Massachusetts based on her last known location.

A federal judge then issued an emergency order temporarily prohibiting the government from moving Lopez Belloza out of Massachusetts or deporting her. She had already been transferred to Texas, however, and was deported to Honduras the following day. Federal officials later acknowledged that her deportation violated the court order, saying an ICE agent misread it.

U.S. District Judge Richard G. Stearns later ordered the government to facilitate her return. ICE arranged a flight back to the United States, but said she would return to detention in Texas and could again face removal. Lopez Belloza declined to board. Stearns subsequently dismissed her case, concluding that he no longer had jurisdiction.

Her appeal could clarify where detainees may file habeas challenges when immigration authorities transfer them before their lawyers can determine their location. Government attorney Mark Sauter argued that Massachusetts lacked jurisdiction because Lopez Belloza was already in Texas when the petition was filed. The appeals court did not immediately rule.
