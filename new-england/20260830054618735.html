🗳️ Massachusetts Senate Primary Enters Final StretchMarkey backed by Sanders and Warren ahead of Tuesday vote 👇

EnvironmentAugust 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 30, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Massachusetts' Democratic primary for the U.S. Senate entered its final days as Sen. Ed Markey campaigned alongside Sen. Bernie Sanders, Sen. Elizabeth Warren and Rep. Ayanna Pressley during a get-out-the-vote rally at IBEW Local 103 in Dorchester. The event marked one of the campaign's final major appearances before voters head to the polls on September 1, with supporters emphasizing labor rights, climate policy and other progressive priorities as Markey seeks another term in the Senate.

During the rally, Sanders praised Markey's long record supporting organized labor and progressive legislation, while Warren and Pressley urged supporters to turn out for the primary. Responding to questions surrounding his age, the 80-year-old Markey encouraged voters to judge him on his legislative record and policy positions rather than his years in office. The campaign portrayed the election as a choice about the future direction of Democratic priorities in Massachusetts.

Markey is being challenged by Rep. Seth Moulton, who has argued that after decades in Congress, Massachusetts should elect a new generation of leadership. Moulton has campaigned on bringing fresh perspectives to Washington while highlighting his military service and congressional experience. The primary has underscored both ideological and generational differences within the state's Democratic Party, even as both candidates broadly support many traditional Democratic policy positions.

Polls for the statewide primary will be open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. on Tuesday, September 1. The Democratic nominee will advance to the November 2026 general election. With nationally recognized Democratic leaders campaigning on Markey's behalf in the race's closing days, the outcome will be closely watched as a measure of voter sentiment within one of the country's most reliably Democratic states.
