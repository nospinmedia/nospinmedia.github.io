⚖️ Federal judge orders bond hearing for Keene ICE detaineeCourt rules New Hampshire man's continued detention was unlawful 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
LegalAugust 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 24, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

A federal judge has ruled that a Keene, New Hampshire, resident arrested by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) was unlawfully detained without an opportunity to seek release on bond, ordering immigration authorities to provide him with an individualized bond hearing. The man, a general contractor who was taken into custody during an ICE enforcement operation on August 8, 2026, was transferred to a detention facility in Massachusetts, where he remained in custody without a scheduled bond determination until the court intervened.

The judge concluded that continued detention without a bond hearing violated applicable legal protections, requiring the Executive Office for Immigration Review to promptly conduct a hearing to determine whether the detainee presents either a flight risk or a danger to the community. The ruling does not require his release but instead guarantees an individualized review before an immigration judge. Attorneys representing the detainee argued that prolonged detention without such a hearing violated both federal law and constitutional due process protections.

The decision follows a recent First Circuit Court of Appeals ruling in Guerrero Orellana v. Moniz, which affirmed that many noncitizens detained under 8 U.S.C. § 1226 in New England are entitled to individualized bond hearings rather than automatic or indefinite detention. The ACLU of Massachusetts, which participated in that broader litigation, said the appellate decision reinforces long-standing due process protections requiring immigration judges to evaluate each case on its own merits.

The Keene arrest prompted significant public attention, including a packed community meeting earlier this month where residents raised concerns about local immigration enforcement and due process rights. The federal court's order now requires immigration officials to move forward with a bond hearing while the underlying removal proceedings continue.
