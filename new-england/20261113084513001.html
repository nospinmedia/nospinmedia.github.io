🗳️ Ranked-choice count confirms LePage win in MaineRepublican secures New England's only GOP-held U.S. House seat 👇

PoliticsNovember 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 13, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Maine's completed ranked-choice tabulation has confirmed Republican Paul LePage as the winner of the state's 2nd Congressional District, closing a race his Democratic opponent conceded more than a week earlier.

Official results released November 12 show LePage with 170,890 votes, or 50.81%, compared with 165,432 votes, or 49.18%, for Democrat Matthew Dunlap. The final margin was 5,458 votes.

LePage led by roughly 5,400 first-choice votes after the November 3 election, and Dunlap conceded November 5. However, Maine law still required the ranked-choice tabulation because blank and write-in ballots left LePage below the required majority when all ballots were included in the initial count.

Secretary of State Shenna Bellows oversaw the tabulation in Augusta. During the ranked-choice process, 1,243 write-in ballots were redistributed, including ballots for declared write-in candidate Gregory Ward. Of those, 254 transferred to Dunlap and 180 to LePage. Additional rankings from ballots with blank first choices resulted in LePage gaining a net 627 votes and Dunlap gaining 587.

The results remain technically unofficial until they are formally transmitted to Governor Janet Mills for certification, but the tabulation establishes LePage as the winner.

The result completes a Republican pickup of the open seat previously held by Democratic Rep. Jared Golden, who did not seek re-election after four terms. LePage will become the only Republican member of the U.S. House from New England when he takes office in January. The district was also one of only three House seats nationwide that shifted from Democratic to Republican control in the 2026 midterms.

LePage previously served two terms as Maine's governor from 2011 through 2019. Dunlap currently serves as Maine's state auditor and previously served as secretary of state.
