🚪 Judge Closes Hearing on Hampton I-95 Redevelopment LawsuitPublic Barred from Injunction Arguments 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EnvironmentSeptember 2, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 2, 2026 at 7:49 PM ET

A Merrimack County Superior Court judge closed a hearing to the public on Sept. 1 as attorneys argued over a lawsuit challenging New Hampshire's planned redevelopment of the Interstate 95 service plazas in Hampton. The case was brought by Seacoast Welcome Center LLC, operated by Common Man Roadside, against the New Hampshire Liquor Commission after the company was disqualified from the state's competitive bidding process for the high-profile project.

The lawsuit seeks a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction to halt the state's vendor selection process while the court reviews the challenge. The redevelopment would transform approximately 88 acres of state-owned property into new travel centers featuring two 22,000-square-foot state liquor stores along with expanded commercial services for motorists entering and leaving New Hampshire. Court records indicate portions of the case were sealed or heavily redacted because they contain confidential financial information and proprietary business materials, leading the judge to close the hearing.

Common Man Roadside argues the procurement process contained procedural errors that unfairly affected its proposal. The company is led by Alex Ray and currently operates the state welcome centers on Interstate 93 in Hooksett. The competing redevelopment proposal is backed by developer Dick Anagnost and Global Partners LP. Earlier this year, the New Hampshire Liquor Commission restarted the procurement process after state officials identified administrative issues and later issued a revised request for proposals.

The commission is expected to select a preferred developer for the proposed 35-year lease later this month, although that timeline could be affected by the pending litigation. The lawsuit highlights the legal and commercial stakes surrounding one of New Hampshire's largest public redevelopment projects and whether the procurement process complied with state requirements before a final contract is awarded.
