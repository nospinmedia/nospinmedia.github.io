🏥 Maine Reviews New Hospital Owner After Patient-Care ComplaintsStaffing And Service Concerns Could Bring Fines Or State Enforcement 👇

HealthNovember 22, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 22, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Maine health officials are reviewing whether Prime Healthcare Foundation is complying with conditions attached to its takeover of Central Maine Healthcare after patients and employees raised concerns about staffing, services and conditions at its hospitals.

The Maine Department of Health and Human Services held a public hearing in Auburn on Friday, November 20, examining requirements imposed when Prime acquired the system earlier this year. Central Maine Healthcare includes Central Maine Medical Center in Lewiston, Bridgton Hospital and Rumford Hospital.

Employees and community members described staffing reductions, difficulty obtaining care, supply shortages and cleanliness concerns. A maternity nurse said the number of OB/GYN providers had fallen from 17 to eight. The testimony represents allegations and experiences presented to regulators, not final state findings.

Prime, meanwhile, announced on November 19 that it has invested $26 million in Central Maine Healthcare for facility, cooling-system and technology upgrades since the acquisition.

Regulators are also monitoring efforts to restore CMMC's Level III trauma center certification, which expired December 1, 2025. Revised acquisition conditions give the hospital two years to regain certification and required a permanent trauma manager to be hired within six months. Emergency services director Meredith Kennedy told regulators she is serving as interim trauma manager while the search continues.

The state review comes amid separate federal scrutiny. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services warned in October that CMMC could lose Medicare funding on January 6, 2027 unless deficiencies involving sterile processing and operating rooms identified during an August inspection are corrected.

Maine's acquisition conditions carry additional enforcement powers. Violations could result in penalties of up to $10,000 per violation, withheld payments or court action requiring services to be restored. Prime says it remains committed to meeting its obligations.

The state has not determined that Prime violated the takeover conditions. Public comments remain open through November 30, with a preliminary state analysis expected by year's end.
