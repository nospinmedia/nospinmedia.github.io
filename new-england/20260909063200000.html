🚨 Cyberattack closes Springfield schools for second dayLoss of student medical records keeps classrooms shut 👇

IllustrationCybersecurity teams responding to a large-scale system breach.Credit: AI-generated illustration (No Spin Media)
EducationSeptember 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 9, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Springfield Public Schools are closed for a second consecutive day Wednesday as officials work to contain a severe cyberattack that has blocked access to systems considered essential for safely operating the Massachusetts district.

Officials say an outside group gained access to the Springfield Public Schools network and blocked the district from accessing certain online programs. The incident has been classified as a districtwide Level 4 severe cyber incident, and federal, state and local law enforcement agencies are involved in the investigation.

A major obstacle to reopening is the district's inability to access vital student medical records. School nurses rely on those records for information needed to dispense medications, identify allergies and handle other student health needs. Officials say schools will reopen once they are confident nurses can again access the medical information required to safely care for students.

Springfield officials said the problems began with intermittent disruptions before escalating into malicious cyber traffic. The full extent of the intrusion remains under investigation, and officials have not determined publicly whether student or staff personal information was compromised.

The district has instructed students and employees to remain off SPS networks until further notice. Students have also been told not to use district-issued laptops while technicians work to identify, contain and remove the threat and begin restoring services.

Even after schools reopen, technology may not immediately return to normal. Officials said many online processes could initially be handled with pen and paper, while teachers may rely on books, notebooks and other non-digital materials as systems are gradually restored. Transportation and food-related information have also been affected by the outage.

The cyberattack first forced Springfield schools to close Tuesday. Officials extended the shutdown through Wednesday after determining that systems necessary for essential school operations remained unavailable, and they have warned that additional closures are possible depending on restoration progress.
