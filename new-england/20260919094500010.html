🏥 20 Lewiston nurses fired or suspended amid union fightCMMC says discipline involved patient-care and workplace violations 👇

HealthSeptember 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 19, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Central Maine Medical Center in Lewiston has fired or suspended 20 nurses as employees prepare to vote on whether to unionize, escalating a dispute between nurses and hospital owner Prime Healthcare. The hospital says 15 nurses have been terminated and five additional cases remain pending. The Maine State Nurses Association says the disciplinary actions are retaliation against nurses advocating for unionization and patient-care improvements. Prime Healthcare denies that claim.

CMMC nurses filed a petition with the National Labor Relations Board on August 24 seeking an election to join the Maine State Nurses Association. The vote is scheduled for September 24. Nurses have been campaigning for safer staffing, better equipment, improved retention and changes they say are needed to protect patients.

Prime Healthcare says none of the employees were disciplined because of union activity or for exercising legally protected rights. The company says the employment actions involved documented violations of hospital policies, including allegations of patient abandonment, timekeeping violations and disruption of patient care. Because they are personnel matters, the hospital has not publicly released details about the individual cases.

The union disputes the hospital's explanation and says the nurses were targeted for advocating for patients and supporting the organizing campaign. An NLRB case involving CMMC was filed on September 14 and remains open, with the listed allegation involving employee discharge.

The Lewiston dispute comes amid organizing efforts at other Prime Healthcare hospitals. Nurses at Olympia Fields Hospital and Saint Francis Hospital in Illinois also filed union-election petitions on August 24. Olympia Fields nurses are scheduled to vote September 23. The planned election at Saint Francis was blocked while federal labor officials consider allegations involving discipline, surveillance, interrogation and other conduct. Prime nurses and union organizers have alleged retaliation at multiple facilities, while Prime has denied disciplining employees for protected union activity.

The dispute has raised concerns about staffing inside CMMC. Nurses involved in the organizing campaign say the hospital was already understaffed and sometimes lacked basic supplies before the suspensions and firings. Prime Healthcare says its focus remains on maintaining patient safety and supporting its care teams.
