🗳️ Democrats strengthen control in Vermont and take over New Hampshire LegislatureRepublican governors remain as voters reshape both Statehouses 👇

EconomyNovember 5, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 5, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Democrats strengthened their position in the Vermont Legislature and took control of both legislative chambers in New Hampshire on Election Day, reshaping political power in the neighboring states even as voters reelected Republican governors Phil Scott and Kelly Ayotte.

In Vermont, Democrats regained a veto-proof majority in the state Senate and, together with Progressives, secured at least the two-thirds majority needed in the House to override gubernatorial vetoes. The results reverse much of the Republican legislative advance from 2024, when Democrats lost their previous supermajorities.

Republicans nevertheless retained Vermont's highest office. Scott won a sixth two-year term, leaving Vermont with a Republican governor facing a Legislature where Democrats and Progressives have enough votes, when unified, to override vetoes.

A change in party control occurred in New Hampshire, where Democrats flipped both the 400-member House and 24-member Senate from Republican control. Democrats also won control of the Executive Council, giving the party significantly more influence over state government than it held before the election.

Ayotte won a second term as New Hampshire governor, creating divided government in Concord. She will begin her next term working with Democratic legislative majorities rather than the Republican-controlled Legislature in place during her first two years as governor.

The results leave both states with Republican governors but substantially strengthened Democratic legislative power. The distinction is important: Democrats already controlled Vermont's Legislature and regained supermajorities, while New Hampshire voters changed which party controls both legislative chambers.
