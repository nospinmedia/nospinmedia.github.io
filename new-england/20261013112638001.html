🚨 Trespasser Dies After Contact With Red Line Third RailIncident Near Neponset Bridge Temporarily Disrupts MBTA Service 👇

New EnglandOctober 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 13, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

An adult man died after trespassing onto the MBTA Red Line right-of-way and coming into contact with the electrified third rail near the Neponset-Anderson Bridge in Boston late Sunday, October 11, 2026, according to transit authorities.

MBTA Transit Police said the man was on the tracks without authorization when he made contact with the third rail, which supplies electrical power to Red Line trains. Emergency personnel responded to the scene, where the man was pronounced dead.

Transit Police said preliminary evidence indicated that no foul play was involved. The incident was also not connected to MBTA maintenance work or other transit operations. Authorities had not publicly identified the man as the investigation continued.

The emergency response required the MBTA to suspend Red Line service between JFK/UMass and Quincy Center. Shuttle buses were deployed to carry passengers around the affected section while emergency personnel and investigators worked along the tracks. Normal rail operations were later restored.

Third rails provide electricity to subway trains and carry enough electrical current to cause severe or fatal injuries. Because of that danger, MBTA tracks and rights-of-way are restricted areas, and transit officials routinely warn passengers never to enter the track area except under the direction of emergency personnel.

The death was reported as the fourth MBTA-related fatality in 2026, adding another incident to a year that has included several deaths connected to the transit system. In this case, however, investigators said the available evidence indicated that the victim had entered a restricted area rather than being involved in normal passenger or transit operations.

Authorities are continuing to review the circumstances surrounding the man's presence on the tracks, including how and where he entered the Red Line right-of-way. No criminal activity was suspected based on the preliminary investigation.

The incident also demonstrates the broader consequences of unauthorized track access. Beyond the immediate danger posed by electrified equipment and moving trains, an emergency on the tracks can require power shutdowns and service suspensions affecting passengers across a significant portion of a subway line.
