🏥 Massachusetts Orders $14.5 Million In Dental Insurance RebatesNation's first dental loss-ratio law returns money to consumers 👇

HealthAugust 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 10, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

The Healey-Driscoll Administration announced on August 6, 2026, that dental insurers will return $14.5 million to Massachusetts consumers and businesses after failing to meet the state's minimum spending requirement on patient care. The rebates are the first issued under Massachusetts' landmark dental insurance law, making it the first state in the nation to require dental insurers to refund policyholders when too little of their premium revenue is spent on clinical care.

The rebates are enforced by the Massachusetts Division of Insurance under a voter-approved Dental Medical Loss Ratio (MLR) law. The law requires dental insurers to spend at least 83% of premium dollars on patient care and quality improvements rather than administration, marketing, or profits. Companies that fail to meet the threshold must return the difference to policyholders through rebates.

State officials said the payments will be distributed to eligible individuals, families, and employers covered by affected dental plans. Supporters say the policy increases accountability by ensuring that a larger share of premium dollars is directed toward dental care instead of insurer overhead, while encouraging companies to improve the value of coverage offered to customers.

Governor Maura Healey said the rebates demonstrate that the law is working as intended by putting money back into the hands of consumers when insurers fail to meet the state's spending requirements. Massachusetts remains the only state with a mandatory dental medical loss ratio that requires insurers to issue rebates when they fall below the minimum standard.
