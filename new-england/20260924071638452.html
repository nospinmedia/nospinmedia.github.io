⚖️ ICE arrest at Connecticut courthouse sparks confrontationOne activist pepper-sprayed and another taken to hospital 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
HealthSeptember 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 24, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A confrontation between immigration activists and U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents at a courthouse in Meriden, Connecticut, ended with one woman being pepper-sprayed and another taken to a hospital after federal agents entered the building and arrested at least one person.

The Connecticut Judicial Branch confirmed that a person was arrested at the Meriden courthouse on September 23 and said Judicial Marshal Services had received a warrant before the arrest. Officials said they were reviewing the warrant and video of the incident to determine whether Connecticut law was followed. ICE did not respond to requests for comment about the operation. Meriden officials said the mayor and local police had not been notified in advance about ICE's presence or the arrest.

Video recorded by activists shows masked federal agents entering and leaving the courthouse as members of a rapid-response group followed and questioned them. Nancy Burton, a Meriden resident and member of the group, said an ICE agent pepper-sprayed her as he was leaving the building. Another activist, Madeline Gallagher, fell after being pushed back by a courthouse security employee as activists crowded around the agents. Witnesses said Gallagher briefly lost consciousness, and she was taken by ambulance to a hospital.

The incident raises questions about Connecticut's Trust Act, which restricts immigration enforcement activity at state courthouses. State law generally requires a judicial warrant or signed court order for immigration arrests in public areas of courthouses and also places restrictions on law enforcement officers concealing their identities with masks. Federal officials have challenged Connecticut's restrictions, with the U.S. Department of Justice suing the state in May and arguing that Connecticut cannot regulate federal immigration agents.

Connecticut House Speaker Matt Ritter said state lawmakers expected legal challenges when the restrictions were adopted and planned to consult the attorney general's office about possible state action if the law was violated.
