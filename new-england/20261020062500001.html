⚖️ Thousands of messages take center stage in Matthew Farwell murder trialFBI testimony details years-long relationship with Sandra Birchmore 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalOctober 20, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 20, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Jurors in the federal murder trial of former Stoughton police officer Matthew Farwell are hearing extensive text-message evidence documenting his years-long relationship with Sandra Birchmore, as an FBI agent continues testimony about tens of thousands of communications recovered during the investigation.

FBI Special Agent Heidi Kelsch testified about messages exchanged between Farwell and Birchmore, including conversations concerning their sexual relationship. Investigators recovered approximately 40,000 communications between the two dating back years.

Prosecutors allege Farwell began having sex with Birchmore in 2013, when she was 15 years old and he was 27. Birchmore had met Farwell through the Stoughton Police Explorers youth program, where he served as an adult adviser.

Birchmore was 23 and pregnant when she was found dead in her Canton apartment in February 2021. Her death was initially investigated as a suicide. Federal prosecutors now allege Farwell killed Birchmore and staged the scene to make her death appear self-inflicted.

The pregnancy is a central part of the case. Prosecutors say Birchmore told Farwell that he was the father and allege he feared their relationship would be exposed. The defense disputes the government's theory and has said DNA testing determined that Farwell was not the biological father of Birchmore's unborn child.

Farwell's attorneys maintain that he did not kill Birchmore and contend that she died by suicide. Prosecutors argue that the messages, testimony from people who knew Birchmore and other evidence will show Farwell had a motive to silence her as details of their relationship became known.

Farwell has pleaded not guilty to federal charges of killing a witness or victim and killing a child in utero. The federal government is not seeking the death penalty; a conviction could result in life imprisonment.
