🌧️ Thanksgiving storm targets all six New England states45–55 mph coastal gusts, heavy rain and mountain snow could disrupt holiday travel 👇

EnvironmentNovember 24, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 24, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A developing Thanksgiving storm is expected to affect all six New England states Thursday into Friday, bringing heavy rain and strong winds to much of the region while colder northern and higher-elevation areas could see snow or mixed precipitation.

The system is expected to reach southern New England late Thanksgiving afternoon into Thursday evening, with rain developing around 4–7 p.m. in parts of Massachusetts before conditions intensify Thursday night into Friday.

In Massachusetts, Rhode Island and Connecticut, the storm is expected to be primarily a rain and wind event. Coastal areas could see gusts of 45 to 55 mph or higher, while gusts above 40 mph are possible farther inland along portions of the I-95 corridor. Those conditions could affect road and air travel and increase the risk of scattered power outages.

Coastal flooding is another concern because the storm coincides with astronomically high tides associated with the November 24 full moon. The combination of strong onshore winds and so-called king tides could produce flooding, splash-over and beach erosion in vulnerable coastal communities.

New Hampshire and Vermont could see heavy rain at lower elevations, with snow or a wintry mix possible across northern areas and higher terrain, including portions of the White and Green mountains. Coastal New Hampshire could also experience strong winds and flooding along the Seacoast.

In Maine, rain and strong winds are expected across much of the state, with coastal flooding and beach erosion possible along the Gulf of Maine. Northern and western areas have a greater chance of snow or mixed precipitation depending on the storm's final track and temperatures.

The forecast could still change because relatively small shifts in the storm's path will influence wind strength, coastal impacts and the location of the rain-snow line.

This is not the same storm that moved through New England Sunday night into Monday. The Thanksgiving system is a second coastal storm arriving during the holiday itself and continuing into the Black Friday travel period.

Travelers and coastal residents should monitor updated forecasts as Thursday approaches, particularly as forecasters refine the storm track and timing of the strongest winds and heaviest precipitation.
