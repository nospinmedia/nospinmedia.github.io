⚖️ Salem teen sentenced after shooting girlfriend in headProsecutors sought 8 to 10 years, but 15-year-old is not expected to serve time in state prison 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalNovember 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 15, 2026 at 8:46 AM ET

A 15-year-old Salem boy who admitted shooting his teenage girlfriend in the head in Beverly has been sentenced to just over three years, but prosecutors say he is not expected to serve any time in state prison because he will remain in the juvenile system until he turns 21.

Alexander Muzarol, who was 14 when the shooting occurred, pleaded guilty in Salem Juvenile Court to armed assault with intent to murder, assault and battery with a dangerous weapon causing serious bodily injury, and carrying a firearm without a license.

The shooting happened near Simon Street in Beverly on October 11, 2025. The victim was also 15 at the time and was Muzarol's girlfriend. According to the Essex County District Attorney's Office, she suffered severe, permanent injuries after being shot in the head. Prosecutors did not identify a motive in announcing the plea and sentence.

Muzarol was indicted as a youthful offender on December 18, 2025, a designation that allowed the case to proceed publicly and made an adult sentence possible despite his age. The shooting was investigated by Beverly Police and the Massachusetts State Police Detective Unit assigned to the district attorney's office.

At sentencing, the victim described how the shooting continues to affect her life. She told the court that she had lost her health and sense of safety and said her family had watched her fight for her life and helped throughout her recovery.

Prosecutors asked for a substantially longer punishment. Chief Family Crimes Prosecutor Kate MacDougall recommended 8 to 10 years in state prison on the armed assault charge, followed by five years of probation on the assault and battery charge. The prosecution also requested that Muzarol remain committed to the Department of Youth Services until age 18.

The judge instead imposed a state-prison sentence of three years and one month to three years, one month and one day, along with a Department of Youth Services commitment until age 21. Because of the DYS commitment, the district attorney's office said Muzarol is not expected to serve any portion of the sentence in state prison.

Essex County District Attorney Paul Tucker said his office sought a substantial prison sentence because of the seriousness of the shooting and what prosecutors described as the victim's lifelong injuries.

The case highlights the different sentencing options available when a Massachusetts juvenile is prosecuted as a youthful offender, including the possibility of an adult sentence while the defendant remains under the supervision of the state's juvenile justice system.
