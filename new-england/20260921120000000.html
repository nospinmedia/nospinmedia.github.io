👟 Boston-based Converse pulls ad after racist imagery backlashCritics said photo evoked KKK hood and lynching imagery 👇

PoliticsSeptember 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 21, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Boston-based Converse has apologized and removed a social media advertisement after critics said imagery in the sneaker campaign evoked the Ku Klux Klan and lynching, prompting questions about how the advertisement passed the company's review process.

The Instagram image featured Karina, a member of the K-pop group Aespa, wearing a white skirt and holding a pair of black Chuck 70 X sneakers. Critics said lighting across the skirt created a white triangular shape resembling a pointed KKK hood, while the position of the dangling shoes reminded them of the feet of a lynching victim.

Converse acknowledged the criticism and said it understood why people found the image deeply upsetting. The company apologized, said it had removed the advertisement from its own channels and was working to remove it everywhere else it appeared. Converse said the situation should not have happened and that the company would do better.

The controversy produced calls on social media for a boycott and for accountability from employees involved in approving the campaign. Other commenters said they did not believe the imagery was intentional. Converse declined to provide additional details about how the advertisement was reviewed or approved before publication.

Civil rights attorney Lee Merritt criticized the company's internal review process, arguing that the concern should have been identified before the advertisement was released. Karina and her management company had not publicly responded to requests for comment in the reporting reviewed for this story.

Converse is owned by Nike and headquartered in Boston. The company was founded in Malden, Massachusetts, in 1908 and is best known for its Chuck Taylor All Star sneakers.
