☢️ Massachusetts blocks Pilgrim nuclear wastewater discharge into Cape Cod BayState rejects Holtec appeal over up to 1.1 million gallons 👇

EnvironmentSeptember 16, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 16, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Massachusetts regulators have rejected Holtec Decommissioning International's appeal seeking permission to discharge up to 1.1 million gallons of treated wastewater from the former Pilgrim Nuclear Power Station in Plymouth into Cape Cod Bay. The Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection announced its final administrative decision on September 15, upholding a permit denial first issued in July 2024.

Holtec is dismantling and cleaning up Pilgrim, which permanently stopped generating electricity in May 2019. The company sought a modification to the plant's state water-discharge permit in 2023, arguing that releasing treated wastewater into the bay was an environmentally responsible disposal option and that radioactive releases would remain below federal safety limits. The wastewater was used in the plant's reactor system and contains radioactive contaminants, including tritium, as well as non-radioactive contaminants such as metals and PFAS.

MassDEP concluded that the proposed discharge is prohibited by the Massachusetts Ocean Sanctuaries Act. Cape Cod Bay is a protected ocean sanctuary, and the law prohibits the discharge of commercial, municipal, domestic or industrial waste into protected waters. State regulators determined that Pilgrim's decommissioning water qualifies as industrial wastewater. Holtec appealed the original denial in August 2024, leading to three days of evidentiary hearings before the state's Office of Appeals and Dispute Resolution. The state's environmental commissioner has now dismissed that appeal and affirmed the permit denial.

Holtec spokesperson Patrick O'Brien said the company is reviewing the decision and considering what options remain. Holtec can appeal the ruling to Massachusetts Superior Court. The company says difficulties disposing of the water have delayed its cleanup schedule, which currently calls for decontamination of the Pilgrim site to be completed in 2034.

In the meantime, wastewater stored at Pilgrim has been decreasing through evaporation. That process releases small amounts of radioactive tritium into the air under federal regulation, and Holtec's reported releases have remained below Nuclear Regulatory Commission safety limits. Environmental groups and some area residents have also opposed that disposal method.
