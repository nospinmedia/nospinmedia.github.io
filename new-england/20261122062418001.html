🦃 New England Grocers Step Up Thanksgiving Food Aid As Demand SurgesHannaford, Stop & Shop, Shaw's And Big Y Support Food Banks Facing Growing Need 👇

IllustrationCommunity food bank distribution.Credit: AI-generated illustration (No Spin Media)
PoliticsNovember 22, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 22, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Major New England grocery chains are supporting Thanksgiving hunger-relief efforts as food banks and community organizations across the region report growing numbers of families needing help putting meals on the table.

In New Hampshire, Hannaford partnered with the New Hampshire Food Bank and Manchester Radio Group for the annual Tons of Turkeys drive. Several stores contributed, with all donations going to the food bank. Last year's drive collected more than 36,000 pounds of turkey.

Hannaford's efforts extend beyond New Hampshire. In Maine, the company committed $500,000 through its Eat Well, Be Well – A Path to Better Health initiative, supporting nutrition and food-access programs serving vulnerable populations.

In Boston, Mayor Michelle Wu announced on Friday, November 20 that a record 6,000 turkeys would be distributed to local families, with grocery partners helping support the effort. Stop & Shop contributed $60,000, while Shaw's, Star Market and Roche Bros. were among the other participating grocery partners.

Big Y is also running its annual Sack Hunger campaign through December 9. Donations benefit the Greater Boston Food Bank, Food Bank of Western Massachusetts, Worcester County Food Bank and Connecticut Foodshare, which collectively supply more than 1,400 member agencies. Big Y is also pledging portions of proceeds from selected products during the holiday period.

The assistance comes as food banks report substantial increases in need. The Greater Boston Food Bank is distributing a record 54,000-plus turkeys this holiday season. In New Hampshire, food-bank officials say pantries that once supplemented household groceries are increasingly becoming families' primary food source.

The pressure extends farther north. Vermont Foodbank partners reported a 30% increase in visits compared with the previous year, while a Maine food center in Piscataquis County described a "huge increase in need" as it provided Thanksgiving food to about 350 households.

Together, the programs show grocery chains, food banks, volunteers and customers responding across New England as more households seek food assistance heading into Thanksgiving.
