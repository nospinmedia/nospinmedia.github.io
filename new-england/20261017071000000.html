🕯️ Manchester Remembers Officer Michael Briggs 20 Years After Fatal ShootingPolice and Community Honor Officer Killed in the Line of Duty in 2006 👇

HealthOctober 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 17, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Manchester police and community members are marking 20 years since Officer Michael Briggs was fatally shot in the line of duty, remembering a case that became one of the most significant police killings in modern New Hampshire history.

Briggs was shot on October 16, 2006, while working as a bicycle patrol officer in Manchester. He died from his injuries the following day at Catholic Medical Center. Briggs was 35 years old, a Marine Corps veteran and the father of two young sons.

Police said Briggs and another officer encountered Michael Addison while investigating a disturbance. Addison shot Briggs in the head and fled, triggering a manhunt before he was arrested.

A jury convicted Addison of capital murder in 2008, and he was sentenced to death. His case became closely connected to New Hampshire's long-running debate over capital punishment. The state abolished the death penalty in 2019, but the repeal did not apply retroactively to Addison's sentence.

Two decades after the shooting, Manchester police continue to honor Briggs as one of the department's fallen officers. His name remains memorialized at the department, and anniversary observances have brought together current officers, retired law enforcement personnel and members of the community.

Briggs' connection to law enforcement also continued through his family. His son Mitchell Briggs later entered law enforcement and graduated from the New Hampshire Police Academy, carrying forward a family tradition of public service.

The anniversary provides an opportunity for Manchester to remember both Briggs' service and the impact his killing had on his family, fellow officers and the wider community.
