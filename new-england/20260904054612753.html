⚖️ Judge rejects bid to remove juror in Lindsay Clancy trialMassachusetts panel enters seventh day after dispute over reasonable doubt 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthSeptember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 4, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A Massachusetts judge refused to remove a juror from the high-profile Lindsay Clancy murder trial after the jury foreperson reported that one member was refusing to follow the court's instructions on reasonable doubt. The dispute unfolded during the sixth day of deliberations at Plymouth Superior Court in Plymouth, where jurors are deciding whether Clancy was criminally responsible when she killed her three young children at the family's Duxbury home in 2023. The panel was ordered to return Friday for a seventh day of deliberations.

Defense attorney Kevin Reddington asked Judge William Sullivan to dismiss the juror after the foreperson submitted the note. Sullivan instead questioned the jurors individually about whether they could follow the law and reminded the full panel that they were required to follow his legal instructions. He reread the definition of reasonable doubt before deliberations continued. Reddington maintained that a juror refusing to apply the court's instructions should be removed. Prosecutors opposed the request, arguing that removing a juror based on disagreements revealed during deliberations could improperly intrude on the jury's decision-making process. Sullivan declined to remove the juror.

Clancy, 36, a former labor and delivery nurse, does not dispute that she strangled 5-year-old Cora, 3-year-old Dawson and 8-month-old Callan on January 24, 2023. The central issue at trial is whether she was criminally responsible at the time. Her attorneys argue that severe postpartum psychosis prevented her from appreciating the wrongfulness of her actions. Prosecutors contend that Clancy understood what she was doing and deliberately planned the killings.

The jury had already reported being deadlocked twice. Sullivan subsequently issued the Massachusetts Tuey-Rodriguez instruction, which directs jurors facing an impasse to reconsider their positions while making clear that no juror should abandon an honestly held judgment simply to produce a unanimous verdict. Thursday's dispute over the individual juror followed that instruction.

No mistrial has been declared, and deliberations are continuing. If the 12-member jury ultimately cannot reach a unanimous verdict, Sullivan could declare a mistrial, potentially leaving prosecutors to decide whether to try the case again.
