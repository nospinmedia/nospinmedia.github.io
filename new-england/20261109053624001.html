🌧️ Rain helps crews gain ground on White Mountains wildfireWild River Fire drops to 821 acres as containment reaches 29% 👇

File photoWildfire burning through forest.Credit: Salam2009
EnvironmentNovember 9, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 9, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Recent rain has helped firefighters make significant progress against the Wild River Fire in New Hampshire's White Mountain National Forest, with the fire estimated at 821 acres and 29% contained Sunday after previously reaching nearly 1,000 acres.

The U.S. Forest Service said the rainfall is helping suppression efforts as crews enter their second week fighting the fire. The blaze began November 1 about seven miles southeast of Gorham on the northwest side of the Wild River in Coos County, near the Maine border. It grew rapidly during its first several days, reaching an estimated 982 acres Wednesday as extremely dry forest conditions and difficult terrain helped the flames spread.

Officials have since revised the mapped acreage several times as firefighters and aircraft gained better access and produced more accurate measurements. The decrease from nearly 1,000 acres does not mean roughly 160 acres of forest somehow became unburned; instead, improved mapping has refined the estimated perimeter while crews have prevented significant additional spread.

Rain is providing useful assistance, but officials have cautioned that the region's underlying drought has not ended. Forest Service officials previously said the fire was burning primarily through dry leaves, forest litter and other surface fuels. Some areas are steep, rocky and difficult for firefighters to reach directly, complicating efforts to completely extinguish the fire.

The blaze is unusually large for northern New England. White Mountain National Forest Supervisor Derek Ibarguen said last week that it was unofficially the largest wildfire in the national forest's history since the forest was established. Officials said no structures were threatened, although smoke and ash have been noticeable in nearby communities.

The Forest Service has kept the affected portion of the national forest closed to the public while suppression operations continue. Another community meeting is scheduled for Tuesday at 7 p.m. at Shelburne Town Hall to update residents on firefighting progress.

So yes, the rain helped considerably, but firefighters still have substantial work ahead before the Wild River Fire is fully contained.
