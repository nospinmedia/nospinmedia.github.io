🏛️ Ex-Connecticut official flees to Greece before corruption sentencingKosta Diamantis left U.S. after conviction on 21 federal counts 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationSeptember 18, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 18, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Former Connecticut official Konstantinos "Kosta" Diamantis fled to Greece rather than appear for his federal corruption sentencing, according to his attorney and federal court proceedings. Diamantis, who previously served as Connecticut's deputy budget director and headed the state's Office of School Construction Grants and Review, had been convicted by a federal jury on 21 counts stemming from a corruption investigation involving school construction contracts.

Prosecutors accused Diamantis of using his state positions to demand payments from contractors in connection with publicly funded school construction projects. His convictions included bribery, extortion and related federal offenses. He had been scheduled for sentencing in federal court in Connecticut on September 17, 2026, but did not appear.

Diamantis' attorney, Norm Pattis, told the court that his client was in Greece. Reporting on the case said Diamantis had surrendered his U.S. passport as part of his release conditions but also held Greek citizenship and obtained a Greek passport. His departure came after prosecutors had previously raised concerns about the possibility that his ties to Greece could make him a flight risk.

According to reporting on his movements, Diamantis traveled from Florida to the Bahamas by boat before continuing through Europe and reaching Greece. Pattis said Diamantis contacted him from Greece shortly before the scheduled sentencing. Diamantis has continued to maintain his innocence despite the jury's verdict and has characterized the prosecution as politically motivated.

U.S. District Judge Stefan Underhill postponed the sentencing following Diamantis' failure to appear. Federal authorities are now faced with determining how to pursue his return to the United States while he remains overseas.

The case is a major development in a years-long federal investigation into corruption surrounding Connecticut's school construction program and leaves a convicted former state official outside the country while awaiting sentencing.
