🇨🇦 Some Haitian TPS holders leave Massachusetts for CanadaAdvocates warn departures could deepen workforce shortages 👇

EconomySeptember 1, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 1, 2026 at 5:29 PM ET

Some Haitian immigrants in Massachusetts are relocating to Canada following the federal termination of Temporary Protected Status (TPS), a shift that immigration advocates and community leaders say could worsen labor shortages in several industries across the state. The departures follow the loss of federal work authorization and deportation protections for many Haitian TPS beneficiaries, prompting some families to pursue legal immigration pathways in Canada instead of remaining in the United States.

Community organizations say many of those leaving have worked for years in essential occupations, including health care, elder care, hospitality, and the skilled trades. Employers and advocacy groups have warned that losing experienced workers could further strain staffing in sectors that already face persistent labor shortages. Massachusetts officials have also expressed concern about the economic and humanitarian effects of the policy change on communities throughout the Commonwealth.

The migration trend accelerated after court decisions allowed the federal government to move forward with ending TPS protections for Haiti. Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell and Representative Ayanna Pressley have criticized the decision, arguing that it places families at risk while reducing the available workforce in critical industries. Supporters of restoring TPS continue urging Congress to pursue legislative solutions that would provide longer-term immigration protections.

Canada has emerged as one option for some affected families seeking legal status and employment opportunities, although immigration experts note that entry into Canada is governed by its own immigration requirements and is not guaranteed. Community organizations continue assisting families as they evaluate legal options and prepare for changes resulting from the federal policy.
