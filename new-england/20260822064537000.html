⚖️ Lindsay Clancy Defense Rests in Triple Murder TrialProsecutors Begin Rebuttal Ahead of Closing Arguments 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
HealthAugust 22, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 22, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The defense has rested its case in the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy, bringing weeks of testimony in Plymouth Superior Court closer to a conclusion. Clancy, a former labor and delivery nurse from Duxbury, Massachusetts, has pleaded not guilty to charges stemming from the January 24, 2023, deaths of her three children: Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months. Her attorneys argue she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis and was not criminally responsible for her actions.

Defense attorney Kevin Reddington concluded the case after presenting testimony from psychiatrists, medical experts, and family members, including forensic psychiatrist Dr. Phillip Resnick, who testified that Clancy was experiencing postpartum psychosis at the time of the killings. Shortly after the defense rested, prosecutors began presenting rebuttal evidence by calling forensic psychiatrist Dr. Avram Mack, who is expected to challenge the defense's conclusion and testify that Clancy understood the nature and wrongfulness of her actions.

The trial has focused largely on whether Clancy met Massachusetts' legal standard for criminal responsibility at the time of the deaths. Under the state's McHoul standard, jurors must determine whether a mental disease or defect deprived a defendant of the substantial capacity either to appreciate the criminality of their conduct or to conform their conduct to the requirements of the law. Prosecutors maintain the killings were intentional and that Clancy was legally responsible despite her mental health struggles, while the defense contends postpartum psychosis rendered her incapable of criminal responsibility.

Judge William Sullivan indicated that once the prosecution completes its rebuttal case, attorneys will deliver closing arguments before the case is turned over to the jury. The panel of 12 jurors, along with six alternates, will then begin deliberating one of the most closely watched criminal trials in Massachusetts in recent years.
