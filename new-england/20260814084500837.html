🌿 Massachusetts Orders Temporary Statewide Kratom BanEmergency Rule Classifies Herbal Product As Schedule I Substance 👇

EducationAugust 14, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 14, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Massachusetts has issued an emergency order temporarily banning the sale of kratom statewide by classifying the herbal substance as a Schedule I Controlled Substance under state law. Governor Maura Healey and the Massachusetts Department of Public Health (DPH) announced the action, citing growing concerns over unregulated products sold in convenience stores, smoke shops, gas stations, and online. The order applies to kratom powders, capsules, gummies, extracts, and other consumer products, with officials warning that some formulations contain highly concentrated alkaloids or synthetic compounds linked to serious health risks.

The emergency regulation was issued by DPH Commissioner Robbie Goldstein, MD, PhD, under 105 CMR 726.000 and authority granted by Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 94C, Section 2A. State health officials said reports of dependence, liver injury, overdose, and inconsistent product labeling prompted immediate action while longer-term regulatory options are considered. Although some consumers use kratom to manage chronic pain or opioid withdrawal symptoms, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration has repeatedly warned that kratom products have not been approved for any medical use and may pose significant safety risks.

The order takes full legal effect on August 28, 2026, following the required notice period, and will remain in effect for up to one year unless modified or replaced by permanent regulations. During that time, state and local health officials, along with law enforcement agencies, will have authority to remove prohibited products from retail shelves and pursue enforcement against businesses that continue selling them. The Healey administration said it will also expand public education efforts and substance use treatment resources through the Bureau of Substance Addiction Services.

Several Massachusetts communities, including Lowell, Hanover, Yarmouth, and Chelmsford, had already adopted local kratom restrictions. State officials said the emergency order establishes a uniform statewide standard across all 351 Massachusetts cities and towns while policymakers evaluate whether permanent legislation or regulations are needed.
