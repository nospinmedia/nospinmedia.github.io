📋 Massachusetts watchdog faults BPS over $857K in improper spendingInspector General urges 10-day vendor payment pause and internal review 👇

EducationAugust 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 27, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Massachusetts Office of the Inspector General says Boston Public Schools improperly or illegally spent at least $857,261 between 2019 and 2024 after repeatedly bypassing procurement rules, using unrelated contracts to pay vendors and failing to correct problems raised by outside watchdogs. Inspector General Jeffrey S. Shapiro called on the district to pause payments for non-essential goods and services in certain departments for 10 business days while conducting an internal review of its contracting and payment systems.

Investigators found multiple violations of Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 30B, the state's Uniform Procurement Act. The report says BPS failed to seek required price quotes or competitive bids, entered some purchases without proper written contracts, used a sole-source arrangement to buy books that were available elsewhere and steered a water-quality testing procurement to a vendor in order to settle unrelated unpaid invoices. The district also routed more than $600,000 in payments through passthrough vendors that added administrative markups of up to 19%, creating nearly $78,000 in extra costs.

The Inspector General also found evidence that BPS employees used false subcontracting relationships to get around Boston's residency requirement for a former district engineer. The office said the district took little or no action after the Boston Finance Commission had previously raised concerns about some of the same practices. Shapiro warned that because the investigation reviewed only a limited number of contracts identified through complaints, similar problems could exist elsewhere in the district's operations.

BPS said the report covers practices dating back to 2019 and that Superintendent Mary Skipper and new operations leaders have implemented reforms since concerns were first raised. The district said it has changed key procurement leadership, streamlined contracting and payment systems and sharply reduced its use of sole-source contracts. BPS also said it will review the findings and develop a plan to implement the Inspector General's 25 recommendations.
