🏠 Maine eviction assistance program running out of moneyNew applications close Nov. 30 after $11 million fund draws heavy demand 👇

EconomyNovember 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 19, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Maine's statewide eviction prevention program will stop accepting new applications at 5:00 p.m. on November 30 because officials expect its funding to be exhausted, just over two months after applications opened.

MaineHousing and nonprofit Project HOME, which operates the program, announced that available money is running out. Maine lawmakers allocated $11 million this year to help renters who have fallen behind on payments and are at risk of eviction. Applications opened on September 21.

Applications are processed on a first-come, first-served basis within three priority levels. Highest priority goes to households that have received a court summons and complaint for forcible entry and detainer. Next are renters who have received an eviction notice for nonpayment. The third group consists of households spending more than 30% of their income on rent.

To qualify, households must earn less than 60% of the area median income, have monthly rent no higher than 125% of HUD's Fair Market Rent, and owe no more than $9,600 in past-due rent. Renters receiving federal housing vouchers or living in subsidized housing are not eligible. Eligible mobile home owners may also qualify for assistance with pad or lot rent, though park fees are excluded.

The program can cover eligible past-due rent and provide up to 12 months of rental assistance, with total assistance capped at $9,600. Landlords must participate because payments are made directly to them.

MaineHousing cautions that submitting an application does not guarantee assistance, since available funding may be committed before every eligible household receives help.

Renters can apply through MaineHousing's online portal, email Project HOME at evictionprevention@projecthomemaine.org, or call (207) 274-1240. Those facing an active eviction case receive the highest priority.
