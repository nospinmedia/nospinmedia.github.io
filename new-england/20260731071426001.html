🏛️ New Hampshire Governor Kelly Ayotte Welcomes Federal Observation Of State ElectionsCritics Raise Concerns As Trump Administration Plans Polling-Place Oversight 👇

EducationJuly 31, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published July 31, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

New Hampshire Governor Kelly Ayotte said she welcomes federal officials and others who want to observe how elections are conducted in the Granite State, responding to reports that the Trump administration plans to monitor polling locations in Manchester and Nashua during the 2026 election cycle.

Speaking with reporters after an Executive Council meeting at Nashua Community College on July 29, Ayotte said New Hampshire conducts its elections fairly and transparently. She said observers who visit the state would be able to see how its election system operates and noted that members of the public may already observe election procedures when they follow applicable rules.

“I would welcome anyone that wants to come to New Hampshire and see how we do elections,” Ayotte said. “I think we do them well.” She added that observers would see that the state conducts elections in a “fair, transparent way.”

Ayotte connected that openness to New Hampshire's longstanding role in presidential politics, saying the state regularly welcomes national attention because of its traditional first-in-the-nation presidential primary. Her remarks did not announce a new state monitoring program or transfer control of election administration to federal officials.

The prospect of Trump administration personnel observing elections has drawn criticism from some New Hampshire Democrats. Former Executive Councilor Andru Volinsky argued that the federal presence could amount to political interference, while Democratic gubernatorial candidate Cinde Warmington said election administration should remain under the control of Granite State officials rather than the president or the federal Department of Justice.

New Hampshire's elections remain administered by the secretary of state, municipal clerks, town moderators and other local election officials. The state's primary election is scheduled for September 8, 2026, followed by the general election on November 3, 2026.

Ayotte did not endorse federal control over ballot counting or voter eligibility decisions. Her position was that outside observers should be allowed to watch the process while state and local officials retain responsibility for conducting the elections.
