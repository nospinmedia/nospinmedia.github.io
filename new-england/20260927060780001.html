⚖️ Vermont AG prosecutes 13 anti-ICE protesters after local prosecutor declined similar casesCharges expose divide among state officials over protest rights and trespassing 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EconomySeptember 27, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 27, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

The Vermont Attorney General's Office is prosecuting 13 people arrested during a July anti-ICE demonstration in Williston, marking the first criminal prosecutions arising from a series of protests at an Immigration and Customs Enforcement surveillance facility there. Twelve defendants pleaded not guilty Thursday to misdemeanor unlawful trespass charges, while the case involving an 18-year-old was transferred to family court.

The charges stem from a July 24 protest at the White Cap Business Park, which houses ICE's National Criminal Analysis and Targeting Center. According to police affidavits, demonstrators blocked entrances to the facility and remained after Vermont State Police issued orders to leave the private property. Thirteen protesters were ultimately arrested or cited.

The prosecution represents a change from how earlier arrests at the facility were handled. Chittenden County State's Attorney Sarah George had declined to prosecute protesters arrested during previous demonstrations, citing factors including the nonviolent nature of the offenses, defendants' lack of criminal histories and her determination that prosecution would not serve the interests of justice. For the July cases, however, State Police did not send the citations to George's office. They were instead routed directly to Attorney General Charity Clark, whose office has independent authority to prosecute criminal cases in Vermont.

Clark, a Democrat who has repeatedly challenged Trump administration policies in court and says she has personally protested at an ICE facility, defended the decision as a matter of applying the law regardless of political views. She said support or opposition to a protest's cause cannot determine whether criminal law applies. Each unlawful trespass charge carries a maximum penalty of three months in jail, a $500 fine, or both.

The decision has created an unusual public disagreement among Vermont officials. Democratic State Treasurer Mike Pieciak called on Clark to dismiss the charges, arguing that prosecutors have discretion and that pursuing peaceful protesters is not in the public interest. Democratic Senate Majority Leader Kesha Ram Hinsdale also criticized the prosecutions. Republican Gov. Phil Scott, however, praised Clark's decision, saying protesters should be held accountable for violating the law.

The cases come as activists plan additional demonstrations targeting the Williston ICE facility. The 12 defendants arraigned Thursday were released with conditions that include staying away from the White Cap Business Park while their cases proceed.
