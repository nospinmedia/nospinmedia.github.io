⚖️ Groups renew challenge to Maine transgender athlete policiesNew signatures target a statewide vote in 2027 👇

EducationNovember 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 3, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

A group seeking to change Maine's rules for transgender student-athletes submitted thousands of additional petition signatures Monday, reviving an effort to put the issue before voters after narrowly missing the requirements for the 2026 ballot.

Protect Girls' Sports in Maine submitted 4,836 additional signatures to the Maine Secretary of State's office in Augusta. The campaign had previously fallen 532 valid signatures short of qualifying its proposed citizen initiative for the 2026 ballot following a dispute over signature validation that reached the Maine Supreme Judicial Court.

Campaign principal officer Leyland Streiff said organizers are now targeting the November 2027 election. They hope to combine newly submitted signatures with signatures previously determined to be valid and reach the required 67,682 validated signatures by the February 2, 2027 deadline.

The proposed initiative would restrict participation on female school athletic teams based on sex recorded on a person's original birth certificate. Supporters argue the change is necessary to protect competitive fairness and athletic opportunities for female students. Several students joined organizers Monday, including Fryeburg Academy junior Reagan Wilson, who spoke in support of the proposal.

Maine currently prohibits discrimination based on gender identity under the Maine Human Rights Act, and the state's existing framework permits transgender students to participate on school athletic teams consistent with their gender identity. Opponents of the proposed change, including LGBTQ+ and civil-rights organizations, argue that restricting participation would discriminate against transgender students.

The petition campaign is one of several avenues being pursued by groups seeking changes, alongside litigation and legislative efforts. Maine's existing protections remain in effect while those challenges continue.
