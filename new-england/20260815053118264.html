🛍️ Connecticut Expands Tax-Free Week for Back-to-School ShoppingHigher $300 exemption aims to save families millions 👇

EducationAugust 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 15, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Connecticut's annual Sales Tax-Free Week begins August 16 with expanded savings for back-to-school shoppers after state lawmakers increased the maximum eligible purchase price from $100 to $300 per item. The change, enacted through the state's latest budget and announced by the Connecticut Department of Revenue Services (DRS), exempts qualifying clothing and footwear priced under $300 from the state's 6.35% sales tax through August 22.

DRS Commissioner Mark Boughton said the expanded exemption is expected to save Connecticut consumers approximately $6 million during the weeklong tax holiday. In addition, a separate permanent exemption on eligible non-electronic school supplies, approved in the same budget package, is projected to provide another $6 million in annual tax relief. Combined, state officials estimate the measures will deliver roughly $12 million in savings for families and students.

The higher exemption threshold significantly broadens the range of qualifying purchases compared with previous years, allowing shoppers to buy more expensive clothing, shoes and other eligible apparel without paying sales tax. State officials said the changes are intended to help families facing higher household costs while encouraging back-to-school shopping at Connecticut retailers. Merchants across the state are preparing for increased customer traffic during one of the busiest retail periods of the year.

Governor Ned Lamont signed the expanded exemption into law as part of the state's biennial budget, describing it as one of several initiatives designed to reduce costs for working families. The permanent exemption for qualifying non-electronic school supplies also extends tax relief beyond the annual shopping holiday, making Connecticut one of the few states to provide year-round sales tax relief on many classroom necessities.

Eligible purchases must meet the requirements established by DRS, with clothing and footwear priced above $300 per item remaining subject to sales tax.
