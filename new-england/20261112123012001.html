🚨 ICE agent in fatal Maine shooting faced prior red flagsFamily emails show anti-immigrant hostility as questions emerge about agency vetting 👇

IllustrationICE immigration enforcement jacket.Credit: AI-generated (Gemini)
EconomyNovember 12, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 12, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The ICE officer identified in the fatal shooting of a Colombian man in Biddeford, Maine, had expressed hostility toward immigrants and was deployed to field work before officials had reviewed his completed background investigation, according to new reporting based on family interviews, emails and employment records.

David Brouillette has been identified by relatives and multiple news organizations as the officer who fatally shot Johan Sebastián Durán Guerrero, 25, during an immigration enforcement operation on July 13. The Department of Homeland Security has not publicly identified Brouillette as the shooter. DHS said Wednesday that it does not comment on personnel matters involving people no longer with the agency, indicating Brouillette is no longer employed by ICE.

Emails from 2021 provided by Brouillette's sister to The New York Times and later reviewed by ABC News show him criticizing her relationship with a Colombian-born man and making derogatory comments about immigrants and the man's background. Family members told reporters the messages reflected views Brouillette had expressed over time.

The new reporting also raises questions about his hiring and screening. Sources familiar with his employment history told the Times that Brouillette received expedited training and entered field operations before officials had reviewed his background investigation. His access to some federal computer systems was restricted because he had not received full clearance.

Previous reporting found additional employment concerns. A Hallowell official said Brouillette had "too many red flags" to be hired by the city's police department in 2024, and public records show he held several jobs in corrections, security and public safety, many lasting less than a year.

DHS rejected claims that its training or vetting was inadequate, saying incoming ICE officers are onboarded under applicable policies and standards and are trained to use the minimum force necessary in dangerous situations.

DHS initially said Durán Guerrero had entered the United States illegally but later withdrew that assertion in communications with Sen. Angus King's office. His family's attorney says he had authorization to work in the United States and was not the target of the ICE operation.

The Maine Attorney General's Office and DHS Inspector General are investigating the shooting. Rep. Chellie Pingree of Maine has called for further scrutiny of ICE's hiring and vetting practices following the new disclosures.
