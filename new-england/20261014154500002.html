🚨 Burlington Man Accused of Killing Wife With Flashlight Pleads Not GuiltyVermont Father Faces Murder and Child Cruelty Charges After Fatal Attack 👇

A 44-year-old Burlington, Vermont, man has pleaded not guilty to charges that he killed his wife by repeatedly striking her with a metal flashlight during an alleged domestic assault while their three children were in the home.

Bal B. Khadka was arraigned on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, in Chittenden County Superior Court on a charge of second-degree murder and three counts of cruelty to a child in connection with the death of his wife, Lila Darjee Khadka.

Burlington police responded to the family's home early Saturday, October 10, following reports of domestic violence. Authorities allege Khadka repeatedly struck his wife in the head with a large flashlight during an altercation inside the residence.

Police found Lila Darjee Khadka suffering from severe injuries. She was transported to the University of Vermont Medical Center, where she remained hospitalized before dying from her injuries on Sunday.

Khadka had initially faced an attempted murder charge, but prosecutors upgraded the case following his wife's death. He entered not-guilty pleas to the new charges during Tuesday's court appearance.

Prosecutors allege the couple's three children were inside the home during the attack, leading to the additional child cruelty charges. Authorities have not accused the children of being physically injured in the incident.

A judge ordered Khadka held without bail while the murder case proceeds. He is being detained at the Northwest State Correctional Facility in St. Albans pending further court proceedings.

The case remains an allegation, and Khadka is presumed innocent unless proven guilty. Prosecutors will be required to establish that he committed the charged offenses, while his defense will have an opportunity to challenge the state's evidence.

The killing has also brought renewed attention to domestic violence in Vermont. Advocates regularly encourage people experiencing abuse, as well as relatives or friends who believe someone may be in danger, to contact local services or law enforcement when it is safe to do so.

Discovery initially identified this case as two separate stories — #10305 and #10422 — but both reports concern the same homicide and arraignment. They should therefore be treated as one story, with the later reporting providing additional details about the alleged weapon, charges and circumstances surrounding the victim's death.
