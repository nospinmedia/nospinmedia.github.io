🗳️ Democrats dominate New England as two GOP governors hold onMaine House race could leave region with just one Republican in Congress 👇

File photoU.S. Capitol building in Washington, D.C.Credit: Architect of the Capitol
PoliticsNovember 4, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 4, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

Democrats won nearly every federal contest across New England in Tuesday's midterm elections, while Republican governors Kelly Ayotte in New Hampshire and Phil Scott in Vermont won reelection, highlighting a sharp difference between the region's choices for Washington and its state governments.

Democrats won every New England U.S. Senate race on the ballot. The largest shift came in Maine, where Democrat Troy Jackson defeated five-term Republican Sen. Susan Collins, ending her nearly 30-year Senate career. In New Hampshire, Democrat Chris Pappas defeated Republican John E. Sununu for the state's open Senate seat. Democrats also retained Senate seats in Massachusetts and Rhode Island.

In the House, Democrats won all nine Massachusetts districts, all five Connecticut districts, both districts in New Hampshire, both in Rhode Island, Vermont's at-large seat and Maine's 1st District.

One federal race remains unresolved. In Maine's 2nd Congressional District, Democrat Matthew Dunlap and former Republican Gov. Paul LePage remain in a close contest for the open seat previously held by Democrat Jared Golden. The outcome will determine whether Republicans have any representation in New England's congressional delegation.

Republicans performed differently in two prominent gubernatorial contests. New Hampshire voters reelected Ayotte, while Vermont voters returned Scott to office. Both victories came despite Democratic success in federal races in their states, providing notable examples of split-ticket voting.

The results leave New England with a predominantly Democratic congressional delegation while voters in two states continue to elect Republican governors. Maine's remaining House contest will determine whether that federal delegation becomes entirely Democratic or includes a single Republican.
