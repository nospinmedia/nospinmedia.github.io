🏗️ 25 Maine areas selected for federal investment tax programNew Opportunity Zones will offer incentives for housing and business development 👇

EconomyNovember 13, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 13, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Twenty-five areas across Maine have been designated as new federal Qualified Opportunity Zones, giving investors tax incentives intended to encourage long-term investment in lower-income communities beginning in 2027.

The U.S. Treasury Department certified the areas after Governor Janet Mills and the Maine Department of Economic and Community Development selected 25 from 78 eligible census tracts. The new designations include 22 rural and three non-rural tracts and will take effect January 1, 2027, remaining in place through the end of 2036.

Communities with designated areas include Sanford, Biddeford, Houlton, Machias, Skowhegan and Dover-Foxcroft. Eight of the 25 tracts were also included in Maine's original Opportunity Zone selections from 2018, including areas in Westbrook, Brunswick, Augusta and East Millinocket.

The federal Opportunity Zone program was created under the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act and made permanent through federal legislation in 2025. Under the updated rules taking effect in 2027, investors can receive a 10% increase in the tax basis of qualifying investments held for five years, rising to 15% for qualifying rural investments. Appreciation on investments held for at least 10 years can also qualify for favorable federal tax treatment.

The designation does not guarantee that investment will occur. Earlier Opportunity Zones supported projects in several Maine communities, but national assessments produced mixed results, and limited reporting requirements made it difficult to measure the program's impact. The 2025 law added annual reporting requirements for Qualified Opportunity Funds, including information on asset values, project locations and employment, with penalties for failing to comply.

Those changes could provide more information about whether the new generation of Opportunity Zones actually produces additional investment and jobs. With 22 of Maine's 25 new zones classified as rural, the enhanced rural incentive could be particularly significant for the state.
