⚖️ Boston judge freezes new USPS mail-ballot requirements14-day order blocks rule as states prepare for midterm voting 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalAugust 28, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 28, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

A federal judge in Boston has temporarily blocked the U.S. Postal Service from enforcing key portions of its newly finalized mail-voting rule ahead of the November 3, 2026 midterm elections. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani issued a 14-day temporary restraining order after a coalition of states and voting-rights organizations argued the requirements exceeded USPS authority and would force election officials to make major operational changes just as absentee-ballot distribution is beginning in parts of the country.

The Postal Service rule, issued in response to an executive order signed by President Trump in March 2026, requires states to obtain USPS approval for federal ballot-envelope designs and submit information about voters receiving mail ballots through an online federal portal. USPS would use those submissions to generate state-specific participation lists. The agency has said it would not determine voter eligibility, maintain voter rolls or count ballots.

The challenge includes Democratic-led states, the District of Columbia, the League of Women Voters and other voting-rights groups. The plaintiffs argued that states have already designed election materials, ordered envelopes and established procedures for the upcoming election, making rapid compliance costly and potentially disruptive. Talwani concluded that the challengers had shown sufficient likelihood of success and potential irreparable harm to justify temporarily freezing enforcement while the court considers whether a longer injunction is warranted.

The Boston ruling comes days after the U.S. Supreme Court lifted an earlier injunction against portions of Trump's executive order because the justices found that challenge had been filed before federal agencies finalized their policies. The Supreme Court specifically noted that its decision did not establish that later agency actions would necessarily be lawful. USPS subsequently finalized its rule, allowing states and voting groups to bring a new challenge focused on the actual requirements.

Talwani scheduled a hearing on a longer-lasting preliminary injunction for September 3. Until then, the disputed USPS requirements remain blocked under the emergency order.
