🛩️ Pilot Killed In Small Plane Crash In Western MaineCessna 182 went down in Sweden after departing nearby Fryeburg airport 👇

HealthNovember 17, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 17, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

A pilot was killed when a single-engine Cessna 182 crashed in the western Maine town of Sweden on Monday, November 16, prompting an investigation by federal aviation authorities.

The aircraft went down near Smarts Hill Road at approximately 3:45 p.m., according to authorities. The Federal Aviation Administration said the pilot was the only person aboard the four-seat aircraft.

Sweden Fire Chief Tommie McKenzie said the plane had departed from White Mountain Regional Airport in Fryeburg before crashing in Sweden, a small Oxford County community northwest of the airport. The airfield was formerly known as Eastern Slopes Regional Airport and is operated by the Eastern Slope Regional Airport Authority.

Emergency response involved the Sweden Fire Department, Oxford County Sheriff's Office and Maine Department of Public Safety. Authorities said no houses or other structures were damaged when the aircraft came down, and no injuries were reported on the ground.

The pilot's identity was not immediately released as officials worked to notify the person's family. An autopsy was expected to be conducted by Maine's medical examiner.

The National Transportation Safety Board is leading the investigation with assistance from the FAA. Federal investigators were expected at the crash site Tuesday to examine the wreckage and circumstances surrounding the flight.

Investigators typically examine the aircraft, weather conditions, pilot information, maintenance records and available flight data before determining a probable cause. Authorities had not announced a suspected cause for Monday's crash or reported what may have happened before the aircraft went down.

The crash occurred in a rural section of western Oxford County near Maine's border with New Hampshire. The NTSB investigation is expected to continue beyond the initial examination of the wreckage before a final probable-cause determination is issued.
