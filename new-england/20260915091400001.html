🚨 Boston rejects future Flock use after surveillance auditPolice plate-reader data was briefly accessible outside city's closed network 👇

LegalSeptember 15, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 15, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

Boston says it will no longer use Flock Safety after a city surveillance report revealed that license-plate and vehicle information collected during a police pilot was briefly accessible to outside law enforcement agencies despite a contract requiring the system to remain closed.

The Boston Police Department began the no-cost pilot on April 1, 2025, deploying approximately 45 automated license-plate reader cameras around the city. The program ultimately ran for 156 days, ending September 4. Boston's agreement specified that Flock would not share the data and that BPD would not provide other agencies with direct access during the trial.

But within three days of the pilot's launch, BPD learned that outside law enforcement agencies could access Boston license-plate and vehicle information through a Flock sharing function that had been enabled despite the contractual restriction. The city's report described the problem as an error by Flock. BPD ordered the company to disable the function immediately. The report does not identify which outside agencies, if any, actually accessed Boston records during those three days or how many motorists' information may have been viewed.

After direct sharing was disabled, BPD received 77 requests from other agencies seeking access to Boston Flock information. Requesters included Massachusetts State Police, Maine State Police, the DEA, ATF and U.S. State Department, among others. The report does not specify which requests were approved.

BPD's audit also found officers conducted 11,004 searches during the five-month trial but entered a crime-related case number for only about half of them. Investigators recommended requiring case numbers for future searches. The report found no recorded complaints or documented misuse during the pilot, and Boston says the Flock data was deleted and equipment removed within 30 days after the trial ended.

City officials have now committed to not using Flock Safety going forward, although Boston continues evaluating automated plate-reader technology from other vendors under separate pilots.
