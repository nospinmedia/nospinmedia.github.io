🛡️ Rhode Island Awarded $1.71 Million For Nonprofit SecurityFederal grants will help protect houses of worship and community organizations 👇

EconomyAugust 21, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 21, 2026 at 11:46 AM ET

Rhode Island will receive $1,710,000 through the Federal Emergency Management Agency's (FEMA) Fiscal Year 2026 Nonprofit Security Grant Program, providing funding to help nonprofit organizations and houses of worship strengthen security against potential terrorist and extremist threats. Members of Rhode Island's congressional delegation announced the awards alongside the Rhode Island Emergency Management Agency (RIEMA), which administers the program within the state.

The grants are intended for nonprofit organizations considered to face elevated security risks, including churches, synagogues, mosques, and other eligible community institutions. Funding may be used for projects such as surveillance cameras, access-control systems, reinforced doors and windows, perimeter security improvements, emergency communications equipment, cybersecurity enhancements, and security planning, training, and exercises. Organizations must demonstrate a heightened risk profile to qualify for assistance.

The Nonprofit Security Grant Program is administered nationwide by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security through FEMA and is designed to improve physical security while integrating nonprofit preparedness efforts with state and local emergency management planning. Rhode Island officials said the funding will help vulnerable organizations improve their ability to prevent, prepare for, respond to, and recover from potential acts of violence or other security threats without placing the financial burden solely on local institutions.

The announcement comes as federal and state officials continue to encourage security investments at community gathering places amid an evolving threat environment. RIEMA oversees the state's grant application process and works with eligible organizations to ensure projects meet federal requirements and can be implemented efficiently. Officials said the program represents an important investment in protecting both the facilities themselves and the people who rely on them for worship, education, and community services.
