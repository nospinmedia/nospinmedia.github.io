🔥 Vermont Households Face Higher Heating Costs This WinterPre-Buy Fuel Plans Rise as Oil Price Volatility Continues 👇

EconomyAugust 22, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 22, 2026 at 6:30 PM ET

Vermont homeowners who rely on heating oil are facing higher costs as fuel dealers begin enrolling customers in winter pre-buy and price-protection programs. Suppliers across the state report that fixed-price and capped-rate contracts are generally running about 20% higher than comparable plans offered in 2025, reflecting continued volatility in global energy markets and uncertainty over wholesale fuel prices. Some dealers have shortened enrollment periods or temporarily limited pre-buy offerings because rapid market swings have made it more difficult to guarantee seasonal pricing.

Residential heating oil prices in Vermont have climbed back toward $5 per gallon, compared with levels below $3.50 per gallon during the fall of last year. Dealers say international supply concerns and instability in global shipping markets have contributed to the increase, leaving many households weighing whether to lock in prices now or risk additional increases later in the heating season. Fuel providers are encouraging customers to secure winter deliveries early, particularly in rural communities where heating oil remains the primary source of home heat.

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, more than 35% of Vermont households rely on heating oil or kerosene as their primary heating fuel, giving the state one of the nation's highest rates of dependence on delivered fuel. The U.S. Energy Information Administration has identified New England as especially vulnerable to winter fuel price fluctuations because of its reliance on heating oil and imported energy supplies.

State officials are encouraging eligible residents to apply for assistance through the Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP), administered by the Vermont Department for Children and Families, while the Vermont Department of Public Service continues providing updated fuel price information and energy-saving guidance. Consumer advocates also recommend improving home weatherization, maintaining heating systems before winter, and considering alternative heating options where practical to help offset potentially higher energy bills.
