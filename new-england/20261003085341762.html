🐾 Three men plead guilty in scheme to poach 41 NH bobcatsPelts were given false New York tags and exported to Canada 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EnvironmentOctober 3, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published October 3, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Three men have pleaded guilty in federal court for their roles in a scheme that illegally killed at least 41 bobcats in New Hampshire and exported their pelts to Canada for sale. Sherwood Dubrey Jr., 48, and Randy Inman, 49, admitted conspiring to violate the federal Lacey Act, while Sherwood Dubrey Sr., 72, pleaded guilty to a separate Lacey Act violation.

Federal prosecutors say Dubrey Jr. and Inman illegally hunted and trapped bobcats in New Hampshire between 2018 and 2022, despite the state having prohibited bobcat hunting and trapping since 1989. Investigators said the men used bait piles, including on state-owned land, and documented some of the kills with photographs and videos taken through rifle scopes.

Court records also revealed text messages showing the men knew their activities were illegal. In one exchange, Inman described shooting or "choking out" an illegally trapped bobcat. In another, Dubrey Jr. told his father that he opposed efforts to legalize bobcat hunting in New Hampshire because legal hunting would create additional "competition."

After the animals were killed, prosecutors say the pelts were transported out of New Hampshire. Dubrey Sr. obtained CITES export tags that falsely represented the bobcats as having been legally harvested in New York. Those tags allowed the pelts to be exported to Canada, where they were offered for sale through the commercial fur market.

As part of their plea agreements, Dubrey Jr. and Inman must pay New Hampshire $500 for each illegally killed bobcat, totaling at least $20,500. Dubrey Jr. and Inman each face up to five years in federal prison and three years of supervised release, while Dubrey Sr. faces up to one year in prison and one year of supervised release.

The case was investigated by federal and state wildlife authorities and prosecuted by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of New Hampshire and the Justice Department's Environmental Crimes Section. Sentencing is scheduled for January 2027.
