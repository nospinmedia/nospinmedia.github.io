⚖️ Mass. judge strikes down $600 million in teacher-training cutsRuling finds Education Department policy violated federal law 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
EducationSeptember 19, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 19, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

A federal judge in Massachusetts has struck down a Trump administration policy that led the U.S. Department of Education to terminate roughly $600 million in teacher-training grants, ruling that the directive violated federal administrative law. U.S. District Judge Angel Kelley found that the department's policy was arbitrary and capricious and failed to adequately account for the reliance of states, schools, educators and students on the programs.

The Education Department issued the directive in February 2025, targeting grants it said supported "divisive ideologies," including diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives. The cancellations affected more than 100 programs funded through the Teacher Quality Partnership and Supporting Effective Educator Development programs, which support teacher preparation and efforts to address shortages in areas including math, science and special education.

Eight states — Massachusetts, California, Colorado, Illinois, Maryland, New Jersey, New York and Wisconsin — challenged the policy. In Massachusetts, canceled funding included a $2.3 million grant supporting a UMass Amherst program that trains paraprofessionals to become licensed early-childhood educators and a $5.9 million grant involving UMass Boston and Boston Public Schools that trains bilingual educators.

Kelley found that the Education Department moved too quickly, including failing to follow requirements for notice and public comment and failing to adequately consider how extensively states and education programs relied on the grants. The litigation previously reached the U.S. Supreme Court, which in April 2025 allowed the federal government to continue withholding the money while the case proceeded.

The new ruling invalidates the department's policy but does not automatically restore the canceled grant money. Kelley said claims seeking recovery of those funds must be pursued separately in the U.S. Court of Federal Claims. The Education Department had not immediately commented on the decision.
