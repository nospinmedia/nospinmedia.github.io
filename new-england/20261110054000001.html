⚖️ Massachusetts State Police agrees to pay Karen Read $100,000Settlement resolves her claims against agency but other lawsuits remain 👇

IllustrationIllustration representing court rulings and legal proceedings.Credit: AI-generated illustration
LegalNovember 10, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published November 10, 2026 at 12:30 PM ET

The Massachusetts State Police has agreed to pay Karen Read $100,000 to resolve her lawsuit against the agency over its hiring and retention of investigators involved in the case surrounding the death of Boston Police Officer John O'Keefe.

Read filed the lawsuit in June against State Police and the Town of Canton, alleging flawed hiring and supervision allowed biased officers to participate in the investigation that led to her prosecution. Read was acquitted of second-degree murder in June 2025 following a retrial in O'Keefe's 2022 death.

Court filings dated November 2 show State Police consented to a $100,000 judgment. The agency argued that Read's remaining tort claims fall under the Massachusetts Tort Claims Act, which limits damages against a public employer to $100,000. Read's attorneys agreed to the judgment while explicitly reserving her ability to pursue other claims and legal arguments against other people and entities.

State Police said it agreed to the settlement to avoid prolonged litigation and additional costs to taxpayers. The agreement does not constitute a finding that the agency committed the misconduct alleged by Read.

Read's lawsuit focused in part on former State Police Trooper Michael Proctor, the lead investigator in her criminal case. Proctor came under scrutiny after derogatory and unprofessional text messages about Read were introduced during her first trial. State Police fired him in March 2025 and said in its recent filing that it condemns racist, sexist and other offensive messages cited in Read's complaint.

The settlement resolves Read's claims against the State Police agency, but it does not end the broader civil litigation surrounding the case. Her claims against the Town of Canton remain active, and separate lawsuits involving Read, investigators, witnesses and O'Keefe's family are also pending.
