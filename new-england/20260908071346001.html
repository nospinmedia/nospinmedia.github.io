🗳️ New Hampshire voters head to polls for statewide primarySenate, governor and House nominees will be decided 👇

HealthSeptember 8, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published September 8, 2026 at 8:45 AM ET

New Hampshire voters head to the polls Tuesday for a statewide primary that will determine party nominees for an open U.S. Senate seat, governor, both congressional districts and hundreds of state and local offices ahead of the November 3 general election.

The highest-profile contest is the race to succeed Democratic U.S. Sen. Jeanne Shaheen, who is not seeking reelection after three terms. Eight Republicans are competing for the nomination, including former U.S. Sens. John E. Sununu and Scott Brown. Sununu represented New Hampshire in the Senate from 2003 until Shaheen defeated him in 2008. Brown previously represented Massachusetts and later challenged Shaheen unsuccessfully in New Hampshire in 2014.

On the Democratic side, U.S. Rep. Chris Pappas is among five candidates seeking the Senate nomination. His opponents include medical scientist Karishma Manzur, who has gained attention during the closing weeks of the primary campaign.

Pappas' Senate campaign has also created an open race in the 1st Congressional District. Seven Democrats, including Stefany Shaheen and former congressional candidate Maura Sullivan, are competing for their party's nomination. Four Republicans are running, including Anthony DiLorenzo and Hollie Noveletsky.

In the 2nd Congressional District, first-term Democratic Rep. Maggie Goodlander faces state Rep. Paige Beauchemin in the Democratic primary. Four Republicans are competing for their party's nomination, including Lily Tang Williams, who was the Republican nominee for the seat in 2024.

Republican Gov. Kelly Ayotte is seeking a second two-year term and faces Shaun Fife and Bob Wayne McClory in the Republican primary. Former Executive Councilor Cinde Warmington is unopposed for the Democratic nomination.

Tuesday's election is also among New Hampshire's final September state primaries. Legislation passed this year moves future state primaries to the second Tuesday in June, beginning in 2028.
