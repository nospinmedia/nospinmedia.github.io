⚖️ Boston Officer Seeks Dismissal of Manslaughter CaseDefense alleges prosecutorial misconduct in fatal Roxbury shooting 👇

LegalAugust 30, 2026By No Spin Media Newsroom · Published August 30, 2026 at 3:45 PM ET

Attorneys representing Boston Police Officer Nicholas O'Malley have filed a 27-page motion in Suffolk Superior Court seeking dismissal of the voluntary manslaughter charge stemming from the fatal March 11, 2026, shooting of Stephenson King Jr. during a reported carjacking investigation in Roxbury. If the indictment is not dismissed, the defense is asking the court to appoint a special prosecutor, alleging that the Suffolk County District Attorney's Office improperly pursued the case.

According to the defense filing, O'Malley fired three rounds because he believed King, who was driving a stolen vehicle, posed an immediate threat to him and his partner after the vehicle accelerated toward officers and struck a police cruiser on Linwood Street. Defense attorneys David Yannetti and Christina Pujals Ronan argue that prosecutors failed to properly apply longstanding legal standards governing police use of force and instead relied on an improper interpretation of the evidence presented to the grand jury.

Prosecutors contend the shooting was not legally justified and obtained a grand jury indictment charging O'Malley with voluntary manslaughter. The case centers on whether the officer's use of deadly force complied with Massachusetts law and department policies governing shootings involving moving vehicles. The defense argues that the officer's actions should be evaluated under the U.S. Supreme Court's decision in Graham v. Connor, which requires courts to judge an officer's conduct from the perspective of a reasonable officer confronting a rapidly evolving situation rather than with hindsight.

The court has not ruled on the defense motion, and the criminal case remains pending. Neither the filing nor the request for a special prosecutor determines the merits of the underlying charges. The judge will decide whether the defense has shown sufficient legal grounds to dismiss the indictment or require another prosecutor to assume responsibility for the case.
